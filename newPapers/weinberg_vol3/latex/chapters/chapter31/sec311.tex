\subsection{The Metric Superfield}

Supergravity necessarily involves spinor as well as tensor fields, so we will
have to describe gravitational fields in terms of a vierbein (or tetrad)
\(e^a{}_\mu(x)\) rather than a metric, which is related to the vierbein by
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\eta_{ab}e^a{}_\mu(x)e^b{}_\nu(x)\,.
  \tag{31.1.1}\label{eq:31.1.1}
\end{equation}
The indices \(\mu,\nu\), etc.\ label general coordinates, while indices
\(a,b\), etc.\ label coordinates in a locally inertial coordinate system,
with \(\eta_{ab}\) the usual diagonal matrix with elements
\(-1,+1,+1,+1\). In the vierbein formalism, the action must be supposed to be
invariant under two different sorts of symmetry transformations: general
coordinate transformations \(x^\mu\to x'^\mu(x)\), under which the vierbein
\(e^a{}_\mu(x)\) is transformed into \(e'^a{}_\mu(x')\), where
\begin{equation}
  e'^a{}_\mu(x')
  =\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x'^\mu}e^a{}_\nu(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.2}\label{eq:31.1.2}
\end{equation}
and local Lorentz transformations, under which
\begin{equation}
  e^a{}_\mu(x)\to\Lambda^a{}_b(x)e^b{}_\mu(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.3}\label{eq:31.1.3}
\end{equation}
with \(\Lambda^a{}_b(x)\) an arbitrary real matrix subject to the constraints
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{ab}\Lambda^a{}_c(x)\Lambda^b{}_d(x)=\eta_{cd}\,.
  \tag{31.1.4}\label{eq:31.1.4}
\end{equation}
An elementary review of this formalism is given in the appendix to this
chapter.

A weak gravitational field is one for which the vierbein is close to the
unit matrix. In such a field, the vierbein may conveniently be written as
\begin{equation}
  e^a{}_\mu(x)=\delta^a{}_\mu+2\kappa\phi^a{}_\mu(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.5}\label{eq:31.1.5}
\end{equation}
where \(\phi^a{}_\mu(x)\) is small. As we shall see in Section 31.2, if
\(\phi^a{}_\mu\) is to be a conventionally normalized field, then the
constant \(\kappa\) should be expressed in terms of Newton's constant \(G\)
by \(\kappa=\sqrt{8\pi G}\). The closeness of the vierbein to the unit matrix
is preserved by a small coordinate transformation
\begin{equation}
  x'^\mu\to x'^\mu+\xi^\mu(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.6}\label{eq:31.1.6}
\end{equation}
and also by small local Lorentz transformations
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda^a{}_b(x)=\delta^a{}_b+\omega^a{}_b(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.7}\label{eq:31.1.7}
\end{equation}
where \(\xi^\mu(x)\) and \(\omega^a{}_b(x)\) are of the same order as
\(\phi^a{}_\mu(x)\), and
\(\omega_{ab}(x)\equiv\eta_{ac}\omega^c{}_b(x)\) is constrained by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.4} to satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{ab}(x)=-\omega_{ba}(x)\,.
  \tag{31.1.8}\label{eq:31.1.8}
\end{equation}
The combined transformations \eqref{eq:31.1.2} and \eqref{eq:31.1.3} then
become
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\to\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)
  +\frac{1}{2\kappa}
   \left[-\frac{\partial\xi_\mu(x)}{\partial x^\nu}
         +\omega_{\mu\nu}(x)\right].
  \tag{31.1.9}\label{eq:31.1.9}
\end{equation}
We are now dropping the distinction between general coordinate indices
\(\mu,\nu\), etc.\ and local Lorentz coordinate indices \(a,b\), etc., and
raising and lowering all indices with \(\eta^{\mu\nu}\) and \(\eta_{\mu\nu}\).
In terms of the metric \eqref{eq:31.1.1}, the weak field assumption
\eqref{eq:31.1.5} becomes
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\eta_{\mu\nu}+2\kappa h_{\mu\nu}(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.10}\label{eq:31.1.10}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  h_{\mu\nu}(x)\equiv\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)+\phi_{\nu\mu}(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.11}\label{eq:31.1.11}
\end{equation}
while the transformation law \eqref{eq:31.1.9} reads
\begin{equation}
  h_{\mu\nu}(x)\to h_{\mu\nu}(x)
  -\frac{1}{2\kappa}
   \left[\frac{\partial\xi_\mu(x)}{\partial x^\nu}
         +\frac{\partial\xi_\nu(x)}{\partial x^\mu}\right].
  \tag{31.1.12}\label{eq:31.1.12}
\end{equation}

By use of the supersymmetry algebra we showed in Section 25.4 that the
graviton has a fermionic superpartner, the gravitino, with helicities
\(\pm3/2\). We saw in Section 5.9 that a self-charge-conjugate massless
particle with helicities \(\pm1\) can only have low-energy interactions if
described by a real field \(A_\mu(x)\) whose interactions are invariant under
a gauge transformation
\(A_\mu(x)\to A_\mu(x)+\partial_\mu\Lambda(x)\). In the same way, a
self-charge-conjugate massless particle with helicities \(\pm3/2\) can only
have low-energy interactions if it is described by a Majorana field
\(\psi_\mu(x)\) with an extra vector index \(\mu\), with invariance under a
gauge transformation
\begin{equation}
  \psi_\mu(x)\to\psi_\mu(x)+\partial_\mu\psi(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.13}\label{eq:31.1.13}
\end{equation}
where \(\psi(x)\) is an arbitrary Majorana
field~\hyperref[ch31-ref-3a]{[3a]}. We need now to consider how fields
\(\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\) and \(\psi_\mu(x)\) with these transformation properties
can be put into a superfield.

We found in Section 27.1 that a gauge field \(V_\nu(x)\) can be regarded as
the \(V_\nu\)-component, defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.10} as the coefficient
of \(i(\bar\theta\gamma_5\gamma_\nu\theta)/2\), of a real scalar superfield
\(V(x,\theta)\). Similarly, we would like to incorporate the vierbein field
\(\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\) and the gravitino field \(\psi_\mu(x)\) into a
\emph{vector} superfield \(H_\mu(x,\theta)\), known as the \emph{metric
superfield}. The question is: how are \(\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\) and
\(\psi_\mu(x)\) related to the components of this superfield?

To address this question, note that supersymmetry requires the `gauge'
transformations \eqref{eq:31.1.9} and \eqref{eq:31.1.13} to be special cases
of a transformation of the whole metric superfield
\begin{equation}
  H_\mu(x,\theta)\to H_\mu(x,\theta)+\Delta_\mu(x,\theta)\,.
  \tag{31.1.14}\label{eq:31.1.14}
\end{equation}
Furthermore, a weak gravitational field \(h_{\mu\nu}\) interacts with an
energy-momentum tensor \(T^{\mu\nu}\) which as we found in Section 26.7 is a
linear combination of components of a real vector superfield \(\Theta^\mu\),
so we expect the interaction of the whole superfield \(H_\mu\) with matter to
take the form
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{int}}
  =2\kappa\int d^4x\,[H_\mu\Theta^\mu]_D\,.
  \tag{31.1.15}\label{eq:31.1.15}
\end{equation}
(Later in this section we will confirm the correctness of the coefficient
\(2\kappa\), with \(\Theta^\mu\) normalized as in Section 26.7.) The
supercurrent \(\Theta^\mu\) was shown in Section 26.7 to satisfy the
conservation conditions
\begin{equation}
  \gamma^\mu\SuperD\Theta_\mu=\SuperD X\,,
  \tag{31.1.16}\label{eq:31.1.16}
\end{equation}
where \(X\) is a real chiral scalar superfield (the sum of a left-chiral
scalar superfield and its complex conjugate) and \(\SuperD\) is the
four-component superderivative \eqref{eq:26.2.26}. It follows that this
interaction is invariant under a transformation of the form
\eqref{eq:31.1.14}, with \(\Delta_\mu\) of the form
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_\mu=(\bar{\SuperD}\gamma_\mu\Xi)\,,
  \tag{31.1.17}\label{eq:31.1.17}
\end{equation}
where \(\Xi(x,\theta)\) is a superfield subject to the manifestly
supersymmetric condition
\begin{equation}
  (\bar{\SuperD}\SuperD)(\bar{\SuperD}\Xi)=0\,.
  \tag{31.1.18}\label{eq:31.1.18}
\end{equation}
This can be seen by recalling from Section 26.7 that the chirality condition
on \(X\) allows us to write it in the form
\begin{equation}
  X=(\bar{\SuperD}\SuperD)\Omega\,,
  \tag{31.1.19}\label{eq:31.1.19}
\end{equation}
with \(\Omega\) in general a non-local superfield. From
Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.16} we then find
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \int[\Theta^\mu(\bar{\SuperD}\gamma_\mu\Xi)]_D
  &=-\int[((\bar{\SuperD}\Theta^\mu)\gamma_\mu\Xi)]_D
   =-\int[((\bar{\SuperD}X)\Xi)]_D
  \\
  &=+\int[\Omega(\bar{\SuperD}\SuperD)(\bar{\SuperD}\Xi)]_D=0\,,
  \end{aligned}
\]
so that the interaction \eqref{eq:31.1.15} is invariant under the
transformation
\(H_\mu\to H_\mu+(\bar{\SuperD}\gamma_\mu\Xi)\). (We can also obtain the same
result without using the representation \eqref{eq:31.1.19} for \(X\), by
noting from Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.25} that the \(D\)-term of the product of a
chiral superfield like \(X\) which satisfies Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.3.1} and
\eqref{eq:26.3.2} and a linear superfield like
\((\bar{\SuperD}\Xi)\) which satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.3.45} is a spacetime
derivative.)
