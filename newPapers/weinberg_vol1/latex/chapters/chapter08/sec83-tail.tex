where $H_M$ is the Hamiltonian for matter fields, excluding their
electromagnetic interactions
\[
H_M\equiv\int d^3x\,
  \bigl(P_n\dot Q^n-\mathcal L_{\mathrm{matter}}\bigr).
\]
Using the solution \eqref{eq:8.2.9} for $A^0$, this is
\begin{equation}
H=\int d^3x\left[
  \frac12\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{\!\perp}^{2}
  +\frac12(\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)^2
  -\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf A+\frac12J^0A^0
\right]+H_M.
\tag{8.3.11}\label{eq:8.3.11}
\end{equation}
The term $\frac12J^0A^0$ may look peculiar, but this is nothing but the
familiar Coulomb energy
\begin{align}
V_{\mathrm{Coul}}
&=\frac12\int d^3x\,J^0A^0
\notag\\
&=\frac12\int d^3x\int d^3y\,
  \frac{J^0(\mathbf x)J^0(\mathbf y)}{4\pi\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf y\rvert}.
\tag{8.3.12}\label{eq:8.3.12}
\end{align}
The reader can verify, using the commutation relations \eqref{eq:8.3.5}, that the
rate of change of any operator function $F$ of $\mathbf A$ and
$\boldsymbol\Pi$ is given by $i\dot F=[F,H]$, as it should be.
