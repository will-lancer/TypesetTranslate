\exerciseeditionsection{Solutions to Wald's Problems}
\ExerciseEditorialNote

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.8em,itemsep=1.2em]
\WaldSolution{1}
Choose a unitary spin frame.  A self-adjoint symmetric two-spinor is represented
by a traceless Hermitian matrix, hence by
\(x^i\sigma_i\) with \(x^i\in\mathbb R\).  This identifies the space with
\(\mathbb R^3\), and direct contraction gives a negative multiple of
\(\delta_{ij}x^iy^j\).  The action
\(X\mapsto UXU^\dagger\) preserves this metric and defines
\(\mathrm{SU}(2)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)\).  Its kernel is \(\{I,-I\}\), and every
rotation is obtained from
\(U=\exp[-i\theta n^i\sigma_i/2]\).  Since \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\cong S^3\) is
simply connected, this is the universal double cover of \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\).

\WaldSolution{2}
In a normalized spin frame,
\[
 \partial_{AA'}=\frac1{\sqrt2}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \partial_t+\partial_z&\partial_x-i\partial_y\\
 \partial_x+i\partial_y&\partial_t-\partial_z
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Writing the two spinors in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:13.1.60} and
\eqref{eq:13.1.61} as two-component columns produces four coupled first-order
component equations.  Assemble the columns into a four-spinor \(\Psi\).  With
the Weyl gamma matrices and a constant phase redefinition of one column, those
four equations are
\[
 (i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-m)\Psi=0,
\]
the standard Dirac equation.

\WaldSolution{3}
Insert
\(F_{AA'BB'}=\phi_{AB}\overline\epsilon_{A'B'}+
\overline\phi_{A'B'}\epsilon_{AB}\) into
\(\nabla^aF_{ab}=0\).  Compatibility of the spin connection with both
epsilon spinors gives
\[
 \nabla^{AA'}F_{AA'BB'}=
 \overline\epsilon^{A'}{}_{B'}\nabla^A{}_{A'}\phi_{AB}
 +\epsilon^A{}_B\nabla_A{}^{A'}\overline\phi_{A'B'}.
\]
Its independent unprimed and primed parts vanish precisely when
\(\nabla^{AA'}\phi_{AB}=0\) and its conjugate vanish.  The latter pair is
equivalent to the divergence equation together with the Bianchi equation for the
real two-form \(F_{ab}\).

\WaldSolution{4}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item Apply \(\partial^{BA_1'}\) to
\(\partial_{A_1A_1'}\phi^{A_1\cdots A_n}=0\).  The flat-space identity
\(\partial^{BA'}\partial_{AA'}=\tfrac12\epsilon_A{}^B\Box\) gives
\(\Box\phi^{B A_2\cdots A_n}=0\).

\item Let \(C_{A_1'}{}^{A_2\cdots A_n}\) denote the constraint.  Commuting
flat derivatives and using \(\Box\phi=0\) gives \(\Box C=0\).  Zero Cauchy
data for \(C\) and its normal derivative imply \(C=0\) by uniqueness.

\item Tangential differentiation of the initial constraint gives the form
stated in the hint,
\(\partial_{BB'}C_{A_1'}=n_{BB'}\alpha_{A_1'}\).  Contracting with
\(\overline\epsilon^{B'A_1'}\), then using \(\Box\phi=0\), makes the left
side vanish.  For timelike \(n^{BB'}\), the map
\(\alpha_{A'}\mapsto n_B{}^{A'}\alpha_{A'}\) is invertible, so
\(\alpha_{A'}=0\).  The normal derivative constraint follows from the
constraint itself.
\end{enumerate}

\WaldSolution{5}
The Weyl tensor has spinor form
\[
 C_{AA'BB'CC'DD'}=
 \Psi_{ABCD}\overline\epsilon_{A'B'}\overline\epsilon_{C'D'}
 +\overline\Psi_{A'B'C'D'}\epsilon_{AB}\epsilon_{CD}.
\]
A null vector is \(k^{AA'}=\kappa^A\overline\kappa^{A'}\).  Every tensor
principal-null-direction contraction therefore factors into the polynomial
\(\Psi_{ABCD}\kappa^A\kappa^B\kappa^C\kappa^D\) and its conjugate.
The multiplicity of the principal spinor \(\kappa^A\) is consequently the
multiplicity of the corresponding principal null direction.  Multiplicities
\(1111,211,22,31,4\) give, respectively, Petrov types I, II, D, III, and N;
the zero spinor gives type O, exactly as in Table~7.1.

\WaldSolution{6}
In vacuum the Riemann tensor equals the Weyl tensor.  Substitute its spinor
decomposition into \(\nabla_{[a}C_{bc]de}=0\), contract the primed epsilon
spinors, and use total symmetry of \(\Psi_{ABCD}\).  The independent
unprimed equation is
\[
 \nabla_{AA'}\Psi^{ABCD}=0,
\]
with the primed equation its complex conjugate.

\WaldSolution{7}
The spinor decomposition of the tensor in Chapter 4, Problem 6 is, up to the
normalization fixed there,
\[
 T_{AA'BB'CC'DD'}=4\Psi_{ABCD}
 \overline\Psi_{A'B'C'D'}.
\]
Each factor is totally symmetric, so \(T_{abcd}=T_{(abcd)}\); contraction of
any two indices vanishes because an epsilon spinor contracts a symmetric pair.
In vacuum, Problem 6 and its conjugate give \(\nabla^aT_{abcd}=0\).  For future
timelike vectors \(t_i^{AA'}\), the contraction with four such vectors is the
squared norm of the corresponding Weyl spinor contraction, hence is
nonnegative.  These are the required Bel--Robinson properties.

\WaldSolution{8}
Use one curved-space Dirac equation to eliminate the primed spinor from the
other, then commute the two spinor derivatives.  The commutator contributes the
Ricci scalar term; with Wald's convention \(R=24\Lambda\), its contraction on an
unprimed one-spinor is \(-6\Lambda\phi^A\).  The result is
\[
 (\Box+m^2-6\Lambda)\phi^A=0.
\]
Conversely, the second spinor reconstructed from the first Dirac equation obeys
the companion equation, so the second-order equation plus the initial Dirac
constraint is equivalent to the original pair.
\end{enumerate}
