\subsection{Conjugate Points}\label{sec:9.3}

\setcounter{theorem}{0}

Let \(M\) be any manifold on which a connection is defined and let \(\gamma\) be a
geodesic with tangent \(v^a\). A solution \(\eta^a\) of the geodesic deviation equation

\begin{equation}
  v^a\nabla_a(v^b\nabla_b\eta^c)
  =-R_{abd}{}^c\eta^bv^av^d
  \tag{9.3.1}\label{eq:9.3.1}
\end{equation}

(see Eq.~\eqref{eq:3.3.18}) is called a \emph{Jacobi field} on \(\gamma\). A pair of points
\(p,q\in\gamma\) are said to be \emph{conjugate} if there exists a Jacobi field \(\eta^a\)
which is not identically zero but vanishes at both \(p\) and \(q\). Thus, roughly
speaking, \(p\) and \(q\) are conjugate if an ``infinitesimally nearby'' geodesic intersects
\(\gamma\) at both \(p\) and \(q\) as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:9.2}. As a simple
example, the north and south poles of the sphere in Riemannian geometry are
conjugate points of every ``longitudinal geodesic.'' Note, however, that the definition
requires only the existence of a Jacobi field vanishing at \(p\) and \(q\); there need not
exist an actual geodesic other than \(\gamma\) which passes through \(p\) and \(q\).
Conversely, the existence of a geodesic other than \(\gamma\) which passes through \(p\)
and \(q\) does not mean that \(p\) and \(q\) are conjugate or even that some point
conjugate to \(p\) exists between \(p\) and \(q\).

Conjugate points are of interest because, as we shall see below, in spacetimes they
characterize the stage at which a timelike geodesic fails to be a local maximum of
proper time between two points and a null geodesic fails to remain on the boundary
of the future of a point. (In Riemannian geometry, they similarly characterize the
stage at which a geodesic fails, locally, to be the minimum length curve connecting
points.) We shall consider, first, conjugate points on timelike geodesics. We begin
by using the results of the previous section to obtain criteria for the existence of
conjugate points.

Let \(\gamma\) be a timelike geodesic with tangent \(\xi^a\) and let \(p\in\gamma\).
Consider the congruence of all timelike geodesics passing through \(p\). (This
congruence, of course, is singular at \(p\) itself.) Then every Jacobi field which
vanishes at \(p\) is a deviation vector for this congruence. We shall show that a
point \(q\in\gamma\) lying to the future of \(p\) is conjugate to \(p\) if and only if the
expansion, \(\theta\), of this congruence approaches \(-\infty\) at \(q\). For this purpose,
it is convenient to introduce an orthonormal basis of spatial vectors
\(e_1^a,e_2^a,e_3^a\) orthogonal to \(\xi^a\) and parallelly propagated along \(\gamma\).
Since the components, \(\eta^\mu\), of the deviation vectors \(\eta^a\) for this
congruence satisfy the linear ordinary differential equations,

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd^2\eta^\mu}{\dd\tau^2}
  =-\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\nu}
    R_{\alpha\beta\nu}{}^\mu\xi^\alpha\eta^\beta\xi^\nu ,
  \tag{9.3.2}\label{eq:9.3.2}
\end{equation}

the value of \(\eta^\mu\) at time \(\tau\) must depend linearly on the initial data
\(\eta^\mu(0)\) and \(\dd\eta^\mu/\dd\tau(0)\) at \(p\). Since, by construction,
\(\eta^\mu(0)=0\) for this congruence, we must have

\begin{equation}
  \eta^\mu(\tau)
  =\sum_{\nu=1}^3 A^\mu{}_\nu(\tau)\frac{\dd\eta^\nu}{\dd\tau}(0) .
  \tag{9.3.3}\label{eq:9.3.3}
\end{equation}

Substituting this in Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.3.2}, we see that the matrix
\(A^\mu{}_\nu(\tau)\) satisfies the equation

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd^2 A^\mu{}_\nu}{\dd\tau^2}
  =-\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\sigma}
    R_{\alpha\beta\sigma}{}^\mu\xi^\alpha\xi^\sigma A^\beta{}_\nu .
  \tag{9.3.4}\label{eq:9.3.4}
\end{equation}

Clearly, we also have \(A^\mu{}_\nu(0)=0\) and
\(\dd A^\mu{}_\nu/\dd\tau(0)=\delta^\mu{}_\nu\). Now, \(q\) will be conjugate to
\(p\) if and only if there exists nontrivial initial data [i.e.,
\(\dd\eta^\mu/\dd\tau(0)\ne0\)] for which \(\eta^\mu=0\) at \(q\). By
Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.3.3}, this occurs if and only if \(\det A^\mu{}_\nu=0\) at \(q\), so
\(\det A^\mu{}_\nu=0\) is the necessary and sufficient condition for a conjugate point
to \(p\). Note that between conjugate points we have \(\det A^\mu{}_\nu\ne0\), so the
inverse of \(A^\mu{}_\nu\) exists.

Clearly, the matrix \(A^\mu{}_\nu\) must be related to the tensor field
\(B_{ab}=\nabla_b\xi_a\) of the congruence. To find this relation, we note that

\begin{align}
  \frac{\dd\eta^\mu}{\dd\tau}
  &=\xi^a\nabla_a\eta^\mu
    =\xi^a\nabla_a\bigl[(e_\mu)_b\eta^b\bigr]\notag\\
  &=(e_\mu)_b\xi^a\nabla_a\eta^b\notag\\
  &=(e_\mu)_bB^b{}_a\eta^a\notag\\
  &=\sum_{\alpha=1}^3B^\mu{}_\alpha\eta^\alpha .
  \tag{9.3.5}\label{eq:9.3.5}
\end{align}

However, using Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.3.3}, we find

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd\eta^\mu}{\dd\tau}
  =\sum_\nu
    \frac{\dd A^\mu{}_\nu}{\dd\tau}
    \frac{\dd\eta^\nu}{\dd\tau}(0) ,
  \tag{9.3.6}\label{eq:9.3.6}
\end{equation}

so we obtain

\begin{equation}
  \sum_\nu
  \frac{\dd A^\mu{}_\nu}{\dd\tau}\frac{\dd\eta^\nu}{\dd\tau}(0)
  =
  \sum_{\alpha,\nu}
  B^\mu{}_\alpha A^\alpha{}_\nu\frac{\dd\eta^\nu}{\dd\tau}(0) .
  \tag{9.3.7}\label{eq:9.3.7}
\end{equation}

Thus, in matrix notation, we find

\begin{equation}
  \dd A/\dd\tau=BA ,
  \tag{9.3.8}\label{eq:9.3.8}
\end{equation}

i.e.,

\begin{equation}
  B=\frac{\dd A}{\dd\tau}A^{-1} .
  \tag{9.3.9}\label{eq:9.3.9}
\end{equation}

Consequently, we obtain

\begin{equation}
  \theta=\operatorname{tr}B
  =\operatorname{tr}\left[\frac{\dd A}{\dd\tau}A^{-1}\right] ,
  \tag{9.3.10}\label{eq:9.3.10}
\end{equation}

where ``tr'' denotes the trace of the matrix. However, it follows from the formula for
the inverse of a matrix that for any nonsingular matrix \(A\), we have\footnote{This formula
previously was used in Eq.~\eqref{eq:3.4.8} above. It is closely related to the identity
\(\exp(\operatorname{tr}C)=\det[\exp(C)]\) for any matrix \(C\), which can be proven by
putting \(C\) in upper triangular form.}

\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{tr}\left[\frac{\dd A}{\dd\tau}A^{-1}\right]
  =\frac1{\det A}\frac{\dd}{\dd\tau}(\det A) ,
  \tag{9.3.11}\label{eq:9.3.11}
\end{equation}

so that

\begin{equation}
  \theta=\frac{\dd}{\dd\tau}\bigl(\ln\lvert\det A\rvert\bigr) .
  \tag{9.3.12}\label{eq:9.3.12}
\end{equation}

Since \(A\) satisfies the ordinary differential equation~\eqref{eq:9.3.4},
\(\dd(\det A)/\dd\tau\) cannot become infinite anywhere along \(\gamma\). Therefore,
if \(\theta\to-\infty\) at \(q\), it follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.3.12} that
\(\det A\to0\) at \(q\). Conversely, if \(\det A\to0\) at \(q\), it follows that
\(\theta\to-\infty\) at \(q\). Thus, as desired, we have proven that a necessary and
sufficient condition for \(q\) to be conjugate to \(p\) is that for the congruence of
timelike geodesics emanating from \(p\), we have \(\theta\to-\infty\) at \(q\).

The congruence of timelike geodesics passing through \(p\) is hypersurface
orthogonal. Indeed, as proven in lemma~4.5.2 of Hawking and Ellis (1973), within a
convex normal neighborhood of \(p\), the geodesics in this congruence are orthogonal
to the surfaces of constant proper time \(\tau\) along the geodesics and, from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.2.14}, it follows that if \(\omega_{ab}\) vanishes at one time, it must
vanish at all times. Thus, we may use Lemma~\ref{lem:9.2.1} to establish immediately
the following result on the existence of conjugate points:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:9.3.1}
Let \((M,g_{ab})\) be a spacetime satisfying \(R_{ab}\xi^a\xi^b\geq0\) for all
timelike \(\xi^a\). Let \(\gamma\) be a timelike geodesic and let \(p\in\gamma\).
Suppose the convergence of the congruence of timelike geodesics emanating into
the future from \(p\) attains the negative value \(\theta_0\) at \(r\in\gamma\).
Then within proper time \(\tau\leq3/\lvert\theta_0\rvert\) from \(r\) along
\(\gamma\) there exists a point \(q\) conjugate to \(p\), assuming, of course,
that \(\gamma\) extends that far.
\end{proposition}

In fact, the existence of a pair of conjugate points on a complete timelike geodesic
\(\gamma\) can be proven under far weaker hypotheses than those of
Proposition~\ref{prop:9.3.1}. If \(R_{ab}\xi^a\xi^b\geq0\) everywhere along the
geodesic and \(R_{ab}\xi^a\xi^b>0\) at point \(r\in\gamma\), then one can show that
for \(p\) sufficiently far from \(r\), the expansion of the timelike geodesic congruence
emanating from \(p\) must be negative at \(r\). Hence \(p\) will have a conjugate
point \(q\) on \(\gamma\). However, even if \(R_{ab}\xi^a\xi^b=0\) everywhere on
\(\gamma\), if the curvature terms on the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.2.12} are
nonzero at \(r\in\gamma\), then \(\sigma_{ab}\) cannot vanish in a neighborhood of
\(r\). Since \(-\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}\) also appears on the right-hand side of
Raychaudhuri's equation, a similar argument establishes existence of conjugate
points. Thus, all that is required for existence of conjugate points on \(\gamma\) is
that \(R_{ab}\xi^a\xi^b\geq0\) everywhere on \(\gamma\) and
\(R_{abcd}\xi^b\xi^d\ne0\) at at least one point of \(\gamma\). The full proof of this
result can be found in proposition~4.4.2 of Hawking and Ellis (1973).

A spacetime \((M,g_{ab})\) is said to satisfy the \emph{timelike generic condition} if each
timelike geodesic possesses at least one point at which \(R_{abcd}\xi^a\xi^d\ne0\).
Although the timelike generic condition may fail to hold in special, idealized spacetime
models, it seems reasonable that it will hold in all physically realistic ``generic''
spacetimes. In view of the remarks of the previous paragraph, we have the following
result which plays an important role in the proof of theorem~9.5.4 quoted in
section~9.5.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:9.3.2}
Let \((M,g_{ab})\) satisfy the timelike generic condition and suppose
\(R_{ab}\xi^a\xi^b\geq0\) for all timelike \(\xi^a\). Then every complete timelike
geodesic possesses a pair of conjugate points.
\end{proposition}


Next, we turn our attention to the relation between conjugate points and the extremal
length properties of timelike geodesics. Let \(p,q\in M\) and consider a smooth
one-parameter family of smooth timelike curves \(\lambda_\alpha(t)\) from \(p\) to \(q\),
where the curve parameter \(t\) is chosen so that for all \(\alpha\) we have
\(\lambda_\alpha(a)=p\), \(\lambda_\alpha(b)=q\). We denote the tangent vectors,
\((\partial/\partial t)^a\), by \(T^a\) and the deviation vectors,
\((\partial/\partial\alpha)^a\), by \(X^a\). Then \(X^a\) vanishes at both \(p\) and
\(q\) and, as usual, we have
\(\mathcal L_TX^a=T^b\nabla_bX^a-X^b\nabla_bT^a=0\) everywhere. The length of each
curve is given by

\begin{equation}
  \tau(\alpha)=\int_a^b f(\alpha,t)\,\dd t ,
  \tag{9.3.13}\label{eq:9.3.13}
\end{equation}

where \(f=(-T^aT_a)^{1/2}\). In section~3.3 we showed that the necessary and
sufficient condition for the curve \(\gamma\) to extremize \(\tau\) for all possible
smooth families \(\lambda_\alpha\) with \(\lambda_0=\gamma\) was that \(\gamma\) be a
geodesic. We repeat, now, this calculation in a coordinate invariant form and then
compute the second variation of arc length. We have

\begin{align}
  \frac{\dd\tau}{\dd\alpha}
    &=\int_a^b\frac{\partial f}{\partial\alpha}\,\dd t\notag\\
    &=\int_a^bX^a\nabla_a(-T^bT_b)^{1/2}\,\dd t\notag\\
    &=-\int_a^b\frac1fT_bX^a\nabla_aT^b\,\dd t\notag\\
    &=-\int_a^b\frac1fT_bT^a\nabla_aX^b\,\dd t\notag\\
    &=-\int_a^bT^a\nabla_a\left[\frac1fT_bX^b\right]\dd t
      +\int_a^bX^bT^a\nabla_a(T_b/f)\,\dd t\notag\\
    &=\int_a^bX^bT^a\nabla_a(T_b/f)\,\dd t ,
  \tag{9.3.14}\label{eq:9.3.14}
\end{align}

since \(T^a\nabla_a(f^{-1}T_bX^b)=\partial(f^{-1}T_bX^b)/\partial t\) and \(X^b\)
vanishes at the endpoints. Thus, setting \(\alpha=0\) we see that
\(\dd\tau/\dd\alpha=0\) for arbitrary \(X^b\) if and only if
\(T^a\nabla_a(T_b/f)=0\) at \(\alpha=0\), which is just the geodesic equation
expressed in an arbitrary parameterization.

The second variation of arc length is

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd^2\tau}{\dd\alpha^2}
  =\int_a^bX^c\nabla_c\bigl[X^bT^a\nabla_a(T_b/f)\bigr]\,\dd t .
  \tag{9.3.15}\label{eq:9.3.15}
\end{equation}

Evaluating this expression at \(\alpha=0\), assuming that \(\lambda_0\) is a geodesic,
we find

\begin{align}
  \left.\frac{\dd^2\tau}{\dd\alpha^2}\right|_{\alpha=0}
    ={}&\int_a^bX^b(X^c\nabla_cT^a)\nabla_a(T_b/f)\,\dd t
       +\int_a^bX^bT^aX^c\nabla_c\nabla_a(T_b/f)\,\dd t\notag\\
    ={}&\int_a^bX^b(T^c\nabla_cX^a)\nabla_a(T_b/f)\,\dd t
       +\int_a^bX^bT^aX^c\nabla_a\nabla_c(T_b/f)\,\dd t\notag\\
      &+\int_a^bX^bT^aX^cR_{cab}{}^dT_d/f\,\dd t\notag\\
    ={}&\int_a^bX^bT^c\nabla_c\bigl[X^a\nabla_a(T_b/f)\bigr]\,\dd t
       +\int_a^bX^bT^aX^cR_{cab}{}^dT_d/f\,\dd t .
  \tag{9.3.16}\label{eq:9.3.16}
\end{align}

The term in square brackets can be reexpressed as

\begin{align}
  X^a\nabla_a(T_b/f)
    &=\frac1fX^a\nabla_aT_b-\frac1{f^2}T_bX^a\nabla_af\notag\\
    &=\frac1fT^a\nabla_aX_b
      +\frac1{f^2}T_bT^a\nabla_a\left[\frac1fT_cX^c\right] ,
  \tag{9.3.17}\label{eq:9.3.17}
\end{align}

where the manipulations of Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.3.14} and the geodesic equation at
\(\alpha=0\) were used. Equation~\eqref{eq:9.3.16} can be put in more easily
recognizable form by choosing the curve parameterization so that \(f=1\) along the
geodesic \(\lambda_0\) and also choosing the deviation vector \(X^a\) to be orthogonal
to \(T^a\) along \(\lambda_0\). With these choices, we find

\begin{align}
  \left.\frac{\dd^2\tau}{\dd\alpha^2}\right|_{\alpha=0}
    &=\int_a^bX^b\left\{T^c\nabla_c(T^a\nabla_aX_b)
      +R_{cab}{}^dT^aT_dX^c\right\}\,\dd t\notag\\
    &=\int_a^bX^b(\mathcal OX)_b\,\dd t .
  \tag{9.3.18}\label{eq:9.3.18}
\end{align}

where \(\mathcal O\) is the operator appearing in the geodesic deviation
equation~\eqref{eq:9.3.1}. Using this formula, we may establish the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:9.3.3}
Let \(\gamma\) be a smooth timelike curve connecting two points \(p,q\in M\).
Then the necessary and sufficient condition that \(\gamma\) locally maximize the
proper time between \(p\) and \(q\) over smooth one parameter variations is that
\(\gamma\) be a geodesic with no point conjugate to \(p\) between \(p\) and \(q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of proof]
If \(\gamma\) is not a geodesic, then using Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.3.14} we can construct a
one-parameter family of curves \(\lambda_\alpha\) with
\(\dd\tau/\dd\alpha>0\) at \(\alpha=0\). If \(\gamma\) is a geodesic but has a
conjugate point, \(r\), between \(p\) and \(q\), then we can find a deviation vector
\(X_0^a\) such that \((\mathcal OX_0)^a=0\) and \(X_0^a\) vanishes at \(p\) and
\(r\). By Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.3.18}, the variation \(X^a=X_0^a\) between \(p\) and \(r\)
followed by \(X^a=0\) between \(r\) and \(q\) produces no change in \(\tau\) to
second order (see Figure~\ref{fig:9.3}). By ``rounding off the corner'' at \(r\) for
this variation,\footnote{Note that this ``rounding off the corner'' argument is made for the
(infinitesimal) deviation vector, not for an actual (finite) geodesic. See Penrose
(1972) for further discussion.} one can produce a smooth deviation for which
\(\dd^2\tau/\dd\alpha^2>0\) at \(\alpha=0\).

\input{figures/chapter09/fig09-03.tex}

Conversely, if \(\gamma\) is a geodesic with no point conjugate to \(p\) between
\(p\) and \(q\), then the matrix \(A\) defined above will be nonsingular between
\(p\) and \(q\), so we can define
\(Y^\mu=\sum_\nu(A^{-1})^\mu{}_\nu X^\nu\). Substituting
\(\sum_\nu A^\mu{}_\nu Y^\nu\) for \(X^\mu\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.3.18}, we can show
that \(\dd^2\tau/\dd\alpha^2\) is manifestly negative definite at \(\alpha=0\).
These calculations can be found in proposition~4.5.8 of Hawking and Ellis (1973).
\end{proof}


A notion similar to conjugacy of two points along a timelike geodesic can be defined
for a point and a smooth (or, at least, \(C^2\)) spacelike hypersurface \(\Sigma\). (By
hypersurface, we mean an embedded, three-dimensional submanifold [see appendix~B].)
First, we define the \emph{extrinsic curvature}, \(K_{ab}\), of \(\Sigma\). Let \(\xi^a\) be
the unit tangent field of the congruence of timelike geodesics orthogonal to \(\Sigma\).
We define \(K_{ab}\) by

\begin{equation}
  K_{ab}=\nabla_a\xi_b=B_{ba} ,
  \tag{9.3.19}\label{eq:9.3.19}
\end{equation}

where evaluation of these tensors on \(\Sigma\) is understood. Note that \(K_{ab}\) is
purely spatial, i.e., \(K_{ab}\xi^a=K_{ab}\xi^b=0\). Since this congruence is
manifestly hypersurface orthogonal, we have \(\omega_{ab}=0\) so \(K_{ab}\) is
symmetric, \(K_{ab}=K_{ba}\). Hence, using Eq.~(C.2.16), we can reexpress \(K_{ab}\) as

\begin{align}
  K_{ab}
    &=\frac12\mathcal L_\xi g_{ab}\notag\\
    &=\frac12\mathcal L_\xi(h_{ab}-\xi_a\xi_b)\notag\\
    &=\frac12\mathcal L_\xi h_{ab} .
  \tag{9.3.20}\label{eq:9.3.20}
\end{align}

where \(h_{ab}\) was defined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:9.2.5} and the geodesic equation was
used in the last step. Now, \(h_{ab}\) is the spatial metric induced on the hypersurfaces
of constant proper time from \(\Sigma\) along the geodesic congruence orthogonal to
\(\Sigma\). Thus, \(K_{ab}\) measures the rate of change of this spatial metric as one
moves along the congruence; i.e., it measures the ``bending'' of \(\Sigma\) in the
spacetime \((M,g_{ab})\). Indeed, since Gaussian normal coordinates (see
section~3.3) are adapted to \(\xi^a\) (see appendix~C), in these coordinates we have

\begin{equation}
  K_{\mu\nu}=\frac12\frac{\partial h_{\mu\nu}}{\partial t} ,
  \tag{9.3.21}\label{eq:9.3.21}
\end{equation}

which reinforces our interpretation of \(K_{ab}\) as representing the ``time derivative''
of \(h_{ab}\). The trace of the extrinsic curvature is often denoted by \(K\),

\begin{equation}
  K\equiv K^a{}_a=h^{ab}K_{ab} .
  \tag{9.3.22}\label{eq:9.3.22}
\end{equation}

Thus, we have

\begin{equation}
  K=\theta ,
  \tag{9.3.23}\label{eq:9.3.23}
\end{equation}

where \(\theta\) is the expansion of the geodesic congruence orthogonal to \(\Sigma\).

A point \(p\) on a geodesic \(\gamma\) of the geodesic congruence orthogonal to
\(\Sigma\) is said to be \emph{conjugate} to \(\Sigma\) along \(\gamma\) if there exists an
orthogonal deviation vector \(\eta^a\) of the congruence which is nonzero on
\(\Sigma\) but vanishes at \(p\). Thus, intuitively, \(p\) is conjugate to \(\Sigma\) if two
``infinitesimally nearby'' geodesics orthogonal to \(\Sigma\) cross at \(p\) as illustrated
in Figure~\ref{fig:9.4}. By the same argument as given above for conjugate pairs of
points, \(p\) will be conjugate to \(\Sigma\) if and only if the expansion, \(\theta\), of the
congruence of geodesics orthogonal to \(\Sigma\) approaches \(-\infty\) at \(p\). Since
this congruence is manifestly hypersurface orthogonal, Lemma~\ref{lem:9.2.1}
immediately yields the following result.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:9.3.4}
Let \((M,g_{ab})\) be a spacetime satisfying \(R_{ab}\xi^a\xi^b\geq0\) for all
timelike \(\xi^a\). Let \(\Sigma\) be a spacelike hypersurface with \(K=\theta<0\)
at a point \(q\in\Sigma\). Then within proper time \(\tau\leq3/\lvert K\rvert\)
there exists a point \(p\) conjugate to \(\Sigma\) along the geodesic \(\gamma\)
orthogonal to \(\Sigma\) and passing through \(q\), assuming that \(\gamma\) can
be extended that far.
\end{proposition}

Furthermore, by arguments similar to those used to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:9.3.3},
the following theorem can be proven.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:9.3.5}
Let \(\gamma\) be a smooth timelike curve connecting a point \(p\in M\) to a point
\(q\) on a smooth spacelike hypersurface \(\Sigma\). Then the necessary and
sufficient condition that \(\gamma\) locally maximize the proper time between \(p\)
and \(\Sigma\) over smooth one-parameter variations is that \(\gamma\) be a geodesic
orthogonal to \(\Sigma\) with no conjugate point to \(\Sigma\) between \(\Sigma\)
and \(p\).
\end{theorem}


We turn our attention, now, to conjugate points of null geodesics. It follows directly
from the geodesic deviation equation that for any Jacobi field \(\eta^a\) on a null
geodesic \(\mu\) with tangent \(k^a\), we have

\begin{equation}
  k^c\nabla_c\bigl[k^b\nabla_b(k^a\eta_a)\bigr]=0 ,
  \tag{9.3.24}\label{eq:9.3.24}
\end{equation}

which immediately implies that \(\eta^a\) cannot vanish at two points
\(p,q\in\mu\) unless \(k^a\eta_a=0\) everywhere along \(\mu\). Furthermore, if
\(\eta^a\) is a Jacobi field, then so is \(\eta^a+(a+b\lambda)k^a\), where \(a\) and
\(b\) are constants, so \(p\) and \(q\) will be conjugate if and only if there exists a
Jacobi field \(\eta^a\) which differs from zero by only a multiple of \(k^a\) at both
\(p\) and \(q\). Thus, along a null geodesic \(\mu\), the points \(p,q\in\mu\) will be
conjugate if and only if a vector \(\widehat\eta^a\) in the quotient vector space
\(\widehat{T_pM}\) (see section~9.2) satisfies the geodesic deviation equation and
vanishes at \(p\) and \(q\). It is clear that all such \(\widehat\eta^a\) which vanish at
\(p\) arise as deviation vectors of any null geodesic congruence containing the
two-dimensional family of null geodesics emerging from \(p\). By the same arguments
as given above in the timelike case, \(q\) will be conjugate to \(p\) if and only if the
expansion, \(\theta\), of such a null geodesic congruence approaches \(-\infty\) at \(q\).
Thus, Lemma~\ref{lem:9.2.2} implies the following result.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:9.3.6}
Let \((M,g_{ab})\) be a spacetime satisfying \(R_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0\) for all null
\(k^a\). Let \(\mu\) be a null geodesic and let \(p\in\mu\). Suppose the
convergence, \(\theta\), of the null geodesics emanating from \(p\) attains the
negative value \(\theta_0\) at \(r\in\mu\). Then within affine length
\(\lambda\leq2/\lvert\theta_0\rvert\) from \(r\), there exists a point \(q\)
conjugate to \(p\) along \(\mu\), assuming that \(\mu\) extends that far.
\end{proposition}

Again, this result can be strengthened to conclude that if \(R_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0\)
everywhere on a complete null geodesic \(\mu\) and there exists at least one point
\(r\in\mu\) at which either \(R_{ab}k^ak^b>0\) or
\(k_{[e}C_{a]bc[d}k_{f]}k^bk^c\ne0\), then \(\mu\) possesses a pair of conjugate
points. A spacetime \((M,g_{ab})\) is said to satisfy the \emph{null generic condition}
if every null geodesic possesses at least one point where either \(R_{ab}k^ak^b\ne0\)
or \(k_{[e}C_{a]bc[d}k_{f]}k^bk^c\ne0\), i.e., at least one point where
\(k_{[e}R_{a]bc[d}k_{f]}k^bk^c\ne0\). Thus, in analogy to
Proposition~\ref{prop:9.3.2}, we have:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:9.3.7}
Suppose \((M,g_{ab})\) satisfies the null generic condition and
\(R_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0\) for all null \(k^a\). Then every complete null geodesic
possesses a pair of conjugate points.
\end{proposition}

As discussed above, for timelike geodesics conjugate points signal when a timelike
geodesic, \(\gamma\), can be varied to yield a curve of greater length between two points
\(p,q\in M\). If a conjugate point, \(r\), to \(p\) exists between \(p\) and \(q\) along
\(\gamma\), we can find a longer curve by perturbing toward an appropriate
``infinitesimally close'' geodesic between \(p\) and \(r\), following \(\gamma\) from
\(r\) to \(q\), and then ``rounding off the corner'' at \(r\). In an exactly similar
manner, for null geodesics, conjugate points signal when a null geodesic \(\mu\)
connecting \(p\) and \(q\) can be varied to yield a timelike curve between \(p\) and
\(q\). Again, if we have a conjugate point, \(r\), between \(p\) and \(q\), we can
perturb toward an ``infinitesimally close'' null geodesic from \(p\) to \(r\), follow
\(\mu\) from \(r\) to \(q\), and then ``round off the corner'' to produce a timelike
curve from \(p\) to \(q\). Thus, in analogy to Theorem~\ref{thm:9.3.3}, we obtain the
following result, the full proof of which can be found in Hawking and Ellis (1973).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:9.3.8}
Let \(\mu\) be a smooth causal curve and let \(p,q\in\mu\). Then there does not
exist a smooth one-parameter family of causal curves \(\lambda_\alpha\) connecting
\(p\) and \(q\) with \(\lambda_0=\mu\) and \(\lambda_\alpha\) timelike for all
\(\alpha>0\) (i.e., \(\mu\) cannot be smoothly deformed to a timelike curve) if
and only if \(\mu\) is a null geodesic with no point conjugate to \(p\) along
\(\mu\) between \(p\) and \(q\).
\end{theorem}

For null geodesics, a notion of conjugacy also can be defined for a point and a
two-dimensional spacelike surface (i.e., embedded submanifold) \(S\). At each
\(q\in S\) there will exist precisely two future directed null vectors \(k_1^a,k_2^a\)
which are orthogonal to \(S\). If \(S\) is orientable, we can make a continuous choice
of \(k_1^a\) and \(k_2^a\) over \(S\) and thereby define two families of null geodesics,
which we may refer to as ``outgoing'' and ``ingoing'' families. (Even if \(S\) is not
orientable, we still can define these two families locally in a neighborhood of any
point.) We will refer to each of these families as congruences even though each spans
only a null hypersurface rather than an open region of spacetime. The expansion
\(\theta\), shear \(\widehat\sigma_{ab}\), and twist \(\widehat\omega_{ab}=0\) of these
congruences are well defined, since all deviation vectors orthogonal to the geodesic
tangents \(k^a\) are included in the congruences. Let \(\mu\) be a null geodesic in one
of these congruences. A point \(p\in\mu\) is said to be conjugate to \(S\) along
\(\mu\) if along \(\mu\) there exists a deviation vector \(\widehat\eta^a\) of the
congruence which is nonzero on \(S\) but vanishes at \(p\). In analogy to
Proposition~\ref{prop:9.3.4}, we have

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:9.3.9}
Let \((M,g_{ab})\) be a spacetime satisfying \(R_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0\) for all null
\(k^a\). Let \(S\) be a smooth two-dimensional spacelike submanifold such that
the expansion, \(\theta\), of, say, the ``outgoing'' null geodesics has the negative
value \(\theta_0\) at \(q\in S\). Then within affine parameter
\(\lambda\leq2/\lvert\theta_0\rvert\), there exists a point \(p\) conjugate to \(S\)
along the outgoing null geodesic \(\mu\) passing through \(q\).
\end{proposition}

In analogy to Theorem~\ref{thm:9.3.5}, we also have:

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:9.3.10}
Let \(S\) be a smooth two-dimensional spacelike submanifold and let \(\mu\) be
a smooth causal curve from \(S\) to \(p\). Then the necessary and sufficient
condition that \(\mu\) cannot be smoothly deformed to a timelike curve connecting
\(S\) and \(p\) is that \(\mu\) be a null geodesic orthogonal to \(S\) with no point
conjugate to \(S\) between \(S\) and \(p\).
\end{theorem}

As a consequence of this theorem, and the results of chapter~8 we obtain the
following theorem which will be used in the proof of singularity theorem~9.5.3.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:9.3.11}
Let \((M,g_{ab})\) be a globally hyperbolic spacetime and let \(K\) be a compact,
orientable, two-dimensional spacelike submanifold of \(M\). Then every
\(p\in\partial I^+(K)\) lies on a future directed null geodesic starting from \(K\)
which is orthogonal to \(K\) and has no point conjugate to \(K\) between \(K\)
and \(p\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
That \(p\) lies on a null geodesic from \(K\) follows from the remarks below
Theorem~8.3.11. If this null geodesic were not orthogonal to \(K\) or had a
conjugate point, then by Theorem~\ref{thm:9.3.10} we would have
\(p\in I^+(K)\) and therefore \(p\notin\partial I^+(K)\).
\end{proof}
