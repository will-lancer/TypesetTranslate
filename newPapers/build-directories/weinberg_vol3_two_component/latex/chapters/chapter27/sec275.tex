\subsection{Supersymmetry Breaking in the Tree Approximation Resumed}

We saw in the previous section that if the Fayet--Iliopoulos constants
\(\xi_A\) all vanish and if there exists a solution of the equations
\(\partial f(\phi)/\partial\phi_n=0\), then there is also some solution of
these equations where the \(D\)-components of the gauge superfields all
vanish, so that supersymmetry is unbroken. It follows that there are only two
(non-exclusive) ways that supersymmetry can be spontaneously broken in the
tree approximation in renormalizable theories of gauge and chiral
superfields: the superpotential \(f(\phi)\) may be arranged so that there are
no solutions of all the equations
\(\partial f(\phi)/\partial\phi_n=0\), or for gauge groups with \(U(1)\)
factors there may be Fayet--Iliopoulos terms in the action.

We have already seen in Section 26.5 how it can happen that there might not
be any value of \(\phi\) for which \(\partial f(\phi)/\partial\phi_n=0\).
No change in that discussion is needed when the chiral superfields interact
with gauge fields, so let's turn to the other possibility: spontaneous
supersymmetry breaking produced by Fayet--Iliopoulos terms. Since this can
only arise for gauge groups with \(U(1)\) factors, the simplest case is a
theory with a single \(U(1)\) gauge group. As discussed in Section 22.4, to
avoid \(U(1)\)-\(U(1)\)-\(U(1)\) and \(U(1)\)-graviton-graviton anomalies
it is necessary that the sum of the \(U(1)\) quantum numbers of all
left-chiral superfields and the sum of their cubes should vanish. We will
consider the simplest possibility: two left-chiral superfields \(\Phi_\pm\),
with \(U(1)\) quantum numbers \(\pm e\). (This is a supersymmetric version
of quantum electrodynamics, with the Weyl components
\(\psi_{-\alpha}\) and \(\psi_{+\alpha}\) of the two superfields providing
the undotted fields of the electron and of its charge conjugate.) The most general
\(U(1)\)-invariant superpotential in a renormalizable theory is just
\(f(\Phi)=m\Phi_+\Phi_-\). The scalar potential \eqref{eq:27.4.9} for the
scalar components \(\phi_\pm\) of these superfields is then
\begin{equation}
  V(\phi_+,\phi_-)
  =m^2|\phi_+|^2+m^2|\phi_-|^2
  +\left(\xi+e^2|\phi_+|^2-e^2|\phi_-|^2\right)^2\,.
  \tag{27.5.1}\label{eq:27.5.1}
\end{equation}
Unless the Fayet--Iliopoulos constant \(\xi\) vanishes, it is evidently not
possible to find a supersymmetric vacuum with \(V=0\). For
\(\xi>m^2/2e^2\) or \(\xi<-m^2/2e^2\) the potential \eqref{eq:27.5.1} has
a minimum with either \(\phi_+=0\) and
\(|\phi_-|^2=(2e^2\xi-m^2)/2e^4\) or \(\phi_-=0\) and
\(|\phi_+|^2=(-2e^2\xi-m^2)/2e^4\), so the \(U(1)\) gauge symmetry is
broken along with supersymmetry. For \(|\xi|<m^2/2e^2\) the minimum of the
potential is at \(\phi_+=\phi_-=0\), so here the gauge symmetry is unbroken.
There is in general no necessary connection between the possible breakdown
of supersymmetry and of gauge symmetries.

Whether supersymmetry is spontaneously broken by the Fayet--Iliopoulos
mechanism discussed here or by the O'Raifeartaigh mechanism of Section 26.5
or by some combination of the two, supersymmetry leaves a remnant in the
pattern of tree-approximation masses. Inspection of the Lagrangian
\eqref{eq:27.4.8} for a general renormalizable supersymmetric theory of gauge
and chiral superfields shows that the spontaneous breakdown of
supersymmetry in this theory produces the following corrections to the
masses calculated in Section 27.4.

\subsubsection*{Spin 0 Masses}

If the \(\mathcal{F}\)-terms
\(\mathcal{F}_n=-(\partial f(\phi)/\partial\phi_n)^*\) do not vanish at the
minimum \(\phi_0\) of the potential, then the terms in the potential of
second order in \(\varphi_n\equiv\phi_n-\phi_{n0}\) have additional terms
beyond those listed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:27.4.15}:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  V_{\mathrm{quad}}(\varphi)={}&
  \sum_{nm}(\mathcal{M}^*\mathcal{M})_{nm}\varphi_n^*\varphi_m
  +\sum_{Anm}(t_A\phi_0)_n(t_A\phi_0)_m^*
    \varphi_n^*\varphi_m
  \\
  &+\frac12\sum_{Anm}(t_A\phi_0)_n^*(t_A\phi_0)_m^*
    \varphi_n\varphi_m
  +\frac12\sum_{Anm}(t_A\phi_0)_n(t_A\phi_0)_m
    \varphi_n^*\varphi_m^*
  \\
  &+\frac12\sum_{nm}\mathcal{N}_{nm}\varphi_n\varphi_m
  +\frac12\sum_{nm}\mathcal{N}_{nm}^*\varphi_n^*\varphi_m^*
  +\sum_{Anm}D_{A0}(t_A)_{nm}\varphi_n^*\varphi_m\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{27.5.2}\label{eq:27.5.2}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{M}\) is again the complex symmetric matrix
\eqref{eq:26.4.11}:
\[
  \mathcal{M}_{nm}\equiv
  \left(
    \frac{\partial^2f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_n\partial\phi_m}
  \right)_{\phi=\phi_0},
\]
\(\mathcal{N}_{nm}\) is a new ingredient
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{N}_{nm}\equiv
  -\sum_\ell\mathcal{F}_{\ell0}
  \left(
    \frac{\partial^3f(\phi)}
    {\partial\phi_n\partial\phi_m\partial\phi_\ell}
  \right)_{\phi=\phi_0},
  \tag{27.5.3}\label{eq:27.5.3}
\end{equation}
and \(\mathcal{F}_0\) and \(D_{A0}\) are again the
\(\mathcal{F}\)-terms and \(D\)-terms of the chiral scalar and gauge
superfields at the minimum of the potential:
\[
  \mathcal{F}_{n0}
  =-\left[
    \frac{\partial f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_n}
  \right]^*_{\phi=\phi_0},
  \qquad
  D_{A0}
  =\xi_A+\sum_{nm}\phi_{n0}^*(t_A)_{nm}\phi_{m0}\,.
\]

If we write the quadratic part \eqref{eq:27.5.2} of the potential in the
form \eqref{eq:27.4.16}:
\[
  V_{\mathrm{quad}}
  =\frac12
  \begin{bmatrix}\varphi\\ \varphi^*\end{bmatrix}^{\!\dagger}
  M_0^2
  \begin{bmatrix}\varphi\\ \varphi^*\end{bmatrix},
\]
then, instead of Eq.~\eqref{eq:27.4.17}, we now have the scalar mass matrix
\begin{equation}
  M_0^2=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathcal{M}^*\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{A}
      +\sum_A D_{A0}t_A
    &
    \mathcal{B}+\mathcal{N}^*
    \\
    \mathcal{B}^*+\mathcal{N}
    &
    \mathcal{M}\mathcal{M}^*+\mathcal{A}^*
      +\sum_A D_{A0}t_A^{\mathsf T}
  \end{bmatrix},
  \tag{27.5.4}\label{eq:27.5.4}
\end{equation}
where
\[
  \mathcal{A}\equiv
  \sum_A(t_A\phi_0)(t_A\phi_0)^\dagger,
  \qquad
  \mathcal{B}\equiv
  \sum_A(t_A\phi_0)(t_A\phi_0)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

\subsubsection*{Spin \(1/2\) Masses}

The fermion mass matrix \(M\) here is given again by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:27.4.30}:
\[
  M_{nm}=\mathcal{M}_{nm},
  \qquad
  M_{nA}=M_{An}=\ii\sqrt{2}(t_A\phi_0)_n^*,
  \qquad
  M_{AB}=0.
\]
However, now instead of Eq.~\eqref{eq:27.4.19}, the gauge invariance
condition \eqref{eq:27.4.18} yields
\begin{equation}
  \sum_m\mathcal{M}_{nm}(t_A\phi_0)_m
  =\sum_m\mathcal{F}_{m0}^*(t_A)_{mn}\,.
  \tag{27.5.5}\label{eq:27.5.5}
\end{equation}
Thus the Hermitian positive matrix whose eigenvalues are the squared masses
of the fermions is given by
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  (M^\dagger M)_{nm}
  &=(\mathcal{M}^\dagger\mathcal{M})_{nm}
    +2\sum_A(t_A\phi_0)_n(t_A\phi_0)_m^*,
  \\
  (M^\dagger M)_{AB}
  &=2(\phi_0^\dagger t_Bt_A\phi_0),
  \\
  (M^\dagger M)_{An}
  &=(M^\dagger M)_{nA}^*
    =\ii\sqrt{2}\sum_m\mathcal{F}_{m0}(t_A)_{mn}.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{27.5.6}\label{eq:27.5.6}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Spin 1 Masses}

The squared masses of the vector bosons are again given by the eigenvalues
of the matrix \eqref{eq:27.4.36}:
\begin{equation}
  (\mu^2)_{AB}
  =\left(\phi_0^\dagger\{t_B,t_A\}\phi_0\right).
  \tag{27.5.7}\label{eq:27.5.7}
\end{equation}

With the exception of the \(D\)-terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:27.5.4}, all of the
changes in the mass-squared matrices are in their off-diagonal components.
Therefore Eqs.~\eqref{eq:27.5.4}, \eqref{eq:27.5.6}, and
\eqref{eq:27.5.7} yield particularly simple results for the traces of these
matrices: for spin 0
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Tr}M_0^2
  =2\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{M}^*\mathcal{M})
  +\operatorname{Tr}\mu^2
  +2\sum_A D_{A0}\operatorname{Tr}t_A\,,
  \tag{27.5.8}\label{eq:27.5.8}
\end{equation}
and for spin \(1/2\)
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Tr}(M^\dagger M)
  =\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{M}^*\mathcal{M})
  +2\operatorname{Tr}\mu^2\,.
  \tag{27.5.9}\label{eq:27.5.9}
\end{equation}
Since the trace is the sum of the eigenvalues, we obtain from this a
\textit{mass sum rule}:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\text{spin }0}\mathrm{mass}^2
  -2\sum_{\text{spin }1/2}\mathrm{mass}^2
  +3\sum_{\text{spin }1}\mathrm{mass}^2
  =-2\sum_A D_{A0}\operatorname{Tr}t_A\,.
  \tag{27.5.10}\label{eq:27.5.10}
\end{equation}
The trace of \(t_A\) automatically vanishes unless \(t_A\) is a \(U(1)\)
generator, and, as mentioned in Section 22.4, the trace must also vanish
(when taken over all left-handed fermions) for \(U(1)\) gauge generators to
avoid gravitational contributions to an anomaly that would violate
conservation of the \(U(1)\) current. Thus \eqref{eq:27.5.10} leads to the
simpler result~\hyperref[ch27-ref-4]{[4]}
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\text{spin }0}\mathrm{mass}^2
  -2\sum_{\text{spin }1/2}\mathrm{mass}^2
  +3\sum_{\text{spin }1}\mathrm{mass}^2
  =0\,.
  \tag{27.5.11}\label{eq:27.5.11}
\end{equation}
Of course, the unbroken conservation of charge, color, and baryon and lepton
numbers prevents the mass matrices from having elements linking particles
with different values of these quantum numbers, so all these results hold
separately for each set of conserved quantum numbers.

The sum rule \eqref{eq:27.5.11} is often quoted as providing evidence
against models in which supersymmetry is spontaneously broken in the tree
approximation within the minimal supersymmetric extension of the standard
model. We will discuss this along with other arguments in Section 28.3.

As already observed in Section 26.5 (and discussed in greater generality in
Sections 29.1 and 29.2), the spontaneous breakdown of supersymmetry
necessarily entails the existence of a massless fermion, the goldstino. For
renormalizable gauge theories in the tree approximation, the goldstino field
\(g\) appears as a term in the spinor components \(\psi_n\) and \(\lambda_A\)
of the chiral and gauge superfields, with coefficients given by
\begin{equation}
  \psi_{n\alpha}=\ii\sqrt{2}\mathcal{F}_{n0}g_\alpha+\cdots,
  \qquad
  \lambda_{A\alpha}=D_{A0}g_\alpha+\cdots,
  \tag{27.5.12}\label{eq:27.5.12}
\end{equation}
where the dots denote terms involving spinor fields of definite non-zero
mass. To check this we have to confirm that
\((\ii\sqrt{2}\mathcal{F}_{n0},D_{A0})\) is an eigenvector of the fermion
mass-squared matrix \(M^\dagger M\) with eigenvalue zero. For this purpose we
will need to use the condition that the potential \eqref{eq:27.4.9} is
stationary at \(\phi=\phi_0\):
\begin{equation}
  0
  =\left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi_n}\right|_{\phi=\phi_0}
  =-\sum_m\mathcal{M}_{nm}\mathcal{F}_{m0}
  +\sum_A D_{A0}(\phi_0^\dagger t_A)_n\,.
  \tag{27.5.13}\label{eq:27.5.13}
\end{equation}
We also need the gauge invariance condition \eqref{eq:27.4.12}, which at
\(\phi=\phi_0\) reads
\begin{equation}
  \sum_n\mathcal{F}_{n0}(t_A\phi_0)_n=0\,.
  \tag{27.5.14}\label{eq:27.5.14}
\end{equation}
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:27.5.13} and \eqref{eq:27.5.14} with
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:27.5.5} and \eqref{eq:27.5.6} then yields
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \ii\sqrt{2}\sum_m(M^\dagger M)_{nm}\mathcal{F}_{m0}
  &=\ii\sqrt{2}\sum_A D_{A0}(t_A\mathcal{F}_0^*)_n
  \\
  &=-\sum_A(M^\dagger M)_{nA}D_{A0}
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{27.5.15}\label{eq:27.5.15}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \ii\sqrt{2}\sum_m(M^\dagger M)_{Am}\mathcal{F}_{m0}
  &=-2\sum_{nm}\mathcal{F}_{n0}(t_A)_{nm}\mathcal{F}_{m0}
  \\
  &=-\sum_B(M^\dagger M)_{AB}D_{B0}\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{27.5.16}\label{eq:27.5.16}
\end{equation}
That is,
\begin{equation}
  M^\dagger M
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \ii\sqrt{2}\mathcal{F}_0\\
    D_0
  \end{pmatrix}
  =0\,,
  \tag{27.5.17}\label{eq:27.5.17}
\end{equation}
as was to be shown.
