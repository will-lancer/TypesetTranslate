\subsection{Direct Construction of Field Supermultiplets}

To illustrate the direct construction of a field supermultiplet, we will
consider fields that can destroy the particles belonging to the simplest
supermultiplet of arbitrary mass discussed in Section 25.5: two spinless
particles and one particle of spin \(1/2\). We saw in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:25.5.15} that the one-particle zero spin state \(\ket{0,0}\) is
annihilated by the supersymmetry generator \(\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\) but not
by \(Q_\alpha\), so we would expect the scalar field \(\phi(x)\) that creates
this particle from the vacuum (which is assumed to be annihilated by all
supersymmetry generators) to commute with \(\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\) but not
with \(Q_\alpha\). That is,
\begin{equation}
  [\bar Q_{\dot\alpha},\phi(x)]=0\,,
  \tag{26.1.1}\label{eq:26.1.1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  -i[Q_\alpha,\phi(x)]
  \equiv\zeta_\alpha(x)\ne0\,.
  \tag{26.1.2}\label{eq:26.1.2}
\end{equation}
It follows that \(\zeta_\alpha(x)\) is a two-component spinor field in the
\((1/2,0)\) representation of the homogeneous Lorentz group.\footnote{At
this point we are not assuming anything about the masses or interactions of
the particles described by these fields, but it may be noted that, as
explained in Section 5.9, a \((1/2,0)\) free field can create massless
particles only of helicity \(+1/2\), in agreement with the result
\eqref{eq:25.5.15} that the massless spinless one-particle state
\(\ket{0,0}\) that is annihilated by \(\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\) is in a
supermultiplet
with a state of helicity \(+1/2\).}

From Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.1.1}--\eqref{eq:26.1.2} and the anticommutation
relation \eqref{eq:25.2.31}, we find
\[
  \{\bar Q_{\dot\beta},\zeta_\alpha\}
  =-i[\{\bar Q_{\dot\beta},Q_\alpha\},\phi(x)]
  =2i\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}[P_\mu,\phi]\,,
\]
and so
\begin{equation}
  \{\bar Q_{\dot\beta},\zeta_\alpha(x)\}
  =2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}\partial_\mu\phi(x)\,.
  \tag{26.1.3}\label{eq:26.1.3}
\end{equation}
On the other hand, Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.1.2} and the anticommutation relation
\eqref{eq:25.2.32} give
\[
  \{Q_\beta,\zeta_\alpha\}
  =-\{Q_\alpha,\zeta_\beta\}\,.
\]
Thus \(\{Q_\beta,\zeta_\alpha\}\) is antisymmetric in
\(\alpha,\beta\), and
therefore proportional to \(\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\):
\begin{equation}
  \{Q_\beta,\zeta_\alpha(x)\}
  =2i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{F}(x)\,.
  \tag{26.1.4}\label{eq:26.1.4}
\end{equation}
Lorentz invariance requires that the coefficient \(\mathcal{F}(x)\) is a
scalar field.

We must now go one step further, and calculate the commutators of the
supersymmetry generators with \(\mathcal{F}(x)\). Using
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.1.4}, \eqref{eq:26.1.2}, and \eqref{eq:25.2.32}, we have
\[
  \epsilon_{\alpha\beta}[Q_\gamma,\mathcal F]
  =-\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}[Q_\beta,\mathcal F]\,.
\]
Contracting two unequal components, we find that this commutator vanishes:
\begin{equation}
  [Q_\alpha,\mathcal{F}(x)]=0\,.
  \tag{26.1.5}\label{eq:26.1.5}
\end{equation}

Finally, using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.1.4}, \eqref{eq:25.2.31}, and
\eqref{eq:26.1.3}, we have
\[
  \epsilon_{\alpha\beta}[\bar Q^{\dot\gamma},\mathcal F]
  =-i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}
    \bar\sigma^{\mu\,\dot\gamma\delta}\partial_\mu\zeta_\delta .
\]
Thus
\begin{equation}
  [\bar Q^{\dot\alpha},\mathcal{F}(x)]
  =\bar\sigma^{\mu\,\dot\alpha\beta}
    \partial_\mu\zeta_\beta(x)\,.
  \tag{26.1.6}\label{eq:26.1.6}
\end{equation}

Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.1.1}--\eqref{eq:26.1.6} show that the fields
\(\phi(x)\), \(\zeta_\alpha(x)\), and \(\mathcal{F}(x)\) furnish a complete
representation of the supersymmetry algebra. These fields are not Hermitian,
so their complex conjugates furnish another supermultiplet:
\begin{equation}
  [Q_\alpha,\phi^*(x)]=0\,,
  \tag{26.1.7}\label{eq:26.1.7}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  -i[\bar Q_{\dot\alpha},\phi^*(x)]
  =\bar\zeta_{\dot\alpha}(x)\,,
  \tag{26.1.8}\label{eq:26.1.8}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \{Q_\beta,\bar\zeta_{\dot\alpha}(x)\}
  =2\sigma^\mu_{\beta\dot\alpha}\partial_\mu\phi^*(x)\,,
  \tag{26.1.9}\label{eq:26.1.9}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \{\bar Q_{\dot\beta},\bar\zeta_{\dot\alpha}(x)\}
  =-2i\epsilon_{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}\mathcal{F}^*(x)\,,
  \tag{26.1.10}\label{eq:26.1.10}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  [\bar Q_{\dot\alpha},\mathcal{F}^*(x)]=0\,,
  \tag{26.1.11}\label{eq:26.1.11}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  [Q_\alpha,\mathcal{F}^*(x)]
  =-\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}
    \partial_\mu\bar\zeta^{\dot\beta}(x)\,.
  \tag{26.1.12}\label{eq:26.1.12}
\end{equation}

We can express these commutation and anticommutation relations as
transformation rules under a supersymmetry transformation, which shifts any
bosonic or fermionic field operator \(\mathcal{O}(x)\) by the infinitesimal
amount
\begin{equation}
  \delta\mathcal{O}(x)
  \equiv-i
  \left[\xi^\alpha Q_\alpha
    +\bar\xi_{\dot\alpha}\bar Q^{\dot\alpha},
    \mathcal{O}(x)\right]\,,
  \tag{26.1.13}\label{eq:26.1.13}
\end{equation}
where \(\xi_\alpha\) is an infinitesimal fermionic c-number spinor and
\(\bar\xi_{\dot\alpha}=\xi_\alpha^*\). (Because the parameters
anticommute with the supercharges, the generator in brackets has the
Hermiticity needed so that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.1.13} gives
\((\delta\mathcal{O})^\dagger=\delta\mathcal{O}^\dagger\).) The commutation
and anticommutation rules \eqref{eq:26.1.1}--\eqref{eq:26.1.6} are
equivalent to the transformation rules
\begin{equation}
  \delta\phi(x)
  =\xi\zeta(x)\,,
  \tag{26.1.14}\label{eq:26.1.14}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \delta\zeta_\alpha(x)
  =-2i\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}\bar\xi^{\dot\alpha}
    \partial_\mu\phi(x)
    +2\xi_\alpha\mathcal{F}(x)\,,
  \tag{26.1.15}\label{eq:26.1.15}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \delta\mathcal{F}(x)
  =-i\bar\xi\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\zeta(x)\,.
  \tag{26.1.16}\label{eq:26.1.16}
\end{equation}

For later use, we record explicitly the conjugation relation for the
transformation parameter:
\begin{equation}
  \bar\xi_{\dot\alpha}\equiv(\xi_\alpha)^* .
  \tag{26.1.17}\label{eq:26.1.17}
\end{equation}
\footnote{In components the two conjugate Grassmann parameters are
\[
  \xi_\alpha=
  \begin{pmatrix}\xi_1\\ \xi_2\end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
  \bar\xi_{\dot\alpha}=
  \begin{pmatrix}\xi_1^*\\ \xi_2^*\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:26.1.17} fixes their relative phase and agrees with the
conjugate supercharge convention in Eq.~\eqref{eq:25.2.34}.}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.1.13} is equivalently
\begin{equation}
  i\delta\mathcal{O}(x)\equiv
  [\xi Q+\bar\xi\bar Q,\mathcal{O}(x)]\,.
  \tag{26.1.18}\label{eq:26.1.18}
\end{equation}

The transformation rules \eqref{eq:26.1.14}--\eqref{eq:26.1.16} and their
complex conjugates may be put into a convenient covariant form by introducing
a set of real bosonic fields \(A\), \(B\), \(F\), and \(G\), defined by
\begin{equation}
  \frac{A+iB}{\sqrt{2}}\equiv\phi\,,
  \qquad
  \frac{F-iG}{\sqrt{2}}\equiv\mathcal{F}\,,
  \tag{26.1.19}\label{eq:26.1.19}
\end{equation}
and a Weyl field \(\psi_\alpha\) with conjugate
\(\bar\psi_{\dot\alpha}\), defined by
\begin{equation}
  \psi_\alpha\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\zeta_\alpha,
  \qquad
  \bar\psi_{\dot\alpha}\equiv
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bar\zeta_{\dot\alpha}.
  \tag{26.1.20}\label{eq:26.1.20}
\end{equation}
For reference, the sigma matrices that join these two index types are
\[
  \sigma^\mu=(\mathbf{1},\boldsymbol{\sigma}),
  \qquad
  \bar\sigma^\mu=(\mathbf{1},-\boldsymbol{\sigma}) .
\]
The transformation rules now take the form
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \delta A&=\xi\psi+\bar\xi\bar\psi\,,
    &\qquad
    \delta B&=-i(\xi\psi-\bar\xi\bar\psi)\,,
    \\
    \delta\psi_\alpha
    &=-i(\sigma^\mu\bar\xi)_\alpha\partial_\mu(A+iB)
      +(F-iG)\xi_\alpha\,,
    \\
    \delta F&=-i\left(
      \xi\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\bar\psi
      +\bar\xi\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi\right)\,,
    &\qquad
    \delta G&=\bar\xi\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi
      -\xi\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\bar\psi\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.1.21}\label{eq:26.1.21}
\end{equation}
A direct but tedious calculation shows that this transformation leaves
invariant the action
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  I=\int d^4x\biggl\{
    &-\frac12\partial_\mu A\,\partial^\mu A
     -\frac12\partial_\mu B\,\partial^\mu B
     +\frac{i}{2}\left(
       \psi\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\bar\psi
       +\bar\psi\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi\right)
  \\
    &+\frac12(F^2+G^2)
     +m\left[FA+GB-\frac12(\psi\psi+\bar\psi\bar\psi)\right]
  \\
    &+g\left[F(A^2+B^2)+2GAB
      -(A+iB)\psi\psi-(A-iB)\bar\psi\bar\psi\right]\biggr\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.1.22}\label{eq:26.1.22}
\end{equation}
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.1.21} and \eqref{eq:26.1.22} agree with the transformation
rules \eqref{eq:24.2.8} and Lagrangian density \eqref{eq:24.2.9} found in the
original work of Wess and Zumino. In the next three sections we will explore a
convenient technique for checking the supersymmetry of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.1.22} and for deriving more general supersymmetric actions.

Where the Weyl field satisfies the free equations
\(-i\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\bar\psi+m\psi=0\) and
\(-i\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi+m\bar\psi=0\), these transformation
rules show that
\(F+mA\) and \(G+mB\) are invariant, and therefore commute with
\(Q_\alpha\) and \(\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\), and hence also with \(P_\mu\).
This does not
prove that \(F=-mA\) and \(G=-mB\), but without changing any of the
commutation and anticommutation rules
\eqref{eq:26.1.1}--\eqref{eq:26.1.6} or transformation rules
\eqref{eq:26.1.21}, we can redefine the fields \(F\) and \(G\) by subtracting
the constants \(F+mA\) and \(B+mG\), respectively, so that the new fields
\(F\) and \(G\) are given by \(F=-mA\) and \(G=-mB\), and therefore
\(\mathcal{F}=-m\phi^*\). This is not true in the presence of interactions,
but even in the interacting case \(\mathcal{F}(x)\), \(F(x)\), and \(G(x)\)
are typically auxiliary fields that can be expressed in terms of the other
fields of the supermultiplet, as is the case for the action
\eqref{eq:26.1.22}.
