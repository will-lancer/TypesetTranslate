\subsection{The Supercurrent}

\providecommand{\SuperQ}{\mathcal{Q}}

Like any other continuous global symmetry, supersymmetry leads to the
existence of a conserved current~\hyperref[ch26-ref-5]{[5]}. The conservation
and commutation properties of the supersymmetry current are operator
equations that will remain valid even when supersymmetry is spontaneously
broken, and that will therefore be useful to us in Chapter 29, when we
consider theories of spontaneous supersymmetry breaking in a
non-perturbative context. Also, the supersymmetry current is related to
components of a superfield known as the \emph{supercurrent}%
~\hyperref[ch26-ref-6]{[6]}, that will be of fundamental importance in our
treatment of supergravity in Chapter 31.

As we saw in Section 7.3, the existence of an ordinary global symmetry of the
Lagrangian density under an infinitesimal transformation
\(\chi^\ell\longrightarrow\chi^\ell+\epsilon\mathcal{F}^\ell\) (with
\(\chi^\ell\) a generic canonical or auxiliary boson or fermion field and
\(\mathcal{F}^\ell\) a function of the canonical and auxiliary fields) leads
to the existence of a current
\[
  J^\mu(x)\propto
  \sum_\ell
  \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}(x)}
       {\partial\bigl(\partial_\mu\chi^\ell(x)\bigr)}
  \mathcal{F}^\ell(x)\,,
\]
which is conserved for fields satisfying the field equations, and generates
the symmetry in the sense that the canonical commutation relations give
\[
  \left[
    \int d^3x\,J^0(x),\chi^\ell(y)
  \right]
  \propto\mathcal{F}^\ell(y)\,.
\]

The supersymmetry current requires a somewhat more complicated treatment for
two reasons. One is that supersymmetry is only a symmetry of the action, not
of the Lagrangian density or the Lagrangian. Instead, the variation of the
Lagrangian density under an infinitesimal supersymmetry transformation is a
spacetime derivative, which we may write in the form
\begin{equation}
  \delta\mathcal{L}
  =\partial_\mu\bigl(\xi K^\mu+\bar\xi\bar K^\mu\bigr)\,,
  \tag{26.7.1}\label{eq:26.7.1}
\end{equation}
with \(K^\mu_\alpha\) a Weyl-spinor current and
\(\bar K^{\mu\dot\alpha}=(K^\mu_\alpha)^*\). In consequence, the
supersymmetry current is not the usual Noether current. The Noether current is
a Weyl-spinor four-vector \(N^\mu_\alpha\), together with its conjugate,
defined by
\begin{equation}
  \sum_\ell
  \frac{\partial_R\mathcal{L}}
       {\partial\bigl(\partial_\mu\chi^\ell\bigr)}
  \delta\chi^\ell
  \equiv-\bigl(\xi N^\mu+\bar\xi\bar N^\mu\bigr)\,,
  \tag{26.7.2}\label{eq:26.7.2}
\end{equation}
whose divergence is given by the Euler--Lagrange equations as
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \xi\partial_\mu N^\mu+\bar\xi\partial_\mu\bar N^\mu
  &=
  \sum_\ell\frac{\partial_R\mathcal{L}}{\partial\chi^\ell}
  \delta\chi^\ell
  -\sum_\ell
  \frac{\partial_R\mathcal{L}}
       {\partial\bigl(\partial_\mu\chi^\ell\bigr)}
  \partial_\mu\delta\chi^\ell
  \\
  &=-\delta\mathcal{L}\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.3}\label{eq:26.7.3}
\end{equation}
(Here \(\partial_R\) denotes the right partial derivative, defined by moving
differentiated fermionic variables to the right before differentiating.)
Instead, we must define the supersymmetry current as
\begin{equation}
  S^\mu_\alpha\equiv N^\mu_\alpha+K^\mu_\alpha\,,
  \qquad
  \bar S^{\mu\dot\alpha}\equiv
  \bar N^{\mu\dot\alpha}+\bar K^{\mu\dot\alpha}\,,
  \tag{26.7.4}\label{eq:26.7.4}
\end{equation}
which Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.1} and \eqref{eq:26.7.3} tell us is conserved:
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\mu S^\mu_\alpha=0\,,
  \qquad
  \partial_\mu\bar S^{\mu\dot\alpha}=0\,.
  \tag{26.7.5}\label{eq:26.7.5}
\end{equation}

The second complication is that the change \(\delta\chi^\ell\) in a canonical
field \(\chi^\ell\) under a supersymmetry transformation is not just a
function of canonical fields, but also involves their canonical conjugates.
For instance, Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.3.15} shows that the change in the
\(\psi\)-component of a chiral scalar superfield involves the time derivative
of the \(\phi\)-component. As a result the commutator of the Noether charge
\(\int d^3x\,N^0\) with a general canonical field does not give the
supersymmetry transformation of that field. Fortunately, this complication
is cancelled by the first complication: when the commutators of
\(\int d^3x\,K^0\) as well as \(\int d^3x\,N^0\) with the fields are taken
into account, the operator \(\int d^3x\,S^0\) does generate supersymmetry
transformations\footnote{This is a general result for currents constructed in
this way. For instance, consider a Lagrangian \(L\) (not Lagrangian density)
that depends on a set of canonical variables \(q^n\) and their time
derivatives \(\dot q^n\), with no constraints of any class. In quantum field
theory the label \(n\) includes spatial coordinates as well as discrete spin
and species labels, and \(L=\int d^3x\,\mathcal{L}\). Our assumption here
that the Lagrangian density is invariant up to spacetime derivatives under
some infinitesimal transformation \(\delta\) means that \(\delta L\) is the
time derivative of some functional \(F\). That is,
\[
  \sum_n\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^n}\delta q^n
  +\sum_n\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q^n}\delta\dot q^n
  =\frac{d}{dt}F\,.
\]
Using the canonical equations of motion, this can be written as a
conservation law \(\dot Q=0\), where the conserved charge is
\[
  Q=-\sum_n\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q^n}\delta q^n+F\,.
\]
In our case here, \(Q=\int d^3x\,[N^0+K^0]\). We assume the usual
unconstrained commutation relations
\[
  \left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q^n},q^m\right]
  =-i\delta_n^m\,,
  \qquad
  [q^n,q^m]=0\,,
\]
and find the commutator
\[
  [Q,q^m]
  =i\delta q^m
  -\sum_{n\ell}
   \frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q^\ell}
   \frac{\partial\delta q^\ell}{\partial\dot q^n}
   [\dot q^n,q^m]
  +\sum_n
   \frac{\partial F}{\partial\dot q^n}
   [\dot q^n,q^m]\,.
\]
To evaluate the second and third terms, we note that the second time
derivatives \(\ddot q^n\) appear linearly in the invariance condition, so
their coefficients must match: even without using the equations of motion,
we have
\[
  \sum_\ell
  \frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q^\ell}
  \frac{\partial\delta q^\ell}{\partial\dot q^n}
  =\frac{\partial F}{\partial\dot q^n}\,.
\]
The second and third terms in the commutator therefore cancel, leaving us
with the desired result
\[
  [Q,q^m]=i\delta q^m\,.
\]
Taking the time derivative gives also
\[
  [Q,\dot q^m]=i\delta\dot q^m\,.
\]
This result has been extended to theories with
constraints~\hyperref[ch26-ref-7]{[7]}.} in the sense that
\begin{equation}
  \left[
    \int d^3x\,\bigl(\xi S^0+\bar\xi\bar S^0\bigr),\chi^\ell
  \right]
  =i\delta\chi^\ell\,,
  \tag{26.7.6}\label{eq:26.7.6}
\end{equation}
consistently with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.2.1} and \eqref{eq:26.2.8}.

For example, we may derive an explicit formula for the supersymmetry current
in the general renormalizable theory of left-chiral superfields \(\Phi_n\),
which can be used to check that it does generate supersymmetry
transformations, in the sense of Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.6}. The Lagrangian
density \eqref{eq:26.4.7} for this theory may be put in the form
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L}
  =\sum_n\biggl[
   &-\partial_\mu\phi_n^*\partial^\mu\phi_n
   +i\bar\psi_n\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi_n
  \biggr]
  +\text{non-derivative terms}\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.7}\label{eq:26.7.7}
\end{equation}
Using the transformation rules \eqref{eq:26.3.15}, \eqref{eq:26.3.17},
\eqref{eq:26.3.18}, and \eqref{eq:26.3.20} (with
\(\widetilde{\phi}=\phi^*\) and
\(\widetilde{\bar\psi}=\bar\psi\)), the Noether current defined by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.2} is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  N^\mu_\alpha=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_n\bigl[
   &2(\partial^\mu\phi_n^*)\psi_{n\alpha}
    -(\partial_\nu\phi_n^*)
      (\sigma^\nu\bar\sigma^\mu\psi_n)_\alpha
    +i\mathcal{F}_n(\sigma^\mu\bar\psi_n)_\alpha
  \bigr]\,,
  \qquad
  \bar N^{\mu\dot\alpha}=(N^\mu_\alpha)^* .
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.8}\label{eq:26.7.8}
\end{equation}
We can calculate the change in the Lagrangian density either directly, or,
more easily, by noting that the changes of \(D\)-terms and
\(\mathcal{F}\)-terms under supersymmetry transformations are given
respectively by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.2.17} and \eqref{eq:26.3.16}. Either way,
we find that the current \(K^\mu\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.1} is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  K^\mu_\alpha=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_n\biggl[
   &(\partial_\nu\phi_n^*)
      (\sigma^\mu\bar\sigma^\nu\psi_n)_\alpha
    -i\mathcal{F}_n(\sigma^\mu\bar\psi_n)_\alpha
  \\
   &-2i\left(\frac{\partial f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_n}\right)^*
      (\sigma^\mu\bar\psi_n)_\alpha
  \biggr]\,,
  \qquad
  \bar K^{\mu\dot\alpha}=(K^\mu_\alpha)^* .
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.9}\label{eq:26.7.9}
\end{equation}
Adding \eqref{eq:26.7.8} and \eqref{eq:26.7.9} gives the supersymmetry
current for this class of theories
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  S^\mu_\alpha=-\sqrt{2}\sum_n\biggl[
    &(\partial_\nu\phi_n^*)
      (\sigma^\nu\bar\sigma^\mu\psi_n)_\alpha
  \\
    &+i\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_n}\right)^*
      (\sigma^\mu\bar\psi_n)_\alpha
  \biggr]\,,
  \qquad
  \bar S^{\mu\dot\alpha}=(S^\mu_\alpha)^* .
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.10}\label{eq:26.7.10}
\end{equation}
It is straightforward then to use the canonical commutation and
anticommutation relations to verify that \(\int d^3x\,S^0\) satisfies the
commutation relations \eqref{eq:26.7.6}.

There is another definition of symmetry currents, in terms of the response
of the matter action to a local symmetry transformation, which is
particularly useful when the associated symmetries are `gauged,' as
supersymmetry will be when we turn to supergravity theories in Chapter 31.
In the absence of supergravity fields, the action is not invariant under
local supersymmetry transformations. If we make such a transformation with a
spacetime-dependent parameter \(\alpha(x)\), the action will change by an
amount that, in order to vanish when \(\alpha(x)\) is constant, must (even
when the field equations are not satisfied) be of the form
\begin{equation}
  \delta I
  =-\int d^4x\,
   \left[(\partial_\mu\xi^\alpha)S^\mu_\alpha
   +(\partial_\mu\bar\xi_{\dot\alpha})\bar S^{\mu\dot\alpha}\right]\,,
  \tag{26.7.11}\label{eq:26.7.11}
\end{equation}
with \(S^\mu_\alpha(x)\) and \(\bar S^{\mu\dot\alpha}(x)\) conjugate
Weyl-spinor operator coefficients.
This does not define \(S^\mu(x)\) uniquely, because when we generalize global
supersymmetry transformations to local transformations, we might in general
give the change \(\delta\chi\) of a field \(\chi\) under a local
supersymmetry transformation an arbitrary dependence on the derivatives of
\(\xi(x)\) and \(\bar\xi(x)\). There is, however, one way of defining local supersymmetry
transformations that guarantees that the coefficient \(S^\mu(x)\) in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.11} is the same as the current defined by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.4}, which as we have seen generates the symmetry
transformations in the sense of Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.6}. It is to specify that
\emph{derivatives of \(\xi(x)\) or \(\bar\xi(x)\) do not appear in the supersymmetry
transformation of the canonical or auxiliary fields \(\chi^\ell\)}. For
instance, the local versions of the transformation rules
\eqref{eq:26.3.15}--\eqref{eq:26.3.17} for the components of a left-chiral
superfield are
\begin{equation}
  \delta\psi_\alpha(x)
  =-i\sqrt{2}\,\partial_\mu\phi(x)
    \sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}\bar\xi^{\dot\alpha}(x)
   +\sqrt{2}\mathcal{F}(x)\xi_\alpha(x)\,,
  \tag{26.7.12}\label{eq:26.7.12}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \delta\mathcal{F}(x)
  =-i\sqrt{2}\,
    \bar\xi(x)\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi(x)\,,
  \tag{26.7.13}\label{eq:26.7.13}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \delta\phi(x)
  =\sqrt{2}\,\xi(x)\psi(x)\,.
  \tag{26.7.14}\label{eq:26.7.14}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.3.21} shows that the superfield may be expressed in terms of
its component fields at \(x_+^\mu\) without derivatives, so the
transformation rule for the superfield may be expressed as
\begin{equation}
  \delta\Phi(x,\theta)
  =\bigl(\xi(x_+)\SuperQ
    +\bar\xi(x_+)\bar{\SuperQ}\bigr)\Phi(x,\theta,\bar\theta)\,,
  \tag{26.7.15}\label{eq:26.7.15}
\end{equation}
where \(\SuperQ_\alpha\) and \(\bar{\SuperQ}_{\dot\alpha}\) are the
operators \eqref{eq:26.2.2}.

With local supersymmetry transformations defined in this way, the change of
the action that they induce consists of two terms. First, although the
variation in canonical fields under a supersymmetry transformation does not
involve derivatives of the local spinor parameters, the variation in
derivatives of canonical fields does. This produces a change in the
Lagrangian density of the same form as Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.2}, with each
parameter replaced by its derivative:
\[
  \delta_1 I
  =-\int d^4x\,
  \left[(\partial_\mu\xi)N^\mu
       +(\partial_\mu\bar\xi)\bar N^\mu\right]\,.
\]
The second term in the change of the action arises from the fact that the
Lagrangian density is not invariant even under the part of the supersymmetry
transformation that does not involve derivatives of \(\alpha(x)\). According
to Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.1}, this produces a change in the action
\[
  \delta_2 I
  =\int d^4x\,
   \left[\xi\partial_\mu K^\mu+\bar\xi\partial_\mu\bar K^\mu\right]
  =-\int d^4x\,
   \left[(\partial_\mu\xi)K^\mu
        +(\partial_\mu\bar\xi)\bar K^\mu\right]\,.
\]
Adding \(\delta_1 I\) and \(\delta_2 I\) gives a total change in the action of
the form \eqref{eq:26.7.11}, with \(S^\mu(x)\) given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.4}, as was to be shown.

Even with this specification of the transformation property of the component
fields, the supersymmetry current \(S^\mu(x)\) is not uniquely specified by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.11}, because we could always introduce a modified current
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^\mu
  =S^\mu_\alpha+\partial_\nu A_\alpha^{\mu\nu}\,,
  \qquad
  \bar S_{\mathrm{new}}^{\mu\dot\alpha}
  =\bar S^{\mu\dot\alpha}+\partial_\nu\bar A^{\mu\nu\dot\alpha}\,,
  \tag{26.7.16}\label{eq:26.7.16}
\end{equation}
where \(A^{\mu\nu}=-A^{\nu\mu}\) is an arbitrary antisymmetric tensor of
Weyl spinors (with conjugate \(\bar A^{\mu\nu}\)). The term
\(\partial_\nu A^{\mu\nu}\) is conserved whether
or not the field equations are satisfied, and its time component is a space
derivative, so \(\int d^3x\,S_{\mathrm{new}}^0=\int d^3x\,S^0\), leaving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.6} unchanged.

There is in fact a particular choice of \(A^{\mu\nu}\) with the convenient
feature that the sigma traces
\(\bar\sigma_\mu S_{\mathrm{new}}^\mu\) and
\(\sigma_\mu\bar S_{\mathrm{new}}^\mu\) turn out to measure the violation of
scale invariance by the theory. By using the Weyl equations
derived from the Lagrangian density \eqref{eq:26.4.7}:
\begin{equation}
  i\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi_m
  =\sum_n
   \left(
    \frac{\partial^2f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_m\partial\phi_n}
   \right)^*\bar\psi_n\,,
  \qquad
  i\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\bar\psi_m
  =\sum_n
   \left(
    \frac{\partial^2f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_m\partial\phi_n}
   \right)\psi_n\,,
  \tag{26.7.17}\label{eq:26.7.17}
\end{equation}
it is straightforward to calculate that
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \bar\sigma_\mu S^\mu
  =-2i\sqrt{2}\sum_n\biggl\{
    &\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu(\phi_n^*\psi_n)
    +i\left(
      \sum_m\phi_m
       \frac{\partial^2f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_n\partial\phi_m}
      -2\frac{\partial f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_n}
     \right)^*\bar\psi_n
  \biggr\}\,.
  \end{aligned}
\]
The conjugate equation gives \(\sigma_\mu\bar S^\mu\).
We can eliminate the first term by introducing a modified supersymmetry
current of the general type of Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.16}:
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^\mu
  =S^\mu_\alpha-\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}
   (\sigma^{\mu\nu})_\alpha{}^\beta
   \sum_n\partial_\nu(\phi_n^*\psi_{n\beta})\,,
  \qquad
  \bar S_{\mathrm{new}}^{\mu\dot\alpha}
  =(S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^\mu)^* ,
  \tag{26.7.18}\label{eq:26.7.18}
\end{equation}
for which
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \bar\sigma_\mu S_{\mathrm{new}}^\mu
  =-2i\sqrt{2}\sum_n
    \left(
      \sum_m\phi_m
       \frac{\partial^2f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_n\partial\phi_m}
      -2\frac{\partial f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_n}
     \right)^*\bar\psi_n\,,
  \qquad
  \sigma_\mu\bar S_{\mathrm{new}}^\mu
  =(\bar\sigma_\mu S_{\mathrm{new}}^\mu)^* .
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.19}\label{eq:26.7.19}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side vanishes for a scale-invariant Lagrangian density, with
\(f(\Phi)\) a homogeneous polynomial of third order in the \(\Phi_n\).

We now turn to the supersymmetry transformation property of the
supersymmetry current. It is straightforward to check that the current given
by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.18} and \eqref{eq:26.7.10} is related to the
\(\omega\)-component \(\omega_\mu^\Theta\) of a real non-chiral superfield
\(\Theta_\mu\) by\footnote{Here we introduce a notation that will be used
extensively in Chapter 31; following Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.10}, the components
\(C^S\), \(\omega^S\), \(M^S\), \(N^S\), \(V_\nu^S\), \(\lambda^S\), and
\(D^S\) of an arbitrary superfield \(S(x,\theta)\) are defined by the
expansion
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  S(x,\theta,\bar\theta)={}&C^S
  -i(\theta\omega^S-\bar\theta\bar\omega^S)
  -\frac{i}{2}(\theta^2-\bar\theta^2)M^S
  -\frac12(\theta^2+\bar\theta^2)N^S\\
  &+\theta\sigma^\nu\bar\theta\,V_\nu^S
  -i\theta^2\bar\theta
    \left(\bar\lambda^S-\frac{i}{2}
      \bar\sigma^\nu\partial_\nu\omega^S\right)\\
  &+i\bar\theta^2\theta
    \left(\lambda^S-\frac{i}{2}
      \sigma^\nu\partial_\nu\bar\omega^S\right)
  +\frac12\theta^2\bar\theta^2
    \left(D^S+\frac12\Box C^S\right).
  \end{aligned}
\]}
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^\mu
  =-2\omega_\alpha^{\Theta\mu}
   -2(\sigma^\mu\bar\sigma^\nu)_\alpha{}^\beta
     \omega_{\nu\beta}^\Theta\,,
  \qquad
  \bar S_{\mathrm{new}}^{\mu\dot\alpha}
  =(S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^\mu)^* ,
  \tag{26.7.20}\label{eq:26.7.20}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \Theta_\mu
  =\frac{i}{12}\sum_n\left[
    4\Phi_n^*\partial_\mu\Phi_n
    -4\Phi_n\partial_\mu\Phi_n^*
    -i(\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Phi_n^*)
       \bar\sigma_\mu^{\dot\alpha\alpha}(D_\alpha\Phi_n)
  \right].
  \tag{26.7.21}\label{eq:26.7.21}
\end{equation}
The superfield \(\Theta^\mu\) is known as the \emph{supercurrent}.

The supercurrent obeys a conservation law that incorporates the conservation
of the supersymmetry current \eqref{eq:26.7.20} and much else besides. To
derive it, we use the anticommutation relation \eqref{eq:26.2.30} in the
index-explicit form\footnote{Dotted and undotted covariant derivatives are
independent operators:
\(\{D_\alpha,\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\}
=2i\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}\partial_\mu\).
Complex conjugation exchanges them; a bar on an index is not an additional
``covariant adjoint'' operation.}
\[
  \{D_\alpha,\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\}
  =2i\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}\partial_\mu,
  \qquad
  \bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Phi_n=D_\alpha\Phi_n^*=0 .
\]
In particular,
\[
  \bar D_{\dot\alpha}D^2\Phi_n
  =-4i\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
    \partial_\mu D^\alpha\Phi_n,
  \qquad
  D_\alpha\bar D^2\Phi_n^*
  =4i\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
    \partial_\mu\bar D^{\dot\alpha}\Phi_n^* .
\]
Using these identities in \eqref{eq:26.7.21}, the terms in which a
spacetime derivative acts on a covariant spinor derivative cancel, leaving
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  D^\alpha\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  ={}&-\frac{1}{6}\sum_n
    (\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Phi_n^*)D^2\Phi_n
    +\frac{1}{12}\sum_n
      \Phi_n^*\bar D_{\dot\alpha}D^2\Phi_n ,
  \end{aligned}
\]
where
\(\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
\equiv\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}\Theta_\mu\). Thus
\begin{equation}
  D^\alpha\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =-\frac{1}{6}\sum_n
    (\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Phi_n^*)D^2\Phi_n
   +\frac{1}{12}\sum_n
    \Phi_n^*\bar D_{\dot\alpha}D^2\Phi_n .
  \tag{26.7.22}\label{eq:26.7.22}
\end{equation}
We saw in Section 26.6 that the field equations for the Lagrangian density
\eqref{eq:26.4.7} may be expressed in the form
\begin{equation}
  D^2\Phi_n
  =4\left(\frac{\partial f(\Phi)}{\partial\Phi_n}\right)^* .
  \tag{26.7.23}\label{eq:26.7.23}
\end{equation}
Using this in Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.22} gives finally
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  D^\alpha\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  &=-\frac{2}{3}\sum_n
    (\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Phi_n^*)
    \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\Phi_n}\right)^*
   +\frac{1}{3}\sum_n
    \Phi_n^*\bar D_{\dot\alpha}
    \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\Phi_n}\right)^*
  \\
  &=\frac{1}{3}\bar D_{\dot\alpha}
    \left[
      \sum_n\Phi_n\frac{\partial f(\Phi)}{\partial\Phi_n}
      -3f(\Phi)
    \right]^* .
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.24}\label{eq:26.7.24}
\end{equation}
The Hermitian adjoint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.24} is
\begin{equation}
  \bar D^{\dot\alpha}\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =\frac{1}{3}D_\alpha
    \left[
      \sum_n\Phi_n\frac{\partial f(\Phi)}{\partial\Phi_n}
      -3f(\Phi)
    \right].
  \tag{26.7.25}\label{eq:26.7.25}
\end{equation}
This is precisely the complex conjugate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.24}.
The pair of conjugate equations may be written as the two-component
supercurrent conservation law
\begin{equation}
  \bar D^{\dot\alpha}\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =D_\alpha\mathcal X\,,
  \qquad
  D^\alpha\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\mathcal X^*\,,
  \tag{26.7.26}\label{eq:26.7.26}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal X\) is left-chiral. In this class of theories it is given,
up to an additive constant, by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal X=\frac{1}{3}
  \left[
    \sum_n\Phi_n\frac{\partial f(\Phi)}{\partial\Phi_n}
    -3f(\Phi)
  \right],
  \qquad
  X\equiv i(\mathcal X^*-\mathcal X)
  =\frac{2}{3}\operatorname{Im}
  \left[
    \sum_n\Phi_n\frac{\partial f}{\partial\Phi_n}-3f
  \right].
  \tag{26.7.27}\label{eq:26.7.27}
\end{equation}

Although it has been derived here only for renormalizable theories of chiral
superfields, we may expect that the conservation law \eqref{eq:26.7.26}
holds more generally, though of course with \(X\) not necessarily given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.27}, because of the other conservation laws that it
incorporates. (A generalized formula for \(X\) will be given in Section
31.4.) To derive these relations, we must use Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.10} to
express \(\Theta_\mu\) in terms of components \(C_\mu^\Theta\),
\(\omega_\mu^\Theta\), etc.\ and use Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.3.9} to express the
chiral superfield \(X\) in terms of components \(A^X\), \(\psi^X\), etc.
With the aid of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.A.9}, \eqref{eq:26.A.16},
\eqref{eq:26.A.17}, and the sigma-matrix identities
\begin{equation}
  \sigma^{\rho\sigma}
  =\frac14(\sigma^\rho\bar\sigma^\sigma
            -\sigma^\sigma\bar\sigma^\rho)
  =-\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu},
  \qquad
  \bar\sigma^{\rho\sigma}
  =\frac14(\bar\sigma^\rho\sigma^\sigma
            -\bar\sigma^\sigma\sigma^\rho)
  =+\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\bar\sigma_{\mu\nu}\,,
  \tag{26.7.28}\label{eq:26.7.28}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \sigma^\mu\bar\sigma^\rho\sigma^\nu
  ={}&-\eta^{\mu\rho}\sigma^\nu
      +\eta^{\mu\nu}\sigma^\rho
      -\eta^{\nu\rho}\sigma^\mu
      +i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\sigma_\sigma\,,
  \\
  \bar\sigma^\mu\sigma^\rho\bar\sigma^\nu
  ={}&-\eta^{\mu\rho}\bar\sigma^\nu
      +\eta^{\mu\nu}\bar\sigma^\rho
      -\eta^{\nu\rho}\bar\sigma^\mu
      -i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\bar\sigma_\sigma\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.29}\label{eq:26.7.29}
\end{equation}
we can then expand both sides of Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.26} in the terms
\[
  \begin{gathered}
  1,\quad \theta_\alpha,\quad\bar\theta_{\dot\alpha},\quad
  \theta^2,\quad\bar\theta^2,\quad
  \theta\sigma^\nu\bar\theta,\\
  \theta^2\bar\theta_{\dot\alpha},\quad
  \bar\theta^2\theta_\alpha,\quad
  \theta^2\bar\theta^2 .
  \end{gathered}
\]
Matching the coefficients in this monomial basis yields the
results\footnote{Note that \(V_{\mu\nu}^\Theta\) is the
\(V_\nu\)-component of \(\Theta_\mu\), not the \(V_\mu\)-component of
\(\Theta_\nu\).}
\begin{equation}
  \psi_\alpha^X
  =-(\sigma^\mu\bar\omega_\mu^\Theta)_\alpha\,,
  \qquad
  \bar\psi_{\dot\alpha}^X
  =(\bar\sigma^\mu\omega_\mu^\Theta)_{\dot\alpha}\,,
  \tag{26.7.30}\label{eq:26.7.30}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  F^X=\partial^\mu C_\mu^\Theta\,,
  \tag{26.7.31}\label{eq:26.7.31}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  G^X=(V^\Theta)^\mu{}_\mu\,,
  \tag{26.7.32}\label{eq:26.7.32}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\mu A^X=-N_\mu^\Theta\,,
  \tag{26.7.33}\label{eq:26.7.33}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\mu B^X=M_\mu^\Theta\,,
  \tag{26.7.34}\label{eq:26.7.34}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  0=V_{\mu\nu}^\Theta-V_{\nu\mu}^\Theta
    +\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial^\sigma C^{\Theta\rho}\,.
  \tag{26.7.35}\label{eq:26.7.35}
\end{equation}
Matching the coefficients of either \(\theta^2\) or \(\bar\theta^2\)
yields the same result:
\begin{equation}
  0=\bar\sigma^\mu\lambda_\mu^\Theta
  =\sigma^\mu\bar\lambda_\mu^\Theta\,,
  \tag{26.7.36}\label{eq:26.7.36}
\end{equation}
and matching the coefficient of \(\theta\sigma^\nu\bar\theta\) yields,
in undotted form,
\begin{equation}
  4i\sigma^{\nu\rho}\partial_\rho\psi^X
  =-2\sigma^\mu\bar\sigma^\nu\lambda_\mu^\Theta
   +4i\sigma^\mu\bar\sigma^{\nu\rho}
      \partial_\rho\bar\omega_\mu^\Theta\,,
  \tag{26.7.37}\label{eq:26.7.37}
\end{equation}
From Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.30}, \eqref{eq:26.7.36}, and
\eqref{eq:26.7.37} we obtain the conservation of the supersymmetry current
\eqref{eq:26.7.20}:
\begin{equation}
  0=\partial_\mu S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^\mu
  =-2\partial^\mu\omega_{\mu\alpha}^\Theta
   -2(\sigma^\mu\bar\sigma^\nu)_\alpha{}^\beta
      \partial_\mu\omega_{\nu\beta}^\Theta\,,
  \tag{26.7.38}\label{eq:26.7.38}
\end{equation}
and a relation among \(\lambda_\mu^\Theta\) and \(\omega_\mu^\Theta\):
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{\nu\alpha}^\Theta
  =i(\sigma^\rho\partial_\rho\bar\omega_\nu^\Theta)_\alpha
   -i\partial_\nu
      (\sigma^\mu\bar\omega_\mu^\Theta)_\alpha\,,
  \qquad
  \bar\lambda_{\nu\dot\alpha}^\Theta
  =(\lambda_{\nu\alpha}^\Theta)^* .
  \tag{26.7.39}\label{eq:26.7.39}
\end{equation}
Matching the coefficients of
\(\theta^2\bar\theta\) and \(\bar\theta^2\theta\) gives relations that can be
obtained by taking the divergence of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.34} and
\eqref{eq:26.7.33}, respectively. Matching the coefficients of
the remaining vector-spinor monomial gives
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\rho G^X
  =\partial^\mu V_{\mu\rho}^\Theta
   +\partial^\mu V_{\rho\mu}^\Theta
   -\partial_\rho V^{\Theta\lambda}{}_\lambda\,,
  \tag{26.7.40}\label{eq:26.7.40}
\end{equation}
which, combined with Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.32}, yields the conservation law
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0\,,
  \tag{26.7.41}\label{eq:26.7.41}
\end{equation}
where \(T^{\mu\nu}\) is the symmetric tensor
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mu\nu}
  \equiv-\frac12V_{\mu\nu}^\Theta-\frac12V_{\nu\mu}^\Theta
         +\eta_{\mu\nu}V^{\Theta\lambda}{}_\lambda\,.
  \tag{26.7.42}\label{eq:26.7.42}
\end{equation}
The conjugate vector-spinor coefficient gives
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\mu F^X
  =2D_\mu^\Theta+\Box C_\mu^\Theta
   +\epsilon_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}\partial^\nu
    V^{\Theta\rho\sigma}\,,
  \tag{26.7.43}\label{eq:26.7.43}
\end{equation}
which, with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.31} and \eqref{eq:26.7.35}, yields a relation
between \(D_\mu^\Theta\) and \(C_\mu^\Theta\):
\begin{equation}
  D_\mu^\Theta
  =-\Box C_\mu^\Theta+\partial_\mu\partial^\nu C_\nu^\Theta\,.
  \tag{26.7.44}\label{eq:26.7.44}
\end{equation}
Matching the final cubic and quartic monomials gives results that already follow from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.34} and from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.38} and
\eqref{eq:26.7.39}, respectively.

The conserved symmetric tensor \(T^{\mu\nu}\) may be identified as the
energy-momentum tensor of the system. To check this, we use
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.1.18} and \eqref{eq:26.2.12} to write the change in
\(\omega_\mu^\Theta(x)\) under a supersymmetry transformation with
infinitesimal parameters \(\xi_\alpha,\bar\xi_{\dot\alpha}\) as
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \delta\omega_{\mu\alpha}^\Theta
  ={}&(-M_\mu^\Theta+iN_\mu^\Theta)\xi_\alpha\\
   &-(\sigma^\nu\bar\xi)_\alpha
     (\partial_\nu C_\mu^\Theta+iV_{\mu\nu}^\Theta),
  \qquad
  \delta\bar\omega_{\mu\dot\alpha}^\Theta
  =(\delta\omega_{\mu\alpha}^\Theta)^* .
  \end{aligned}
\]
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.33}--\eqref{eq:26.7.35} allow us to put this in the
form
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \{\omega_{\mu\alpha}^\Theta,\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\}
  ={}&\sigma^\nu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
      (V_{\mu\nu}^\Theta-i\partial_\nu C_\mu^\Theta),\\
  \{\omega_{\mu\alpha}^\Theta,Q_\beta\}
  ={}&-i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}
      (M_\mu^\Theta-iN_\mu^\Theta),
  \end{aligned}
\]
with the conjugate relations obtained by complex conjugation.
In terms of the currents \eqref{eq:26.7.20} and \eqref{eq:26.7.42}, this
reads
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \{S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^\mu,\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\}
  ={}&-2\sigma_{\nu\,\alpha\dot\alpha}T^{\mu\nu}
      +\partial_\rho
       \mathcal I^{[\rho\mu]}_{\alpha\dot\alpha},\\
  \{\bar S_{\mathrm{new}\,\dot\alpha}^\mu,Q_\alpha\}
  ={}&-2\sigma_{\nu\,\alpha\dot\alpha}T^{\mu\nu}
      +\partial_\rho
       \overline{\mathcal I}^{[\rho\mu]}_{\alpha\dot\alpha},
  \\
  \{S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^\mu,Q_\beta\}
  ={}&4i(\sigma^{\mu\rho})_{\alpha\beta}
       \partial_\rho(B^X+iA^X),
  \\
  \{\bar S_{\mathrm{new}\,\dot\alpha}^\mu,\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\}
  ={}&-4i(\bar\sigma^{\mu\rho})_{\dot\alpha\dot\beta}
       \partial_\rho(B^X-iA^X).
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.45}\label{eq:26.7.45}
\end{equation}
Here the local improvement tensors \(\mathcal I\) and
\(\overline{\mathcal I}\) collect the terms involving
\(C_\mu^\Theta\) in the two mixed-chirality blocks and are antisymmetric in
\(\rho,\mu\).  The last two lines are the same-chirality blocks: they follow
directly from
\(\{\omega_{\mu\alpha}^\Theta,Q_\beta\}
=-i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}(M_\mu^\Theta-iN_\mu^\Theta)\), together with
the current definition and component relations immediately above.  Since
\(\sigma^{\mu\rho}\) and
\(\bar\sigma^{\mu\rho}\) are antisymmetric in their Lorentz indices, these
terms are divergences of local antisymmetric improvement tensors as well.
Thus the four displayed relations are the complete two-component block
decomposition of the original current algebra, and every improvement has a
surface-term \(\mu=0\) integral.
For \(\mu=0\) all the terms on the right except the first are space
derivatives, and therefore vanish when we integrate over space, leaving us
with
\begin{equation}
  \left\{
    \int d^3x\,S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^0,\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}
  \right\}
  =-2\sigma_{\nu\,\alpha\dot\alpha}
    \int d^3x\,T^{0\nu}\,.
  \tag{26.7.46}\label{eq:26.7.46}
\end{equation}
We have defined the supersymmetry current \(S_{\mathrm{new}}^\mu\) to give
\(\int d^3x\,S_{\mathrm{new}\,\alpha}^0=Q_\alpha\), so the fundamental anticommutation
relation \eqref{eq:25.2.36} tells us that
\begin{equation}
  \int d^3x\,T^{0\nu}=P^\nu\,,
  \tag{26.7.47}\label{eq:26.7.47}
\end{equation}
which, with the conservation condition \eqref{eq:26.7.41}, allows us to
identify \(T^{\mu\nu}\) as the energy-momentum tensor.

It is important to note \emph{which} energy-momentum tensor we have
constructed in this way. Either directly from Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.21}, or by
considering the supersymmetry transformation of the current
\eqref{eq:26.7.18}, we can calculate that the energy-momentum tensor
\(T^{\mu\nu}\) for renormalizable theories of chiral superfields is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  T^{\mu\nu}
  ={}&\sum_n\left[
       \partial^\mu\phi_n^*\partial^\nu\phi_n
       +\partial^\nu\phi_n^*\partial^\mu\phi_n
      \right]
  \\
   &-\eta^{\mu\nu}\sum_n\left[
       \partial^\lambda\phi_n^*\partial_\lambda\phi_n
       +\left|
          \frac{\partial f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_n}
        \right|^2
      \right]
  \\
   &+\frac13
    (\eta^{\mu\nu}\Box-\partial^\mu\partial^\nu)
    \sum_n|\phi_n|^2+\cdots\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{26.7.48}\label{eq:26.7.48}
\end{equation}
where the dots denote terms involving fermions, which do not concern us here.
We see that the last term, which is related by supersymmetry to the correction
term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.18}, has the effect that the energy-momentum
tensor is traceless for the massless free-field theory with no
superpotential, in which case \(\Box\phi_n=0\). A simple calculation shows
that \(T^{\mu\nu}\) is also traceless more generally for scale-invariant
theories, with \(f(\phi)\) a homogeneous polynomial of third order in the
\(\phi_n\).

Supersymmetry also imposes an interesting relation among violations of scale
invariance and \(R\) conservation. Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.30}--\eqref{eq:26.7.32}
show that \(\bar\sigma_\mu S_{\mathrm{new}}^\mu
=6\bar\sigma_\mu\omega^{\Theta\mu}\) (together with its conjugate),
\(\partial^\mu C_\mu^\Theta\), and
\(T^\lambda{}_\lambda=2V^{\Theta\mu}{}_\mu\) (which measures the violation
of scale invariance) are proportional to components of a chiral superfield
\(X\), so if any one of these vanishes as an operator equation (that is, not
just for some particular field configuration), then they all do. In this
case, we can show that \(C^{\Theta\rho}\) is proportional to the current of
an \(R\) quantum number. To see this, note that Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.11} gives
\[
  \delta C_\sigma^\Theta
  =-i[C_\sigma^\Theta,\xi Q+\bar\xi\bar Q]
  =i(\xi\omega_\sigma^\Theta-\bar\xi\bar\omega_\sigma^\Theta)\,,
\]
so that in general
\begin{equation}
  [C_\sigma^\Theta,Q_\alpha]=\omega_{\sigma\alpha}^\Theta\,,
  \qquad
  [C_\sigma^\Theta,\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}]
  =-\bar\omega_{\sigma\dot\alpha}^\Theta\,.
  \tag{26.7.49}\label{eq:26.7.49}
\end{equation}
We have seen that if \(C_\sigma^\Theta\) is conserved then
\(\bar\sigma_\mu S^\mu=\sigma_\mu\bar S^\mu=0\), so that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.20} gives
\(S_{\sigma\alpha}=-2\omega_{\sigma\alpha}^\Theta\) and its conjugate.
Setting \(\sigma=0\) and integrating
over \(\mathbf{x}\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.49} then gives
\begin{equation}
  \left[
    \int d^3x\,C^{0\Theta},Q_\alpha
  \right]
  =-\frac12Q_\alpha\,,
  \qquad
  \left[
    \int d^3x\,C^{0\Theta},\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}
  \right]
  =+\frac12\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\,.
  \tag{26.7.50}\label{eq:26.7.50}
\end{equation}
We can therefore introduce a current
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}^\mu\equiv2C^{\mu\Theta}\,,
  \tag{26.7.51}\label{eq:26.7.51}
\end{equation}
which \emph{if} conserved is the current of a quantum number
\(\mathcal{R}\equiv\int d^3x\,\mathcal{R}^0\) for which \(Q_\alpha\) and
\(\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\)
destroy the values \(+1\) and \(-1\), respectively. Since the commutator of
\(Q_\alpha\) with a scalar superfield \(\Phi\) involves a term
\(\partial\Phi/\partial\theta^\alpha\), this means that \(\theta^\alpha\) carries the
\(\mathcal{R}\) value \(+1\), in accord with the usual definition. A theory
in which the superfield \(X\) vanishes, or, equivalently, in which
\(T^\mu{}_\mu\), both sigma traces of the supercurrent, and
\(\partial_\mu\mathcal{R}^\mu\)
all vanish, is invariant under an enlarged set of supersymmetry
transformations, generated by the superconformal algebra described at the
end of Section 25.2.

In scale-invariant theories the value of the \(\mathcal{R}\) quantum number
carried by various superfields is fixed by the structure of the Lagrangian.
For instance, in a scale-invariant theory of chiral scalar superfields, the
superpotential must be a homogeneous polynomial of third order in the
superfields. The \(\mathcal{F}\)-term of the superpotential is proportional
to the coefficient of \(\theta^2\), which has \(\mathcal{R}\) quantum
number \(+2\), so the \(\mathcal{R}\) quantum number of the
\(\mathcal{F}\)-term of the superpotential is the \(\mathcal{R}\) quantum
number of the superpotential itself minus two. \(\mathcal{R}\) invariance
then requires that we give the scalar superfields an \(\mathcal{R}\) quantum
number \(+2/3\), so that the superpotential will have \(\mathcal{R}\)
quantum number \(+2\), and its \(\mathcal{F}\)-term will have
\(\mathcal{R}\) quantum number zero. That is, the scalar components \(\phi_n\)
have \(\mathcal{R}=2/3\) and the spinor components \(\psi_{n\alpha}\)
(proportional to the coefficient of \(\theta^\alpha\) in the superfield) have
\(\mathcal{R}=-1/3\). This can be verified by calculating the current
\(\mathcal{R}^\mu\) from the \(C\)-term of the supercurrent
\eqref{eq:26.7.21} for this class of theories:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_\mu
  =\frac{2i}{3}
    [\phi^*\partial_\mu\phi-\phi\partial_\mu\phi^*]
   +\frac13\psi\sigma_\mu\bar\psi\,.
  \tag{26.7.52}\label{eq:26.7.52}
\end{equation}
\emph{(The coefficient uses
\(\bar\psi\bar\sigma_\mu\psi=-\psi\sigma_\mu\bar\psi\) for
anticommuting Weyl fields.)}

Quantum corrections can introduce violations of \(\mathcal{R}\) invariance
(through Adler--Bell--Jackiw anomalies) and of scale invariance (through the
renormalization group running of the coupling constants) but supersymmetry
continues to impose a relation between these symmetry violations despite
these corrections~\hyperref[ch26-ref-7a]{[7a]}. We will see an example of
this in Section 29.3.

\end{center}

The conservation condition \eqref{eq:26.7.26} does not uniquely determine
either the supercurrent \(\Theta^\mu\) or the associated chiral superfield
\(X\). In particular, we may add to \(\Theta^\mu\) a change
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\Theta^\mu=\partial^\mu Y\,,
  \tag{26.7.53}\label{eq:26.7.53}
\end{equation}
with \(Y\) an arbitrary real chiral superfield. The left-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.7.26} is then changed by
\[
  \bar D^{\dot\alpha}\Delta\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
    \partial_\mu\bar D^{\dot\alpha}Y,
  \qquad
  D^\alpha\Delta\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}\partial_\mu D^\alpha Y .
\]
Write \(Y=Y_L+Y_L^*\), with
\(\bar D_{\dot\alpha}Y_L=D_\alpha Y_L^*=0\). The anticommutation relations
\eqref{eq:26.2.30} give
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  D_\alpha\bar D^2Y_L^*
  &=4i\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
    \partial_\mu\bar D^{\dot\alpha}Y_L^*,\\
  \bar D_{\dot\alpha}D^2Y_L
  &=-4i\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
    \partial_\mu D^\alpha Y_L .
  \end{aligned}
\]
The same equations vanish identically on the opposite chirality, and hence
\[
  \bar D^{\dot\alpha}\Delta\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =-\frac{i}{4}D_\alpha\bar D^2Y .
\]
Its conjugate is
\[
  D^\alpha\Delta\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =+\frac{i}{4}\bar D_{\dot\alpha}D^2Y .
\]
Thus the improvement obeys
\begin{equation}
  \bar D^{\dot\alpha}\Delta\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =D_\alpha\Delta\mathcal X,
  \qquad
  D^\alpha\Delta\Theta_{\alpha\dot\alpha}
  =\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Delta\mathcal X^* .
  \tag{26.7.54}\label{eq:26.7.54}
\end{equation}
This is of the same form as the conservation condition \eqref{eq:26.7.26},
with the associated chiral superfield \(X\) changed by the chiral superfield
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathcal X=-\frac{i}{4}\bar D^2Y,\qquad
  \Delta\mathcal X^*=+\frac{i}{4}D^2Y,\qquad
  \Delta X=-\frac14(D^2+\bar D^2)Y\,.
  \tag{26.7.55}\label{eq:26.7.55}
\end{equation}

It is easy to check that the addition of \(\Delta\Theta^\mu\) to
\(\Theta^\mu\) changes \(T^{\mu0}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{new}}^0\) only by space
derivatives, and therefore does not change the energy-momentum four-vector
\(P^\mu\) or the supercharge \(Q\).

We saw in Section 26.6 that any chiral superfield \(X\) may be expressed in
the form \(X=(D^2+\bar D^2)S\), and hence can be removed by adding a
term of the form \eqref{eq:26.7.55} to \(\Theta^\mu\) with \(Y=4S\). But in
general \(S\) and the new \(\Theta^\mu\) constructed in this way will not be
local. This situation is already familiar from our experience with triangle
anomalies, discussed in Chapter 22---we saw there that, although it is always
possible to construct terms that if added to the Lagrangian density would
cancel these anomalies, in general these terms would not be local, and hence
must be excluded from the Lagrangian density. There are chiral superfields
that can be expressed as \((D^2+\bar D^2)S\) with \(S\) local, and
that therefore, if present in the associated chiral superfield \(X\), could
be eliminated by adding local terms of the form \eqref{eq:26.7.53} to
\(\Theta^\mu\). They include, for instance, a term of the form
\(\operatorname{Re}(k\,\partial f(\Phi)/\partial\Phi)\), with \(k\) an
arbitrary complex constant, because the field equations \eqref{eq:26.6.15}
and \eqref{eq:26.6.16} show that
\((D^2+\bar D^2)\operatorname{Re}(k^*\Phi)
=4\operatorname{Re}(k\,\partial f(\Phi)/\partial\Phi)\). But in general the
changes in \(X\) that can be made in this way are quite limited.
