\subsection{Gravity-Mediated Supersymmetry Breaking}

We now take up again the problem of supersymmetry breaking. As discussed at
the beginning of Chapter 28, if supersymmetry is to be of use in solving the
hierarchy problem---that is, in understanding the large ratio of the Planck
mass \(m_{\mathrm{Pl}}\equiv 1/\sqrt{8\pi G}\) to the mass scale of observed
particles---then supersymmetry must be unbroken at the Planck scale, and
broken spontaneously only at some much lower mass scale. The only plausible
mechanism known that would naturally produce a very large ratio of mass
scales is the non-perturbative effect of asymptotically free gauge
interactions. If these interactions are moderately weak at the Planck scale
then their slow growth with decreasing energy will make them become strong
at a much lower scale \(\Lambda\ll m_{\mathrm{Pl}}\). The known elementary
particles do not feel such strong forces, so whatever supersymmetry
breakdown is produced directly or indirectly by these strong gauge
interactions must be communicated to the observed particles by some
interaction in which they do participate.

In Section 28.3 we noted two possible mechanisms for communicating the
breakdown of supersymmetry to observable particles. One mechanism,
gauge-mediated supersymmetry breaking, was discussed in detail in Section
28.6. We are now ready to consider the other mechanism, mediation of
supersymmetry breaking by effects of gravitational strength.

In the early 1980s, when gravitation was first considered as the mediator of
supersymmetry breaking~\hyperref[ch31-ref-14]{[14]}, it was generally assumed
that the superpotential consisted of two terms: a function \(f(\Phi)\) of
various left-chiral superfields \(\Phi_r\) of an \emph{observable sector},
including all the superfields of observable particles, plus a function
\(\widetilde f(Z)\) of various left-chiral superfields \(Z_k\) of a
\emph{hidden sector}~\hyperref[ch31-ref-15]{[15]}, all of which are neutral
under the \(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) gauge group of the standard model.
Further, the superpotential of the hidden sector was assumed to take the
form
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde f(Z)=\epsilon^3 F(\kappa Z)\,,
  \tag{31.7.1}\label{eq:31.7.1}
\end{equation}
where \(\epsilon\) is some mass that is much less than the Planck mass, and
\(F(\kappa Z)\) is a power series in \(\kappa Z\) with coefficients of order
unity. The assumption that the total superpotential should be a sum
\(f(\Phi)+\widetilde f(Z)\) is somewhat arbitrary but, as we shall see, it is
not difficult to think of reasons why this should be at least approximately
true. A more serious criticism of this approach is that it did not offer any
hope of solving the hierarchy problem; the energy \(\epsilon\) was simply
assumed to be much less than the Planck mass.

After the development of these first models of gravity-mediated
supersymmetry breaking there appeared other models in which the hierarchy
of energy scales is explained naturally, in terms of the slow growth with
decreasing energy of a gauge coupling which becomes strong at an energy
\(\Lambda\ll m_{\mathrm{Pl}}\). These models come in two versions that are
distinguished by different assumptions regarding the source of
supersymmetry breaking. As we shall see, in both versions the squarks and
sleptons get supersymmetry-breaking masses of the order of the gravitino
mass \(m_g\), but they differ in the formula for \(m_g\); in the first
version \(m_g\approx\kappa\Lambda^2\), while in the second version
\(m_g\approx\kappa^2\Lambda^3\), giving \(\Lambda\approx10^{11}\) GeV and
\(\Lambda\approx10^{13}\) GeV, respectively. The two versions will turn out
also to differ in the formulas for other soft supersymmetry-breaking
parameters, including \(B\mu\), the \(A\)-parameters, and the gaugino
masses.

\subsubsection*{First Version~\hyperref[ch31-ref-16]{[16]}}

In this version of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking it is assumed
that the superfields of the theory fall into two sectors:

\noindent\textbf{Observable Sector:} These are the superfields of the
minimal supersymmetric standard model: the
\(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) gauge superfields together with the quark,
antiquark, lepton, antilepton, and Higgs left-chiral superfields which we
will generically call \(\Phi_r\).

\noindent\textbf{Hidden Sector:} These are gauge superfields of an
asymptotically free gauge interaction that becomes strong at an intermediate
energy scale \(\Lambda\) with
\(m_W\ll\Lambda\ll m_{\mathrm{Pl}}\), together with left-chiral superfields
\(Z_k\) that feel this gauge interaction.

The \(Z_k\) must be assumed to be neutral under the
\(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) gauge group, since otherwise we would be back
in the case of gauge-mediated supersymmetry breaking. Also, we know enough
about the observable sector to be sure that its chiral superfields do not
feel the gauge interactions of the hidden sector.

In order naturally to have the renormalizable part of the total
superpotential of the form \(f(\Phi)+\widetilde f(Z)\), we can assume that
the symmetries that survive below the Planck scale include a group \(G_H\)
(which may be part of the gauge group of the hidden sector), under which all
of the fields of the observable sector and none of the fields of the hidden
sector are invariant, and a group \(G_O\) (which may be part of the
\(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) gauge group of the observable sector), under
which all of the fields of the hidden sector and none of the fields of the
observable sector are invariant. In this case if any observable sector
fields appear in a term of the superpotential then there must be at least
two of them, and also if any hidden sector fields appear in a term of the
superpotential then there must be at least two of them, so there can be no
term in a cubic polynomial superpotential that involves both hidden and
observable sector fields. This argument leaves open the possibility of
non-renormalizable terms in the superpotential that involve two or more
factors of both hidden and observable sector superfields, a possibility to
which we will return later. Of course, we are assuming that the strong
interactions of the hidden sector produce additional non-perturbative terms
in the total hidden sector superpotential \(\widetilde f(Z)\), but these too
depend only on the hidden sector superfields.

Assuming then that the superpotential takes the form
\(f(\Phi)+\widetilde f(Z)\), the potential of the scalar components of these
superfields is given by Eq. \eqref{eq:31.6.71} as
\begin{multline}
  V=e^{\kappa^2\dd}\Biggl[
  \sum_{rs}g_{rs}^{-1}
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial\phi_r}
  \right)
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_s}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial\phi_s}
  \right)^*
  \\
  +2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{rk}g_{rk}^{-1}
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial\phi_r}
  \right)
  \left(
    \frac{\partial\widetilde f}{\partial z_k}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial z_k}
  \right)^*
  \\
  +\sum_{kl}g_{kl}^{-1}
  \left(
    \frac{\partial\widetilde f}{\partial z_k}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial z_k}
  \right)
  \left(
    \frac{\partial\widetilde f}{\partial z_l}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial z_l}
  \right)^*
  -3\kappa^2|f+\widetilde f|^2
  \Biggr]
  \\
  +\frac12\operatorname{Re}\sum_{AB}f_{AB}^{-1}
  \left(
    \sum_{kl}\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial z_k}(t_A)_{kl}z_l
  \right)
  \left(
    \sum_{mn}\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial z_m}(t_B)_{mn}z_n
  \right)^*
  \\
  +\frac12\operatorname{Re}\sum_{AB}f_{AB}^{-1}
  \left(
    \sum_{rs}\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial\phi_r}(t_A)_{rs}\phi_s
  \right)
  \left(
    \sum_{tu}\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial\phi_t}(t_B)_{tu}\phi_u
  \right)^* .
  \tag{31.7.2}\label{eq:31.7.2}
\end{multline}
In writing the terms arising from gauge interactions, we are assuming here
that there is no mixing of the gauge bosons of the hidden and observable
sectors---that is, \(f_{AB}^{-1}\) vanishes for any pair of gauge generators
\(t_A\) and \(t_B\), for which \(t_A\) acts non-trivially on the \(\phi_r\)
and \(t_B\) acts non-trivially on the \(z_k\), or vice-versa.

We are interested in exploring a region of field space where the scalar
fields \(z_k\) of the hidden sector are of order \(\Lambda\), the variable
part of the superpotential \(\widetilde f(z)\) of the hidden sector is of
order \(\Lambda^3\), and, on dimensional grounds,
\(\partial\widetilde f/\partial z_k\) is of order \(\Lambda^2\). We will
leave the magnitude of the constant part of \(\widetilde f\) open for the
moment; as we shall see, we must include a constant term in
\(\widetilde f\) that is much larger than \(\Lambda^3\) in order to cancel
the cosmological constant.

We further define the region of field space to be explored as one in which
the fields \(\phi_r\) of the observable sector are of order
\(\kappa\Lambda^2\) because, as we shall see, this is the characteristic
mass scale arising in the observable sector from gravitational effects of
supersymmetry breaking in the hidden sector. The observable sector
superpotential \(f(\phi)\) for fields of order \(\kappa\Lambda^2\) is
assumed to be of order \(\kappa^3\Lambda^6\), and its derivatives
\(\partial f(\phi)/\partial\phi_r\) are taken to be of order
\(\kappa^2\Lambda^4\).

In consequence of the definition of the superpotential and the scalar
fields discussed at the end of the previous section, and the symmetry
\(G_H\times G_O\) assumed above, the modified Kähler potential takes the
form\footnote{By \(O(\kappa^2z^{*2}z^2)\) is meant a term of the form
\(\kappa^2\sum_{klmn}C_{klmn}z_k^*z_l^*z_mz_n\) with constant coefficients
\(C_{klmn}\) of order unity, and likewise for the other terms in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.7.3}--\eqref{eq:31.7.6}.}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  d(\phi,\phi^*,z,z^*)={}&
    \sum_r|\phi_r|^2+\sum_k|z_k|^2+O(\kappa^2z^{*2}z^2)
  \\
  &+O(\kappa^2z^*z^3)+O(\kappa^2z^{*3}z)
    +O(\kappa^2\phi^{*2}z^2)+O(\kappa^2\phi^{*2}z^*z)
  \\
  &+O(\kappa^2z^{*2}\phi^2)+O(\kappa^2z^*z\phi^2)
    +O(\kappa^2\phi^*\phi z^2)
  \\
  &+O(\kappa^2z^{*2}\phi^*\phi)
    +O(\kappa^2\phi^*z^*\phi z)+O(\kappa^2\phi^{*2}\phi^2)
  \\
  &+O(\kappa^2\phi^*\phi^3)+O(\kappa^2\phi^{*3}\phi)+\cdots\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.3}\label{eq:31.7.3}
\end{equation}
with dots indicating terms of higher order. The metric \eqref{eq:31.6.66}
then has components
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  g_{rs}={}&\delta_{rs}
  +O(\kappa^2z^2)+O(\kappa^2z^{*2})+O(\kappa^2z^*z)
  \\
  &+O(\kappa^2\phi^2)+O(\kappa^2\phi^{*2})
  +O(\kappa^2\phi^*\phi)+\cdots\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.4}\label{eq:31.7.4}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  g_{kl}={}&\delta_{kl}
  +O(\kappa^2z^2)+O(\kappa^2z^{*2})+O(\kappa^2z^*z)
  \\
  &+O(\kappa^2\phi^2)+O(\kappa^2\phi^{*2})
  +O(\kappa^2\phi^*\phi)+\cdots\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.5}\label{eq:31.7.5}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  g_{rk}=g_{kr}^*
  =O(\kappa^2\phi z^*)+O(\kappa^2\phi z)
   +O(\kappa^2\phi^*z^*)+O(\kappa^2\phi^*z)+\cdots\,.
  \tag{31.7.6}\label{eq:31.7.6}
\end{equation}
(The characteristic energy scale in \(d\) is assumed to be \(1/\kappa\),
because \(d\) is the modified Kähler potential produced by unknown
dynamical effects at the Planck scale, in contrast with \(\widetilde f\),
which gets its structure from dynamical effects of the strong gauge
couplings at scale \(\Lambda\).) The \(g_{rs}\) and \(g_{kl}\) are
generically of order unity, while the mixed components \(g_{rk}\) and
\(g_{kr}\) are of order of
\(\kappa^2(\kappa\Lambda^2)\Lambda=\kappa^3\Lambda^3\ll1\). It follows
that the same is also true of the components of \(g^{-1}\):
\((g^{-1})_{rs}\) and \((g^{-1})_{kl}\) are generically of order unity,
while the \((g^{-1})_{rk}\) and \((g^{-1})_{kr}\) are of order of
\(\kappa^3\Lambda^3\ll1\).

It follows from these estimates that unless cancellations intervene the
dominant terms in the potential \eqref{eq:31.7.2} will be at least of order
\(\Lambda^4\), and take the form
\begin{equation}
  [V]_{\Lambda^4}
  =\sum_k\left|\frac{\partial\widetilde f}{\partial z_k}\right|^2
   -3\kappa^2|\widetilde f^0|^2\,,
  \tag{31.7.7}\label{eq:31.7.7}
\end{equation}
with \(\widetilde f^0\) the constant term in \(\widetilde f\), which will be
needed to cancel the vacuum energy. We assume that supersymmetry is
spontaneously broken in the hidden sector, which requires that there is a
point \(z_k^0\) at which
\(\sum_k|\partial\widetilde f/\partial z_k|^2\) is at least a local minimum,
but not zero. Then in order to cancel the vacuum energy to this order, we
must take
\begin{equation}
  3\kappa^2|\widetilde f^0|^2
  =\sum_k\left|
    \left(\frac{\partial\widetilde f}{\partial z_k}\right)^0
  \right|^2\,,
  \tag{31.7.8}\label{eq:31.7.8}
\end{equation}
the superscript 0 on the right-hand side indicating that the quantity is to
be evaluated at \(z=z^0\). Hence \(\widetilde f^0\) must be given an
anomalously large value, of order \(\Lambda^2/\kappa\). This is a more
extreme fine tuning than will turn out to be necessary in the second
version of gravitationally mediated supersymmetry breaking but, in the
absence of a real understanding of the cosmological constant, some fine
tuning will be necessary in any theory of supersymmetry breaking.

We can calculate the supersymmetry-breaking parameter \(F\) in our formula
\eqref{eq:31.3.17} for the gravitino mass by setting the vacuum energy
density \(F^2/2\) equal to the flat-space value
\(\sum_k|(\partial\widetilde f/\partial z_k)^0|^2\). The gravitino mass is
then given by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.3.17} and \eqref{eq:31.7.8} as
\begin{equation}
  m_g=\kappa\sqrt{
    \frac13\sum_k\left|
      \left(\frac{\partial\widetilde f(z)}{\partial z_k}\right)^0
    \right|^2}
  =\kappa^2|\widetilde f^0|\,.
  \tag{31.7.9}\label{eq:31.7.9}
\end{equation}
This is of the same order \(\approx\kappa\Lambda^2\) as the scalar fields
of the observable sector.

Now let us turn to the terms in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} that \emph{do} depend
on the observable sector scalars \(\phi_r\). We are considering field values
for which the usual supersymmetric term
\(\sum_r|\partial f/\partial\phi_r|^2\) is of order
\(m_g^4\approx\kappa^4\Lambda^8\), so we have to collect all
\(\phi\)-dependent terms in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} of this order or greater.
Let us look in turn at each of the six lines on the right-hand side of
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2}.

\begin{sloppypar}
The leading term in
\(\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\partial d/\partial\phi_r\) is
\(\kappa^2\widetilde f^0\phi_r^*\), which like
\(\partial f/\partial\phi_r\) is of order
\(\kappa^2(\Lambda^2/\kappa)(\kappa\Lambda^2)
\allowbreak=\kappa^2\Lambda^4\), while
other terms in \(\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\partial d/\partial\phi_r\) are
much smaller. To leading order we can approximate \(\exp(\kappa^2d)\) by
unity and \(g_{rs}^{-1}\) by \(\delta_{rs}\), so in this order the first line
of Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} gives
\end{sloppypar}
\[
  \sum_r\left|
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2\widetilde f^0\phi_r^*
  \right|^2 .
\]
This is of the desired order \(\kappa^4\Lambda^8\), so it is not necessary
to consider higher-order corrections.

The leading term in \(g_{rk}^{-1}\) is of order
\(\kappa^3\Lambda^3\); the leading term in
\(\partial\widetilde f/\partial z_k\) is of order \(\Lambda^2\), while
\(\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\partial d/\partial z_k\) is smaller, of order
\(\kappa\Lambda^3\); and we have seen that the leading term in
\(\partial f/\partial\phi_r+
\kappa^2(f+\widetilde f)\partial d/\partial\phi_r\) is of order
\(\kappa^2\Lambda^4\), so the term on the second line of
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} is of order
\((\kappa^3\Lambda^3)(\Lambda^2)(\kappa^2\Lambda^4)
=\kappa^5\Lambda^9\), which may be neglected in comparison with the terms
in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} of order \(\kappa^4\Lambda^8\).

The leading terms in the third and fourth lines of Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2}
are of order \(\Lambda^4\), but these are independent of \(\phi_r\). There
is a \(\phi\) dependence coming from the terms on the third line involving
\(f\); the leading terms of this sort are
\(2\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}
[f\sum_k z_k^*(\partial\widetilde f/\partial z_k)^*]\), which are of
order \(\kappa^5\Lambda^9\ll\kappa^4\Lambda^8\), and hence may be
neglected. Also, there is a \(\phi\) dependence arising from the terms on
the fourth line of Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} involving \(f\). The leading terms
of this sort are
\[
  -6\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}[f\widetilde f^{0*}]
\]
which are of order
\(\kappa^2(\kappa\Lambda^2)^3\Lambda^2/\kappa
=\kappa^4\Lambda^8\). The factor \(\exp(\kappa^2d)\) contains
\(\phi\)-dependent terms of order \(\kappa^2(\kappa\Lambda^2)^2\), but
these multiply a potential whose leading terms \eqref{eq:31.7.7} have been
adjusted to cancel to order \(\Lambda^4\), so the \(\phi\)-dependent terms
arising from this source are much less than of order
\(\kappa^4\Lambda^8\). There is one other type of \(\phi\)-dependent term,
arising from the \(\phi\)-dependent terms in \(g_{kl}^{-1}\). According to
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.7.4}--\eqref{eq:31.7.6}, these terms may be written as
\(\kappa^2u_{kl}(\phi,\phi^*)\), with \(u_{kl}\) a homogeneous quadratic
polynomial in \(\phi_r\) and \(\phi_r^*\) with coefficients of order unity.
These give rise to a \(\phi\)-dependent term in the third line of
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2}, of the form
\[
  \kappa^2\sum_{kl}u_{kl}(\phi,\phi^*)
  \left(\frac{\partial\widetilde f}{\partial z_k}\right)^0
  \left(\frac{\partial\widetilde f}{\partial z_l}\right)^{0*},
\]
which is of order
\(\kappa^2(\kappa\Lambda^2)^2\Lambda^4=\kappa^4\Lambda^8\).

The leading \(\phi\)-dependent term in the fifth line of
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} arises either from \(\kappa^2\phi^2\) terms in
\(f_{AB}^{-1}\), with both \(d\)s given by their leading-order terms, of
order \(z^*z\), or from terms in one of the \(d\)s with two factors of
\(\kappa\), two factors of \(\phi\) and/or \(\phi^*\), and two factors of
\(z\) and/or \(z^*\), with \(f_{AB}\) and the other \(d\) given by their
leading-order terms, of order 1 and \(z^*z\) respectively. Both types of
\(\phi\)-dependent term make a contribution of order
\(\kappa^2(\kappa\Lambda^2)^2\Lambda^4=\kappa^4\Lambda^8\), so
higher-order terms may be neglected.

The leading term in the sixth line of Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} arises from the
leading term in \(f_{AB}^{-1}\), which is of order unity, and the leading
\(\phi\)-dependent terms in \(d\), which are of order \(\phi^*\phi\). This
makes a contribution to the potential of order
\((\kappa\Lambda^2)^4\), so here too higher-order terms may be neglected.

The potential \eqref{eq:31.7.2} also contains \(\phi\)-independent terms of
order \(\kappa^2\Lambda^6\), \(\kappa^4\Lambda^8\), and so on. The terms of
order \(\kappa^2\Lambda^6\) may be cancelled and the terms of order
\(\kappa^4\Lambda^8\) can be given an arbitrary value \(\mathcal C\) by
making a small shift of the constant term in \(\widetilde f(z)\) away from
the value given by Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.8}.

Putting these results together, to order
\(\kappa^4\Lambda^8\approx m_g^4\) the potential of the observable sector
is now
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  V_O(\phi,\phi^*)={}&
  \sum_r\left|
    \frac{\partial f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2\widetilde f^0\phi_r^*
  \right|^2
  -6\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}
    \left[f(\phi)\widetilde f^{0*}\right]
  \\
  &+\frac12\sum_A\left|
    \sum_{rs}\phi_r^*(t_A)_{rs}\phi_s
  \right|^2
  +Q(\phi,\phi^*)+\mathcal C\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.10}\label{eq:31.7.10}
\end{equation}
where \(Q(\phi,\phi^*)\) is a quadratic polynomial in \(\phi\) and/or
\(\phi^*\) with coefficients of order
\(\kappa^2\Lambda^4\approx m_g^2\), which arises from the
\(\phi\)-dependent terms in \(g_{kl}^{-1}\) on the third line of
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2} and from the \(\phi\)-dependent terms in
\(f_{AB}^{-1}\) and \(d\) on the fifth line of Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2}. We
have normalized the gauge superfields so that \(f_{AB}=\delta_{AB}\) when
all scalar fields vanish. (There is also a \(\phi\) dependence arising from
a \(\phi\)-dependent shift in the equilibrium value of the hidden sector
scalars \(z_k\), but this shift is at most of order
\((\kappa\Lambda^2)^4/\Lambda^3\), and since \eqref{eq:31.7.7} is supposed
to be stationary at \(z=z^0\) this shift enters quadratically in the
effective potential of the observable sector fields, and may therefore be
neglected.) The constant \(\mathcal C\) may be chosen so that the value of
this potential at its minimum is zero.

Finally, let us return to the non-renormalizable terms in the
superpotential. As already mentioned, the leading \(\Phi\)-dependent terms
of this sort may be expected to be of order \(\kappa\Phi^2Z^2\). When the
hidden sector superfields \(Z_k\) are set equal to their equilibrium values
\(z_k^0\) these terms become a second-order polynomial in the \(\Phi_r\),
with coefficients of order \(\kappa\Lambda^2\). Thus to leading order, we
can take account of these non-renormalizable terms by simply including a
quadratic polynomial function of the \(\Phi_r\) in the superpotential, with
coefficients of order \(\kappa\Lambda^2\approx m_g\).

In this way theories of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking avoid the
problem of the \(\mu\)-term, discussed in Sections 28.1 and 28.5. Recall
that the \(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) symmetry of the standard model
allows a single superrenormalizable term in the superpotential of the
minimal supersymmetric standard model, a term \(\mu(H_1^{\mathsf T}eH_2)\).
In order to explain in a natural way why the coefficient \(\mu\) is not of
the order of the Planck mass, it is necessary to impose some sort of
symmetry, like the `Peccei--Quinn' symmetry discussed in connection with
strong CP violation in Section 23.6, under which the product
\((H_1^{\mathsf T}eH_2)\) is not neutral. But a \(\mu\)-term with \(\mu\)
of the same order \(m_g\) as other supersymmetry-breaking masses was found
phenomenologically necessary in Section 28.5. Such a term can arise
naturally from the breaking of the Peccei--Quinn symmetry by the vacuum
expectation value of the hidden sector fields,\footnote{This is known as
the Giudice--Masiero mechanism~\hyperref[ch31-ref-17]{[17]}. It is often
described in terms of non-renormalizable holomorphic and antiholomorphic
terms in the modified Kähler potential \(d\) but, as discussed at the end
of the previous section, any such terms can be traded for holomorphic
factors in the superpotential. Here we have \emph{defined} \(d\) to not
contain holomorphic and antiholomorphic terms, and with this definition the
\(\mu\)-term can only arise from non-renormalizable terms in the
superpotential.} if the superpotential contains a non-renormalizable term
in which \((H_1^{\mathsf T}eH_2)\) appears multiplied with two powers of the
hidden sector fields \(z_k\), with a coefficient of order \(\kappa\).

We assume then that the effective superpotential \(f(\phi)\) consists of a
homogeneous polynomial \(f^{(3)}(\phi)\) of third order in the fields
\(\phi_r\), with coefficients very roughly of order unity, plus a
\(\mu\)-term that takes the form of a homogeneous polynomial
\(f^{(2)}(\phi)\) of second order in the fields \(\phi_r\), with
coefficients of order \(m_g\approx\kappa\Lambda^2\). The potential
\eqref{eq:31.7.10} then becomes
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  V_O(\phi,\phi^*)={}&
  \sum_r\left|\frac{\partial f(\phi)}{\partial\phi_r}\right|^2
  +\frac12\sum_{AB}\left|
    \sum_{rs}\phi_r^*(t_A)_{rs}\phi_s
  \right|^2
  \\
  &-2\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}
    \left[f^{(2)}(\phi)\widetilde f^{0*}\right]
  +\kappa^4|\widetilde f^0|^2\sum_r|\phi_r|^2
  \\
  &+Q(\phi,\phi^*)+\mathcal C\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.11}\label{eq:31.7.11}
\end{equation}
The terms on the first line of the right-hand side give a supersymmetric
potential, while the terms on the second and third lines represent a soft
breaking of supersymmetry. With \(\widetilde f^0\approx\Lambda^2/\kappa\)
and \((\partial\widetilde f/\partial z)^0\approx\Lambda^2\), the dimensional
constants in the soft supersymmetry-breaking terms in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.10} are all powers of
\(\kappa\Lambda^2\approx m_g\), so that this is where we expect to find the
expectation values of the observable sector scalar fields, justifying our
choice of this as the region of field space to explore.

Setting \(\kappa\Lambda^2\) equal to a typical mass \(\approx1\) TeV in the
effective Lagrangian of the supersymmetric standard model, we now find
\(\Lambda\approx10^{11}\) GeV. It is mildly encouraging that, as discussed
in Section 23.6, the spontaneous breakdown of the Peccei--Quinn symmetry at
a scale \(\Lambda\approx10^{11}\) GeV is just what is needed to resolve the
strong CP problem with a symmetry-breaking scale in the window from
\(10^{10}\) GeV to \(10^{12}\) GeV allowed by astronomical observations.

Comparing the potential with the scalar field terms in the Lagrangian
density \eqref{eq:28.4.1} of the minimal supersymmetric standard model, we
see that this version of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking predicts
that supersymmetry is broken only by soft scalar mass terms with
coefficients (including \(B\mu\)) of order \(m_g^2\). To leading order in
\(\kappa\Lambda\), the coefficients \(A\) and \(C\) of trilinear
supersymmetry-breaking terms vanish.

One serious problem with these results is that there is no reason why the
quadratic polynomial \(Q(\phi,\phi^*)\) in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.11} should
respect the degeneracy among squark masses and among slepton masses
discussed in Section 28.4, that would avoid unobserved flavor-changing
processes. However, the fourth term in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.11} for the
potential makes an additional contribution
\(\kappa^4|\widetilde f^0|^2\approx\kappa^2\Lambda^4\) to scalar squared
masses that is the same for all scalars, so the constraints imposed by
experimental upper bounds on flavor-changing processes might be satisfied
if the coefficients in \(Q(\phi,\phi^*)\) (which arise from terms on the
third and fifth lines of Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.2}) happen to be small compared
with \(\kappa^2\Lambda^4\).

We would have an interesting relation among the parameters of the minimum
supersymmetric standard model if it were really true that \(Q(\phi,\phi^*)\)
could be neglected. Taking the quadratic part \(f^{(2)}\) of the
superpotential as \(\mu(\phi_1^{\mathsf T}e\phi_2)\), the coefficient
\(B\mu\) in Eq. \eqref{eq:28.4.1} would be given by the second term of
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.11} as
\(B\mu=-\kappa^2\mu\widetilde f^{0*}\), so
\[
  |B|=\kappa^2|\widetilde f^0|=m_g\,,
\]
in agreement with Eq. \eqref{eq:31.4.13}. Also, all the squark and slepton
masses \(M_s\) would be given by the third term in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.11} as \(\kappa^2|\widetilde f^0|\), and so we would have
the new relation
\begin{equation}
  |B|=M_s\,.
  \tag{31.7.12}\label{eq:31.7.12}
\end{equation}
With all squark and slepton masses equal, there would be no inconsistency
with limits on flavor-changing processes. Furthermore, with \(Q\) neglected
there would be only one complex parameter \(\widetilde f^0\) in the
supersymmetry-breaking part of the potential \eqref{eq:31.7.11}, which can
be chosen to be real by a redefinition of the over-all phase of the
superpotential, so now the supersymmetry-breaking part of the potential
would introduce no new violation of CP invariance. But there is no known
reason why \(Q\) should be small.

Another serious problem with this version of gravitationally-mediated
supersymmetry breaking is that it does not yield sufficiently large gaugino
masses~\hyperref[ch31-ref-18]{[18]}. According to Eq.
\eqref{eq:31.6.75}, the mass matrix of the
\(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) gauginos is given in the tree approximation
by
\begin{equation}
  m_{AB}
  =\exp(\kappa^2d/2)\sum_{NM}[g^{-1}]_{NM}L_N
    \left(\frac{\partial f_{AB}}{\partial\varphi_M}\right)^*,
  \tag{31.7.13}\label{eq:31.7.13}
\end{equation}
where here \(\varphi_N\) runs over all the scalar fields \(\phi_r\) and
\(z_k\) on which \(f_{AB}\) may depend, with \(g_{NM}\) and \(L_M\) given by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.66} and \eqref{eq:31.6.69}. According to the estimates
we have made here, \(\kappa^2d=O(\kappa^2\Lambda^2)\ll1\);
\(L_k=O(\Lambda^2)\) while \(L_r\) is much smaller; and \(g_{kl}^{-1}\) is
of order unity. Also, we assume that
\(f_{AB}\) is a term of order unity plus a term of order \(\kappa^2\) times
a bilinear in scalar fields and their complex conjugates, so
\(\partial f_{AB}/\partial z_k\) is of order \(\kappa^2\Lambda\). This gives
gaugino masses of order \(\Lambda^2\cdot\kappa^2\Lambda\). This is much
smaller than the gravitino mass \(m_g\approx\kappa\Lambda^2\) (which sets
the scale of the supersymmetry-breaking terms in the potential
\eqref{eq:31.7.11} of the observable sector scalar fields) by a factor of
order \(\kappa\Lambda\approx10^{-7}\), so if the scalar masses produced by
supersymmetry breaking are of order 1 TeV, then the gaugino masses will be
of order 100 keV, which is far too low to be consistent with the fact that
gauginos have not yet been observed.

There are several ways that this problem might be avoided. One is to include
gauge-singlet scalar fields among the \(z_k\) of the hidden sector, which can
appear linearly in \(f_{AB}\)~\hyperref[ch31-ref-19]{[19]}. In this case
\(\partial f_{AB}/\partial z_k\) will be of order \(\kappa\) rather than
\(\kappa^2\Lambda\), yielding gaugino masses of order
\(\kappa\Lambda^2\), which is comparable to the squark and slepton masses.
One trouble with this approach is that the inclusion of scalars that are
neutral with respect to all gauge groups would make it no longer natural for
the renormalizable part of the superpotential to take the form
\(f(\phi)+\widetilde f(z)\).

Even without gauge singlets, there are one-loop contributions to the gluino,
wino, and bino masses (and to the \(A\)-parameters), calculated in Section
31.4. For instance, if we take \(m_g\) at the largest value
\(\approx10\) TeV allowed by the `naturalness' bound discussed in Section
28.1, then with \(g_s^2/4\pi=0.118\), Eq. \eqref{eq:31.4.13} would give a
gluino mass \(3g_s^2m_g/16\pi^2=280\) GeV, which is certainly high enough to
allow the gluino to have escaped detection. The bino and wino masses depend
on the unknown ratio of the \(\mu\) parameter and the pseudoscalar Higgs
mass \(m_A\). Taking this ratio equal to unity and \(m_g<10\) TeV,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.4.15} and \eqref{eq:31.4.16} would give
\(m_{\mathrm{bino}}=9g'^2m_g/16\pi^2<73\) GeV and
\(m_{\mathrm{wino}}=g^2m_g/16\pi^2<27\)
GeV~\hyperref[ch31-ref-19a]{[19a]}. This bound on the wino mass is in
conflict with the fact that wino pairs have not been seen at LEP in
\(e^+e^-\) collisions at an energy sufficiently high to produce \(W\)
boson pairs, so that \(m_{\mathrm{wino}}>m_W\). In order to avoid this
contradiction it would be necessary~\hyperref[ch31-ref-11b]{[11b]} either
to take \(m_g>30\) TeV, which is awkward from the point of view of
naturalness, or \(\mu^2/m_A^2>8\). In any case, this model has the general
consequence that the gauginos are much lighter than the squarks and
sleptons.

If the polynomial \(Q(\phi,\phi^*)\) can be neglected then, as shown in
Section 31.4, the \(A\)-parameters are also given by one-loop corrections.
These are of order \(g_s^2m_g/16\pi^2\) for squarks and
\(g^2m_g/16\pi^2\) or \(g'^2m_g/16\pi^2\) for sleptons.

\subsubsection*{Second Version~\hyperref[ch31-ref-20]{[20]}}

This version of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking has an observable
sector with chiral superfields \(\Phi_r\) and a hidden sector with chiral
superfields \(Z_k\), just as in the first version. The difference is that
supersymmetry is now assumed to be \emph{not} spontaneously broken in the
hidden sector. Instead, the gauge couplings of the hidden sector, which
become strong at an energy \(\Lambda\ll m_{\mathrm{Pl}}\), produce a
non-perturbative superpotential for the scalar fields of a third sector of
superfields, the \emph{modular superfields}. In various theories such as
modern superstring theories there are extra dimensions that are not
observed because they have been `rolled up' into a tiny compact manifold,
with size roughly of the order of \(\kappa\). Typically some of the
parameters that are needed to describe this compact manifold are not fixed
in any order of perturbation theory. The values of these parameters may
vary from point to point in four-dimensional spacetime and appear at
energies far below the Planck scale \(\kappa^{-1}\) as gauge-invariant scalar
fields \(y_a\), known as \emph{modular fields}. (The indices \(a,b\), etc.\
here of course have nothing to do with the local Lorentz frame indices used
in Section 31.6.) Assuming that supersymmetry is not broken in the
compactification of the extra dimensions, these fields must be accompanied
by fermionic superpartners and auxiliary fields, which together form
gauge-invariant left-chiral modular superfields \(Y_a\) and their
adjoints.\footnote{Of course, modular fields may exist even under the
assumptions of the first version of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking,
but because of the smaller value of \(\Lambda\) in that case the couplings
of the modular fields are too weak to be of interest there.}

Just below the compactification scale we have a supersymmetric theory with a
superpotential that can depend on all the superfields, but in which
\(\kappa\) is the only dimensional parameter. The circumstance that the
\(y_a\) are not fixed in perturbation theory typically arises because the
compactification does not result in any superpotential for the modular
superfields \(Y_a\) alone. As we saw in Section 28.1, the
\(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) gauge symmetries rule out any terms in the
superpotential with just one or two factors of observable sector
superfields, except for a possible term bilinear in the Higgs superfields
\(H_1\) and \(H_2\). We will again assume that this bilinear term in the
bare superpotential is either accidentally absent (in which case it does not
appear in any order of perturbation theory), or is ruled out by some
symmetry, such as the `Peccei--Quinn' symmetry discussed in connection with
strong CP violation in Section 23.6. The gauge symmetry of the hidden sector
rules out any terms in the superpotential with just one factor of the
\(Z_k\), and we shall assume that either accident or some symmetry (perhaps
the same Peccei--Quinn symmetry) also rules out terms with two factors of
the \(Z_k\).

The bare superpotential therefore takes the form
\begin{equation}
  f_{\mathrm{bare}}(\Phi,Y,Z)
  =\sum_{rst}f_{rst}(\kappa Y)\Phi_r\Phi_s\Phi_t
   +\sum_{klm}f_{klm}(\kappa Y)Z_kZ_lZ_m+\cdots\,,
  \tag{31.7.14}\label{eq:31.7.14}
\end{equation}
where \(f_{rst}\) and \(f_{klm}\) are power series in their arguments with
coefficients roughly of order unity, and the dots indicate terms involving
\(n>3\) factors of the \(\Phi\)s and \(Z\)s, as well as any number of
factors of \(\kappa Y_a\), suppressed by factors proportional to
\(\kappa^{n-3}\).

We assume that non-perturbative effects in the hidden sector such as
`gaugino condensation' (the appearance of expectation values of bilinear
functions of gaugino fields) without themselves breaking supersymmetry
produce a superpotential for the modular superfields. Since \(\Lambda\) is
the only scale in the problem (aside from gravitational effects suppressed
by factors of \(\kappa\Lambda\)), this superpotential would have to be of
the form
\begin{equation}
  \widehat f(Y)=\Lambda^3F(\kappa Y)\,.
  \tag{31.7.15}\label{eq:31.7.15}
\end{equation}
Such terms can also be produced by expectation values of the scalar
components of the \(Z\) superfields in the \(Z^3\) term in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.14}. At the same time, the replacement of the \(Z_k\) by
the expectation values of their scalar components in the non-renormalizable
terms indicated by dots in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.14} will produce additional
\(\Phi\)-dependent terms in the effective superpotential, about which more
later. The superpotential \eqref{eq:31.7.15} is of the form
\eqref{eq:31.7.1} originally assumed in theories of gravitationally
mediated supersymmetry breaking, but with \(\epsilon\) now identified as the
intermediate scale \(\Lambda\) at which the gauge interactions of the
hidden sector become strong.

It is plausible that the superpotential \eqref{eq:31.7.15} can lead to
supersymmetry breaking by the appearance of \(\mathcal F\)-terms for the
modular chiral superfields. For the moment, let's ignore the other
superfields, leaving the justification for later. The potential for the
modular scalars is given by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.68} and
\eqref{eq:31.6.69} as
\begin{equation}
  \widehat V(y,y^*)
  =\exp\left(\kappa^2\widehat d(y,y^*)\right)
  \left[
    \sum_{ab}[\widehat g^{-1}(y,y^*)]_{ab}
      \widehat L_a(y)\widehat L_b(y)^*
    -3\kappa^2|\widehat f(y)|^2
  \right]\,,
  \tag{31.7.16}\label{eq:31.7.16}
\end{equation}
where \(\widehat d(y,y^*)\) is the Kähler \(d\) function with the scalar
components of the \(Z_k\) and \(\Phi_r\) neglected, and
\begin{equation}
  \widehat g_{ab}
  =\frac{\partial^2\widehat \dd}{\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\,,
  \tag{31.7.17}\label{eq:31.7.17}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \widehat L_a
  =\frac{\partial\widehat f}{\partial y_a}
   +\kappa^2\widehat f\frac{\partial\widehat \dd}{\partial y_a}\,.
  \tag{31.7.18}\label{eq:31.7.18}
\end{equation}
We are assuming here that \(\widehat V\) has a stationary point, labelled
with a superscript 0, where \(\widehat L_a^0\ne0\), so that supersymmetry is
broken, but that \(\widehat V^0\) is very small, so that it can be cancelled
by terms arising from the observable sector, leaving us with a flat
spacetime. Since \(\widehat f\) is of the form \eqref{eq:31.7.15} and
\(\widehat d\) equals \(\kappa^{-2}\) times a power series in \(\kappa y_a\)
and \(\kappa y_a^*\) with coefficients of order unity, the whole potential
is of the form \(\kappa^2\Lambda^6\) times a power series in \(\kappa y_a\)
and \(\kappa y_a^*\), again with coefficients of order unity. The orders of
magnitude of the various ingredients in the potential are therefore
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  y_a^0=O(\kappa^{-1})\,,\qquad
  \widehat f^0=O(\Lambda^3)\,,\qquad
  \widehat \dd^0=O(\kappa^{-2})\,,
  \\
  \widehat L_a^0=O(\kappa\Lambda^3)\,,\qquad
  \widehat g_{ab}^0=O(1)\,.
  \end{gathered}
  \tag{31.7.19}\label{eq:31.7.19}
\end{equation}

With the modular and hidden sector fields fixed at their expectation values,
the superpotential of the observable sector is now of the form
\begin{equation}
  f(\Phi)
  =\sum_{rs}\mu_{rs}\Phi_r\Phi_s
   +\sum_{rst}g_{rst}\Phi_r\Phi_s\Phi_t+\cdots\,,
  \tag{31.7.20}\label{eq:31.7.20}
\end{equation}
where \(g_{rst}\) is \(f_{rst}(\kappa y^0)\), which is assumed to be roughly
of order unity, plus terms suppressed by powers of \(\kappa\Lambda\). The
dots here denote terms with more than three factors of \(\Phi\) that are
suppressed by additional factors of \(\kappa\Phi\). The coefficient
\(\mu_{rs}\) arises from the non-renormalizable terms denoted by dots in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.14}; if it comes from a term with \(n>1\) factors of
\(Z\)s as well as two factors of \(\Phi\)s, then it has an order of
magnitude
\begin{equation}
  \mu_{rs}=O(\kappa^{n-1}\Lambda^n)\,.
  \tag{31.7.21}\label{eq:31.7.21}
\end{equation}
We shall see that the desired order of magnitude of \(\mu\) is
\(m_g=O(\kappa^2\Lambda^3)\), which would come from a term with \(n=3\)
factors of \(Z\)s.

The supersymmetry breaking in the modular sector will be transmitted to the
observable sector by effects of the gravitational field and its
superpartners. Eq. \eqref{eq:31.6.68} gives the potential of the observable
sector as
\begin{multline}
  V_O=e^{\kappa^2\dd^0}\Biggl[
  \sum_{rs}[g^{0-1}]_{rs}
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widehat f^0)\frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_r}
  \right)
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_s}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widehat f^0)\frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_s}
  \right)^*
  \\
  +2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{ra}[g^{0-1}]_{ra}
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widehat f^0)\frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_r}
  \right)\widehat L_a^{0*}
  \\
  +\sum_{ab}[g^{0-1}]_{ab}\widehat L_a^0\widehat L_b^{0*}
  -3\kappa^2|f+\widehat f^0|^2
  \Biggr]
  \\
  +\frac12\operatorname{Re}\sum_{AB}[f_{AB}^{-1}]^0
  \left(
    \sum_{rs}\frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_r}
      (t_A)_{rs}\phi_s
  \right)
  \left(
    \sum_{tu}\frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_t}
      (t_B)_{tu}\phi_u
  \right)^*,
  \tag{31.7.22}\label{eq:31.7.22}
\end{multline}
with the superscript zero again indicating that the modular and hidden
sector scalar fields are fixed at their equilibrium values. (This will be
reconsidered later.) Note that, although Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} involves
terms like \(\widehat L_a^0\) that arise in the modular sector, there are no
terms here that refer explicitly to the hidden sector. This is because in
this version of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking, supersymmetry is
assumed not to be broken in this sector, so that \(L_k^0=0\), and
\(D_A^0=0\) for any gauge field that interacts with the hidden sector
fields.

We are interested in exploring a region of field space in which the
observable sector fields are of order \(\kappa^2\Lambda^3\), because, as we
shall see, this is the characteristic mass arising in the observable sector
from gravitational effects of supersymmetry breaking in the hidden sector.
According to Eq. \eqref{eq:31.6.48} and our estimate
\(\widehat f^0\approx\Lambda^3\), this is also the order of magnitude of the
gravitino mass \(m_g\):
\[
  m_g\approx\kappa^2\Lambda^3\,.
\]

In order to calculate the potential for fields of this order, we note that
the Kähler \(d\) function for the observable and modular scalar fields takes
the form
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  d(\phi,\phi^*,y,y^*)={}&
  \kappa^{-2}\widehat d(\kappa y,\kappa y^*)
  +\sum_{rs}\phi_r\phi_s^*A_{rs}(\kappa y,\kappa y^*)
  \\
  &+\sum_{rs}\phi_r\phi_sB_{rs}(\kappa y,\kappa y^*)
  +\sum_{rs}\phi_r^*\phi_s^*B_{rs}^*(\kappa y,\kappa y^*)
  +\cdots\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.23}\label{eq:31.7.23}
\end{equation}
where \(\widehat d\), \(A_{rs}\), and \(B_{rs}\) are power series in their
arguments with coefficients of order unity, and the dots indicate terms
with \(n>2\) factors of \(\phi\) and/or \(\phi^*\), suppressed by factors
\(\kappa^{n-2}\). According to Eq. \eqref{eq:31.6.72}, we can remove any
holomorphic term in \(d\) along with its complex conjugate by multiplying
the total superpotential by a suitable holomorphic factor. In particular,
by multiplying the total superpotential with a factor
\(\exp[\kappa^2\widehat \dd^0+
\kappa^2\sum_{rs}B_{rs}^0\phi_r\phi_s]\), we can arrange that the
transformed \(d\) function has
\begin{equation}
  \widehat \dd^0=0,\qquad B_{rs}^0=0\,.
  \tag{31.7.24}\label{eq:31.7.24}
\end{equation}
We shall assume that this has been done. Note that, because the total
superpotential contains a constant term \(\widehat f^0\) of order
\(\Lambda^3\), while \(B_{rs}^0\) was of order unity, this transformation
generates a term in the superpotential that is quadratic in \(\phi_r\),
making a contribution to its coefficient \(\mu_{rs}\) of order
\(\kappa^2\Lambda^3\), of the same order as \(\phi\) and \(m_g\).

There can be another contribution to this coefficient of the same order of
magnitude, arising from terms in the superpotential with \(n>1\) factors of
\(Y\)s as well as two factors of \(\Phi\)s. Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.21} tells us
that, in order for the contribution to the \(\mu_{rs}\) coefficients from
this also to be of order \(\kappa^2\Lambda^3\), we must have \(n=3\). We can
arrange that this is allowed, while terms with two factors of \(\Phi\)s and
\(n=2\) factors of \(Y\)s are forbidden, by giving \(H_1\) and \(H_2\)
Peccei--Quinn quantum numbers \(+1\) and the \(Y\)s quantum numbers
\(-2/3\). The breaking of this Peccei--Quinn symmetry by the vacuum
expectation values of the \(Y_k\) then produces an axion.

With \(\mu_{rs}\) of order \(\kappa^2\Lambda^3\), both the bilinear and
trilinear terms in the superpotential of the observable sector are of order
\(\kappa^6\Lambda^9\). The usual supersymmetric potential term
\(\sum_r|\partial f/\partial\phi_r|^2\) is then of order
\(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\), so we have to collect all terms in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} of this order or greater.

With \(\phi_r\) of order \(\kappa^2\Lambda^3\) and \(y_a\) fixed at its
equilibrium value \(y_a^0\approx\kappa^{-1}\), Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.23}
gives
\begin{equation}
  g_{rs}^0=A_{rs}^0+O(\kappa^3\Lambda^3)\,,
  \tag{31.7.25}\label{eq:31.7.25}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  g_{ab}^0={}&
  \left(\frac{\partial^2\widehat \dd}
    {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  +\sum_{rs}\phi_r\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2A_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  \\
  &+\sum_{rs}\phi_r\phi_s
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  +\sum_{rs}\phi_r^*\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}^*}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  +O(\kappa^6\Lambda^6)\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.26}\label{eq:31.7.26}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  g_{ra}^0
  =\sum_s\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial A_{rs}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0
   +O(\kappa^6\Lambda^6)
  =g_{ar}^{0*}\,,
  \tag{31.7.27}\label{eq:31.7.27}
\end{equation}
where the zero superscript again indicates that the \(y_a\) are fixed at
their equilibrium values \(y_a^0\). We will subject the observable-sector
superfields \(\Phi_r\) and the modular superfields \(Y_a\) to separate
linear transformations, designed so that
\begin{equation}
  A_{rs}^0=\delta_{rs},\qquad
  \left(\frac{\partial^2\widehat \dd}
    {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  =\delta_{ab}\,.
  \tag{31.7.28}\label{eq:31.7.28}
\end{equation}
With the metric \eqref{eq:31.7.25}--\eqref{eq:31.7.27} given by the unit
matrix plus terms that are much less than unity, it is easy to calculate
the inverse:
\begin{equation}
  g_{rs}^{0-1}=\delta_{rs}+O(\kappa^3\Lambda^3)\,,
  \tag{31.7.29}\label{eq:31.7.29}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  g_{ab}^{0-1}={}&\delta_{ab}
  -\sum_{rs}\phi_r\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2A_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  -\sum_{rs}\phi_r\phi_s
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  \\
  &-\sum_{rs}\phi_r^*\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}^*}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  +O(\kappa^6\Lambda^6)\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.30}\label{eq:31.7.30}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  g_{ra}^{0-1}
  =-\sum_s\phi_s
    \left(\frac{\partial A_{rs}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0
   +O(\kappa^6\Lambda^6)
  =g_{ar}^{0-1*}\,.
  \tag{31.7.31}\label{eq:31.7.31}
\end{equation}
In particular, our assumption about the form of the functions \(A_{rs}\)
and \(B_{rs}\) gives the order-of-magnitude estimates
\begin{equation}
  \left(\frac{\partial^2A_{rs}}
    {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0=O(\kappa^2),
  \qquad
  \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}}
    {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0=O(\kappa^2),
  \qquad
  \left(\frac{\partial A_{rs}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0=O(\kappa),
  \tag{31.7.32}\label{eq:31.7.32}
\end{equation}
so that, for \(\phi_r=O(\kappa^2\Lambda^3)\),
\begin{equation}
  [g^{0-1}]_{rs}=O(1),\qquad
  [g^{0-1}]_{ab}=O(1),\qquad
  [g^{0-1}]_{ra}=O(\kappa^3\Lambda^3)\,.
  \tag{31.7.33}\label{eq:31.7.33}
\end{equation}

Further,
\begin{equation}
  f=O(\phi^3)=O(\kappa^6\Lambda^9),\qquad
  \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
  =O(\phi^2)=O(\kappa^4\Lambda^6),
  \tag{31.7.34}\label{eq:31.7.34}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_r}
  =O(\phi)=O(\kappa^2\Lambda^3)\,.
  \tag{31.7.35}\label{eq:31.7.35}
\end{equation}
With \(\widehat f^0\) of order \(\Lambda^3\), the quantities
\(\kappa^2(f+\widehat f^0)\partial d/\partial\phi_r\) in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} are dominated by
\(\kappa^2\widehat f^0\phi_r^*\), which is of order
\(\kappa^2\cdot\Lambda^3\cdot\kappa^2\Lambda^3
=\kappa^4\Lambda^6\). This is of the same order of magnitude as
\(\partial f/\partial\phi_r\), so in leading order we must keep both terms:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
  +\kappa^2(f+\widehat f^0)\frac{\partial \dd}{\partial\phi_r}
  \simeq
  \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
  +\kappa^2\widehat f^0\phi_r^*
  =O(\kappa^4\Lambda^6)\,.
  \tag{31.7.36}\label{eq:31.7.36}
\end{equation}
In this approximation, and with \(g_{rs}^{0-1}\) replaced by its dominant
term \(\delta_{rs}\), the first term in the square brackets in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} is already of the desired order
\(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\), so we may use these approximations to write
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\sum_{rs}[g^{0-1}]_{rs}
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widehat f^0)\frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_r}
  \right)
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_s}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widehat f^0)\frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_s}
  \right)^*
  \\
  &\hspace{30mm}\simeq
  \sum_r\left|
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2\widehat f^0\phi_r^*
  \right|^2 .
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.37}\label{eq:31.7.37}
\end{equation}
The second term in the square brackets in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} is of
order
\(\kappa^3\Lambda^3\cdot\kappa^4\Lambda^6\cdot\kappa\Lambda^3
=\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\), so we may evaluate it using only leading terms and
find
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{ra}[g^{0-1}]_{ra}
  \left(
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2(f+\widehat f^0)\frac{\partial \dd^0}{\partial\phi_r}
  \right)\widehat L_a^{0*}
  \\
  &\hspace{12mm}\simeq
  -2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{ras}\phi_s
  \left(\frac{\partial A_{rs}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0
  \left[
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2\widehat f^0\phi_r^*
  \right]\widehat L_a^{0*}.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.38}\label{eq:31.7.38}
\end{equation}
The third and fourth terms in the square brackets in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} are individually of order
\(\kappa^2\Lambda^6\), but are assumed nearly to cancel, so non-leading
terms must be included in evaluating their contribution to the quantity in
square brackets. One contribution comes from the terms in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.30} for \(g_{ab}^{0-1}\) that are of second order in
\(\phi\) and/or \(\phi^*\); this contribution is of order
\(\kappa^2\phi^2\cdot(\kappa\Lambda^3)^2\), which is of order
\(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\). Another non-cancelling contribution to the
potential from the last term in square brackets in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22}
comes from the interference between \(f\) and \(\widehat f^0\), which makes
a contribution of order \(\kappa^2\phi^3\Lambda^3\), which is also of order
\(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\). There is also a constant contribution
\(\mathcal C\) from a possible failure of the last two terms in the square
brackets in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} to cancel when \(\phi_r=0\); to avoid a
large cosmological constant, we shall have to assume that \(\mathcal C\) is
also of order \(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\). (This is an unnatural fine-tuning,
which so far has been necessary in any theory that avoids a huge
cosmological constant.) Putting these estimates together, to order
\(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\) the last two terms in the square brackets in
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\sum_{ab}[g_{ab}^{-1}]^0\widehat L_a^0\widehat L_b^{0*}
  -3\kappa^2|f+\widehat f^0|^2
  \simeq
  -\sum_{abrs}\Biggl[
    \phi_r\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2A_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  \\
  &\qquad\qquad
  +\phi_r\phi_s
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  +\phi_r^*\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}^*}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  \Biggr]\widehat L_a^0\widehat L_b^{0*}
  -6\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}(f\widehat f^{0*})+\mathcal C\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.39}\label{eq:31.7.39}
\end{equation}
All these terms are of the desired order \(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\), while
Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.24} gives
\(\kappa^2\dd^0=O(\kappa^2\phi^2)=O(\kappa^6\Lambda^6)\), so we can ignore the
factor \(\exp(\kappa^2\dd^0)\) in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22}. Finally, the gauge
terms on the last line of Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.22} are of order
\(\phi^4=O(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12})\), so these can be evaluated using the
leading term \(\phi_r^*\) in \(\partial \dd^0/\partial\phi_r\).

Putting this all together, the complete scalar potential of the observable
sector to order \(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}\) is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  V_O\simeq{}&
  \sum_r\left|
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2\widehat f^0\phi_r^*
  \right|^2
  \\
  &-2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{ras}\phi_s
  \left(\frac{\partial A_{rs}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0
  \left[
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2\widehat f^0\phi_r^*
  \right]\widehat L_a^{0*}
  \\
  &-\sum_{abrs}\Biggl[
    \phi_r\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2A_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  +\phi_r\phi_s
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  \\
  &\hspace{36mm}
  +\phi_r^*\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}^*}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  \Biggr]\widehat L_a^0\widehat L_b^{0*}
  -6\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}(f\widehat f^{0*})
  \\
  &+\frac12\operatorname{Re}\sum_{AB}[f_{AB}^{-1}]^0
  \left(\sum_{rs}\phi_r^*(t_A)_{rs}\phi_s\right)
  \left(\sum_{tu}\phi_t^*(t_B)_{tu}\phi_u\right)^*
  +\mathcal C\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.40}\label{eq:31.7.40}
\end{equation}

The potential \eqref{eq:31.7.40} of the observable sector scalar fields
takes the form assumed in the minimal supersymmetric standard models
discussed in Section 28.4: it is the sum of a supersymmetric term
\(V_{\mathrm{susy}}\) and a soft supersymmetry-breaking term
\(V_{\mathrm{soft}}\)
\begin{equation}
  V_O=V_{\mathrm{susy}}+V_{\mathrm{soft}}\,.
  \tag{31.7.41}\label{eq:31.7.41}
\end{equation}
The supersymmetric term is, as usual,
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{susy}}
  =\sum_r\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}\right|^2
  +\frac12\operatorname{Re}\sum_{AB}[f_{AB}^{-1}]^0
  \left(\sum_{rs}\phi_r^*(t_A)_{rs}\phi_s\right)
  \left(\sum_{tu}\phi_t^*(t_B)_{tu}\phi_u\right)^*,
  \tag{31.7.42}\label{eq:31.7.42}
\end{equation}
and the soft supersymmetry breaking term here is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  V_{\mathrm{soft}}\simeq{}&
  2\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}\sum_r
    \left(\phi_r\frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
      \widehat f^{0*}\right)
  +\kappa^4|\widehat f^0|^2\sum_r|\phi_r|^2
  \\
  &-2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{ras}\phi_s
  \left(\frac{\partial A_{rs}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0
  \left[
    \frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_r}
    +\kappa^2\widehat f^0\phi_r^*
  \right]\widehat L_a^{0*}
  \\
  &-\sum_{abrs}\Biggl[
    \phi_r\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2A_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  +\phi_r\phi_s
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  \\
  &\hspace{36mm}
  +\phi_r^*\phi_s^*
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}^*}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
  \Biggr]\widehat L_a^0\widehat L_b^{0*}
  -6\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}(f\widehat f^{0*})
  +\mathcal C\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.43}\label{eq:31.7.43}
\end{equation}
With \(f(\phi)\) given by Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.20}, this takes the form
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{soft}}
  =\sum_{rs}M_{rs}^2\phi_r\phi_s^*
  +2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{rs}N_{rs}^2\phi_r\phi_s
  +2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{rst}A_{rst}\phi_r\phi_s\phi_t
  +\mathcal C\,,
  \tag{31.7.44}\label{eq:31.7.44}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  M_{rs}^2={}&
  \kappa^4|\widehat f^0|^2\delta_{rs}
  -2\kappa^2\operatorname{Re}\left[
    \widehat f^0\sum_a
    \left(\frac{\partial A_{sr}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0
    \widehat L_a^{0*}
  \right]
  \\
  &-\sum_{ab}
    \left(\frac{\partial^2A_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
    \widehat L_a^0\widehat L_b^{0*}\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.45}\label{eq:31.7.45}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  N_{rs}^2={}&
  2\kappa^2\mu_{rs}\widehat f^{0*}
  -\frac12\sum_{at}\mu_{tr}
    \left(\frac{\partial A_{ts}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0
    \widehat L_a^{0*}
  \\
  &-\frac12\sum_{at}\mu_{ts}
    \left(\frac{\partial A_{tr}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0
    \widehat L_a^{0*}
  -\sum_{ab}
    \left(\frac{\partial^2B_{rs}}
      {\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}\right)^0
    \widehat L_a^0\widehat L_b^{0*}\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.7.46}\label{eq:31.7.46}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  A_{rst}
  =-\sum_{au}\left[
    \left(\frac{\partial A_{ur}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0g_{ust}
   +\left(\frac{\partial A_{us}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0g_{urt}
   +\left(\frac{\partial A_{ut}}{\partial y_a^*}\right)^0g_{urs}
  \right]\widehat L_a^{0*}.
  \tag{31.7.47}\label{eq:31.7.47}
\end{equation}
Our previous order-of-magnitude estimates give
\begin{equation}
  M_{rs}^2=O(\kappa^4\Lambda^6),\qquad
  N_{rs}^2=O(\kappa^4\Lambda^6),\qquad
  A_{rst}=O(\kappa^2\Lambda^3)\,.
  \tag{31.7.48}\label{eq:31.7.48}
\end{equation}
Together with our estimates \(g_{rst}=O(1)\) and
\(\mu_{rs}=O(\kappa^2\Lambda^3)\) for the constants in
\(V_{\mathrm{susy}}\), this shows that if the potential has a stationary
point at some \(\phi_a^0\ne0\), then
\(\phi_a^0=O(\kappa^2\Lambda^3)\), justifying our decision to explore fields
of this order. For an equilibrium value of the \(\phi_r\) of this order, the
equilibrium value of the various terms in the potential is
\(O(\phi^4)=O(\kappa^8\Lambda^{12})\), so this is the order of magnitude of
the constant \(\mathcal C\) that is needed to cancel the vacuum energy.

In order for the characteristic mass \(\kappa^2\Lambda^3\) in the minimal
supersymmetric standard model to be of order 1 TeV we need
\(\Lambda\approx10^{13}\) GeV. If it is a Peccei--Quinn symmetry that
forbids a term in the bare superpotential with two superfield factors each
from the observable and hidden sectors, as suggested above, then vacuum
expectation values of the hidden sector scalars will break this symmetry,
with a symmetry-breaking scale (called \(M\) in Section 23.6) of order
\(10^{13}\) GeV, and an axion mass which is then given by
Eq. \eqref{eq:23.6.26} as of order \(10^{-6}\) eV. This value for the
symmetry-breaking scale is somewhat above the upper bound of \(10^{12}\)
GeV quoted in Section 23.6, but given the uncertainty of cosmological
arguments, this contradiction is not decisive.

Before considering further physical implications of these results, let's
pause to reconsider a short-cut that we took in deriving them. In
calculating the potential for the observable sector scalars \(\phi_r\), we
fixed the modular fields at the equilibrium values \(y_a^0\) that they would
have in the absence of the observable sector fields \(\phi_r\). Instead, we
ought to set the modular fields at their equilibrium values \(y_a(\phi)\)
for the actual values of the \(\phi_r\), by finding the stationary point of
the potential
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{total}}(\phi,\phi^*,y,y^*)
  =\widehat V(y,y^*)+V_O(\phi,\phi^*,y,y^*)\,,
  \tag{31.7.49}\label{eq:31.7.49}
\end{equation}
and only then seek an equilibrium value for the \(\phi_r\). Since \(V_O\)
is much smaller than \(\widehat V\) for fields of interest, the equilibrium
value of \(y_a\) can be written as
\begin{equation}
  y_a(\phi,\phi^*)=y_a^0+\delta y_a(\phi,\phi^*)\,,
  \tag{31.7.50}\label{eq:31.7.50}
\end{equation}
where \(y_a^0\) is at the minimum of \(\widehat V(y,y^*)\) and
\begin{equation}
  \sum_b\frac{\partial^2\widehat V}{\partial y_a\partial y_b}
    \delta y_b
  +\sum_b\frac{\partial^2\widehat V}{\partial y_a\partial y_b^*}
    \delta y_b^*
  =-\frac{\partial V_O}{\partial y_a}\,.
  \tag{31.7.51}\label{eq:31.7.51}
\end{equation}
The second derivatives of \(\widehat V\) are of order
\(\kappa^2\cdot(\kappa\Lambda^3)^2=\kappa^4\Lambda^6\), while the first
derivatives of \(V_O\) are of order
\(\kappa\cdot\kappa^8\Lambda^{12}=\kappa^9\Lambda^{12}\), so the
\(\delta y_a\) are of order \(\kappa^5\Lambda^6\). The change in the
potential due to this \(\phi\)-dependent shift in the equilibrium values of
the \(y_a\) is quadratic in the \(\delta y_a\) and \(\delta y_a^*\), with
coefficients given by second derivatives of \(\widehat V\) with respect to
\(y_a\) and/or \(y_a^*\), and is therefore of order
\[
  (\kappa^5\Lambda^6)^2\cdot\kappa^2\cdot(\kappa\Lambda^3)^2
  =\kappa^{14}\Lambda^{18}\,,
\]
which is less than the potential we have calculated by a factor
\((\kappa\Lambda)^6\ll1\).

Without further assumptions regarding the functions \(A_{rs}(y,y^*)\) and
\(B_{rs}(y,y^*)\), we can learn nothing whatever from
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.7.45}--\eqref{eq:31.7.47} about the precise values of the
coefficients \(M_{rs}^2\), \(N_{rs}^2\), and \(A_{rst}\) in the soft
supersymmetry-breaking potential \eqref{eq:31.7.44}. The only definite
prediction that emerges from these results is that the coefficients
\(C_{ij}\) in the soft supersymmetry-breaking Lagrangian \eqref{eq:28.4.1}
are all negligible, as has generally been assumed anyway.

The greatest problem presented by the results
\eqref{eq:31.7.44}--\eqref{eq:31.7.48} is that without further assumptions
they do not insure the degeneracy of squark masses and of slepton masses
that would avoid the quark and lepton flavor-changing processes discussed
in Section 28.4. The \(\phi_r\phi_s\)-terms in the soft
supersymmetry-breaking potential \eqref{eq:31.7.44} and in the
superpotential \eqref{eq:31.7.20} are not allowed by
\(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) to depend on anything but the Higgs scalars,
so they cannot lead to flavor-changing processes. The problem therefore
arises only from the fact that the coefficients \(M_{rs}^2\) and \(A_{rst}\)
in Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.44} may not conserve flavor in the same basis as the
Yukawa couplings \(g_{rst}\). One way to avoid this problem is to assume
that for some reason the functions \(A_{rs}(y,y^*)\) happen to depend only
weakly on the \(y_a\) and \(y_a^*\), so that Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.45} gives
\(M_{rs}^2\propto\delta_{rs}\) and Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.47} makes \(A_{rst}\)
(and hence the \(A_{ij}\) coefficients in Eq. \eqref{eq:28.4.1})
anomalously small. Another possibility is that, although not slowly varying,
for some reason the whole function \(A_{rs}(y,y^*)\) (or at least its first
and second derivatives at \(y_a=y_a^0\)) is proportional to
\(\delta_{rs}\). In this case, Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.45} again gives
\(M_{rs}^2\propto\delta_{rs}\), and now Eq. \eqref{eq:31.7.47} gives
trilinear couplings \(A_{rst}\propto g_{rst}\), so that the \(A_{ij}\)
coefficients in Eq. \eqref{eq:28.4.1} would be all
equal~\hyperref[ch31-ref-21]{[21]}.

We must also check the gaugino masses produced by this version of
gravitationally mediated supersymmetry breaking. According to Eq.
\eqref{eq:31.6.75}, the mass matrix of the
\(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) gauginos is given in general by
\begin{equation}
  m_{AB}
  =\exp(\kappa^2d/2)\sum_{NM}[g^{-1}]_{NM}L_N
    \left(\frac{\partial f_{AB}}{\partial\varphi_M}\right)^*,
  \tag{31.7.52}\label{eq:31.7.52}
\end{equation}
where \(\varphi_N\) here runs over all the fields on which \(f_{AB}\) may
depend, with \(g_{NM}\) and \(L_M\) given by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.66} and
\eqref{eq:31.6.69}. According to the estimates we have made here,
\(\kappa^2\dd\ll1\); \(L_a=O(\kappa\Lambda^3)\) for the modular fields \(y_a\)
and is much smaller for the other fields; and \(g_{ab}^{-1}\simeq
\delta_{ab}\). Also, we assume that \(f_{AB}\) is a power series in
\(\kappa y\) with coefficients of order unity, so
\(\partial f_{AB}/\partial y_a\) is of order \(\kappa\). The gaugino masses
\eqref{eq:31.7.52} are therefore of order \(\kappa^2\Lambda^3\), which is
the same as the order of magnitude of the scalar masses and expectation
values, and hence is likely to be large enough to avoid conflict with
observation. The one-loop corrections considered in Section 31.4 are much
smaller here, and do not need to be taken into account.

In summary, the first version of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking has
the advantage of giving an axion mass that is within cosmological bounds,
while the second version has the advantage of giving the gauginos masses
that are comparable to the masses of the squarks and sleptons. Both
versions of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking have an advantage over
theories of gauge-mediated supersymmetry breaking: they naturally give
\(\mu\)-terms of the experimentally necessary order of magnitude. On the
other hand, theories of gauge-mediated supersymmetry breaking have the
advantage of naturally yielding generation-independent squark and slepton
masses.

Theories with either version of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking may
naturally entail the existence of slowly decaying superheavy particles,
which could have interesting astrophysical
effects~\hyperref[ch31-ref-22]{[22]}. It is plausible that the gauge
interactions of the hidden sector that become strong at energy \(\Lambda\)
can bind composite particles with masses of order \(\Lambda\). These
superheavy particles may be long-lived if their decay is forbidden by
accidental symmetries of the renormalizable part of the hidden sector
Lagrangian and only occurs through non-renormalizable terms in the
Lagrangian, which are suppressed by factors of \(\kappa\Lambda\).
