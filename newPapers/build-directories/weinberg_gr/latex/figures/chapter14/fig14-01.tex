% Source: Weinberg GR, physical PDF p. 439 (printed p. 419).
% Coverage: Figure 14.1, light-ray geometry for parallax and luminosity.
% Status: vector reconstruction; source-reviewed and compile-clean.
% Geometry: the ray is Eq. (14.4.5) itself.  Writing s = sigma/r_1 and
%           K = k r_1^2, and resolving x(sigma) into the radial direction
%           x_1-hat (drawn upward) and the transverse direction epsilon
%           (drawn to the left), that equation reads
%             transverse:  |epsilon| r_1 s
%             radial:      r_1 [ sqrt(1 - K s^2) - s sqrt(1 - K) ]
%           so the ray leaves the source at s = 0 and reaches its closest
%           approach to the origin at s = 1, where the radial part vanishes
%           and the transverse offset is |epsilon| r_1 -- the impact parameter
%           b of Eq. (14.4.6).  Here K = -1.6 (so sqrt(1 - K) = 1.61245),
%           r_1 = 6.6 and |epsilon| r_1 = 1.5.  The K < 0 branch is the one
%           the source draws: it makes the ray steepen towards the observer,
%           and gives |epsilon| = 8.0 deg at the source against a line-of-sight
%           shift of vartheta = 20.1 deg at the origin.
% Uncertainties: source caption states that angles and curvature are
%                deliberately exaggerated.
