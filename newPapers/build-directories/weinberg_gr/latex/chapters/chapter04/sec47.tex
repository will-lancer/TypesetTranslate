\subsection{Gradient, Curl, and Divergence}\label{sec:4.7}

There are some special cases where the covariant derivative takes a
particularly simple form. Simplest of all is, of course, the covariant
derivative of a scalar, which is just the ordinary gradient:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu S=\partial_\mu S.
  \tag{4.7.1}\label{eq:4.7.1}
\end{equation}
Another simple special case is the covariant curl. Recall that
\[
  \nabla_\nu V_\mu
  =
  \partial_\nu V_\mu-\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}V_\lambda.
\]
Since \(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\) is symmetric in \(\mu\) and \(\nu\),
the covariant curl is just the ordinary curl:
\begin{equation}
  2\nabla_{[\nu}V_{\mu]}
  =
  \nabla_\nu V_\mu-\nabla_\mu V_\nu
  =
  \partial_\nu V_\mu-\partial_\mu V_\nu
  =
  2\partial_{[\nu}V_{\mu]}.
  \tag{4.7.2}\label{eq:4.7.2}
\end{equation}

Another special case that will take a little more work is the covariant
divergence of a contravariant vector:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu V^\mu
  =
  \partial_\mu V^\mu+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}V^\lambda.
  \tag{4.7.3}\label{eq:4.7.3}
\end{equation}
We note that \(\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}\) is given by
\begin{align}
  \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}
  &=
  \frac12 g^{\mu\rho}
  \left(
    \partial_\mu g_{\rho\lambda}
    +\partial_\lambda g_{\rho\mu}
    -\partial_\rho g_{\mu\lambda}
  \right)
  \notag\\
  &=
  \frac12 g^{\mu\rho}\partial_\lambda g_{\rho\mu}.
  \tag{4.7.4}\label{eq:4.7.4}
\end{align}
We may evaluate this easily if we recall that, for an arbitrary matrix
\(M\),
\begin{equation}
  \Tr\!\left(M^{-1}\partial_\lambda M\right)
  =
  \partial_\lambda\ln\det M,
  \tag{4.7.5}\label{eq:4.7.5}
\end{equation}
where \(\det\) denotes the determinant and \(\Tr\) the trace, that is, the
sum of the diagonal elements. To prove Eq.~\eqref{eq:4.7.5}, consider the
variation in \(\ln\det M\) owing to a variation \(\delta x^\lambda\):
\begin{align*}
  \delta\ln\det M
  &\equiv\ln\det(M+\delta M)-\ln\det M
  \\
  &=\ln\det\!\left[M^{-1}(M+\delta M)\right]
  \\
  &=\ln\det(1+M^{-1}\delta M)
  \\
  &\longrightarrow\ln\!\left(1+\Tr(M^{-1}\delta M)\right)
  \longrightarrow\Tr(M^{-1}\delta M).
\end{align*}
Taking the coefficient of \(\delta x^\lambda\) in both sides gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:4.7.5}. Applying it to the case where \(M\) is the matrix
\(g_{\mu\nu}\), we find from Eq.~\eqref{eq:4.7.4} that
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}
  =
  \frac12\partial_\lambda\ln(-g)
  =
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\lambda\sqrt{-g}.
  \tag{4.7.6}\label{eq:4.7.6}
\end{equation}
With Eq.~\eqref{eq:4.7.3}, we find that the covariant divergence is
precisely
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu V^\mu
  =
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}
  \partial_\mu\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,V^\mu\right).
  \tag{4.7.7}\label{eq:4.7.7}
\end{equation}
One immediate consequence is a covariant form of Gauss's theorem: If
\(V^\mu\) vanishes at infinity, then
\begin{equation}
  \int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu V^\mu=0.
  \tag{4.7.8}\label{eq:4.7.8}
\end{equation}
Note the appearance here of a factor \(\sqrt{-g}\) that makes
\(\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\) invariant.

We can also use Eq.~\eqref{eq:4.7.6} to simplify the formula for the
covariant divergence of a tensor. For instance,
\[
  \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}
  =
  \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}
  +\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda\nu}
  +\Gamma^\nu{}_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda},
\]
and, applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:4.7.6}, we find that
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}
  \partial_\mu\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}\right)
  +\Gamma^\nu{}_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda}.
  \tag{4.7.9}\label{eq:4.7.9}
\end{equation}
In particular, if \(A^{\mu\lambda}=-A^{\lambda\mu}\), then the last term
drops out, so
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu A^{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}
  \partial_\mu\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,A^{\mu\nu}\right),
  \qquad A^{\mu\nu}=A^{[\mu\nu]}.
  \tag{4.7.10}\label{eq:4.7.10}
\end{equation}

There is one other special case of some importance. For a covariant tensor
\(A_{\mu\nu}\), the covariant derivative is
\[
  \nabla_\lambda A_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \partial_\lambda A_{\mu\nu}
  -\Gamma^\rho{}_{\lambda\mu}A_{\rho\nu}
  -\Gamma^\rho{}_{\lambda\nu}A_{\mu\rho}.
\]
Suppose that \(A_{\mu\nu}\) is antisymmetric, that is,
\(A_{\mu\nu}=-A_{\nu\mu}\). If we twice add to
\(\nabla_\lambda A_{\mu\nu}\) the same tensor with indices cyclically
permuted, we find by virtue of the symmetry of
\(\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\) and the antisymmetry of \(A_{\mu\nu}\) that all
connection terms cancel, yielding
\begin{equation}
  3\nabla_{[\lambda}A_{\mu\nu]}
  =
  \nabla_\lambda A_{\mu\nu}
  +\nabla_\mu A_{\nu\lambda}
  +\nabla_\nu A_{\lambda\mu}
  =
  3\partial_{[\lambda}A_{\mu\nu]}.
  \tag{4.7.11}\label{eq:4.7.11}
\end{equation}
