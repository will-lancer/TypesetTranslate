\subsection[Algebraic Properties of the Riemann Tensor]
{Algebraic Properties of \(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\)}\label{sec:6.6}

The algebraic properties of the curvature tensor are greatly clarified if we
consider, instead of \(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\), its fully covariant form
\begin{equation}
  R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
  \equiv
  g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}.
  \tag{6.6.1}\label{eq:6.6.1}
\end{equation}
Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:6.1.5} and \eqref{eq:3.3.7}, this is
\begin{align*}
  R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
  ={}&
  g_{\rho\lambda}
  \left(
    \partial_\mu\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}
    -\partial_\nu\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}
    +\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\kappa}\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\nu\sigma}
    -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\kappa}\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\mu\sigma}
  \right)
  \\
  ={}&
  \partial_\mu(g_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma})
  -\partial_\nu(g_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma})
  -(\partial_\mu g_{\rho\lambda})\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}
  +(\partial_\nu g_{\rho\lambda})\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}
  \\
  &+
  g_{\rho\lambda}
  \left(
    \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\kappa}\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\nu\sigma}
    -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\kappa}\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\mu\sigma}
  \right).
\end{align*}
We use the relation
\[
  \partial_\mu g_{\rho\lambda}
  =
  g_{\rho\eta}\Gamma^\eta{}_{\lambda\mu}
  +g_{\lambda\eta}\Gamma^\eta{}_{\rho\mu},
\]
and most of the terms quadratic in the connection cancel, leaving
\begin{align}
  R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
  ={}&
  \frac12
  \left(
    \partial_\sigma\partial_\mu g_{\rho\nu}
    +\partial_\rho\partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu}
    -\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu g_{\rho\mu}
    -\partial_\rho\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu}
  \right)
  \notag\\
  &+
  g_{\lambda\kappa}
  \left(
    \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu}\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\rho\nu}
    -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\sigma\nu}\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\rho\mu}
  \right).
  \tag{6.6.2}\label{eq:6.6.2}
\end{align}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.6.2} we may read off the algebraic properties of the
curvature tensor:

\medskip
\noindent\textit{(A) Symmetry:}
\begin{equation}
  R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
  =
  R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
  \tag{6.6.3}\label{eq:6.6.3}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{(B) Antisymmetry:}
\begin{equation}
  R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
  =
  -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}
  =
  -R_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu}.
  \tag{6.6.4}\label{eq:6.6.4}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{(C) Cyclicity:}
\begin{equation}
  R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
  +R_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma}
  +R_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}
  =0.
  \tag{6.6.5}\label{eq:6.6.5}
\end{equation}

We have already mentioned that \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\) may be contracted to
give the Ricci tensor,
\begin{equation}
  R_{\sigma\nu}
  =
  g^{\rho\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}.
  \tag{6.6.6}\label{eq:6.6.6}
\end{equation}
The symmetry property (A) shows that the Ricci tensor is symmetric,
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mu\nu}=R_{\nu\mu},
  \tag{6.6.7}\label{eq:6.6.7}
\end{equation}
and the antisymmetry property (B) tells us that \(R_{\mu\nu}\) is essentially
the only second-rank tensor that can be formed from
\(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\). Indeed,
\[
  g^{\rho\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
  =R_{\sigma\nu},
  \qquad
  g^{\rho\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
  =-R_{\sigma\mu},
  \qquad
  g^{\rho\sigma}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}=0.
\]
From the antisymmetry property (B) we also see that there is essentially only
one way of contracting \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\) to construct a scalar:
\[
  R
  =
  g^{\sigma\nu}R_{\sigma\nu}
  =
  g^{\rho\mu}g^{\sigma\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu},
  \qquad
  g^{\rho\sigma}g^{\mu\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}=0.
\]
Finally, (C) eliminates the one other orientation-odd scalar that might have
been formed in four dimensions; that is,
\[
  \varepsilon^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}=0.
\]
