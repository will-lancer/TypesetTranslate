\subsection{Commutation of Covariant Derivatives}\label{sec:6.5}

There is another way to see that the tensor
\(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\) expresses the presence or absence of a true
gravitational field. Consider the second covariant derivative of a covariant
vector \(V_\rho\):
\begin{align*}
  \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V_\rho
  ={}&
  \partial_\mu(\nabla_\nu V_\rho)
  -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\nabla_\lambda V_\rho
  -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu}\nabla_\nu V_\lambda
  \\
  ={}&
  \partial_\mu\partial_\nu V_\rho
  -(\partial_\mu V_\lambda)\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho\nu}
  -V_\lambda\partial_\mu\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho\nu}
  -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\partial_\lambda V_\rho
  +\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\rho\lambda}V_\sigma
  \\
  &-\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu}\partial_\nu V_\lambda
  +\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu}\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\lambda\nu}V_\sigma.
\end{align*}
The terms involving first and second derivatives of \(V_\rho\) are symmetric
in \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), but the terms involving \(V_\rho\) itself contain an
antisymmetric part:
\begin{equation}
  [\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V_\rho
  =
  -R^\sigma{}_{\rho\mu\nu}V_\sigma.
  \tag{6.5.1}\label{eq:6.5.1}
\end{equation}
In the same way we could show that
\begin{equation}
  [\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho
  =
  R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma.
  \tag{6.5.2}\label{eq:6.5.2}
\end{equation}
Similar formulas hold for any tensor; for instance,
\begin{equation}
  [\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]T^\rho{}_\sigma
  =
  R^\rho{}_{\lambda\mu\nu}T^\lambda{}_\sigma
  -R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}T^\rho{}_\lambda.
  \tag{6.5.3}\label{eq:6.5.3}
\end{equation}
Thus, if the curvature tensor vanishes, then covariant derivatives commute,
as would be expected for a coordinate system that can be transformed into a
Minkowski coordinate system.
