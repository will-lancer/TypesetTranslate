\subsection{Gravitational Disturbances in Gravitational Fields\optionalreading}
\label{sec:10.9}
\begingroup

The foregoing sections have described a Lorentz-invariant theory for the
behavior of weak gravitational waves in a Minkowskian spacetime. It will be
useful later in Chapter~15 on cosmology to have available a generally
covariant theory for the propagation of weak gravitational disturbances in a
preexisting gravitational field \(g_{\mu\nu}\).

According to Eq.~(6.1.5), if \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is changed by some disturbance
to \(g_{\mu\nu}+\delta g_{\mu\nu}\), with \(\delta g_{\mu\nu}\) small,
then to first order
\[
  \begin{split}
  \delta R_{\mu\nu}
  ={}&
  \partial_\lambda\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
  -\partial_\nu\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\lambda}
  +\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\lambda\rho}
   \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}
  +\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\lambda\rho}
   \delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}
  \\
  &-
  \delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\rho}
  \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}
  -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\rho}
  \delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda},
  \end{split}
\]
where \(\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\) is the change in the affine
connection:
\[
  \begin{split}
  \delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
  ={}&
  -g^{\lambda\rho}\delta g_{\rho\sigma}
  \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}
  \\
  &+
  \frac12g^{\lambda\rho}
  \left(
    \partial_\mu\delta g_{\rho\nu}
    +\partial_\nu\delta g_{\rho\mu}
    -\partial_\rho\delta g_{\mu\nu}
  \right).
  \end{split}
\]
We note that \(\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\) can be expressed as a
tensor:
\begin{equation}
  \delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac12g^{\lambda\rho}
  \left(
    \nabla_\mu\delta g_{\rho\nu}
    +\nabla_\nu\delta g_{\rho\mu}
    -\nabla_\rho\delta g_{\mu\nu}
  \right),
  \tag{10.9.1}\label{eq:10.9.1}
\end{equation}
the covariant derivatives being, of course, constructed using the
unperturbed affine connection \(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\). Since
\(\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\) is a tensor, the change in the Ricci
tensor can also be written in terms of covariant derivatives:
\begin{equation}
  \delta R_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \nabla_\lambda\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
  -\nabla_\nu\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\lambda}.
  \tag{10.9.2}\label{eq:10.9.2}
\end{equation}
This is known as the \emph{Palatini identity}. In terms of
\(\delta g_{\mu\nu}\), it reads
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  \delta R_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac12\Bigl(
    &\nabla_\lambda\nabla_\mu
      \delta g^\lambda{}_\nu
    +\nabla_\lambda\nabla_\nu
      \delta g^\lambda{}_\mu
  \\
    &-\Box\,\delta g_{\mu\nu}
    -\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu
      \delta g^\lambda{}_\lambda
  \Bigr).
  \end{split}
  \tag{10.9.3}\label{eq:10.9.3}
\end{equation}

The Einstein field equations are here presumed to be satisfied for the
undisturbed gravitational field \(g_{\mu\nu}\) and energy-momentum tensor
\(T_{\mu\nu}\). The condition that they should also be satisfied for
\(g_{\mu\nu}+\delta g_{\mu\nu}\) and
\(T_{\mu\nu}+\delta T_{\mu\nu}\) is then
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  \frac12\Bigl(
    &\nabla_\lambda\nabla_\mu
      \delta g^\lambda{}_\nu
    +\nabla_\lambda\nabla_\nu
      \delta g^\lambda{}_\mu
    -\Box\,\delta g_{\mu\nu}
    -\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu
      \delta g^\lambda{}_\lambda
  \Bigr)
  \\
  ={}&
  8\pi G
  \left[
    \delta T_{\mu\nu}
    -\frac12g_{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}\delta T_{\rho\sigma}
    +\frac12g_{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\rho\sigma}T^{\rho\sigma}
    -\frac12\delta g_{\mu\nu}T^\lambda{}_\lambda
  \right].
  \end{split}
  \tag{10.9.4}\label{eq:10.9.4}
\end{equation}
Also, the source term \(\delta T_{\mu\nu}\) obeys the conservation law
\begin{equation}
  0
  =
  \nabla_\mu\delta T^{\nu\mu}
  +T^{\rho\nu}\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\mu}
  +T^{\rho\mu}\delta\Gamma^\nu{}_{\rho\mu}.
  \tag{10.9.5}\label{eq:10.9.5}
\end{equation}
The general covariance of these equations is manifest.

Just as for gravitational waves in a Minkowskian spacetime, it is important
here to distinguish physical disturbances from mere changes in the coordinate
system. To this end, let us consider a general infinitesimal coordinate
transformation
\begin{equation}
  x^\mu\longrightarrow x'^\mu=x^\mu-\xi^\mu(x),
  \tag{10.9.6}\label{eq:10.9.6}
\end{equation}
where \(\xi^\mu(x)\) is an arbitrary infinitesimal vector field. The partial
derivatives occurring in the tensor transformation rules are here
\[
  \frac{\partial x'^\mu}{\partial x^\nu}
  =
  \delta^\mu_\nu-\partial_\nu\xi^\mu(x),
\]
\[
  \frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x'^\mu}
  =
  \delta^\nu_\mu+\partial_\mu\xi^\nu(x)+O(\xi^2).
\]
Since Einstein's equations are generally covariant, and \(g_{\mu\nu}(x)\)
is a solution for an energy-momentum tensor \(T_{\mu\nu}(x)\), it follows
that \(g'_{\mu\nu}(x)\) is a solution for \(T'_{\mu\nu}(x)\), where
\[
  \begin{split}
  g'_{\mu\nu}(x)
  &=
  g'_{\mu\nu}(x')
  +\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(x)\,\xi^\lambda(x)
  +O(\xi^2)
  \\
  &=
  g_{\mu\nu}(x)
  +g_{\lambda\nu}(x)\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda(x)
  +g_{\mu\lambda}(x)\partial_\nu\xi^\lambda(x)
  +\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(x)\,\xi^\lambda(x),
  \end{split}
\]
and likewise for \(T'_{\mu\nu}(x)\). In covariant terms, we conclude that
\begin{equation}
  g'_{\mu\nu}(x)
  =
  g_{\mu\nu}(x)+\mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}(x)
  \tag{10.9.7}\label{eq:10.9.7}
\end{equation}
is a solution of Einstein's equations for an energy-momentum tensor
\begin{equation}
  T'_{\mu\nu}(x)
  =
  T_{\mu\nu}(x)+\mathcal L_\xi T_{\mu\nu}(x),
  \tag{10.9.8}\label{eq:10.9.8}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}
  \equiv
  \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu+\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu,
  \tag{10.9.9}\label{eq:10.9.9}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_\xi T_{\mu\nu}
  \equiv
  \xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda T_{\mu\nu}
  +T_{\lambda\nu}\nabla_\mu\xi^\lambda
  +T_{\mu\lambda}\nabla_\nu\xi^\lambda.
  \tag{10.9.10}\label{eq:10.9.10}
\end{equation}
Note that \(\mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}\) has the same form as
\(\mathcal L_\xi T_{\mu\nu}\), except that \(g_{\mu\nu}\) has vanishing
covariant derivatives, whereas \(T_{\mu\nu}\) does not. It follows, and it is
straightforward to verify directly, that
\(\delta g_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}\) is a solution of the field
equation \eqref{eq:10.9.4} for a source perturbation
\(\delta T_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal L_\xi T_{\mu\nu}\). But
Eq.~\eqref{eq:10.9.4} is a \emph{linear} differential equation, and so,
given any solution \(\delta g_{\mu\nu}\), we can always find other solutions
of the form
\(\delta g_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}\) with precisely the same
physical content. The freedom to add terms
\(\mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}\) for arbitrary functions \(\xi^\mu(x)\)
corresponds to the ``gauge invariance'' discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:10.1}.

The Lie derivative introduced in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:10.9.9} and
\eqref{eq:10.9.10} can be generalized to arbitrary tensors by specifying
that a term involving the contraction of the tensor with
\(\nabla_\mu\xi^\nu\) should be included with a plus sign for each covariant
index and a minus sign for each contravariant index. Thus, for scalars,
\[
  \mathcal L_\xi S=\xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda S;
\]
for covectors and vectors,
\[
  (\mathcal L_\xi V)_\mu
  =
  \xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda V_\mu
  +V_\lambda\nabla_\mu\xi^\lambda,
\]
\[
  (\mathcal L_\xi U)^\mu
  =
  \xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda U^\mu
  -U^\lambda\nabla_\lambda\xi^\mu;
\]
and for contravariant and mixed tensors of second rank,
\[
  (\mathcal L_\xi T)^{\mu\nu}
  =
  \xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda T^{\mu\nu}
  -T^{\lambda\nu}\nabla_\lambda\xi^\mu
  -T^{\mu\lambda}\nabla_\lambda\xi^\nu,
\]
\[
  (\mathcal L_\xi T)^\mu{}_\nu
  =
  \xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda T^\mu{}_\nu
  -T^\lambda{}_\nu\nabla_\lambda\xi^\mu
  +T^\mu{}_\lambda\nabla_\nu\xi^\lambda,
\]
and so on. In general, the effect of an infinitesimal coordinate
transformation on any tensor \(T\) is that the new tensor equals the old
tensor at the same coordinate point, plus the Lie derivative
\(\mathcal L_\xi T\).

It is easy to show that the operator \(\mathcal L_\xi\) has the same abstract
properties as ordinary derivatives or covariant derivatives. It is linear,
\[
  \mathcal L_\xi(aA+bB)
  =
  a\mathcal L_\xi A+b\mathcal L_\xi B
  \qquad
  (a,b\ \text{constant scalars});
\]
it obeys the Leibniz rule,
\[
  \mathcal L_\xi(A\otimes B)
  =
  (\mathcal L_\xi A)\otimes B
  +A\otimes(\mathcal L_\xi B);
\]
and it commutes with contraction,
\[
  \mathcal L_\xi(T^\mu{}_\mu)
  =
  (\mathcal L_\xi T)^\mu{}_\mu.
\]

In particular, the Lie derivative of the energy-momentum tensor for a perfect
fluid is
\[
  \begin{split}
  \mathcal L_\xi T_{\mu\nu}
  ={}&
  p\,\mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}
  +g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal L_\xi p
  \\
  &+
  (p+\rho)
  \left[
    u_\mu\mathcal L_\xi u_\nu
    +u_\nu\mathcal L_\xi u_\mu
  \right]
  +u_\mu u_\nu
  \left(
    \mathcal L_\xi p+\mathcal L_\xi\rho
  \right).
  \end{split}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal L_\xi g_{\mu\nu}\) is a solution of Einstein's equations for
a fluid whose velocity, pressure, and density are perturbed by
\(\mathcal L_\xi u_\mu\), \(\mathcal L_\xi p\), and
\(\mathcal L_\xi\rho\), respectively.

The solution of the field equations \eqref{eq:10.9.4} is quite complicated,
except for the simple case of a homogeneous and isotropic unperturbed metric
\(g_{\mu\nu}\). This case will be considered in Section~16.10.
