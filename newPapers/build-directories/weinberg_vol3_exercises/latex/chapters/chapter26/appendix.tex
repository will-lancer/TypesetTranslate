\chapterappendix{}{Majorana Spinors}

This appendix summarizes some algebraic properties of Majorana spinors that
are needed in dealing with superfields.

Consider a four-component fermionic Majorana spinor $s$ that like $Q$ or
$\theta$ may be expressed in the form
\begin{equation}
  s=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    e\zeta^*\\
    \zeta
  \end{pmatrix},
  \tag{26.A.1}\label{eq:26.A.1}
\end{equation}
where $\zeta$ is some two-component spinor and $e$ is the $2\times2$ matrix
\[
  e\equiv
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0&1\\
    -1&0
  \end{pmatrix}
  =i\sigma_2\,.
\]
Such a spinor is related to its complex conjugate by
\begin{equation}
  s^*=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0&e\\
    -e&0
  \end{pmatrix}s
  =-\beta\gamma_5\epsilon s\,,
  \tag{26.A.2}\label{eq:26.A.2}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon$ is the $4\times4$ matrix
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon\equiv
  \begin{pmatrix}
    e&0\\
    0&e
  \end{pmatrix}
  \tag{26.A.3}\label{eq:26.A.3}
\end{equation}
and as usual $\gamma_5$ and $\beta$ are $4\times4$ matrices
\[
  \gamma_5=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \mathbf{1}&0\\
    0&-\mathbf{1}
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \beta=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0&\mathbf{1}\\
    \mathbf{1}&0
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
with $\mathbf{1}$ and $0$ understood here as $2\times2$ submatrices. Taking
the transpose of Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.2} and multiplying on the right by
$\beta$ gives the equivalent formula
\begin{equation}
  \bar{s}\equiv s^\dagger\beta=s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\gamma_5\,.
  \tag{26.A.4}\label{eq:26.A.4}
\end{equation}

The anticommutation of the spinor components limits the variety of covariants
that can be formed from a Majorana spinor. To see this, it will be convenient
first to consider the symmetry properties of bilinear covariants, which will
be of some interest in themselves. For a pair of Majorana spinors $s_1$ and
$s_2$ and any $4\times4$ numerical matrix $M$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.4} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \bar{s}_1Ms_2
  &=\sum_{\alpha\beta}s_{1\alpha}s_{2\beta}
    (\epsilon\gamma_5M)_{\alpha\beta}
   =-\sum_{\alpha\beta}s_{2\alpha}s_{1\beta}
    (\epsilon\gamma_5M)_{\beta\alpha}
  \\
  &=+\sum_{\alpha\beta}s_{2\alpha}s_{1\beta}
    (M^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\alpha\beta}
   =\bar{s}_2(\epsilon\gamma_5)^{-1}
    M^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\gamma_5s_1\,,
\end{aligned}
\]
with the minus sign following the second equal sign arising from the
fermionic nature of these spinors. In Section 5.4 we found that the 16
covariant matrices formed from Dirac matrices satisfy
\begin{equation}
  M^{\mathsf T}=
  \begin{cases}
    +\mathcal{C}M\mathcal{C}^{-1},
      &M=\mathbf{1},\ \gamma_5\gamma_\mu,\ \gamma_5,\\
    -\mathcal{C}M\mathcal{C}^{-1},
      &M=\gamma_\mu,\ [\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu],
  \end{cases}
  \tag{26.A.5}\label{eq:26.A.5}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}$ is the matrix
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}=\gamma_2\beta=-\epsilon\gamma_5
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    -e&0\\
    0&e
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \tag{26.A.6}\label{eq:26.A.6}
\end{equation}
It follows that
\begin{equation}
  (\bar{s}_1Ms_2)=
  \begin{cases}
    +(\bar{s}_2Ms_1),
      &M=\mathbf{1},\ \gamma_5\gamma_\mu,\ \gamma_5,\\
    -(\bar{s}_2Ms_1),
      &M=\gamma_\mu,\ [\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu].
  \end{cases}
  \tag{26.A.7}\label{eq:26.A.7}
\end{equation}
In particular, setting $s_1=s_2=s$, we find that
\begin{equation}
  \bar{s}\gamma_\mu s=\bar{s}[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu]s=0\,,
  \tag{26.A.8}\label{eq:26.A.8}
\end{equation}
so the only bilinear covariants formed from a single Majorana spinor $s$ are
$\bar{s}s$, $\bar{s}\gamma_5\gamma_\mu s$, and $\bar{s}\gamma_5s$.

In considering the form of the most general superfields, we need to have
expressions for the products of two or more Majorana spinors. For two
spinors, we recall that any $4\times4$ matrix may be expanded as a sum of the
16 covariant matrices $\mathbf{1}$, $\gamma_\mu$, $[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu]$,
$\gamma_5\gamma_\mu$, $\gamma_5$. Lorentz invariance tells us that for the
matrix $s_\alpha\bar{s}_\beta$ this expansion must take the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
  s\bar{s}
  ={}&k_S(\bar{s}s)
     +k_V\gamma_\mu(\bar{s}\gamma^\mu s)
     +k_T[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu]
       (\bar{s}[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]s)
  \\
  &+k_A\gamma_5\gamma_\mu(\bar{s}\gamma_5\gamma^\mu s)
    +k_P\gamma_5(\bar{s}\gamma_5s)\,,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the $k$'s are constants to be determined. Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.8} shows
that we may take $k_V=k_T=0$. The remaining coefficients may be calculated
by multiplying on the right with $\mathbf{1}$, $\gamma_5\gamma^\mu$, and
$\gamma_5$ and taking the trace, which gives $k_S=-1/4$, $k_A=+1/4$, and
$k_P=-1/4$. In this way we find that
\begin{equation}
  s\bar{s}
  =-\frac14(\bar{s}s)
   +\frac14\gamma_5\gamma_\mu(\bar{s}\gamma_5\gamma^\mu s)
   -\frac14\gamma_5(\bar{s}\gamma_5s)\,.
  \tag{26.A.9}\label{eq:26.A.9}
\end{equation}
By multiplying on the right with $-\epsilon\gamma_5$ and using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.4}, we may put this in the form
\begin{equation}
  s_\alpha s_\beta
  =\frac14(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\alpha\beta}(\bar{s}s)
   +\frac14(\gamma_\mu\epsilon)_{\alpha\beta}
     (\bar{s}\gamma_5\gamma^\mu s)
   +\frac14\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}(\bar{s}\gamma_5s)\,,
  \tag{26.A.10}\label{eq:26.A.10}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently,
\begin{equation}
  s_\alpha s_\beta
  =\frac14(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\alpha\beta}
     (s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\gamma_5s)
   +\frac14(\gamma_\mu\epsilon)_{\alpha\beta}
     (s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\gamma^\mu s)
   +\frac14\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}(s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)\,.
  \tag{26.A.11}\label{eq:26.A.11}
\end{equation}

Now consider the product $s_\alpha s_\beta s_\gamma$ of three components of
a Majorana spinor $s$. We can divide $s$ into left- and right-handed parts
\begin{equation}
  s=s_L+s_R\,,
  \qquad
  s_L=\frac12(\mathbf{1}+\gamma_5)s\,,
  \qquad
  s_R=\frac12(\mathbf{1}-\gamma_5)s\,.
  \tag{26.A.12}\label{eq:26.A.12}
\end{equation}
Each of $s_L$ and $s_R$ has only two independent components, so since the
square of any fermionic c-number vanishes, we have
$s_{L\alpha}s_{L\beta}s_{L\gamma}=0$ and
$s_{R\alpha}s_{R\beta}s_{R\gamma}=0$ for all $\alpha$, $\beta$, and
$\gamma$, and therefore
\[
  s_\alpha s_\beta s_\gamma
  =s_{L\alpha}s_{L\beta}s_{R\gamma}
   +s_{L\alpha}s_{R\beta}s_{L\gamma}
   +s_{R\alpha}s_{L\beta}s_{L\gamma}
   +L\leftrightarrow R\,,
\]
with `$L\leftrightarrow R$' denoting the sum of previous terms with labels
$L$ and $R$ interchanged. To evaluate this expression, we multiply
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.11} by suitable factors of
$(\mathbf{1}+\gamma_5)/2$, and find
\[
  s_{L\alpha}s_{L\beta}
  =\frac14[\epsilon(\mathbf{1}+\gamma_5)]_{\alpha\beta}
   (s_L^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s_L)\,.
\]
If we now multiply this with $s_{R\gamma}$, since
$(s_R^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s_R)s_{R\gamma}=0$ we can drop the label $L$ on the spinors
in the bilinear $(s_L^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s_L)$:
\[
  s_{L\alpha}s_{L\beta}s_{R\gamma}
  =\frac14[\epsilon(\mathbf{1}+\gamma_5)]_{\alpha\beta}
   (s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)s_{R\gamma}\,.
\]
The same arguments also yield
\[
  s_{R\alpha}s_{R\beta}s_{L\gamma}
  =\frac14[\epsilon(\mathbf{1}-\gamma_5)]_{\alpha\beta}
   (s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)s_{L\gamma}\,.
\]
Adding the sum of these two expressions to the same quantity with $\gamma$
replaced with $\alpha$ or $\beta$ yields finally
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  s_\alpha s_\beta s_\gamma
  =\frac14(s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)\bigl[
    &\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}s_\gamma
     -(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\alpha\beta}(\gamma_5s)_\gamma
     -\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}s_\beta
  \\
    &+(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\alpha\gamma}(\gamma_5s)_\beta
     +\epsilon_{\beta\gamma}s_\alpha
     -(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\beta\gamma}(\gamma_5s)_\alpha
  \bigr].
\end{aligned}
  \tag{26.A.13}\label{eq:26.A.13}
\end{equation}

To calculate the products of four Majorana spinor components, we note that
$(s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)$ contains only terms with two $s_L$s or two $s_R$s, so
\[
  (s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)s_\gamma s_\delta
  =(s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)
   [s_{R\gamma}s_{R\delta}+s_{L\gamma}s_{L\delta}]\,.
\]
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.11} to evaluate the sum in square brackets, and
noting that
\[
  (s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)(s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\gamma_5s)
  =(s_L^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s_L)(s_R^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s_R)
   -(s_R^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s_R)(s_L^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s_L)=0\,,
\]
we find that
\begin{equation}
  (s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)s_\gamma s_\delta
  =\frac14\epsilon_{\gamma\delta}(s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)^2\,.
  \tag{26.A.14}\label{eq:26.A.14}
\end{equation}
Multiplying Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.13} with $s_\delta$ therefore yields the
result
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  s_\alpha s_\beta s_\gamma s_\delta
  =\frac1{16}(s^{\mathsf T}\epsilon s)^2\bigl[
    &\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\gamma\delta}
     -(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\alpha\beta}
      (\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\gamma\delta}
     -\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}\epsilon_{\beta\delta}
  \\
    &+(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\alpha\gamma}
      (\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\beta\delta}
     +\epsilon_{\beta\gamma}\epsilon_{\alpha\delta}
     -(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\beta\gamma}
      (\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\alpha\delta}
  \bigr].
\end{aligned}
  \tag{26.A.15}\label{eq:26.A.15}
\end{equation}
Any product of five components of $s$ vanishes, so this completes the list of
formulas for products of components of a Majorana spinor.

We can use these formulas to derive some additional relations that will be
useful in dealing with superfields. By contracting Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.13}
with $(\epsilon\gamma_5)_{\beta\gamma}$ and
$(\epsilon\gamma_\mu)_{\beta\gamma}$, we find
\begin{equation}
  s_\alpha(\bar{s}s)
  =-(\gamma_5s)_\alpha(\bar{s}\gamma_5s)
  \tag{26.A.16}\label{eq:26.A.16}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  s_\alpha(\bar{s}\gamma_5\gamma_\mu s)
  =-(\gamma_\mu s)_\alpha(\bar{s}\gamma_5s)\,.
  \tag{26.A.17}\label{eq:26.A.17}
\end{equation}
From Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.A.16} and \eqref{eq:26.A.17} we may derive `Fierz'
identities
\begin{equation}
  (\bar{s}s)^2=-(\bar{s}\gamma_5s)^2\,,
  \qquad
  (\bar{s}\gamma_5\gamma_\mu s)(\bar{s}\gamma_5\gamma_\nu s)
  =-\eta_{\mu\nu}(\bar{s}\gamma_5s)^2\,.
  \tag{26.A.18}\label{eq:26.A.18}
\end{equation}
Also, Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.14} may be put in a covariant form
\begin{equation}
  (\bar{s}\gamma_5s)s\bar{s}
  =-\frac14\gamma_5(\bar{s}\gamma_5s)^2\,.
  \tag{26.A.19}\label{eq:26.A.19}
\end{equation}

It will also be useful to record the reality properties of bilinear products
of Majorana spinors. For any pair of Majorana spinors $s_1$, $s_2$ satisfying
the phase convention \eqref{eq:26.A.1}, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.A.2} and
\eqref{eq:26.A.4} give
\[
  (\bar{s}_1Ms_2)^*
  =-(s_1^\dagger\epsilon\gamma_5M^*s_2^*)
  =(\bar{s}_1\beta\epsilon\gamma_5M^*
     \beta\epsilon\gamma_5s_2)\,.
\]
(The minus sign in the middle expression arises from reversing the
interchange of $s_1$ and $s_2$ that occurs when we take the complex
conjugate.) But Eqs.~\eqref{eq:5.4.40} and \eqref{eq:26.A.6} give
$\beta\epsilon\gamma_5\gamma_\mu^*\beta\epsilon\gamma_5=\gamma_\mu$, so
\begin{equation}
  \beta\epsilon\gamma_5M^*\beta\epsilon\gamma_5
  =
  \begin{cases}
    +M,&M=\mathbf{1},\ \gamma_\mu,\ [\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\mu],\\
    -M,&M=\gamma_\mu\gamma_5,\ \gamma_5,
  \end{cases}
  \tag{26.A.20}\label{eq:26.A.20}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
  (\bar{s}_1Ms_2)^*
  =
  \begin{cases}
    +(\bar{s}_1Ms_2),
      &M=\mathbf{1},\ \gamma_\mu,\ [\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\mu],\\
    -(\bar{s}_1Ms_2),
      &M=\gamma_\mu\gamma_5,\ \gamma_5.
  \end{cases}
  \tag{26.A.21}\label{eq:26.A.21}
\end{equation}

Finally, we mention that any spinor $u$ may be written in terms of a pair of
Majorana spinors $s_\pm$ as
\begin{equation}
  u=s_++is_-\,,
  \tag{26.A.22}\label{eq:26.A.22}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  s_+\equiv\frac12(u-\beta\epsilon\gamma_5u^*)\,,
  \qquad
  s_-\equiv\frac1{2i}(u+\beta\epsilon\gamma_5u^*)\,.
  \tag{26.A.23}\label{eq:26.A.23}
\end{equation}
To check that $s_\pm$ are Majorana spinors satisfying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.A.2}, it is only necessary to recall that
$\beta\epsilon\gamma_5$ is real, and that
$(\beta\epsilon\gamma_5)^2=\mathbf{1}$.
