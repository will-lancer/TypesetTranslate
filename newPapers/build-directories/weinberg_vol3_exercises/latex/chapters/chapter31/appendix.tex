\chapterappendix{}{The Vierbein Formalism}

The familiar formulation of gravity in terms of a metric tensor
\(g_{\mu\nu}\) is adequate for theories with matter fields restricted to
scalars, vectors, and tensors, but not for supergravity, where spinors are
an indispensable ingredient. Unlike vectors and tensors, spinors have a
Lorentz transformation rule that has no natural generalization to
arbitrary coordinate systems. Instead, to deal with spinors, we have to
introduce systems of coordinates \(\xi_X^a(x)\) with \(a=0,1,2,3\) that
are locally inertial at any given point \(X\) in an arbitrary coordinate
system. The Principle of Equivalence tells us that gravitation has no
effect in these locally inertial coordinates, so the action may then be
expressed in terms of matter fields like spinors, vectors, etc.\ that are
defined in these locally inertial frames, as well as the vierbein, which
arises from the transformation between the locally inertial and general
coordinates
\begin{equation}
  e^a{}_\mu(X)
  \equiv
  \left.
    \frac{\partial\xi_X^a(x)}{\partial x^\mu}
  \right|_{x=X}.
  \tag{31.A.1}\label{eq:31.A.1}
\end{equation}
The action will be invariant under general coordinate transformations
\(x^\mu\longrightarrow x'^\mu\) and local Lorentz transformations
\(\xi^a\longrightarrow\xi'^a=\Lambda^a{}_b(x)\xi^b\), with
\(\Lambda^a{}_c(x)\Lambda^b{}_d(x)\eta_{ab}=\eta_{cd}\). The definition
\eqref{eq:31.A.1} of the vierbein shows that, under general coordinate
transformations \(x\longrightarrow x'\), it transforms as
\begin{equation}
  e^a{}_\mu(x)
  \longrightarrow
  e'^a{}_\mu(x')
  =\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x'^\mu}e^a{}_\nu(x),
  \tag{31.A.2}\label{eq:31.A.2}
\end{equation}
while, under a local Lorentz transformation
\(\xi^a(x)\longrightarrow\Lambda^a{}_b(x)\xi^b(x)\), it transforms as
\begin{equation}
  e^a{}_\mu(x)
  \longrightarrow
  \Lambda^a{}_b(x)e^b{}_\mu(x).
  \tag{31.A.3}\label{eq:31.A.3}
\end{equation}

For instance, theories of pure gravitation may be expressed in terms of
a field that is invariant under local Lorentz transformations and that
transforms as a tensor under general coordinate transformations, the
metric
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu}\equiv e^a{}_\mu e^b{}_\nu\eta_{ab}.
  \tag{31.A.4}\label{eq:31.A.4}
\end{equation}
Vectors may be regarded either as quantities \(V^a\) that transform as
vectors under local Lorentz transformations
\begin{equation}
  V^a(x)\longrightarrow\Lambda^a{}_b(x)V^b(x)
  \tag{31.A.5}\label{eq:31.A.5}
\end{equation}
but as scalars under general coordinate transformations, or as quantities
\(v^\mu\) that transform as scalars under local Lorentz transformations
but as vectors under general coordinate transformations, the two being
related by
\[
  V^a=e^a{}_\mu v^\mu.
\]
But the supergravity action also involves spinor fields, which necessarily
transform like scalars under general coordinate transformations but as
spinors under local Lorentz transformations
\begin{equation}
  \psi_\alpha(x)
  \longrightarrow
  D_{\alpha\beta}(\Lambda(x))\psi_\beta(x),
  \tag{31.A.6}\label{eq:31.A.6}
\end{equation}
where \(D_{\alpha\beta}(\Lambda)\) is the spinor representation of the
homogeneous Lorentz group.

Because the Lorentz transformations in Eqs. \eqref{eq:31.A.5} and
\eqref{eq:31.A.6} depend on the coordinate \(x^\mu\), the spacetime
derivative of a quantity like \(V^a(x)\) or \(\psi_\alpha(x)\) is not
just another quantity that transforms in the same way under local Lorentz
transformations and as a covariant vector under general coordinate
transformations. For instance, the derivative of Eq. \eqref{eq:31.A.6}
gives the local Lorentz transformation rule
\[
  \partial_\mu\psi_\alpha
  \longrightarrow
  D_{\alpha\beta}(\Lambda)
  \left\{
    \partial_\mu\psi_\beta
    +\bigl[D^{-1}(\Lambda)\partial_\mu D(\Lambda)\bigr]_{\beta\gamma}
      \psi_\gamma
  \right\}.
\]
To cancel the second term in the brackets on the right-hand side we
introduce a connection matrix \(\Omega_\mu\) with the local Lorentz
transformation property
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_\mu
  \longrightarrow
  D(\Lambda)\Omega_\mu D^{-1}(\Lambda)
  -\bigl(\partial_\mu D(\Lambda)\bigr)D^{-1}(\Lambda)
  \tag{31.A.7}\label{eq:31.A.7}
\end{equation}
and define a covariant derivative
\begin{equation}
  \mathscr{D}_\mu\psi
  \equiv\partial_\mu\psi+\Omega_\mu\psi,
  \tag{31.A.8}\label{eq:31.A.8}
\end{equation}
which transforms under local Lorentz transformations like \(\psi\)
itself:
\begin{equation}
  \mathscr{D}_\mu\psi
  \longrightarrow
  D(\Lambda)\mathscr{D}_\mu\psi.
  \tag{31.A.9}\label{eq:31.A.9}
\end{equation}
Also, \(\Omega_\mu\) must transform like a covariant vector under general
coordinate transformations, so that \(\mathscr{D}_\mu\) will give a
covariant vector when acting on a coordinate scalar. In order that
\(\mathscr{D}_\mu\) should give a tensor with one extra lower index when
acting on a tensor, it must be supplemented with the usual affine
connection term. For instance, when acting on the gravitino field
\(\psi_\nu\), the covariant derivative is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \mathscr{D}_\mu\psi_\nu
  \equiv\psi_{\nu;\mu}+\Omega_\mu\psi_\nu
  \equiv\partial_\mu\psi_\nu
    -\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\psi_\lambda+\Omega_\mu\psi_\nu.
  \tag{31.A.10}\label{eq:31.A.10}
\end{equation}

Eqs. \eqref{eq:31.A.8}--\eqref{eq:31.A.10} apply not only for spinors,
but for fields that transform under local Lorentz transformations
according to arbitrary representations \(D(\Lambda)\) of the Lorentz
group. The matrix \(\Omega_\mu\) depends on this representation, but in
any representation it can be written in the form
\begin{equation}
  [\Omega_\mu]_{\alpha\beta}(x)
  =\frac{1}{2}\ii[\mathscr{J}_{ab}]_{\alpha\beta}\omega_\mu^{ab}(x),
  \tag{31.A.11}\label{eq:31.A.11}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathscr{J}_{ab}\) are the matrices representing the generators
of the homogeneous Lorentz group in the representation furnished by the
fields in question:
\begin{equation}
  \ii[\mathscr{J}_{ab},\mathscr{J}_{cd}]
  =\eta_{bc}\mathscr{J}_{ad}
   -\eta_{ac}\mathscr{J}_{bd}
   +\eta_{bd}\mathscr{J}_{ca}
   -\eta_{ad}\mathscr{J}_{cb},
  \tag{31.A.12}\label{eq:31.A.12}
\end{equation}
and \(\omega_\mu^{ab}\) is a representation-independent field known as
the \emph{spin connection} that transforms as a covariant vector under
general coordinate transformations. To satisfy the inhomogeneous local
Lorentz transformation rule \eqref{eq:31.A.7} we can take
\begin{equation}
  \omega_\mu^{ab}
  =g^{\nu\lambda}e^a{}_\nu e^b{}_{\lambda;\mu},
  \tag{31.A.13}\label{eq:31.A.13}
\end{equation}
with the semi-colon again denoting an ordinary covariant derivative,
constructed using the affine connection \(\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\).
(This is antisymmetric in \(a\) and \(b\) because Eq.
\eqref{eq:31.A.4} gives
\(g^{\nu\lambda}e^a{}_\nu e^b{}_\lambda=\eta^{ab}\), a quantity with
vanishing covariant derivative.) This is not the unique spin-connection
for which Eq. \eqref{eq:31.A.7} is satisfied; to it, we can add any field
that is a covariant vector under general coordinate transformations and
a tensor under local Lorentz transformations, a freedom of some
importance in supergravity theories.

For any choice of spin connection there is a corresponding curvature
tensor. From Eq. \eqref{eq:31.A.7} it is straightforward to show that the
quantity
\(\partial_\nu\Omega_\mu-\partial_\mu\Omega_\nu+
[\Omega_\nu,\Omega_\mu]\) transforms homogeneously under local Lorentz
transformations
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\nu\Omega_\mu-\partial_\mu\Omega_\nu
  +[\Omega_\nu,\Omega_\mu]
  \longrightarrow
  D(\Lambda)
  \bigl(
    \partial_\nu\Omega_\mu-\partial_\mu\Omega_\nu
    +[\Omega_\nu,\Omega_\mu]
  \bigr)D^{-1}(\Lambda).
  \tag{31.A.14}\label{eq:31.A.14}
\end{equation}
Using Eqs. \eqref{eq:31.A.11} and \eqref{eq:31.A.12}, this matrix can be
expressed as
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\nu\Omega_\mu-\partial_\mu\Omega_\nu
  +[\Omega_\nu,\Omega_\mu]
  =\frac{1}{2}\ii\mathscr{J}_{ab}R_{\mu\nu}^{ab},
  \tag{31.A.15}\label{eq:31.A.15}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mu\nu}^{ab}
  \equiv
  \partial_\nu\omega_\mu^{ab}-\partial_\mu\omega_\nu^{ab}
  +\omega_\nu^{ac}\omega_{\mu c}{}^b
  -\omega_\mu^{ac}\omega_{\nu c}{}^b.
  \tag{31.A.16}\label{eq:31.A.16}
\end{equation}
From Eq. \eqref{eq:31.A.14} it follows that \(R_{\mu\nu}^{ab}\)
transforms as a tensor under local Lorentz transformations
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mu\nu}^{ab}
  \longrightarrow
  D(\Lambda)^a{}_cD(\Lambda)^b{}_dR_{\mu\nu}^{cd}.
  \tag{31.A.17}\label{eq:31.A.17}
\end{equation}
It also obviously transforms as a tensor under general coordinate
transformations
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mu\nu}^{ab}
  \longrightarrow
  \frac{\partial x^\rho}{\partial x'^\mu}
  \frac{\partial x^\sigma}{\partial x'^\nu}
  R_{\rho\sigma}^{ab}.
  \tag{31.A.18}\label{eq:31.A.18}
\end{equation}
We can therefore form a coordinate tensor of fourth rank by writing
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mu\nu}^{ab}
  =e^a{}_\kappa e^b{}_\lambda R_{\mu\nu}^{\kappa\lambda}.
  \tag{31.A.19}\label{eq:31.A.19}
\end{equation}
The tensor \(R_{\mu\nu}^{\kappa\lambda}\) constructed in this way is the
Riemann--Christoffel curvature tensor corresponding to the particular
spin connection \(\omega_\nu^{ab}\).
