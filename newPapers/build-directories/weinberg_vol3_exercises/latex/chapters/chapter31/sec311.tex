\subsection{The Metric Superfield}

Supergravity necessarily involves spinor as well as tensor fields, so we will
have to describe gravitational fields in terms of a vierbein (or tetrad)
\(e^a{}_\mu(x)\) rather than a metric, which is related to the vierbein by
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\eta_{ab}e^a{}_\mu(x)e^b{}_\nu(x)\,.
  \tag{31.1.1}\label{eq:31.1.1}
\end{equation}
The indices \(\mu,\nu\), etc.\ label general coordinates, while indices
\(a,b\), etc.\ label coordinates in a locally inertial coordinate system,
with \(\eta_{ab}\) the usual diagonal matrix with elements
\(-1,+1,+1,+1\). In the vierbein formalism, the action must be supposed to be
invariant under two different sorts of symmetry transformations: general
coordinate transformations \(x^\mu\to x'^\mu(x)\), under which the vierbein
\(e^a{}_\mu(x)\) is transformed into \(e'^a{}_\mu(x')\), where
\begin{equation}
  e'^a{}_\mu(x')
  =\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x'^\mu}e^a{}_\nu(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.2}\label{eq:31.1.2}
\end{equation}
and local Lorentz transformations, under which
\begin{equation}
  e^a{}_\mu(x)\to\Lambda^a{}_b(x)e^b{}_\mu(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.3}\label{eq:31.1.3}
\end{equation}
with \(\Lambda^a{}_b(x)\) an arbitrary real matrix subject to the constraints
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{ab}\Lambda^a{}_c(x)\Lambda^b{}_d(x)=\eta_{cd}\,.
  \tag{31.1.4}\label{eq:31.1.4}
\end{equation}
An elementary review of this formalism is given in the appendix to this
chapter.

A weak gravitational field is one for which the vierbein is close to the
unit matrix. In such a field, the vierbein may conveniently be written as
\begin{equation}
  e^a{}_\mu(x)=\delta^a{}_\mu+2\kappa\phi^a{}_\mu(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.5}\label{eq:31.1.5}
\end{equation}
where \(\phi^a{}_\mu(x)\) is small. As we shall see in Section 31.2, if
\(\phi^a{}_\mu\) is to be a conventionally normalized field, then the
constant \(\kappa\) should be expressed in terms of Newton's constant \(G\)
by \(\kappa=\sqrt{8\pi G}\). The closeness of the vierbein to the unit matrix
is preserved by a small coordinate transformation
\begin{equation}
  x'^\mu\to x'^\mu+\xi^\mu(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.6}\label{eq:31.1.6}
\end{equation}
and also by small local Lorentz transformations
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda^a{}_b(x)=\delta^a{}_b+\omega^a{}_b(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.7}\label{eq:31.1.7}
\end{equation}
where \(\xi^\mu(x)\) and \(\omega^a{}_b(x)\) are of the same order as
\(\phi^a{}_\mu(x)\), and
\(\omega_{ab}(x)\equiv\eta_{ac}\omega^c{}_b(x)\) is constrained by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.4} to satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{ab}(x)=-\omega_{ba}(x)\,.
  \tag{31.1.8}\label{eq:31.1.8}
\end{equation}
The combined transformations \eqref{eq:31.1.2} and \eqref{eq:31.1.3} then
become
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\to\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)
  +\frac{1}{2\kappa}
   \left[-\frac{\partial\xi_\mu(x)}{\partial x^\nu}
         +\omega_{\mu\nu}(x)\right].
  \tag{31.1.9}\label{eq:31.1.9}
\end{equation}
We are now dropping the distinction between general coordinate indices
\(\mu,\nu\), etc.\ and local Lorentz coordinate indices \(a,b\), etc., and
raising and lowering all indices with \(\eta^{\mu\nu}\) and \(\eta_{\mu\nu}\).
In terms of the metric \eqref{eq:31.1.1}, the weak field assumption
\eqref{eq:31.1.5} becomes
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\eta_{\mu\nu}+2\kappa h_{\mu\nu}(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.10}\label{eq:31.1.10}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  h_{\mu\nu}(x)\equiv\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)+\phi_{\nu\mu}(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.11}\label{eq:31.1.11}
\end{equation}
while the transformation law \eqref{eq:31.1.9} reads
\begin{equation}
  h_{\mu\nu}(x)\to h_{\mu\nu}(x)
  -\frac{1}{2\kappa}
   \left[\frac{\partial\xi_\mu(x)}{\partial x^\nu}
         +\frac{\partial\xi_\nu(x)}{\partial x^\mu}\right].
  \tag{31.1.12}\label{eq:31.1.12}
\end{equation}

By use of the supersymmetry algebra we showed in Section 25.4 that the
graviton has a fermionic superpartner, the gravitino, with helicities
\(\pm3/2\). We saw in Section 5.9 that a self-charge-conjugate massless
particle with helicities \(\pm1\) can only have low-energy interactions if
described by a real field \(A_\mu(x)\) whose interactions are invariant under
a gauge transformation
\(A_\mu(x)\to A_\mu(x)+\partial_\mu\Lambda(x)\). In the same way, a
self-charge-conjugate massless particle with helicities \(\pm3/2\) can only
have low-energy interactions if it is described by a Majorana field
\(\psi_\mu(x)\) with an extra vector index \(\mu\), with invariance under a
gauge transformation
\begin{equation}
  \psi_\mu(x)\to\psi_\mu(x)+\partial_\mu\psi(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.13}\label{eq:31.1.13}
\end{equation}
where \(\psi(x)\) is an arbitrary Majorana
field~\hyperref[ch31-ref-3a]{[3a]}. We need now to consider how fields
\(\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\) and \(\psi_\mu(x)\) with these transformation properties
can be put into a superfield.

We found in Section 27.1 that a gauge field \(V_\nu(x)\) can be regarded as
the \(V_\nu\)-component, defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.10} as the coefficient
of \(\ii(\bar\theta\gamma_5\gamma_\nu\theta)/2\), of a real scalar superfield
\(V(x,\theta)\). Similarly, we would like to incorporate the vierbein field
\(\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\) and the gravitino field \(\psi_\mu(x)\) into a
\emph{vector} superfield \(H_\mu(x,\theta)\), known as the \emph{metric
superfield}. The question is: how are \(\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\) and
\(\psi_\mu(x)\) related to the components of this superfield?

To address this question, note that supersymmetry requires the `gauge'
transformations \eqref{eq:31.1.9} and \eqref{eq:31.1.13} to be special cases
of a transformation of the whole metric superfield
\begin{equation}
  H_\mu(x,\theta)\to H_\mu(x,\theta)+\Delta_\mu(x,\theta)\,.
  \tag{31.1.14}\label{eq:31.1.14}
\end{equation}
Furthermore, a weak gravitational field \(h_{\mu\nu}\) interacts with an
energy-momentum tensor \(T^{\mu\nu}\) which as we found in Section 26.7 is a
linear combination of components of a real vector superfield \(\Theta^\mu\),
so we expect the interaction of the whole superfield \(H_\mu\) with matter to
take the form
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{int}}
  =2\kappa\int \dd^4x\,[H_\mu\Theta^\mu]_D\,.
  \tag{31.1.15}\label{eq:31.1.15}
\end{equation}
(Later in this section we will confirm the correctness of the coefficient
\(2\kappa\), with \(\Theta^\mu\) normalized as in Section 26.7.) The
supercurrent \(\Theta^\mu\) was shown in Section 26.7 to satisfy the
conservation conditions
\begin{equation}
  \gamma^\mu\SuperD\Theta_\mu=\SuperD X\,,
  \tag{31.1.16}\label{eq:31.1.16}
\end{equation}
where \(X\) is a real chiral scalar superfield (the sum of a left-chiral
scalar superfield and its complex conjugate) and \(\SuperD\) is the
four-component superderivative \eqref{eq:26.2.26}. It follows that this
interaction is invariant under a transformation of the form
\eqref{eq:31.1.14}, with \(\Delta_\mu\) of the form
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_\mu=(\bar{\SuperD}\gamma_\mu\Xi)\,,
  \tag{31.1.17}\label{eq:31.1.17}
\end{equation}
where \(\Xi(x,\theta)\) is a superfield subject to the manifestly
supersymmetric condition
\begin{equation}
  (\bar{\SuperD}\SuperD)(\bar{\SuperD}\Xi)=0\,.
  \tag{31.1.18}\label{eq:31.1.18}
\end{equation}
This can be seen by recalling from Section 26.7 that the chirality condition
on \(X\) allows us to write it in the form
\begin{equation}
  X=(\bar{\SuperD}\SuperD)\Omega\,,
  \tag{31.1.19}\label{eq:31.1.19}
\end{equation}
with \(\Omega\) in general a non-local superfield. From
Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.16} we then find
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \int[\Theta^\mu(\bar{\SuperD}\gamma_\mu\Xi)]_D
  &=-\int[((\bar{\SuperD}\Theta^\mu)\gamma_\mu\Xi)]_D
   =-\int[((\bar{\SuperD}X)\Xi)]_D
  \\
  &=+\int[\Omega(\bar{\SuperD}\SuperD)(\bar{\SuperD}\Xi)]_D=0\,,
  \end{aligned}
\]
so that the interaction \eqref{eq:31.1.15} is invariant under the
transformation
\(H_\mu\to H_\mu+(\bar{\SuperD}\gamma_\mu\Xi)\). (We can also obtain the same
result without using the representation \eqref{eq:31.1.19} for \(X\), by
noting from Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.25} that the \(D\)-term of the product of a
chiral superfield like \(X\) which satisfies Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.3.1} and
\eqref{eq:26.3.2} and a linear superfield like
\((\bar{\SuperD}\Xi)\) which satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.3.45} is a spacetime
derivative.)

At the end of this section we shall show that Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.1.17} and
\eqref{eq:31.1.18} yield conditions on the components of the superfield
\(\Delta_\mu\):
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mu\nu}^\Delta(x)+V_{\nu\mu}^\Delta(x)
  =\frac{\partial v_\mu(x)}{\partial x^\nu}
   +\frac{\partial v_\nu(x)}{\partial x^\mu}
   -2\eta_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial v^\lambda(x)}{\partial x^\lambda}\,,
  \tag{31.1.20}\label{eq:31.1.20}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_\mu^\Delta(x)
  -\frac13\gamma_\mu\gamma^\rho\lambda_\rho^\Delta(x)
  -\frac13\gamma_\mu\partial^\rho\omega_\rho^\Delta(x)
  =\partial_\mu\chi(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.21}\label{eq:31.1.21}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  -\frac12\epsilon^{\nu\mu\kappa\sigma}
    \partial_\kappa V_{\nu\mu}^\Delta(x)
  =D^{\Delta\sigma}(x)+\partial^\sigma\partial^\rho C_\rho^\Delta(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.22}\label{eq:31.1.22}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \partial^\mu M_\mu^\Delta(x)
  =\partial^\mu N_\mu^\Delta(x)=0\,,
  \tag{31.1.23}\label{eq:31.1.23}
\end{equation}
with \(v_\mu(x)\) a real vector field, and \(\chi(x)\) a Majorana spinor
field. (Here we introduce a notation that will be used throughout this
chapter; following Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.10}, the components \(C^S\),
\(\omega^S\), \(M^S\), \(N^S\), \(V_\nu^S\), \(\lambda^S\), and \(D^S\) of
an arbitrary superfield \(S(x,\theta)\) are defined by the expansion
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  S(x,\theta)={}&C^S(x)-\ii\bigl(\bar\theta\gamma_5\omega^S(x)\bigr)
  -\frac{\ii}{2}(\bar\theta\gamma_5\theta)M^S(x)
  -\frac12(\bar\theta\theta)N^S(x)
  \\
  &+\frac{\ii}{2}(\bar\theta\gamma_5\gamma^\nu\theta)V_\nu^S(x)
  -\ii(\bar\theta\gamma_5\theta)
    \left(\bar\theta\left[
      \lambda^S(x)+\frac12\sl{\partial}\omega^S(x)
    \right]\right)
  \\
  &-\frac14(\bar\theta\gamma_5\theta)^2
    \left(D^S(x)+\frac12\Box C^S(x)\right).
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.1.24}\label{eq:31.1.24}
\end{equation}
Also, \(V_{\mu\nu}^\Delta(x)\) is the \(V_\nu\)-component of
\(\Delta_\mu\).) This leads us to define the fields
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{\mu\nu}(x)
  \equiv V_{\mu\nu}^H(x)
  -\frac13\eta_{\mu\nu}V^{H\lambda}{}_\lambda(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.25}\label{eq:31.1.25}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \frac12\psi_\mu(x)
  \equiv\lambda_\mu^H(x)
  -\frac13\gamma_\mu\gamma^\rho\lambda_\rho^H(x)
  -\frac13\gamma_\mu\partial^\rho\omega_\rho^H(x)\,,
  \tag{31.1.26}\label{eq:31.1.26}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  b^\sigma(x)
  \equiv D^{H\sigma}(x)
  +\frac12\epsilon^{\nu\mu\kappa\sigma}
    \partial_\kappa V_{\nu\mu}^H(x)
  +\partial^\sigma\partial^\rho C_\rho^H(x)\,.
  \tag{31.1.27}\label{eq:31.1.27}
\end{equation}
(As discussed in Section 31.3, the factor \(1/2\) introduced on the left-hand
side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.26} will give the field \(\psi_\mu\) a
conventional normalization.) From Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.1.20} and
\eqref{eq:31.1.21} it follows that the transformation \eqref{eq:31.1.14}
induces on \(\phi_{\mu\nu}(x)\) and \(\psi_\mu(x)\) the gauge transformations
\eqref{eq:31.1.9} and \eqref{eq:31.1.13}, with
\begin{equation}
  \xi_\mu=-2\kappa v_\mu,\qquad
  \omega_{\mu\nu}
  =\kappa\left[
    -\frac{\partial v_\mu}{\partial x^\nu}
    +\frac{\partial v_\nu}{\partial x^\mu}
    +V_{\mu\nu}^\Delta-V_{\nu\mu}^\Delta
  \right],\qquad
  \psi=2\chi\,,
  \tag{31.1.28}\label{eq:31.1.28}
\end{equation}
while Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.22} shows that \(b_\mu(x)\) is invariant. Also,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.23} shows that the transformation \eqref{eq:31.1.14}
induces shifts of \(M_\mu^H(x)\) and \(N_\mu^H(x)\) that leave invariant the
fields
\begin{equation}
  s\equiv\partial^\mu M_\mu^H(x),\qquad
  p\equiv\partial^\mu N_\mu^H(x)\,.
  \tag{31.1.29}\label{eq:31.1.29}
\end{equation}
Finally, since \(C_\mu^\Delta(x)\),
\(V_{\mu\nu}^\Delta(x)-V_{\nu\mu}^\Delta(x)\), and
\(\omega_\mu^\Delta(x)\) are unconstrained by supersymmetry, the
transformation \eqref{eq:31.1.14} allows us to make the components
\(C_\mu^H(x)\),
\(V_{\mu\nu}^H(x)-V_{\nu\mu}^H(x)=\phi_{\mu\nu}-\phi_{\nu\mu}\), and
\(\omega_\mu^H(x)\) anything we like. In particular, in analogy with the
Wess--Zumino gauge for gauge superfields discussed in Section 27.1, we can
take \(C_\mu^H(x)\),
\(V_{\mu\nu}^H(x)-V_{\nu\mu}^H(x)=\phi_{\mu\nu}-\phi_{\nu\mu}\), and
\(\omega_\mu^H(x)\) all to vanish. The fields \(h_{\mu\nu}(x)\) and
\(\psi_\mu(x)\) are identified by their transformation properties as the
fields of the graviton and gravitino, respectively, while \(b_\mu(x)\),
\(s(x)\), and \(p(x)\) are auxiliary
fields~\hyperref[ch31-ref-4]{[4]} that are important in understanding the
coupling of the graviton superfield to matter.

Incidentally, note that the number of independent components of the symmetric
tensor \(h_{\mu\nu}\) modulo the gauge transformations \eqref{eq:31.1.12} is
\(10-4=6\), which with the auxiliary fields \(s,p\), and \(b_\mu\) gives a
total of \(6+6=12\) independent physical bosonic fields, while the number of
independent physical components of the Majorana spinor field \(\psi_\mu\)
modulo the gauge transformations \eqref{eq:31.1.13} is \(16-4=12\). This
satisfies the condition, discussed at the end of Section 26.2, that in any
supermultiplet of fields that furnishes a representation of the supersymmetry
algebra, there must be equal numbers of independent bosonic and fermionic
field components.

Let us now return to the interaction of matter and gravitation. In general,
the integrated \(D\)-term of the product of two superfields \(\Theta^\mu\)
and \(H_\mu\) is given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:26.2.25} as
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \int \dd^4x\,[\Theta^\mu H_\mu]_D
  =\int \dd^4x\Bigl[&
    -\partial_\mu C^{H\sigma}\partial^\mu C_\sigma^\Theta
    +C^{H\sigma}D_\sigma^\Theta
    +D^{H\sigma}C_\sigma^\Theta
  \\
  &-\left(\bar\omega^{H\sigma}
    \left[\lambda_\sigma^\Theta
      +\frac12\sl{\partial}\omega_\sigma^\Theta\right]\right)
    -\left(
      \left[\bar\lambda^{H\sigma}
        +\frac12\bar\omega^{H\sigma}\sl{\partial}\right]
      \omega_\sigma^\Theta
    \right)
  \\
  &+M^{H\sigma}M_\sigma^\Theta
    +N^{H\sigma}N_\sigma^\Theta
    -V^{H\kappa\sigma}V_{\kappa\sigma}^\Theta
  \Bigr].
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.1.30}\label{eq:31.1.30}
\end{equation}
Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.1.25}--\eqref{eq:31.1.27}, we may express
\(V_{\mu\nu}^H\), \(\lambda_\mu^H\), and \(D_\mu^H\) respectively in terms
of \(\phi_{\mu\nu}\), \(\psi_\mu\), and \(b_\mu\), and find
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  I_{\mathrm{int}}
  ={}&2\kappa\int \dd^4x\,[\Theta^\mu H_\mu]_D
  \\
  ={}&2\kappa\int \dd^4x\Bigl[
    C^{H\sigma}\left(
      \Box C_\sigma^\Theta
      -\partial_\sigma\partial^\rho C_\rho^\Theta
      +D_\sigma^\Theta
    \right)
    +b^\sigma C_\sigma^\Theta
  \\
  &+\left(\bar\omega^{H\sigma}
    \left[
      -\lambda_\sigma^\Theta
      -\sl{\partial}\omega_\sigma^\Theta
      +\partial_\sigma\gamma^\rho\omega_\rho^\Theta
    \right]\right)
    -(\bar\psi^\sigma\omega_\sigma^\Theta)
    +(\bar\psi^\sigma\gamma_\sigma\gamma^\rho\omega_\rho^\Theta)
  \\
  &+M^{H\sigma}M_\sigma^\Theta
    +N^{H\sigma}N_\sigma^\Theta
    +\frac12\epsilon^{\nu\mu\kappa\sigma}
      \phi_{\nu\mu}\partial_\kappa C_\sigma^\Theta
  \\
  &-\phi^{\lambda\sigma}
    \left(
      V_{\lambda\sigma}^\Theta
      -\eta_{\lambda\sigma}V^{\Theta\rho}{}_\rho
    \right)
  \Bigr].
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{31.1.31}\label{eq:31.1.31}
\end{equation}
We saw in Section 26.7 that the conservation condition
\eqref{eq:31.1.16} yields conditions \eqref{eq:26.7.44},
\eqref{eq:26.7.39}, and \eqref{eq:26.7.35}:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  D_\mu^\Theta
  &=-\Box C_\mu^\Theta+\partial_\mu\partial^\nu C_\nu^\Theta\,,
  \\
  \lambda_\nu^\Theta
  &=-\sl{\partial}\omega_\nu^\Theta
    +\partial_\nu\gamma^\mu\omega_\mu^\Theta\,,
  \\
  0
  &=V_{\mu\nu}^\Theta-V_{\nu\mu}^\Theta
    +\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial^\sigma C^{\Theta\rho}\,,
  \end{aligned}
\]
which respectively tell us that the coefficients in Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.31}
of \(C^{H\sigma}\), of \(\bar\omega^{H\sigma}\), and of the antisymmetric
part of \(\phi^{\mu\nu}\) all vanish. Also, we may write the remaining terms
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.31} in terms of the supersymmetry current
\eqref{eq:26.7.20}, the energy-momentum tensor \eqref{eq:26.7.42}, the
\(\mathcal{R}\)-current \eqref{eq:26.7.51}:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  S^\mu
  &=-2\omega^{\Theta\mu}
    +2\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\omega_\nu^\Theta\,,
  \\
  T_{\mu\nu}
  &=-\frac12V_{\mu\nu}^\Theta-\frac12V_{\nu\mu}^\Theta
    +\eta_{\mu\nu}V^{\Theta\lambda}{}_\lambda\,,
  \\
  \mathcal{R}^\mu&=2C^{\Theta\mu}\,,
  \end{aligned}
\]
and densities \(\mathcal{M}\) and \(\mathcal{N}\) defined by
\begin{equation}
  M_\mu^\Theta=\partial_\mu\mathcal{M},\qquad
  N_\mu^\Theta=\partial_\mu\mathcal{N}\,,
  \tag{31.1.32}\label{eq:31.1.32}
\end{equation}
and given by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:26.7.33} and \eqref{eq:26.7.34} as:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{N}=-A^X,\qquad
  \mathcal{M}=B^X\,,
  \tag{31.1.33}\label{eq:31.1.33}
\end{equation}
where \(X\) is the real chiral superfield appearing on the right-hand side of
the conservation equation \eqref{eq:31.1.16}. (A label `new' on the
supersymmetry current will be understood in this chapter.) The first-order
interaction between matter and the components of the metric superfield is
then
\begin{equation}
  2\kappa\int \dd^4x\,[\Theta^\mu H_\mu]_D
  =\kappa\int \dd^4x
  \left[
    \mathcal{R}_\sigma b^\sigma
    +\frac12\bar S^\sigma\psi_\sigma
    -2\mathcal{M}s-2\mathcal{N}p
    +T^{\kappa\sigma}h_{\sigma\kappa}
  \right].
  \tag{31.1.34}\label{eq:31.1.34}
\end{equation}
We note that the gravitino field interacts with the supersymmetry current, in
much the same way as the gravitational field interacts with the
energy-momentum tensor.

We can now check the constant factor appearing in this interaction. The usual
definition of \(T^{\mu\nu}\) is that the variation of the matter action under
a change \(\delta g_{\mu\nu}\) in the metric
is~\hyperref[ch31-ref-5]{[5]}
\[
  \delta I_M
  =\frac12\int \dd^4x\,\sqrt{\operatorname{Det}g}\,
    T^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}\,.
\]
The interaction between matter and a weak gravitational field given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.10} is then
\[
  \kappa\int \dd^4x\,T^{\mu\nu}(x)h_{\mu\nu}(x)\,,
\]
which agrees with the \(h_{\mu\nu}\)-dependent part of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.34}, thus confirming the normalization of the interaction
\eqref{eq:31.1.15}.

We shall now check that Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.1.17} and \eqref{eq:31.1.18} yield
the conditions \eqref{eq:31.1.20}--\eqref{eq:31.1.23}. The superfield
\(Y\equiv(\bar{\SuperD}\Xi)\) has the components
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  C^Y&=-\ii\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\omega^\Xi)\,,
  \\
  \omega^Y
  &=-\ii\gamma_5\sl{\partial}C^\Xi
    +M^\Xi-\ii\gamma_5N^\Xi+\sl{V}^\Xi\,,
  \\
  M^Y&=-\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\gamma_5\lambda^\Xi)\,,
  \\
  N^Y&=\ii\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\lambda^\Xi)\,,
  \\
  V_\nu^Y
  &=-\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\gamma_5\gamma_\nu\lambda^\Xi)
    -\frac12\operatorname{Tr}
      \bigl(\epsilon\gamma_5[\gamma_\nu,\sl{\partial}]\lambda^\Xi\bigr)\,,
  \\
  \lambda^Y
  &=-\sl{\partial}M^\Xi-\ii\gamma_5\sl{\partial}N^\Xi
    -\ii\gamma_5(D^\Xi+\Box C^\Xi)-\partial_\nu V^{\Xi\nu}\,,
  \\
  D^Y
  &=\ii\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\sl{\partial}\lambda^\Xi)
    +\ii\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\Box\omega^\Xi)\,,
  \end{aligned}
\]
and the condition \eqref{eq:31.1.18} yields
\[
  M^Y=N^Y=D^Y+\Box C^Y
  =\partial^\lambda V_\lambda^Y
  =\lambda^Y+\sl{\partial}\omega^Y=0\,.
\]
The vanishing of \(M^Y\) and \(N^Y\) tells us that \(\lambda^\Xi\) is a
linear combination of the form
\begin{equation}
  \lambda^\Xi\epsilon
  =f_\mu\gamma^\mu+g_\mu\gamma_5\gamma^\mu
   +k_{\mu\nu}[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]\,.
  \tag{31.1.35}\label{eq:31.1.35}
\end{equation}
The vanishing of \(\partial^\lambda V_\lambda^Y\) and
\(D^Y+\Box C^Y\) then implies that
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\mu f^\mu=\partial_\mu g^\mu=0\,.
  \tag{31.1.36}\label{eq:31.1.36}
\end{equation}
Also, the vanishing of \(\lambda^Y+\sl{\partial}\omega^Y\) yields
\begin{equation}
  D^\Xi
  =\frac{\ii}{2}\gamma_5[\gamma^\nu,\sl{\partial}]V_\nu^\Xi\,.
  \tag{31.1.37}\label{eq:31.1.37}
\end{equation}
The components of \(\Delta_\mu(x,\theta)\) are
\begin{equation}
  C_\mu^\Delta
  =\ii\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\omega^\Xi)\,,
  \tag{31.1.38}\label{eq:31.1.38}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \omega_\mu^\Delta
  =\ii\sl{\partial}\gamma_\mu C^\Xi
   +\gamma_5\gamma_\mu M^\Xi-\ii\gamma_\mu N^\Xi
   +\gamma_5\gamma^\rho\gamma_\mu V_\rho^\Xi\,,
  \tag{31.1.39}\label{eq:31.1.39}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  M_\mu^\Delta
  =-\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\gamma_\mu\lambda^\Xi)\,,
  \tag{31.1.40}\label{eq:31.1.40}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  N_\mu^\Delta
  =\ii\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\lambda^\Xi)\,,
  \tag{31.1.41}\label{eq:31.1.41}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mu\nu}^\Delta
  =\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\gamma_\nu\gamma_\mu\lambda^\Xi)
   -\frac12\operatorname{Tr}
    \bigl(\epsilon[\gamma_\nu,\sl{\partial}]
      \gamma_\mu\omega^\Xi\bigr)\,,
  \tag{31.1.42}\label{eq:31.1.42}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_\mu^\Delta
  =\gamma_5\sl{\partial}\gamma_\mu M^\Xi
   +\ii\sl{\partial}\gamma_\mu N^\Xi
   -\ii\gamma_\mu(D^\Xi+\Box C^\Xi)
   +\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\partial^\nu V_\nu^\Xi\,,
  \tag{31.1.43}\label{eq:31.1.43}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  D_\mu^\Delta
  =\ii\operatorname{Tr}
    (\epsilon\sl{\partial}\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\lambda^\Xi)
   +\ii\Box\operatorname{Tr}
    (\epsilon\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\omega^\Xi)\,.
  \tag{31.1.44}\label{eq:31.1.44}
\end{equation}
The symmetric part of Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.42} gives condition
\eqref{eq:31.1.20}, with
\begin{equation}
  v_\mu=-\operatorname{Tr}(\epsilon\gamma_\mu\omega^\Xi)
  +\text{constant}\,.
  \tag{31.1.45}\label{eq:31.1.45}
\end{equation}
A linear combination of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.1.39} and
\eqref{eq:31.1.43} yields condition \eqref{eq:31.1.21}, with
\begin{equation}
  \chi=2\gamma_5M^\Xi+2\ii N^\Xi+\text{constant}\,.
  \tag{31.1.46}\label{eq:31.1.46}
\end{equation}
We then use the antisymmetric part of Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.1.42}, together with
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.1.37}, \eqref{eq:31.1.38}, and the identities
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  [\gamma_\nu,\gamma_\rho]\gamma_\mu
  -[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\rho]\gamma_\nu
  &=
  2\eta_{\mu\rho}\gamma_\nu-2\eta_{\nu\rho}\gamma_\mu
  +2\ii\epsilon_{\nu\rho\mu\lambda}\gamma_5\gamma^\lambda\,,
  \\
  \epsilon^{\nu\mu\kappa\sigma}[\gamma_\nu,\gamma_\mu]
  &=2\ii\gamma_5[\gamma^\kappa,\gamma^\sigma]\,,
  \end{aligned}
\]
and find the condition \eqref{eq:31.1.22}. Finally,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.1.40} and \eqref{eq:31.1.41}\hfil\break
along with
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.1.35} and \eqref{eq:31.1.36} yield the condition
\eqref{eq:31.1.23}.
