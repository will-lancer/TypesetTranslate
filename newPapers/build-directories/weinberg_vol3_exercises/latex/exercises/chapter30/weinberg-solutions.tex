\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Weinberg Exercises}
\ExerciseEditorialNote

\begin{enumerate}
  \WeinbergSolution{1}
  Write \(P_L=(1+\gamma _5)/2\) and \(P_R=(1-\gamma _5)/2\).  The
  component fields are obtained from the chiral superfield at
  \(\theta=0\) by taking zero, one, and two left Grassmann derivatives,
  respectively.  Applying these operations at the two ends of
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:30.2.18}, and using
  \(\mathcal D_R^2\mathcal D_L^2\mathcal D_R^2
  =-16\Box\mathcal D_R^2\), gives the kernels
  \[
    \Delta_{\phi\phi^*}(x-x')=\Delta_F(x-x'),
    \qquad
    \Delta_{\psi_L\overline{\psi_L}}(x-x')
      =P_L\slashed{\partial}\Delta_F(x-x')P_R ,
  \]
  and
  \[
    \Delta_{\mathcal F\mathcal F^*}(x-x')
      =\Box\Delta_F(x-x')=-\delta^4(x-x').
  \]
  Here a spacetime derivative acts on \(x\).  These signs can also be
  checked directly from the quadratic component action: after integration
  by parts its operators are \(-\Box\), \(-\slashed{\partial}\), and
  \(-1\), whose inverses are precisely the three kernels above.  The
  auxiliary propagator is a contact term, as it must be because
  \(\mathcal F\) has no kinetic term.  If propagator line factors rather
  than inverse quadratic kernels are desired, the common Minkowski
  Gaussian factor is to be included according to the convention used in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:30.2.10}.

  \WeinbergSolution{2}
  Split the chiral field into a classical background and a quantum field,
  \(\Phi\to\Phi+\varphi\).  The quadratic background-dependent term is
  governed by
  \[
    \mathcal M(z)\equiv
      \frac{\partial^2}{\partial\Phi^2}\bigl(g\Phi^3\bigr)
      =6g\Phi(z).
  \]
  Let
  \[
    \mathcal P_L=-\frac{1}{16\Box}\mathcal D_L^2\mathcal D_R^2
  \]
  be the chiral projector of Eq.~\eqref{eq:30.2.16}.  Performing the
  Gaussian integral over \(\varphi,\varphi^*\), or equivalently summing
  the one-loop ring supergraphs, gives
  \[
    \Gamma^{(1)}
      =-\frac{\ii}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1n
       \operatorname{Tr}\!
       \left[
         \left(
           \mathcal P_L\mathcal M^*\frac1{\Box}\mathcal M
         \right)^n\mathcal P_L
       \right]
       +\mathrm{c.c.}
  \]
  The trace includes spacetime and superspace integration.  At every
  order the fermionic delta functions identify all vertex coordinates,
  so it contains only one remaining integral \(\dd^4\theta\), even though
  the spacetime kernel is generally nonlocal.  This is the requested
  one-loop effective action in a form valid for a nonconstant background.

  As a useful check, take a slowly varying background so that spinor and
  spacetime derivatives of \(\mathcal M\) can be neglected.  The momentum
  trace then yields, up to an additive local subtraction,
  \[
    \Gamma_{\mathrm{K}}^{(1)}
      =-\frac{1}{32\pi^2}
       \int \dd^4x\,\dd^4\theta\,
       |\mathcal M|^2
       \left[
         \ln\!\left(\frac{|\mathcal M|^2}{\mu^2}\right)-2
       \right],
    \qquad \mathcal M=6g\Phi .
  \]
  It is a full-superspace, hence \(D\)-term, correction.  No loop term
  with only the chiral measure is generated, in agreement with the
  counting argument of Section~30.3.

  \WeinbergSolution{3}
  In an Abelian theory Eq.~\eqref{eq:27.3.12} linearizes to
  \(W_L=\mathcal D_R^2\mathcal D_LV\).  Introduce the mutually orthogonal
  chiral, antichiral, and transverse projectors
  \[
    \mathcal P_L=-\frac{\mathcal D_L^2\mathcal D_R^2}{16\Box},
    \qquad
    \mathcal P_R=-\frac{\mathcal D_R^2\mathcal D_L^2}{16\Box},
    \qquad
    \mathcal P_T=\mathbf{1}-\mathcal P_L-\mathcal P_R .
  \]
  The quadratic operator obtained from Eq.~\eqref{eq:27.3.17} is
  \(-\Box\mathcal P_T\).  It has no inverse on the longitudinal
  subspace, which is just the freedom
  \(V\to V+\Omega+\Omega^*\), with \(\Omega\) chiral.  Therefore the
  gauge-invariant propagator is the equivalence class
  \[
    \Delta^V(z,z')
      =-\frac{\mathcal P_T}{\Box}
       \delta^4(x-x')\delta^4(\theta-\theta')
       +\text{longitudinal gauge terms}.
  \]
  Adding the supersymmetric Fermi--Feynman gauge-fixing operator
  \(-\Box(\mathcal P_L+\mathcal P_R)\) gives the particularly simple
  representative
  \[
    \Delta^V_{\mathrm{FF}}(z,z')
      =-\frac1{\Box}\delta^4(x-x')\delta^4(\theta-\theta')
      =\Delta_F(x-x')\delta^4(\theta-\theta').
  \]
  Longitudinal additions disappear between gauge-invariant insertions, so
  both forms give the same amplitudes.
\end{enumerate}
