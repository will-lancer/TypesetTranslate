\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Supplementary Exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
  \SupplementarySolution{1}{Vierbein Gauge Counting}
  At a point, \(e^a{}_\mu\) is an invertible \(4\times4\) real matrix and
  therefore has sixteen components.  An infinitesimal local Lorentz
  transformation acts on the Lorentz index:
  \[
    \delta_\Lambda e^a{}_\mu=\Lambda^a{}_b e^b{}_\mu,\qquad
    \Lambda_{ab}=-\Lambda_{ba}.
  \]
  Its six independent parameters remove the antisymmetric part of the
  vierbein, leaving ten components.  These are precisely the components
  of
  \[
    g_{\mu\nu}=e^a{}_\mu e^b{}_\nu\eta_{ab},
  \]
  which is unchanged by the local Lorentz transformation.

  Put \(e^a{}_\mu=\delta^a{}_\mu+\kappa f^a{}_\mu\).  To first order,
  \[
    g_{\mu\nu}
      =\eta_{\mu\nu}
       +\kappa(f_{\mu\nu}+f_{\nu\mu})+O(\kappa^2).
  \]
  A Lorentz transformation shifts
  \(f_{\mu\nu}\) by an antisymmetric tensor.  Choosing that tensor to set
  \(f_{[\mu\nu]}=0\) leaves
  \(g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+2\kappa f_{(\mu\nu)}\), so Weinberg's
  \(h_{\mu\nu}\) is the symmetric part of the linearized vierbein.

  \SupplementarySolution{2}{Spinor Curvature Commutator}
  Write
  \(\Omega_\mu=[\gamma_a,\gamma_b]\omega_\mu{}^{ab}/8\).
  Acting on a spinor,
  \[
    [D_\mu,D_\nu]\chi
      =(\partial_\mu\Omega_\nu-\partial_\nu\Omega_\mu
        +[\Omega_\mu,\Omega_\nu])\chi .
  \]
  The Dirac-matrix Lorentz algebra converts the commutator of the two
  \(\Omega\)'s into the quadratic spin-connection terms.  With the
  curvature convention of Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.A.16}, the result is
  \[
    [D_\mu,D_\nu]\chi
      =-\frac18R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}
        [\gamma_a,\gamma_b]\chi ,
  \]
  where the overall sign reverses if the two curvature indices in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.A.16} are reversed.

  Under a local Lorentz transformation,
  \(D_\mu\chi\to D(\Lambda)D_\mu\chi\).  Applying a second covariant
  derivative shows that the commutator transforms in the same way.
  This agrees with the tensor transformation of \(R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}\)
  and the similarity transformation of the Lorentz generators
  \([\gamma_a,\gamma_b]\).

  \SupplementarySolution{3}{Rarita--Schwinger Gauge Invariance}
  Under \(\delta\psi_\sigma=\partial_\sigma\varepsilon\),
  \[
    \delta L^\mu
      =i\gamma_5\gamma_\nu\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
        \partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\varepsilon=0,
  \]
  since the derivatives are symmetric in \(\rho,\sigma\) while the
  epsilon tensor is antisymmetric.  The variation from
  \(\delta\bar\psi_\mu=\partial_\mu\bar\varepsilon\) is
  \[
    -\frac12(\partial_\mu\bar\varepsilon)L^\mu
      =-\frac12\partial_\mu(\bar\varepsilon L^\mu)
       +\frac12\bar\varepsilon\,\partial_\mu L^\mu.
  \]
  The second term vanishes by the same antisymmetry argument.  Hence the
  action changes only by a surface term.

  In a curved background, covariant derivatives do not commute:
  \([D_\rho,D_\sigma]\varepsilon\) is proportional to curvature.
  The uncancelled curvature variation is precisely what requires the
  Einstein action, the vierbein transformation, and the nonlinear
  torsion terms of supergravity.

  \SupplementarySolution{4}{Massless Gravitino Degrees of Freedom}
  Choose \(p^\mu=(E,0,0,E)\).  The free Rarita--Schwinger equation,
  together with \(\gamma^\mu\psi_\mu=0\), implies
  \(p^\mu\psi_\mu=0\) and \(\slashed p\psi_\mu=0\).  The vector index is
  therefore transverse, with helicities \(+1,-1\), while the massless
  spinor index has helicities \(+1/2,-1/2\).  Their tensor product
  initially contains helicities
  \(\pm3/2\) and \(\pm1/2\).

  Residual transformations
  \(\psi_\mu\to\psi_\mu+p_\mu\varepsilon\), with
  \(\slashed p\varepsilon=0\), remove the two spin-\(1/2\) combinations.
  The Majorana condition relates particle and antiparticle
  polarizations but retains one state of each helicity.  Thus the
  physical spectrum is
  \[
    n_{\rm dof}=2,\qquad \lambda=+\frac32,-\frac32.
  \]

  \SupplementarySolution{5}{Super-Higgs Degree Count}
  A massless Majorana gravitino has the two transverse helicities
  \(\pm3/2\).  A massless Majorana goldstino has two helicities
  \(\pm1/2\).  In a theory with local supersymmetry, a transformation
  with parameter proportional to the goldstino sets the goldstino field
  to zero.  This is unitary gauge; it does not discard its degrees of
  freedom, but moves them into the longitudinal gravitino polarizations.

  A massive spin-\(3/2\) representation has
  \[
    2s+1=4
  \]
  states, with helicities \(+3/2,+1/2,-1/2,-3/2\).  Hence the count
  before and after symmetry breaking is
  \[
    2_{\psi_\mu}+2_{\widetilde G}
      =4_{\psi_\mu\ {\rm massive}}.
  \]
  This equality is the representation-theory core of the super-Higgs
  mechanism.

  \SupplementarySolution{6}{Minkowski Gravitino-Mass Relation}
  Let \(F\) use Weinberg's normalization, in which the positive
  flat-space breaking energy is \(F^2/2\).  The constant
  superpotential contribution gives the negative energy
  \(-3m_g^2/\kappa^2\).  A Minkowski vacuum therefore requires
  \[
    \frac{F^2}{2}-\frac{3m_g^2}{\kappa^2}=0.
  \]
  Solving,
  \[
    m_g=\frac{\kappa F}{\sqrt6}
       =\sqrt{\frac{4\pi G F^2}{3}},
  \]
  because \(\kappa^2=8\pi G\).  This is
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.48}.  In the common convention in which
  \(F_{\rm std}^2\) denotes the positive energy itself,
  \(F_{\rm std}^2=F^2/2\), and the same statement is
  \(F_{\rm std}^2=3m_g^2/\kappa^2\).

  \SupplementarySolution{7}{Torsion from a Gravitino}
  In first-order form, \(\omega_\mu{}^{ab}\) appears algebraically after
  an integration by parts.  Varying the Einstein term gives the
  torsion-free condition in the absence of fermions.  Varying the
  covariant derivative in the Rarita--Schwinger term contributes a
  bilinear spin current.  The combined equation has the form
  \[
    T_{\mu\nu}{}^a
      \equiv2D_{[\mu}e^a{}_{\nu]}
      =\frac{\kappa^2}{2}\bar\psi_{[\mu}\gamma^a\psi_{\nu]},
  \]
  with a convention-dependent overall sign.

  Write \(\omega=\omega(e)+K(\psi)\), where the contorsion \(K\) is
  linear in \(T\) and therefore quadratic in \(\psi\).  Substitution in
  \(R(\omega)\) and in \(D(\omega)\psi\) produces terms proportional to
  \(K^2\) and \(\bar\psi K\psi\).  Both contain four gravitino fields.
  Equation~\eqref{eq:31.6.7} is the explicit contorsion solution in the
  chapter's conventions.

  \SupplementarySolution{8}{Why One-and-a-Half Order Works}
  Let \(\widehat I(e,\psi)=I(e,\psi,\omega(e,\psi))\).  The chain rule
  gives
  \[
    \delta\widehat I
      =\frac{\delta I}{\delta e}\delta e
       +\frac{\delta I}{\delta\psi}\delta\psi
       +\frac{\delta I}{\delta\omega}
        \left(
          \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial e}\delta e
          \frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\psi}\delta\psi
        \right).
  \]
  But \(\omega(e,\psi)\) is defined by
  \(\delta I/\delta\omega=0\).  The last line vanishes identically after
  the algebraic connection equation is imposed.  Therefore a symmetry
  check may vary only \(e\) and \(\psi\), treating \(\omega\) as fixed,
  and impose the connection equation at the end.  The name
  ``one-and-a-half order'' reflects this intermediate use of a
  first-order independent connection in an otherwise second-order
  calculation.

  \SupplementarySolution{9}{Local-Supersymmetry Closure}
  To lowest order retain only
  \(\delta e^a{}_\mu=\kappa\bar\alpha\gamma^a\psi_\mu\) and
  \(\delta\psi_\mu=2\partial_\mu\alpha/\kappa\).  Then
  \[
    [\delta_1,\delta_2]e^a{}_\mu
      =2\bar\alpha_2\gamma^a\partial_\mu\alpha_1
       -2\bar\alpha_1\gamma^a\partial_\mu\alpha_2
      =\partial_\mu\xi^a
  \]
  with
  \[
    \xi^a=2\bar\alpha_2\gamma^a\alpha_1,
  \]
  using the Majorana interchange identity.  In a general vierbein
  background write \(\xi^\mu=\xi^ae_a{}^\mu\).  The covariant
  completion is the Lie derivative
  \[
    \delta_{\rm gc}e^a{}_\mu
      =\xi^\nu\partial_\nu e^a{}_\mu
       +(\partial_\mu\xi^\nu)e^a{}_\nu
  \]
  plus a local Lorentz rotation whose parameter contains
  \(-\xi^\nu\omega_\nu{}^{ab}\).  Higher-order gravitino and auxiliary
  terms in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.1}--\eqref{eq:31.6.5} supply the
  corresponding nonlinear completion.

  \SupplementarySolution{10}{Off-Shell Balance}
  The vierbein has sixteen components.  Four coordinate and six local
  Lorentz gauge functions leave \(16-4-6=6\) off-shell bosonic
  components.  The auxiliary fields contribute
  \[
    1_s+1_p+4_{b_\mu}=6,
  \]
  for a total of twelve.

  A Majorana vector-spinor has \(4\times4=16\) real components.
  Local supersymmetry has four real spinor gauge functions, leaving
  \(16-4=12\) off-shell fermionic components.  Thus
  \[
    n_B^{\rm off}=6+6=12=n_F^{\rm off}.
  \]
  Without \(s,p,b_\mu\), the bosonic side would have only six
  components, and the algebra could close only after using field
  equations.  The auxiliaries make closure possible off shell.

  \SupplementarySolution{11}{AdS Supersymmetry Scale}
  At \(L_n=0\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.68} gives
  \[
    V_0=-3\kappa^2e^{\kappa^2d_0}|f_0|^2
       =-\frac{3m_g^2}{\kappa^2},
  \]
  where \(m_g=\kappa^2e^{\kappa^2d_0/2}|f_0|\).  Einstein's equation for
  a maximally symmetric vacuum then gives
  \(R_{\mu\nu}=-3m_g^2g_{\mu\nu}\), up to the curvature-sign convention,
  so the AdS radius is
  \[
    \ell_{\rm AdS}=m_g^{-1}.
  \]
  Supersymmetry is unbroken because the matter-fermion variations
  vanish and the gravitino variation has an AdS Killing-spinor
  solution.  A constant spinor is appropriate only in flat spacetime;
  local supersymmetry instead permits a covariantly varying Killing
  spinor.

  \SupplementarySolution{12}{No-Scale Cancellation}
  Put \(H=h+h^*\).  Then
  \[
    \mathcal D_\phi=-3\kappa^{-2}\frac{h'}H,\qquad
    g_{\phi\bar\phi}
      =3\kappa^{-2}\frac{|h'|^2}{H^2},
    \qquad
    g^{\phi\bar\phi}
      =\frac{\kappa^2H^2}{3|h'|^2}.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \kappa^4g^{\phi\bar\phi}
      |\mathcal D_\phi|^2
      =3\kappa^2.
  \]
  The square bracket in Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.77} vanishes, and there is
  no gauge \(D\)-term for a neutral field.  Hence \(V=0\) for every
  \(\phi\) in the coordinate patch.  Positivity of the kinetic energy
  requires \(g_{\phi\bar\phi}>0\), which holds where \(H\ne0\) and
  \(h'\ne0\); conventionally one also chooses \(H>0\) so the logarithm
  is real.

  \SupplementarySolution{13}{Anomaly-Mediated Gaugino Mass}
  Introduce a chiral compensator
  \(\mathcal C=1+\theta^2m_g\).  Super-Weyl invariance makes the running
  coupling depend on \(\mu/\mathcal C\).  Expanding the holomorphic
  gauge coefficient,
  \[
    \frac1{g^2(\mu/\mathcal C)}
      =\frac1{g^2(\mu)}
       -m_g\theta^2\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}\frac1{g^2}+\cdots .
  \]
  The \(\theta^2W^\alpha W_\alpha\) component is a gaugino mass.
  Canonical normalization gives
  \[
    M_\lambda=\frac{\beta_g}{g}m_g
  \]
  up to the phase chosen for \(m_g\).  The compensator is part of the
  supergravity multiplet, so its auxiliary component is present even
  when no gauge-singlet hidden field can appear in the gauge kinetic
  function.  This is why the quantum contribution evades the
  tree-level singlet requirement.

  \SupplementarySolution{14}{One-Loop Gauge Example}
  Substitution of
  \(\beta_g=bg^3/(16\pi^2)\) gives
  \[
    M_\lambda=\frac{bg^2}{16\pi^2}m_g.
  \]
  It is suppressed by one loop relative to \(m_g\).  A direct
  tree-level contribution from a field-dependent gauge kinetic
  function has the form
  \[
    M_A^{\rm tree}
      =\frac{F^i\partial_i f_A}{2\operatorname{Re}f_A}.
  \]
  If \(\partial_i f_A=O(\kappa)\) and \(F^i=O(m_g/\kappa)\), it is
  \(O(m_g)\), parametrically larger than anomaly mediation.  If a
  symmetry forbids the derivative or no singlet exists, the tree term
  vanishes and the loop result is leading.

  \SupplementarySolution{15}{Anomaly-Mediated Trilinear Term}
  Canonical fields are obtained from wavefunction factors \(Z_i\).
  The physical Yukawa coupling is
  \(y^{\rm phys}_{ijk}=y_{ijk}(Z_iZ_jZ_k)^{-1/2}\).  Replacing
  \(\mu\) by \(\mu/\mathcal C\) and expanding each \(Z\) in
  \(\theta^2m_g\) produces a soft scalar vertex.  With
  \(\gamma_i=d\ln Z_i/d\ln\mu\),
  \[
    a_{ijk}
      =-\frac12(\gamma_i+\gamma_j+\gamma_k)m_g\,y_{ijk}
  \]
  in this definition of \(\gamma\).  If instead
  \(\gamma_i=\tfrac12d\ln Z_i/d\ln\mu\), the factor \(1/2\) is absent:
  \[
    a_{ijk}=-(\gamma_i+\gamma_j+\gamma_k)m_g\,y_{ijk}
            =-m_g\beta_{y_{ijk}}.
  \]
  The invariant statement is that the \(A\)-term is minus \(m_g\) times
  the logarithmic beta function of the corresponding Yukawa coupling.

  \SupplementarySolution{16}{Scalar Mass from Running}
  The real wavefunction factor must depend on the invariant combination
  \(\mu/(\mathcal C\mathcal C^*)^{1/2}\).  Expanding twice,
  \[
    \ln Z_i
      =\ln Z_i(\mu)
       -\frac12(\theta^2m_g+\bar\theta^2m_g^*)\gamma_i
       +\frac14\theta^2\bar\theta^2|m_g|^2
         \frac{d\gamma_i}{d\ln\mu}.
  \]
  The last component becomes a scalar squared mass after canonical
  normalization:
  \[
    m_i^2=-\frac14|m_g|^2\frac{d\gamma_i}{d\ln\mu}.
  \]
  Some authors define \(\gamma_i=-d\ln Z_i/d\ln\mu\), or absorb a
  factor \(1/2\) in it.  Both the displayed sign and factor then change.
  The convention-independent statement is that \(m_i^2\) is fixed by
  the RG derivative of the anomalous dimension and is two-loop order.

  \SupplementarySolution{17}{Ultraviolet Insensitivity}
  Above a supersymmetric threshold,
  \(M_\lambda^+=(\beta_g^+/g)m_g\).  The mass \(M\) occurs as the
  compensator-dressed chiral parameter \(M\mathcal C\).  Integrating out
  the massive multiplet therefore gives a gaugino threshold
  \[
    \Delta M_\lambda
      =-\frac{\Delta\beta_g}{g}m_g,\qquad
    \Delta\beta_g\equiv\beta_g^+-\beta_g^-.
  \]
  Matching yields
  \[
    M_\lambda^-
      =M_\lambda^++\Delta M_\lambda
      =\frac{\beta_g^-}{g}m_g.
  \]
  Thus a supersymmetric heavy threshold leaves no independent memory of
  ultraviolet soft parameters.  Nonsupersymmetric threshold splittings
  would spoil the cancellation and are outside the premise.

  \SupplementarySolution{18}{The Slepton-Sign Problem}
  With negligible lepton Yukawa couplings, the lepton anomalous
  dimensions are dominated by electroweak gauge terms.  In the standard
  convention
  \(\gamma_{\tilde\ell}\sim-[C_1g_1^2+C_2g_2^2]/(8\pi^2)\).
  Both electroweak couplings have positive one-loop beta coefficients in
  the MSSM, so \(d\gamma_{\tilde\ell}/d\ln\mu<0\).  The standard
  anomaly-mediated formula
  \(m_i^2=\tfrac12|m_g|^2\dot\gamma_i\) then gives
  \[
    m_{\tilde L}^2<0,\qquad m_{\tilde e}^2<0.
  \]
  Equivalent anomalous-dimension conventions reproduce the same signs.
  A negative squared mass makes the origin a maximum of the scalar
  potential; charged sleptons acquire expectation values and break
  electromagnetism.  It is therefore a physical tachyonic instability,
  not a harmless sign convention.

  \SupplementarySolution{19}{Kähler-Transformation Invariance}
  The first combination transforms as
  \[
    e^{\kappa^2(d+a+a^*)}
      |fe^{-\kappa^2a}|^2
      =e^{\kappa^2d}|f|^2.
  \]
  The metric is unchanged because
  \(\partial_n\partial_{\bar m}(a+a^*)=0\).  For the covariant
  derivative,
  \[
    L'_n
      =\partial_n(fe^{-\kappa^2a})
       +\kappa^2fe^{-\kappa^2a}(d_n+a_n)
      =e^{-\kappa^2a}L_n.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    e^{\kappa^2d'}g^{n\bar m}L'_nL_{\bar m}^{\prime *}
      =e^{\kappa^2d}g^{n\bar m}L_nL_{\bar m}^*.
  \]
  Both pieces of the \(F\)-term potential are invariant separately.

  \SupplementarySolution{20}{Minkowski \(F\)-Term Sum Rule}
  With the definition in the question,
  \[
    g_{n\bar m}F^nF^{*\bar m}
      =e^{\kappa^2d}g^{n\bar m}L_nL_{\bar m}^*.
  \]
  Also
  \(m_g^2=\kappa^4e^{\kappa^2d}|f|^2\).  Thus the potential is
  \[
    V_F=g_{n\bar m}F^nF^{*\bar m}
        -\frac{3m_g^2}{\kappa^2}.
  \]
  Vanishing \(D\)-terms and zero vacuum energy imply
  \[
    \boxed{\quad
    g_{n\bar m}F^nF^{*\bar m}
      =\frac{3m_g^2}{\kappa^2}\quad .}
  \]
  This is the multi-field form of the cancellation used in
  Supplementary Exercise 6.

  \SupplementarySolution{21}{Tree-Level Gaugino Mass Matrix}
  For diagonal \(f_{AB}=\delta_{AB}f_A\), the kinetic term is
  \(-(\operatorname{Re}f_A)\bar\lambda_A\slashed{\mathcal D}\lambda_A/2\).
  Rescale
  \(\lambda_A^{(c)}=(\operatorname{Re}f_A)^{1/2}\lambda_A\).
  The bilinear in Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.75}, after identifying the
  on-shell auxiliary field \(F^n\), gives
  \[
    M_A=\frac{F^n\partial_nf_A}
              {2\operatorname{Re}f_A},
  \]
  with complex conjugation moved between \(F^n\) and \(f_A\) according
  to the Majorana mass convention.  If \(f_A\) is constant, every
  \(\partial_nf_A\) vanishes, and so does the tree-level mass.  The
  anomaly-mediated contribution is not included in this classical
  statement.

  \SupplementarySolution{22}{Visible Scalar Soft Mass}
  Near \(Q=0\), write
  \(d=d_{\rm hid}(z,z^*)+Z_Q(z,z^*)|Q|^2+\cdots\).
  The canonically normalized field is \(Q_c=Z_Q^{1/2}Q\).  Expanding the
  supergravity potential to quadratic order and using the stationary
  hidden-sector equations gives
  \[
    m_Q^2=m_g^2+\frac{V_0\kappa^2}{3}
      -F^mF^{*\bar n}
       \partial_m\partial_{\bar n}\ln Z_Q.
  \]
  For a tuned Minkowski vacuum, \(V_0=0\), hence
  \[
    \boxed{\quad m_Q^2=m_g^2
      -F^mF^{*\bar n}
       \partial_m\partial_{\bar n}\ln Z_Q\quad .}
  \]
  The second term is the sectional Kähler curvature sampled along the
  supersymmetry-breaking direction.

  \SupplementarySolution{23}{Gravity-Mediated \(A\)-Term}
  The physical Yukawa coupling is
  \[
    Y^{\rm phys}_{ijk}
      =e^{\kappa^2d/2}
        \frac{Y_{ijk}}{(Z_iZ_jZ_k)^{1/2}}.
  \]
  Varying it along the hidden auxiliary direction gives the scalar
  trilinear.  With the convention
  \(V_{\rm soft}\supset A_{ijk}Y^{\rm phys}_{ijk}q_iq_jq_k+\mathrm{c.c.}\),
  \[
    A_{ijk}
      =F^m\left[
        \kappa^2d_m+\partial_m\ln Y_{ijk}
        -\partial_m\ln(Z_iZ_jZ_k)
      \right],
  \]
  where a factor \(1/2\) moves between \(A\) and the definition of the
  canonically normalized Yukawa in alternative conventions.  Equivalently,
  the hidden dependence is summarized by
  \[
    A_{ijk}\propto
    F^m\partial_m\ln\!
      \left(\frac{e^{\kappa^2d}Y_{ijk}}{Z_iZ_jZ_k}\right).
  \]

  \SupplementarySolution{24}{Flavor Universality Criterion}
  A sufficient condition is
  \(Z_{i\bar j}=Z(z,z^*)\delta_{i\bar j}\) for fields with identical
  gauge quantum numbers, together with hidden-independent ratios among
  the corresponding Yukawa tensors.  Then
  \[
    \partial_m\partial_{\bar n}\ln Z_i
  \]
  is generation independent, so the scalar mass matrix is proportional
  to the identity.  Likewise
  \(\partial_m\ln(Z_iZ_jZ_k)\) is the same for every entry of a given
  Yukawa tensor, making the trilinear matrix proportional to that
  tensor.

  Flavor violation arises from nonuniversal first derivatives
  \(\partial_mZ_{i\bar j}\), which misalign \(A\)-terms, and especially
  from nonuniversal mixed second derivatives
  \(\partial_m\partial_{\bar n}Z_{i\bar j}\), which generate off-diagonal
  scalar squared masses.  Gauge symmetries alone do not generally forbid
  these Kähler couplings.

  \SupplementarySolution{25}{Planck-Suppressed Mediation Scale}
  A representative operator is
  \[
    \int d^2\theta\,\frac{X}{M_{\rm Pl}}W^\alpha W_\alpha
    \quad\hbox{or}\quad
    \int d^4\theta\,\frac{X^*X}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}Q^*Q,
  \]
  with \(M_{\rm Pl}=\kappa^{-1}\).  Replacing
  \(X|_{\theta^2}\) by \(F\) gives
  \[
    m_{\rm soft}=O\!\left(\frac{F}{M_{\rm Pl}}\right)
                =O(\kappa F).
  \]
  Tuning the vacuum energy gives
  \(F_{\rm std}=\sqrt3\,m_g/\kappa\), so
  \(m_{\rm soft}=O(m_g)\), with coefficients controlled by unknown
  Planck-scale operators.  This is the characteristic scale of gravity
  mediation.

  \SupplementarySolution{26}{Goldstino Direction}
  Let the positive breaking energy be
  \[
    F_{\rm tot}^2=\sum_i|F_i|^2+\frac12\sum_A D_A^2.
  \]
  The normalized goldstino is, up to phases,
  \[
    \widetilde G
      =\frac1{F_{\rm tot}}\left(
        \sum_iF_i^*\psi_i
        -\frac{i}{\sqrt2}\sum_AD_A\gamma_5\lambda_A
      \right).
  \]
  The fermion variations contain
  \(\delta\psi_i\supset F_i\alpha\) and
  \(\delta\lambda_A\supset iD_A\gamma_5\alpha\).  Taking the same linear
  combination gives
  \[
    \delta\widetilde G=F_{\rm tot}\alpha+\text{derivative terms}.
  \]
  A nonzero constant shift is the defining signature of the Goldstone
  fermion of broken global supersymmetry.  In supergravity it is removed
  in unitary gauge and supplies the longitudinal gravitino.

  \SupplementarySolution{27}{Longitudinal-Gravitino Equivalence}
  A high-energy helicity-\(1/2\) polarization of a massive gravitino has
  the leading form
  \[
    u_\mu(q,\tfrac12)
      =\sqrt{\frac23}\frac{q_\mu}{m_g}u(q,\tfrac12)
       +O(m_g/E),
  \]
  and similarly for helicity \(-1/2\).  Contracting this with the
  supercurrent coupling replaces \(q_\mu S^\mu/m_g\) by the divergence
  of the supercurrent.  The broken-supersymmetry Ward identity relates
  that divergence to the goldstino coupling.  Hence
  \[
    \mathcal M(\psi_{\mu,\rm long})
      =\mathcal M(\widetilde G)+O(m_g/E).
  \]
  The theorem concerns only the longitudinal helicities; the transverse
  \(\pm3/2\) amplitudes remain gravitationally coupled.

  \SupplementarySolution{28}{Gravitino Decay Scaling}
  Goldstino interactions are derivative couplings suppressed by \(F\).
  For a parent of mass \(m\), the amplitude therefore scales as
  \(m^2/F\).  Two-body phase space supplies one inverse power of \(m\),
  so
  \[
    \Gamma(\widetilde X\to X+\widetilde G)
      =C\,\frac{m^5}{16\pi F^2},
  \]
  where \(C\) contains mixing angles and
  \((1-m_X^2/m^2)^4\).  With Weinberg's flat-vacuum relation
  \(F^2=6m_g^2/\kappa^2\),
  \[
    \Gamma
      =C\,\frac{\kappa^2m^5}{96\pi m_g^2}.
  \]
  Thus a lighter gravitino couples more strongly through its
  longitudinal component, while a Planck-scale \(F\) produces a very
  long-lived parent.

  \SupplementarySolution{29}{Polonyi Stability Check}
  Use \(x=\kappa\operatorname{Re}\phi\),
  \(y=\kappa\operatorname{Im}\phi\), and \(b=2-\sqrt3\).  Expanding the
  potential of Weinberg Solution 6 about
  \(x_0=\sqrt3-1,\ y_0=0\), the mixed derivative vanishes.  Canonically
  normalized fluctuations are
  \(a=\sqrt2\,\delta x/\kappa\) and
  \(c=\sqrt2\,\delta y/\kappa\).  The Hessian gives
  \[
    m_a^2=2\sqrt3\,m_g^2,\qquad
    m_c^2=(4-2\sqrt3)m_g^2.
  \]
  Both coefficients are positive:
  \(2\sqrt3\simeq3.464\) and
  \(4-2\sqrt3\simeq0.536\).  The tuned Minkowski point is therefore a
  local minimum in both real scalar directions, not merely a stationary
  point of the real-field restriction.

  \SupplementarySolution{30}{Hidden-Sector Scale Comparison}
  If the dominant auxiliary order parameter is
  \(F=O(\Lambda^2)\), Planck-suppressed interactions give
  \[
    m_{\rm soft}=O(\kappa\Lambda^2).
  \]
  If instead the nonperturbative quantity is a superpotential or
  condensate \(W=O(\Lambda^3)\), the gravitino and soft scales are
  \[
    m_{\rm soft}=O(\kappa^2\Lambda^3).
  \]
  Taking \(\kappa^{-1}=2.4\times10^{18}\) GeV and
  \(m_{\rm soft}=10^3\) GeV gives
  \[
    \Lambda_F\simeq
      \sqrt{\frac{10^3}{\kappa}}
      \simeq5\times10^{10}\ {\rm GeV},
  \]
  and
  \[
    \Lambda_W\simeq
      \left(\frac{10^3}{\kappa^2}\right)^{1/3}
      \simeq2\times10^{13}\ {\rm GeV}.
  \]
  Order-one coefficients round these to the \(10^{11}\) and
  \(10^{13}\) GeV scales quoted in Section~31.7.
\end{enumerate}
