\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Weinberg Exercises}
\ExerciseEditorialNote

\begin{enumerate}
  \WeinbergSolution{1}
  For a real scalar multiplet the flat-space transformation rules may be
  read successively as
  \[
    \delta C=\ii\bar\alpha\gamma _5\omega,\qquad
    \delta\omega=
    (-\ii\gamma _5\slashed{\partial}C-M+\ii\gamma _5N+\slashed V)\alpha ,
  \]
  followed by the transformations of \(M,N,V_\nu,\lambda,D\).  Apply
  these rules to each fixed vector index \(\mu\) of \(E_\mu\).  Starting
  with \(C_\mu^E=b_\mu\), the first transformation gives
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.2.2}.  The variation of that result can be reduced with
  \[
    \gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^\tau
    =\eta^{\rho\sigma}\gamma^\tau-\eta^{\rho\tau}\gamma^\sigma
     +\eta^{\sigma\tau}\gamma^\rho
     +\ii\epsilon^{\rho\sigma\tau\lambda}\gamma _5\gamma_\lambda .
  \]
  Separating the scalar, pseudoscalar, and vector coefficients of
  \(\alpha\) gives
  \[
    M_\mu^E=\partial_\mu s,\qquad
    N_\mu^E=\partial_\mu p,
  \]
  and
  \[
    V_{\mu\nu}^E
      =-\frac32E_{\mu\nu}
       +\frac12\eta_{\mu\nu}E^\rho{}_\rho
       +\frac12\epsilon_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho}
          \partial^\sigma b^\rho .
  \]
  This proves Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.2.3} and \eqref{eq:31.2.4}.  Notice that
  the symmetric part is built from the only gauge-invariant second
  derivative of \(h_{\mu\nu}\), while the antisymmetric part is the curl
  of the gauge-invariant auxiliary vector.

  The \(M,N,V\) transformations all determine the same next component.
  Using the linearized Bianchi identity
  \(\partial^\nu E_{\mu\nu}=0\), their common answer is
  \[
    \lambda_\mu^E
      =\partial_\mu(\gamma^\nu\omega_\nu^E)
       -\slashed{\partial}\omega_\mu^E .
  \]
  In particular, the two possible orders of differentiation agree
  because ordinary derivatives commute.  This is
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.2.5}.

  Finally insert this expression in
  \(\delta\lambda=-[\gamma^\rho,\gamma^\sigma]\alpha
  F_{\rho\sigma}/4+\ii D\gamma _5\alpha\).  The curl of the symmetric
  Einstein-tensor part drops out by the Bianchi identity; the remaining
  \(\gamma _5\)-scalar coefficient is
  \[
    D_\mu^E=\partial_\mu\partial^\nu b_\nu-\Box b_\mu ,
  \]
  which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.2.6}.  Its divergence vanishes identically.
  Thus the components close into a real, gauge-invariant multiplet, and
  the two identities
  \[
    \partial^\nu E_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad
    \partial^\mu D_\mu^E=0
  \]
  provide useful independent checks on the calculation.

  \WeinbergSolution{2}
  At first order in the gravitational coupling the interaction is
  \[
    I_{\rm int}=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\int \dd^4x\,
      \bar\psi_\mu S^\mu .
  \]
  Let a gravitino of momentum \(q\), helicity \(r\), and vector-spinor
  wavefunction \(u_\mu(q,r)\) be added to a process
  \(\InKet{\alpha}\to\OutKet{\beta}\).  LSZ reduction gives
  \[
    \mathcal M_{\beta\alpha+\psi}(q,r)
      =-\frac{\kappa}{2}\,
       \bar u_\mu(q,r)
       \OutBra{\beta}S^\mu(0)\InKet{\alpha}.
  \]
  The singular terms as \(q\to0\) arise by attaching the supercurrent to
  each external on-shell line.  If \(\mathcal Q_i\) denotes the
  one-particle representation of the supercharge on leg \(i\), and
  \(\eta_i=+1\) for an outgoing leg and \(-1\) for an incoming leg, pole
  factorization gives the soft theorem
  \[
    \boxed{\;
    \mathcal M_{\beta\alpha+\psi}(q,r)
      =\frac{\kappa}{2}
       \sum_i\eta_i\,\frac{p_i^\mu}{p_i\mathbin{\cdot}q}\,
       \bar u_\mu(q,r)\,\mathcal Q_i\,
       \mathcal M_{\beta\alpha}
       +O(q^0)\; .}
  \]
  Here \(\mathcal Q_i\mathcal M\) means: replace external particle \(i\)
  by the member of its supermultiplet obtained by acting with the
  supercharge, including its standard wavefunction and statistics sign.
  The formula therefore expresses every leading soft-gravitino amplitude
  in terms of amplitudes without the gravitino.

  Gauge invariance supplies a useful check.  Replacing
  \(u_\mu\) by \(q_\mu u\) makes the pole factors cancel and leaves
  \[
    \frac{\kappa}{2}\bar u\sum_i\eta_i\mathcal Q_i
      \mathcal M_{\beta\alpha}=0.
  \]
  This is precisely the Ward identity for unbroken supersymmetry.  Terms
  regular at \(q=0\) are not fixed by the soft theorem.

  \WeinbergSolution{3}
  The economical all-orders check uses the first-order, or
  ``one-and-a-half-order,'' form of the action.  Treat
  \(e^a{}_\mu,\psi_\mu,\omega_\mu{}^{ab},s,p,b_\mu\) as independent,
  vary the action, and impose the algebraic \(\omega\) equation only
  after taking the variation.  That equation is the torsion constraint
  whose solution is exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.7}.  Consequently the
  term
  \((\delta I/\delta\omega_\mu{}^{ab})\delta\omega_\mu{}^{ab}\)
  vanishes, so no explicit variation of the composite spin connection
  is needed.

  The terms containing a derivative of \(\alpha(x)\) cancel first.
  From \(\delta e^a{}_\mu=\kappa\bar\alpha\gamma^a\psi_\mu\), variation
  of the Einstein term gives, after one integration by parts,
  \[
    \delta I_{\rm EH}\big|_{D\alpha}
      =-\frac1\kappa\int \dd^4x\,e\,
        \bar\psi_\mu\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}
        R_{\nu\rho}{}^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\alpha ,
  \]
  up to a boundary term.  The same expression with the opposite sign
  follows from the Rarita--Schwinger term under
  \(\delta\psi_\mu=2D_\mu\alpha/\kappa+\cdots\), using
  \[
    [D_\mu,D_\nu]\alpha
      =\frac18R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}[\gamma_a,\gamma_b]\alpha
  \]
  and the curvature Bianchi identity.

  The remaining terms can be sorted according to the independent
  auxiliary fields.  Terms linear in \(s,p,b_\mu\) from the
  nonderivative part of \(\delta\psi_\mu\) cancel the variation of
  \(-4e(s^2+p^2-b^2)/3\) under
  Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.3}--\eqref{eq:31.6.5}.  Terms quadratic in the
  auxiliaries cancel against the determinant variation
  \[
    \delta e=e\,e_a{}^\mu\delta e^a{}_\mu
            =\kappa e\,\bar\alpha\gamma^\mu\psi_\mu .
  \]
  Finally, all apparently cubic and quartic gravitino terms are the
  torsion terms hidden in \(R(\omega)\) and \(D(\omega)\); in the
  one-and-a-half-order calculation they cancel by the spin-connection
  field equation.  Fierz rearrangement uses only the Majorana identity
  \[
    (\bar\chi_1\gamma^a\chi_2)(\bar\chi_3\gamma_a\chi_4)
      =-(\bar\chi_1\gamma^a\chi_4)
        (\bar\chi_3\gamma_a\chi_2)
       -2(\bar\chi_1\chi_4)(\bar\chi_3\chi_2)
       +2(\bar\chi_1\gamma_5\chi_4)
        (\bar\chi_3\gamma_5\chi_2).
  \]
  Thus
  \[
    \delta I_{\rm SUGRA}=\int \dd^4x\,\partial_\mu\mathscr J^\mu
  \]
  for arbitrary local \(\alpha(x)\).  With fields or variations of
  compact support the boundary integral vanishes.  No expansion in
  \(\kappa\), and hence none in \(G=\kappa^2/(8\pi)\), entered the
  argument.

  \WeinbergSolution{4}
  Substitute Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.12}--\eqref{eq:31.6.22} and
  Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.1}--\eqref{eq:31.6.5} in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.40}.  It is important to vary \(e\), the curved
  gamma matrices, and the spin connection before integrating by parts.
  The result may be organized as
  \[
    \delta[S]_D=
      e\,\ii\bar\alpha\gamma_5\slashed{\mathcal D}\lambda^S
      (\delta e/e)\,D^S+\delta\mathcal C^S ,
  \]
  where \(\mathcal C^S\) denotes all five compensating terms in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.40}.  The connection term in the first expression
  combines with \(\delta e\) to give
  \(\partial_\mu(ie\bar\alpha\gamma_5\gamma^\mu\lambda^S)\).
  Every remaining bulk term cancels in pairs:
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|c}
    \text{term from }\delta D^S&
    \text{compensator producing its negative}\\ \hline
    F^S_{ab}\psi_\mu&\bar\psi^\mu\gamma_\mu\gamma_5\lambda^S\\
    (s,p,b_\mu)\lambda^S&(sN^S-pM^S+b^\mu V_\mu^S)\\
    L^\mu\omega^S&\bar\omega^S\gamma_5\slashed L\\
    \psi\psi V^S,\ \psi\psi\psi\omega^S&
      \epsilon V^S\bar\psi\gamma\psi,\
      \epsilon(\bar\omega^S\psi)\bar\psi\gamma\psi\\
    C^S\,\delta\mathcal L_{\rm SUGRA}&
      -2\kappa^2 C^S\mathcal L_{\rm SUGRA}/3 .
  \end{array}
  \]
  After these cancellations the answer is a divergence,
  \[
    \boxed{\quad
      \delta[S]_D=\partial_\mu\mathscr J_D^\mu(S,\alpha)\quad}
  \]
  with a convenient improved current beginning
  \[
    \mathscr J_D^\mu
      =ie\,\bar\alpha\gamma_5\gamma^\mu\lambda^S
       -\frac{\ii\kappa e}{3}
        \bar\alpha\gamma_5\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\omega^S b_\nu
       +\frac{\kappa e}{3}
        \bar\alpha\gamma^\mu(s-\ii\gamma_5p)\omega^S
       +\mathscr J_{\psi}^{\mu}.
  \]
  Here \(\mathscr J_{\psi}^{\mu}\) is the unique local completion
  obtained by retaining the three explicit gravitino compensators of
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.40}; currents that differ from this one by
  \(\partial_\nu X^{[\mu\nu]}\) are equivalent.  The displayed
  cancellation table is often more useful than a particular improved
  representative: it proves directly that
  \(\int \dd^4x\,[S]_D\) is invariant for arbitrary \(S\).

  \WeinbergSolution{5}
  It is clearest first to give the exact component answer, before
  eliminating auxiliary fields.  Put a vertical bar with subscript
  ``F'' on the sum of all monomials containing at least one fermion.
  Direct substitution of the composite multiplets \(K(\Phi,\Phi^*)\)
  and \(f(\Phi)\) in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.40} and
  \eqref{eq:31.6.42} gives
  \begin{multline}
    \mathcal L_{\rm F}
    =-\frac12(\bar\psi_\mu L^\mu)
      -\frac{\kappa^2}{3}C^K
        \left[-\frac12(\bar\psi_\mu L^\mu)\right]\\
    +\frac e2\left[
      D^K-\frac{\ii\kappa}{2}
        \bar\psi^\mu\gamma_\mu\gamma_5\lambda^K
      +\frac{4\kappa}{3}(-sN^K+pM^K-b^\mu V_\mu^K)
      -\frac{\ii\kappa}{3}\bar\omega^K\gamma_5\slashed L\right.\\
    \left.
      -\frac{\kappa^2}{4}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}V_\sigma^K
        \bar\psi_\rho\gamma_\tau\psi_\mu
      -\frac{\kappa^2}{8}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}
        (\bar\omega^K\psi_\sigma)
        (\bar\psi_\rho\gamma_\tau\psi_\mu)
    \right]_{\rm F}\\
    +2e\,\operatorname{Re}\left[
      \mathcal F^f
      +\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt2}
        \bar\psi_{\mu R}\gamma^\mu\psi_L^f
      +\frac{\kappa^2}{4}
        \bar\psi_{\mu R}[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]\psi_{\nu R}\,f
      +2\kappa(s-\ii p)f
    \right]_{\rm F}.
    \tag{*}
  \end{multline}
  This expression includes the quadratic, cubic, and quartic fermion
  terms and is exact to all orders in \(\kappa\).  Its entries are not
  formal new fields: they are fixed algebraically by
  Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.23}--\eqref{eq:31.6.29}.  For example,
  \[
    C^K=K,\qquad
    \omega^K=-\ii\sqrt2\gamma_5
      (K_n\psi_{nL}+K_{\bar n}\psi_{nR}),
  \]
  \[
    \psi_L^f=f_n\psi_{nL},\qquad
    \mathcal F^f
      =f_n\mathcal F_n
       -\frac12f_{nm}\psi_{nL}^{\mathsf T}
          \epsilon\psi_{mL}.
  \]
  Repeated holomorphic and antiholomorphic field labels are summed.
  The remaining \(K\)-components follow by repeated use of the product
  rule; this is an unambiguous finite calculation for every polynomial
  \(K\), and extension to an analytic \(K\) is by its power series.

  To display the physical content, retain terms through second order in
  matter fermions and perform the Weyl and fermion rescalings that give
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:31.6.67}.  Writing
  \(g_{n\bar m}=d_{n\bar m}\),
  \(L_n=f_n+\kappa^2fd_n\), and
  \(\nabla_nL_m=\partial_nL_m-\Gamma^r{}_{nm}L_r\), one obtains
  \begin{multline}
    \widetilde e^{-1}\mathcal L_{\rm F}^{(2)}
      =-\frac12\bar\psi_\mu \ii\gamma_5\gamma_\nu
        \mathcal D_\rho\psi_\sigma\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
       -g_{n\bar m}\bar\psi_m\slashed{\mathcal D}\psi_n\\
      -\operatorname{Re}\left[
        e^{\kappa^2d/2}(\nabla_nL_m)
        \psi_{nL}^{\mathsf T}\epsilon\psi_{mL}\right]
      +\sqrt2\kappa\,\operatorname{Re}\left[
        e^{\kappa^2d/2}L_n
        \bar\psi_{\mu R}\gamma^\mu\psi_{nL}\right]\\
      +\frac{\kappa^2}{2}\operatorname{Re}\left[
        e^{\kappa^2d/2}f\,
        \bar\psi_{\mu R}[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]\psi_{\nu R}\right]
      +\mathcal L_{\rm aux}^{(2)} .
  \end{multline}
  The last term consists of the bilinears proportional to the
  uneliminated \(\mathcal F_n,s,p,b_\mu\); their exact coefficients are
  already contained in (*).  Eliminating those fields completes the
  squares and replaces ordinary derivatives by the Kähler-covariant
  quantities displayed above.  Setting \(\kappa=0\) checks the result:
  it reduces to the ordinary chiral kinetic term and Yukawa coupling
  \(-\operatorname{Re}(f_{nm}\psi_{nL}^{\mathsf T}
  \epsilon\psi_{mL})\).

  \WeinbergSolution{6}
  With \(x=\kappa\phi\) and \(b=\kappa\beta\), choose the phase of
  \(M^2\) so that a candidate stationary point is real.  From
  Eqs.~\eqref{eq:31.6.68} and \eqref{eq:31.6.69},
  \[
    \frac{V}{|M|^4}
      =e^{x^2}\left\{
        [1+x(x+b)]^2-3(x+b)^2
      \right\}.
  \]
  Flat spacetime requires the expression in braces to vanish.  At such a
  point stationarity is equivalent to the derivative of that expression
  vanishing.  Define
  \(A=1+x(x+b)\) and \(B=x+b\).  The two equations are
  \[
    A^2=3B^2,\qquad
    2A(2x+b)-6B=0.
  \]
  Taking the solution continuously connected to positive real \(M^2\),
  \(A=\sqrt3B\), the second equation gives \(2x+b=\sqrt3\).
  Combining this with \(A=\sqrt3(x+b)\) yields
  \[
    x=\sqrt3-1,\qquad b=2-\sqrt3 .
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \boxed{\quad
      \beta=\frac{2-\sqrt3}{\kappa},\qquad
      \phi=\frac{\sqrt3-1}{\kappa}\quad .}
  \]
  The conjugate phase-related solution is obtained by a common field and
  superpotential rephasing.  At the displayed solution
  \(L=M^2\sqrt3\ne0\), so supersymmetry is broken, while the negative
  \(-3\kappa^2|f|^2\) term cancels the positive auxiliary contribution
  and the classical vacuum energy is zero.
\end{enumerate}
