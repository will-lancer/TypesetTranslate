\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Weinberg Exercises}
\ExerciseEditorialNote

\begin{enumerate}
  \WeinbergSolution{1}
  In six dimensions the massless little group is
  \[
    \operatorname{Spin}(4)\simeq SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R .
  \]
  We denote its representation of dimensions \(a\) and \(b\) by
  \((a,b)\).  A Weyl spinor is pseudoreal, so the numbers \(N_+\) and
  \(N_-\) of supercharges of the two chiralities must both be even.  It
  is conventional to write
  \[
    (p,q)=\left(\frac{N_+}{2},\frac{N_-}{2}\right),\qquad
    {\cal N}_{\rm real}=8(p+q).
  \]
  The automorphism group is
  \(USp(2p)\times USp(2q)\).  Parity interchanges \(p\leftrightarrow q\)
  and \(SU(2)_L\leftrightarrow SU(2)_R\).

  Choose a null momentum and divide the surviving supercharges into
  fermionic creation and annihilation operators.  There are
  \(2(p+q)\) complex creators.  Thus, for any chosen little-group
  Clifford vacuum \(R_0\), the complete multiplet is
  \[
    {\cal M}_{p,q}(R_0)
      =R_0\mathbin{\otimes}\Lambda^\bullet A_{p,q},
      \qquad
      \dim{\cal M}_{p,q}(R_0)
      =\dim(R_0)\,2^{\,2(p+q)} ,
  \]
  followed, when necessary, by CPT completion.  Here \(A_{p,q}\) is
  the space of the surviving supercharge creators, with \(p\)
  left-handed and \(q\) right-handed symplectic doublets.  This
  exterior-algebra formula classifies every central-charge-free
  massless multiplet, including multiplets based on nontrivial
  \(R_0\).

  For reference, the familiar irreducible multiplets with spins no
  greater than two are as follows.  Multiplicities precede the
  little-group representation:
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|l|l}
    \text{supersymmetry}&\text{multiplet}&
       \text{bosons}\ ;\ \text{fermions}\\ \hline
    (1,0)&\text{hypermultiplet}&
      4(1,1)\ ;\ 2(2,1)\\
    (1,0)&\text{vector}&
      (2,2)\ ;\ 2(1,2)\\
    (1,0)&\text{tensor}&
      (3,1)+(1,1)\ ;\ 2(2,1)\\
    (1,0)&\text{gravity}&
      (3,3)+(1,3)\ ;\ 2(2,3)\\[2pt]
    (2,0)&\text{tensor}&
      (3,1)+5(1,1)\ ;\ 4(2,1)\\
    (2,0)&\text{gravity}&
      (3,3)+5(1,3)\ ;\ 4(2,3)\\[2pt]
    (1,1)&\text{vector}&
      (2,2)+4(1,1)\ ;\ 2(2,1)+2(1,2).
  \end{array}
  \]
  The chirality assignments in this table are conventional; reversing
  every chirality gives the parity-conjugate table.  For example, the
  \((1,0)\) tensor has one chiral two-form, three physical
  polarizations, while its scalar supplies the fourth bosonic state.

  The nonchiral \((1,1)\) gravity multiplet, obtained by tensoring the
  two minimal chiral oscillator modules, has
  \[
  \begin{split}
    B={}&(3,3)+(3,1)+(1,3)+4(2,2)+(1,1),\\
    F={}&2(3,2)+2(2,3)+2(2,1)+2(1,2),
  \end{split}
  \]
  with \(32\) bosonic and \(32\) fermionic states.  The maximal
  \((2,2)\) gravity multiplet is
  \[
  \begin{split}
    B={}&(3,3)+5(3,1)+5(1,3)+16(2,2)+25(1,1),\\
    F={}&4(3,2)+4(2,3)+20(2,1)+20(1,2),
  \end{split}
  \]
  with \(128\) states of each statistics.

  Finally, the possible algebra types with at most \(32\) real
  supercharges are, up to parity,
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|c}
    {\cal N}_{\rm real}&(p,q)\\ \hline
     8&(1,0)\\
    16&(2,0),(1,1)\\
    24&(3,0),(2,1)\\
    32&(4,0),(3,1),(2,2).
  \end{array}
  \]
  Their massless representations are all furnished by the same
  exterior-algebra construction above.  Some of the highly chiral
  \(24\)- and \(32\)-supercharge choices have exotic little-group
  tensors rather than an ordinary graviton multiplet; imposing the
  existence of an ordinary spin-two field selects the familiar
  gravity multiplets.

  \WeinbergSolution{2}
  Let \({\cal N}\) denote the number of real supercharge components.
  For null momentum, half of the components have vanishing
  anticommutators and the remaining half form
  \({\cal N}/4\) fermionic creation-annihilation pairs.  Acting across a
  massless multiplet changes a chosen spin component from \(j\) to
  \[
    j-\frac{{\cal N}}{8}.
  \]
  If spins from \(+3\) through \(-3\) are allowed, but none outside
  this interval, then
  \[
    3-\frac{{\cal N}}8\geq-3,\qquad
    \boxed{{\cal N}\leq48}.
  \]
  The hypothesis that a spin-two particle exists rules out the
  otherwise trivial choice of a multiplet whose spin range misses
  spin two.

  The minimal real spinor size, together with the reality and chirality
  conditions of Section~32.1, determines the allowed multiples in each
  dimension.  Taking the largest multiple not exceeding \(48\) gives
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|c|c}
    d&\text{minimal number of real components}&
       {\cal N}_{\max}\\ \hline
    4&4&48\\
    5&8&48\\
    6&8&48\\
    7&16&48\\
    8&16&48\\
    9&16&48\\
    10&16&48\\
    11&32&32 .
  \end{array}
  \]
  In \(d=12\) a real supersymmetry generator already has \(64\) real
  components, which violates the bound.  Hence, within the
  \(\dd\geq4\) classification used in this chapter,
  \[
    \boxed{d_{\max}=11}.
  \]
  The entries \(48\) include all chirality distributions compatible
  with the appropriate reality condition; the table records the
  maximum total number of generators, not a unique chiral algebra.

  \WeinbergSolution{3}
  Go to the rest frame \(P^\mu=(m,\boldsymbol0)\).  By an
  \(R\)-symmetry rotation, the scalar central-charge matrix can be
  placed in its canonical real form.  The supercharge
  anticommutator then splits into positive Hermitian blocks whose
  eigenvalues are proportional to
  \[
    m+|Z_a|,\qquad m-|Z_a|,
  \]
  where \(Z_a\) are the independent skew eigenvalues (or singular
  values) of the scalar charge matrix.  Positivity of the norm of
  every state therefore requires
  \[
    \boxed{\quad m\geq\max_a|Z_a|\quad}.
  \]
  With only one independent scalar charge in either type-II algebra,
  this is simply \(m\geq|Z|\), with \(Z\) normalized as in the
  anticommutation relation.

  At \(m=|Z|\), one block has zero eigenvalue.  The corresponding
  sixteen real linear combinations of the thirty-two supercharges
  annihilate the state; the other sixteen real combinations give
  eight fermionic creation operators.  A minimal saturated
  multiplet consequently contains
  \[
    2^8=256=128_B+128_F
  \]
  states.  Its massive little group is \(\operatorname{Spin}(9)\), and the unique
  minimal half-BPS content is
  \[
    \boxed{\quad
      B:\ {\bf44}\oplus{\bf84},\qquad
      F:\ {\bf128}\quad}.
  \]
  These are respectively a traceless symmetric tensor, a three-form,
  and a gamma-traceless vector-spinor of \(\operatorname{Spin}(9)\).
  Equivalently,
  this is the massless eleven-dimensional supergravity multiplet,
  viewed as a massive ten-dimensional one.

  In type IIA the two ten-dimensional supercharges have opposite
  chirality, and the scalar charge occurs in their mixed
  anticommutator.  The BPS projector therefore relates opposite
  chiralities; Kaluza--Klein momentum in an eleventh dimension is the
  standard realization.  In type IIB the two supercharges have the
  same chirality, and the scalar charge lies in the antisymmetric
  \(R\)-symmetry matrix, so the projector instead mixes the two
  members of the \(R\)-symmetry doublet.  This changes the embedding
  of the preserved supersymmetry but not the \(256\)-state
  \(\operatorname{Spin}(9)\) multiplet.  For \(m>|Z|\) no supercharge is null and a
  generic long multiplet has sixteen creation operators.

  \WeinbergSolution{4}
  In \(d=9=2n+1\), \(n=4\), Hodge duality lets us retain only
  \(p\)-form gamma matrices with \(0\leq p\leq4\).  For one Majorana
  supercharge, the matrix multiplying a bosonic charge must be
  symmetric in its two spinor indices.  Equation~(32.3.7) gives the
  symmetry sign
  \[
    (-1)^{pn+n(n+1)/2+p(p-1)/2}
      =(-1)^{p(p-1)/2}.
  \]
  It is positive for
  \[
    p=0,\ 1,\ 4
  \]
  and negative for \(p=2,3\).  The \(p=1\) structure is the unique
  vector slot already occupied by the momentum \(P_\mu\).  Thus the
  independent central extensions, beyond momentum, are a scalar and
  a four-form:
  \[
    \boxed{\quad Z,\qquad Z_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\quad}.
  \]
  Up to conventional numerical factors, the algebra is
  \[
    \{Q,Q^{\mathsf T}\}
      =\ii\gamma^\mu C P_\mu
       +C Z
       +\gamma^{[\mu\nu\rho\sigma]}C
          Z_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
  \]
  Completeness is checked by counting symmetric \(16\times16\)
  matrices:
  \[
    9+\binom90+\binom94
      =9+1+126
      =136
      =\frac{16\cdot17}{2}.
  \]
  A putative extra vector charge would use the same single gamma-matrix
  structure as \(P_\mu\), rather than furnishing another independent
  spinor matrix.
\end{enumerate}
