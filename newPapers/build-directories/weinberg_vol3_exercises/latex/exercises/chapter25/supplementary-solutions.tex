\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Supplementary Exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
  \SupplementarySolution{1}{The Supertrace Test}
  For homogeneous block matrices the supertrace has graded cyclicity,
  \[
    \operatorname{str}(XY)
      =(-1)^{|X||Y|}\operatorname{str}(YX).
  \]
  If either matrix is even, this is ordinary cyclicity separately in the
  diagonal blocks.  If both are odd, write
  \(X=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&B\\C&0\end{psmallmatrix}\) and
  \(Y=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&E\\F&0\end{psmallmatrix}\).  Then
  \[
    \operatorname{str}(XY)=\operatorname{tr}(BF)-\operatorname{tr}(CE)
      =-\operatorname{str}(YX),
  \]
  using ordinary cyclicity.  Consequently
  \[
    \operatorname{str}[X,Y\}
      =\operatorname{str}(XY)
       -(-1)^{|X||Y|}\operatorname{str}(YX)=0.
  \]
  The bracket also has degree \(|X|+|Y|\); hence supertraceless matrices
  form a graded subalgebra.

  \SupplementarySolution{2}{The Signed Jacobi Identity}
  Substitute the definition of the bracket twice in each cyclic term.  The
  first term, for example, is
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    (-1)^{|X||Z|}[X,[Y,Z\}\}
    ={}&(-1)^{|X||Z|}XYZ
       -(-1)^{|X||Z|+|Y||Z|}XZY\\
      &-(-1)^{|X||Y|}YZX
       +(-1)^{|X||Y|+|Y||Z|}ZYX .
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Expanding the other two cyclic terms produces the same six ordered
  products, each twice with opposite coefficients.  Their sum is zero.
  When all three degrees are one, the inner bracket of each term is an
  anticommutator, while an odd element bracketed with that even result uses
  a commutator.  The identity then reads
  \([X,\{Y,Z\}]+[Y,\{Z,X\}]+[Z,\{X,Y\}]=0\).

  \SupplementarySolution{3}{Why a SUSY Variation Is Even}
  Degrees add modulo two, so
  \(|\epsilon^\alpha Q_\alpha|=1+1=0\), and the adjoint term is even as
  well.  Thus \(G\) is even.  The ordinary commutator with an even operator
  has the same degree as its argument:
  \[
    |[G,X]|=|G|+|X|=|X|.
  \]
  Hence a supersymmetry variation maps bosonic operators to bosonic
  variations and fermionic operators to fermionic variations, although
  \(Q\) itself reverses statistics on states.

  If \(\epsilon\) were commuting, \(\epsilon Q\) would remain odd.
  Moving parameters through odd operators would then miss the Grassmann
  minus signs.  In particular, the commutator of two transformations would
  be represented by the wrong combination of \(QQ^\dagger\) and
  \(Q^\dagger Q\), giving a commutator where the supersymmetry algebra
  requires an anticommutator and therefore failing to close on a
  translation.

  \SupplementarySolution{4}{Why Supercharges Have Spin One-Half}
  The product of two spin-\(A\) Lorentz representations contains a
  highest-spin component \(2A\).  Positivity prevents the anticommutator
  \(\{Q,Q^\dagger\}\) from vanishing identically for a nonzero \(Q\).
  Thus for \(A>1/2\) it would contain an even conserved tensor of spin
  greater than one.  Under the stated scattering assumptions,
  Coleman--Mandula excludes precisely such additional spacetime tensor
  charges.

  Spin \(1/2\) is exceptional because
  \(1/2\otimes1/2=0\oplus1\): the vector part may be the already allowed
  momentum \(P_\mu\), and the scalar part may be a central charge.  Spin
  zero is not ruled out by this highest-spin argument, because its square is
  another scalar; it does not, however, produce the characteristic
  square-root of translations.  The nontrivial spacetime extension selected
  by the theorem is therefore generated by spin-\(1/2\) charges.

  \SupplementarySolution{5}{Central Charges in Three Dimensions}
  Lorentz covariance permits
  \[
    \{Q^I_\alpha,Q^J_\beta\}
      =A^{IJ}(\gamma^\mu C)_{\alpha\beta}P_\mu
       +C_{\alpha\beta}Z^{IJ}.
  \]
  The left side is unchanged when the complete labels
  \((\alpha,I)\) and \((\beta,J)\) are exchanged.  Because
  \((\gamma^\mu C)_{\alpha\beta}\) is symmetric, this demands
  \(A^{IJ}=A^{JI}\).  Positivity allows a real change of basis that makes
  \(A^{IJ}\) proportional to \(\delta^{IJ}\).  Because
  \(C_{\alpha\beta}\) is antisymmetric, the second term is symmetric only
  when
  \[
    Z^{IJ}=-Z^{JI}.
  \]
  A real antisymmetric \(N\times N\) matrix has
  \(N(N-1)/2\) independent entries, which is therefore the maximum number
  of real scalar central charges in this form.

  \SupplementarySolution{6}{Positive Energy from the Algebra}
  Taking the trace over the two spinor components eliminates the spatial
  Pauli matrices:
  \[
    \sum_{\alpha=1}^2\{Q_\alpha,Q_\alpha^\dagger\}
      =2\operatorname{tr}(\sigma^\mu)P_\mu=4P^0.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    H=\frac14\sum_{\alpha=1}^2
       \bigl(Q_\alpha Q_\alpha^\dagger+
             Q_\alpha^\dagger Q_\alpha\bigr).
  \]
  On a normalized state \(\ket{\psi}\),
  \[
    \braket{\psi|H|\psi}
      =\frac14\sum_\alpha
       \left(\lVert Q_\alpha\ket{\psi}\rVert^2+
             \lVert Q_\alpha^\dagger\ket{\psi}\rVert^2\right)\geq0.
  \]
  Thus the spectrum of \(H\) is nonnegative in any unitary
  representation.

  \SupplementarySolution{7}{The Supersymmetric Vacuum Criterion}
  Applying the preceding norm identity to an energy eigenstate
  \(\ket{0}\) gives
  \[
    E_0=\frac14\sum_\alpha
      \left(\lVert Q_\alpha\ket{0}\rVert^2+
            \lVert Q_\alpha^\dagger\ket{0}\rVert^2\right).
  \]
  If every supercharge and adjoint annihilates the vacuum, the right-hand
  side is zero.  Conversely, if \(E_0=0\), it is a sum of nonnegative
  squared norms and each norm must vanish.  The converse is the implication
  that uses positive definiteness of the Hilbert-space inner product.
  Translation invariance ensures that \(P_i\ket0=0\), so this zero is the
  full vacuum four-momentum.

  \SupplementarySolution{8}{Boson--Fermion Pairing}
  On an energy-\(E\) rest-frame eigenspace, choose a spinor component with
  normalization
  \[
    \{Q_1,Q_1^\dagger\}=2E.
  \]
  Set
  \[
    A=\frac{Q_1+Q_1^\dagger}{\sqrt{2E}}.
  \]
  Since \(Q_1^2=(Q_1^\dagger)^2=0\), one has \(A^2=1\).  Also
  \(\{A,(-1)^F\}=0\).  Thus \(A\) is an invertible map from the bosonic
  subspace to the fermionic subspace, with inverse \(A\), and the same map
  goes in the opposite direction.  The two subspaces consequently have
  equal dimension.  The argument fails at \(E=0\), exactly where
  supersymmetric vacuum states can remain unpaired.

  \SupplementarySolution{9}{Mass Degeneracy in a Supermultiplet}
  From \([P_\mu,Q_\alpha]=0\),
  \[
    [P^2,Q_\alpha]
      =P^\mu[P_\mu,Q_\alpha]+[P^\mu,Q_\alpha]P_\mu=0,
  \]
  and similarly for \(Q^\dagger\).  Acting with any sequence of
  supercharges therefore preserves the eigenvalue of \(P^2\), hence the
  mass.  The charges transform as spinors, so they do not commute with
  rotations; applying them changes the spin content and produces the
  familiar neighboring spins.

  If \(T\) commutes with all supercharges, then
  \(TQ\ket\psi=QT\ket\psi=tQ\ket\psi\).  All nonzero descendants have the
  same eigenvalue \(t\).  Irreducibility then makes \(T\) proportional to
  the identity on the whole supermultiplet.

  \SupplementarySolution{10}{The \(U(1)_R\) Automorphism}
  The phases cancel in
  \(\{Q_\alpha,Q^\dagger_{\dot\beta}\}\), while
  \(\{Q,Q\}=\{Q^\dagger,Q^\dagger\}=0\) remain zero.  The Poincar\'e
  brackets are also unchanged, so this is an automorphism.  Writing it as
  \(e^{i\theta R}Q_\alpha e^{-i\theta R}=e^{-i\theta}Q_\alpha\) gives
  \[
    [R,Q_\alpha]=-Q_\alpha,\qquad
    [R,Q^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}]=+Q^\dagger_{\dot\alpha}.
  \]
  An ordinary internal symmetry in the Coleman--Mandula direct product
  would commute with \(Q\).  Here \(R\) rotates the supersymmetry generators
  themselves and is therefore an \(R\)-symmetry automorphism.

  \SupplementarySolution{11}{Antisymmetry of \(Z_{rs}\)}
  Symmetry of the anticommutator under exchange of its two entries yields
  \[
    \epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Z_{rs}
      =\epsilon_{\beta\alpha}Z_{sr}
      =-\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Z_{sr}.
  \]
  Since \(\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\) is nonzero for some components,
  \(Z_{rs}=-Z_{sr}\).  For \(N=1\), this says \(Z_{11}=-Z_{11}\), hence
  \(Z_{11}=0\).  At least two supercharges are needed for a scalar central
  charge in this four-dimensional algebra.

  \SupplementarySolution{12}{Canonical Blocks of the Central Charge}
  In the unitary skew-normal basis,
  \[
    Z=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}
       z_i\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}
       \ \oplus\ 0 .
  \]
  Each nonzero block satisfies
  \(Z_i^\dagger Z_i=|z_i|^2\mathbf1_2\).  Thus every
  \(|z_i|^2\) is an eigenvalue of \(Z^\dagger Z\) with multiplicity two;
  for odd \(N\) there is one additional zero eigenvalue.  Consequently
  \[
    \operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{Z^\dagger Z}
      =2\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}|z_i|.
  \]

  \SupplementarySolution{13}{The \(N=2\) BPS Bound}
  After rephasing the charges, define two spinor pairs schematically by
  \[
    A_\alpha=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
      \left(Q_{\alpha1}
       \epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Q^\dagger_{\dot\beta2}\right),
    \qquad
    B_\alpha=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
      \left(Q_{\alpha1}
       -\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Q^\dagger_{\dot\beta2}\right),
  \]
  with the dotted and undotted rest-frame indices identified.  The signs
  for the second component are fixed by \(\epsilon_{12}=1\).  Direct use of
  the algebra gives, up to the common Kronecker delta,
  \[
    \{A_\alpha,A_\beta^\dagger\}=(2M+|z|)\delta_{\alpha\beta},
    \qquad
    \{B_\alpha,B_\beta^\dagger\}=(2M-|z|)\delta_{\alpha\beta}.
  \]
  Positivity of the second anticommutator requires \(2M-|z|\geq0\).
  Identifying \(z\) with this chapter's \(Z_{12}\) gives
  \[
    M\geq\frac{|Z_{12}|}{2}.
  \]
  Other common normalizations absorb the factor of two into the definition
  of the central charge; the positive-operator argument is unchanged.

  \SupplementarySolution{14}{Null Oscillators and Shortening}
  At \(2M=|z|\),
  \[
    \lVert B_\alpha^\dagger\ket\psi\rVert^2
      +\lVert B_\alpha\ket\psi\rVert^2=0
  \]
  for every state in the representation.  Positivity makes both terms
  vanish, so the \(B\) pair acts trivially.  A generic massive \(N=2\)
  representation has four independent fermionic creation operators and,
  on a spin-zero vacuum, \(2^4=16\) states.  At half-BPS saturation only
  two creation operators remain, giving \(2^2=4\) states: two scalar states
  and the two spin projections of a spin-\(1/2\) state.  This factor-of-four
  reduction is the shortening.

  \SupplementarySolution{15}{A Long Massive \(N=1\) Multiplet}
  The zero-creation level is one spin-\(j\) representation.  Because the
  creation operators transform as spin \(1/2\), the one-creation level is
  \[
    j\otimes\frac12
      =\left(j+\frac12\right)\oplus
       \left(j-\frac12\right).
  \]
  Antisymmetry leaves only the spin-singlet product of the two creation
  operators, so the two-creation level is a second spin-\(j\)
  representation.  The full content is therefore
  \[
    \left(j+\frac12\right)\oplus j\oplus j
      \oplus\left(j-\frac12\right).
  \]
  The two even levels contain \(2(2j+1)=4j+2\) spin states.  The odd level
  contains \((2j+2)+(2j)=4j+2\).  Which side is bosonic depends on whether
  the Clifford vacuum has integral or half-integral spin, but the equality
  is the same.

  \SupplementarySolution{16}{The Collapsed Massive Multiplet}
  For \(j=0\), the tensor product
  \(0\otimes1/2\) contains only spin \(1/2\); a negative-spin
  representation has no meaning and is absent.  The zero- and two-creation
  levels are each spin zero, while the one-creation level is spin \(1/2\).
  Thus the spectrum is
  \[
    0\oplus0\oplus\frac12.
  \]
  The two scalars give two physical spin states in total, and the massive
  spinor gives two, so bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom again
  match.

  \SupplementarySolution{17}{Parity Along the Massive Ladder}
  Parity exchanges annihilation and creation operators.  It therefore
  exchanges the spin-\(j\) zero-creation state \(\ket{j,m}\) with the
  two-creation state
  \(\ket{j,m}^{\mathrm b}\propto
    Q^\dagger_{1/2}Q^\dagger_{-1/2}\ket{j,m}\).
  With the stated convention, applying parity twice through the two odd
  operators gives the relative action
  \[
    P\ket{j,m}=\eta\ket{j,m}^{\mathrm b},\qquad
    P\ket{j,m}^{\mathrm b}=-\eta\ket{j,m},
  \]
  after fixing the common phase by assigning parity \(\eta\) to the
  one-creation multiplets.  Diagonalizing this \(2\times2\) action gives
  \[
    \frac{\ket{j,m}\pm i\ket{j,m}^{\mathrm b}}{\sqrt2},
    \qquad P=\pm i\eta .
  \]
  The Clebsch--Gordan combinations at the one-creation level, of spins
  \(j\pm1/2\), both retain parity \(\eta\).  At \(j=0\), the two scalars
  have parities \(\pm i\eta\), while the spin-\(1/2\) particle has parity
  \(\eta\).

  \SupplementarySolution{18}{The Massless-Frame Oscillator}
  In the chosen null frame the anticommutator matrix has eigenvalues
  \(4E\) and \(0\):
  \[
    \{Q_\alpha,Q^\dagger_{\dot\beta}\}
      =4E\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}_{\alpha\dot\beta}
  \]
  after a possible relabeling of components.  For every state
  \(\ket\psi\),
  \[
    \lVert Q_2\ket\psi\rVert^2+
    \lVert Q_2^\dagger\ket\psi\rVert^2=0.
  \]
  Unitarity therefore makes both \(Q_2\) and \(Q_2^\dagger\) act trivially.
  The normalized nonzero pair
  \(a=Q_1/\sqrt{4E}\), \(a^\dagger=Q_1^\dagger/\sqrt{4E}\)
  obeys \(\{a,a^\dagger\}=1\).  In the highest-helicity construction,
  \(a^\dagger\) lowers helicity by \(1/2\), and its adjoint \(a\) raises it
  by \(1/2\).

  \SupplementarySolution{19}{The Exterior Algebra of Helicity}
  The \(N\) creation operators anticommute, so a basis at level \(k\) is
  \[
    a^\dagger_{i_1}\cdots a^\dagger_{i_k}\ket\Omega,
    \qquad i_1<\cdots<i_k.
  \]
  This is \(\bigwedge^k\mathbb C^N\), of dimension \(\binom Nk\).
  Every creation operator lowers helicity by \(1/2\), giving
  \(\lambda_k=\lambda_{\max}-k/2\).  Summing all levels,
  \[
    \dim\mathcal H=\sum_{k=0}^N\binom Nk=(1+1)^N=2^N.
  \]

  \SupplementarySolution{20}{The CPT Self-Conjugacy Test}
  CPT reverses every helicity, so the interval
  \([\lambda_{\max}-N/2,\lambda_{\max}]\) is invariant only if its endpoints
  are negatives:
  \[
    \lambda_{\max}-\frac N2=-\lambda_{\max},
    \qquad\text{or}\qquad
    \lambda_{\max}=\frac N4.
  \]
  The binomial multiplicities are then also symmetric because
  \(\binom Nk=\binom N{N-k}\).  If this condition fails, CPT produces a
  second multiplet whose top helicity is the negative of the original
  bottom helicity,
  \[
    \lambda_{\max}^{\mathrm{CPT}}
      =-\lambda_{\max}+\frac N2.
  \]

  \SupplementarySolution{21}{The Massless Chiral Multiplet}
  Beginning with helicity \(+1/2\), the single \(N=1\) creation operator
  gives
  \[
    \left(+\frac12,\,0\right).
  \]
  CPT supplies \((0,-1/2)\).  The two helicity-zero states are the particle
  and antiparticle states of one complex scalar, while the
  \(\pm1/2\) states are the two on-shell states described by one Weyl
  fermion and its conjugate.  Thus there are two real bosonic and two
  fermionic on-shell degrees of freedom.

  \SupplementarySolution{22}{The Massless Vector Multiplet}
  The multiplet beginning at \(+1\) contains
  \((+1,+1/2)\).  Its CPT conjugate contains
  \((-1,-1/2)\).  The \(\pm1\) states are the two transverse polarizations
  of a massless vector, and the \(\pm1/2\) states form a Majorana gaugino
  (equivalently, one Weyl gaugino with its conjugate).  Each side has two
  physical polarizations, as supersymmetry requires.

  \SupplementarySolution{23}{The \(N=2\) Hypermultiplet}
  The undoubled exterior-algebra levels are
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|ccc}
    \lambda&+\frac12&0&-\frac12\\ \hline
    \text{multiplicity}&1&2&1 .
  \end{array}
  \]
  Although this helicity table is symmetric, the two scalar states form an
  \(SU(2)_R\) doublet and must be complex.  CPT completion therefore doubles
  the table to multiplicities \(2,4,2\).  It describes two Weyl fermions
  combined into one Dirac fermion and two complex scalars
  \(q^I\), \(I=1,2\), transforming as the fundamental doublet of
  \(SU(2)_R\).  There are four bosonic and four fermionic on-shell degrees
  of freedom.

  \SupplementarySolution{24}{The \(N=2\) Vector Multiplet}
  From helicity \(+1\), the three levels are
  \(+1\) with multiplicity \(1\), \(+1/2\) with multiplicity \(2\), and
  \(0\) with multiplicity \(1\).  Adding CPT gives
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
    \lambda&1&\frac12&0&-\frac12&-1\\ \hline
    \text{multiplicity}&1&2&2&2&1 .
  \end{array}
  \]
  The two fermions at each sign of helicity form an \(SU(2)_R\) doublet.
  The two helicity-zero states make one complex scalar, and the
  \(\pm1\) states make one vector.  The vector plus complex scalar has four
  bosonic degrees of freedom; the two Weyl gauginos have four fermionic
  degrees of freedom.

  \SupplementarySolution{25}{Pascal's Triangle and \(N=4\)}
  Four lowering operators acting on helicity \(+1\) give
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
    \lambda&1&\frac12&0&-\frac12&-1\\ \hline
    \text{multiplicity}&1&4&6&4&1 .
  \end{array}
  \]
  Both the helicities and Pascal multiplicities are symmetric, so no
  separate CPT multiplet is needed.  The vector states are \(SU(4)_R\)
  singlets; the four positive-helicity gauginos transform as the
  \(\mathbf4\), the negative-helicity ones as the
  \(\overline{\mathbf4}\), and the six real scalars transform in
  \(\bigwedge^2\mathbf4=\mathbf6\).  The content is one vector, four Weyl
  fermions, and six real scalars.

  \SupplementarySolution{26}{The \(N=8\) Gravity Multiplet}
  The binomial construction gives
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrr}
    \lambda&2&\frac32&1&\frac12&0&
      -\frac12&-1&-\frac32&-2\\ \hline
    \text{multiplicity}&1&8&28&56&70&56&28&8&1 .
  \end{array}
  \]
  Hence the multiplet contains one graviton, eight gravitini, 28 vectors,
  56 spin-\(1/2\) particles, and 70 real scalar states.  The table is
  invariant under \(\lambda\mapsto-\lambda\), so it is CPT
  self-conjugate.

  \SupplementarySolution{27}{Helicity Cutoffs on Extended SUSY}
  A CPT-self-conjugate multiplet has
  \(\lambda_{\max}=N/4\) and
  \(\lambda_{\min}=-N/4\).  If all helicities satisfy
  \(|\lambda|\leq1\), then \(N/4\leq1\), hence \(N\leq4\).  If gravity is
  allowed but \(|\lambda|\leq2\), the same argument gives \(N\leq8\).
  The first bound concerns perturbative interacting global supersymmetry
  with no massless higher-spin particles.  The second concerns local
  supersymmetry with a graviton but no massless fields above spin two.
  Dropping a particle interpretation or allowing free higher-spin systems
  evades the corresponding physical premise.

  \SupplementarySolution{28}{Why Perturbative \(N=3\) Becomes \(N=4\)}
  The \(N=3\) multiplet beginning at \(+1\) has
  \[
    (1,3,3,1)\quad\text{at}\quad
    \left(1,\frac12,0,-\frac12\right).
  \]
  Its CPT conjugate has the same multiplicities at
  \((+1/2,0,-1/2,-1)\).  Adding them gives
  \[
    (1,4,6,4,1)
  \]
  at helicities \(1,1/2,0,-1/2,-1\), precisely the \(N=4\) vector
  multiplet.  The spectrum therefore supplies the states on which a fourth
  supercharge can act and suggests \(N=4\) enhancement.  The reasoning
  assumes a weakly coupled regime with well-defined one-particle states;
  intrinsically strongly coupled \(N=3\) theories need not admit that
  premise.

  \SupplementarySolution{29}{Helicity Supertraces}
  Expanding near \(t=0\),
  \[
    e^{t\lambda_{\max}}(1-e^{-t/2})^N
      =e^{t\lambda_{\max}}
       \left(\frac t2+O(t^2)\right)^N
      =\frac{t^N}{2^N}+O(t^{N+1}).
  \]
  But the \(m\)th derivative at the origin is exactly
  \(B_m=\sum(-1)^F\lambda^m\).  All derivatives of order \(m<N\) vanish,
  while
  \[
    B_N=\frac{N!}{2^N}.
  \]
  This sign assumes the highest-helicity state is bosonic, as in the given
  generating function; a fermionic Clifford vacuum multiplies every
  supertrace by \(-1\).

  \SupplementarySolution{30}{Parity and the Central-Charge Phase}
  Transforming both supercharges gives
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    P^{-1}\{Q_{\alpha r},Q_{\beta s}\}P
      &=-\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}\epsilon_{\beta\delta}
        \{Q^\dagger_{\dot\gamma r},
          Q^\dagger_{\dot\delta s}\}\\
      &=-\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Z^\dagger_{rs}.
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Comparison with the original algebra therefore yields
  \[
    P^{-1}Z_{rs}P=-Z^\dagger_{rs}.
  \]
  If \(Z_{rs}\) has eigenvalue \(z_{rs}\), parity maps that sector to the
  one with eigenvalue \(-z_{rs}^*\).  A single sector can carry parity
  eigenstates only when it is invariant under this map (for one charge
  block, \(z=-z^*\) in the stated convention); otherwise the parity
  representation must include and exchange the two sectors.  Rephasing
  \(Q_r\) changes the phase of \(Z_{rs}\) twice as fast and simultaneously
  changes the phase in the parity law.  Thus ``purely imaginary'' is
  convention-dependent, while the requirement that parity preserve or
  pair central-charge sectors is invariant.
\end{enumerate}
