\begin{bankssolutions}{Solutions to Implicit Exercises for Appendix E}

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-APPE-001}
\textit{(a) Tensor-product reduction.}
We work over $\mathbb C$ when decomposing representations.  Complexification
does not change the multiplicities relevant here.  Compactness gives complete
reducibility.  Simplicity makes the adjoint representation irreducible.
An invariant positive inner product on the compact real algebra has a
nondegenerate complex-bilinear extension, which identifies
$\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ with its dual.  Consequently,
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_G(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\otimes
  \mathfrak g_{\mathbb C},\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C})
  \simeq (\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}^{\otimes3})^G.
  \tag{E.1}
\]
The exchange of the two input factors splits both sides into symmetric and
antisymmetric sectors.

The precise tensor-product input is the invariant three-tensor theorem for a
complex simple Lie algebra:
\[
  (\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}^{\otimes3})^G
  =(\Lambda^3\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C})^G
   \oplus(S^3\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C})^G.
\]
The standard, mixed-symmetry representation of the permutation group $S_3$
is absent.  A highest-weight decomposition of
$\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g$ proves this theorem.  The root-space raising
equations determine the invariant tensors.  Their alternating sector is
spanned by the Cartan three-form.  The symmetric sector consists of
Weyl-invariant cubics on a Cartan subalgebra.  The standard $S_3$ sector has
zero solutions.  Chevalley restriction extends each cubic uniquely to an
invariant polynomial on $\mathfrak g$.  This is the adjoint-square theorem
underlying Banks's tensor-product argument.

The antisymmetric map is the bracket,
\[
  B(X,Y)=-\ii[X,Y].
\]
It is nonzero and equivariant by the Jacobi identity.  Its lowered tensor is
the Cartan three-form
\[
  F(X,Y,Z)=\langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle,
\]
whose components are proportional to $f^{abc}$.  For a simple Lie algebra the
preceding theorem therefore gives
\[
  [\mathfrak g:\Lambda^2\mathfrak g]=1.
  \tag{E.2}
\]

A totally symmetric invariant trilinear form can be raised with the invariant
inner product to give an equivariant map
$D:S^2\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$.  Polarizing an invariant cubic $p(X)$ gives
\[
  d(X,Y,Z)=\frac{1}{6}
  \left.\frac{\partial^3}{\partial s\,\partial t\,\partial u}
  p(sX+tY+uZ)\right|_{s=t=u=0},
  \tag{E.3}
\]
and raising one index produces such a map $D$.  The converse is the
non-obvious point supplied by the adjoint-square theorem: a symmetric
equivariant map has no mixed-symmetry component after its output index is
lowered.  Thus
\[
  [\mathfrak g:S^2\mathfrak g]
  =\dim(S^3\mathfrak g^*)^G.
  \tag{E.4}
\]
This is the representation-theoretic step behind Banks's statement.  A
nonzero $D$ is onto because its image is an invariant subspace of the
irreducible adjoint.  Its adjoint map embeds one copy of $\mathfrak g$ in
$S^2\mathfrak g$.

\medskip\noindent
\textit{(b) The symmetric copy for $SU(N)$.}
For $SU(N)$ the construction is visible in fundamental matrices.  Regard
the compact algebra in the physicists' convention as the real span of
traceless Hermitian matrices, and set
\[
  D(X,Y)=\{X,Y\}-\frac{2}{N}\operatorname{tr}_F(XY)\,\mathbf1_N.
  \tag{E.5}
\]
This matrix is traceless and Hermitian.  Conjugation by $U\in SU(N)$ gives
\[
  D(UXU^\dagger,UYU^\dagger)=U D(X,Y)U^\dagger,
\]
so $D$ is an equivariant symmetric map.  It is nonzero for $N\geq3$.  For
example, the square of
$\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)$ has a traceless part.  Every traceless
Hermitian $2\times2$ matrix is a real linear combination of Pauli matrices,
and their anticommutator is proportional to the identity.  Formula (E.5)
therefore vanishes for $SU(2)$.

The matrices $\mathbf1_N,T^c$ form an orthogonal basis of all Hermitian
matrices.  Taking the trace fixes the scalar part of their product:
\[
  T^aT^b=\frac{1}{2N}\delta^{ab}\mathbf1_N+C^{ab}{}_cT^c.
\]
The commutator fixes the antisymmetric part of $C$, and (E.5) fixes its
symmetric part.  With the normalization in the question one obtains
\[
  \{T^a,T^b\}=\frac1N\delta^{ab}\mathbf1_N+d^{abc}T^c,
  \qquad
  T^aT^b=\frac{1}{2N}\delta^{ab}\mathbf1_N
  +\frac12\bigl(d^{abc}+\ii f^{abc}\bigr)T^c.
  \tag{E.6}
\]
Cyclicity of the trace makes $d^{abc}$ totally symmetric.  Under the
commutator and trace conventions stated earlier in Appendix E, (E.6) is the
coefficient-explicit version of the product displayed on printed page 258.
Taking the commutator of (E.6) fixes the factor $1/2$ multiplying
$\ii f^{abc}$.  With the Chapter 8 normalization of $d^{abc}$ used here, the
same factor multiplies $d^{abc}$, and the scalar term multiplies
$\mathbf1_N$.  These factors are absent from the printed display.

\medskip\noindent
\textit{(c) Classification and low-rank checks.}
Uniqueness follows from the classification of invariant polynomials.  The
Chevalley restriction theorem says that $(S\mathfrak g^*)^G$ is freely
generated by homogeneous polynomials whose degrees are the exponents plus
one.  The relevant degree lists are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
  A_r & 2,3,\ldots,r+1\\
  B_r,\ C_r & 2,4,6,\ldots,2r\\
  D_r\ (r\geq4) & 2,4,6,\ldots,2r-2,\ r\\
  G_2 & 2,6\\
  F_4 & 2,6,8,12\\
  E_6 & 2,5,6,8,9,12\\
  E_7 & 2,6,8,10,12,14,18\\
  E_8 & 2,8,12,14,18,20,24,30.
\end{array}
\tag{E.7}
\]
In the $D_r$ row the final degree $r$ is counted again when it already occurs
in the even sequence; this records the second basic invariant of that degree.
A simple algebra has no invariant linear polynomial, so a homogeneous cubic
can only arise from a basic generator of degree three.  Formula (E.7) gives
\[
  \dim(S^3\mathfrak g^*)^G=
  \begin{cases}
    1,&\mathfrak g=A_r,\quad r\geq2,\\
    0,&\text{all other simple types}.
  \end{cases}
  \tag{E.8}
\]
For $A_r=\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}$ the generator is
$p_3(X)=\operatorname{tr}_F(X^3)$, whose polarization is proportional to the
tensor in (E.6).  Hence $SU(N)$ has one symmetric adjoint for $N\geq3$.
The algebra $A_1\simeq\mathfrak{su}(2)$ has only its quadratic invariant.
Combining this result with (E.2), the adjoint-square decomposition contains
\[
  \mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g\ \supset\
  \begin{cases}
    \mathfrak g_A\oplus\mathfrak g_S,
      &\mathfrak g=A_r,\quad r\geq2,\\
    \mathfrak g_A,
      &\text{every other complex simple type},
  \end{cases}
\]
with each displayed adjoint occurring once; the subscripts record the
exchange symmetry of the two tensor factors.  This is the decomposition
claim on printed page 258.

The assertion concerns Lie-algebra type, which resolves the low-rank names.
In particular,
$\mathfrak{so}(6)\simeq\mathfrak{su}(4)=A_3$ carries the cubic tensor.
In rank notation,
$\mathfrak{so}(3)\simeq\mathfrak{sp}(1)\simeq\mathfrak{su}(2)=A_1$;
Banks calls the corresponding matrix group $Sp(2)$.  These presentations lie
in the cubic-free case.  For the remaining
orthogonal, symplectic, and exceptional simple algebras, (E.8) sets every
invariant symmetric rank-three tensor to zero.
$\mathfrak{so}(5)\simeq\mathfrak{sp}(2)$ stays in that class; Banks calls the
corresponding symplectic group $Sp(4)$.
$\mathfrak{so}(4)\simeq\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ lies outside
the simple-algebra assumption.

The familiar $SU(3)$ Clebsch--Gordan series gives a direct check:
\[
  \boldsymbol8\otimes\boldsymbol8
  =\bigl(\boldsymbol1\oplus\boldsymbol8\oplus\boldsymbol{27}\bigr)_S
   \oplus
   \bigl(\boldsymbol8\oplus\boldsymbol{10}
          \oplus\overline{\boldsymbol{10}}\bigr)_A.
  \tag{E.9}
\]
The projectors onto the symmetric and antisymmetric copies of
$\boldsymbol8$ are built from $d^{abc}$ and $f^{abc}$, respectively.  The
dimension check $1+8+27+8+10+10=64$ verifies the full decomposition.

\textit{(d) Representation dependence and the anomaly coefficient.}
Now let $R$ be any finite-dimensional unitary representation whose Hermitian
generators obey the same commutation relations.  Cyclicity gives
\[
  \mathcal D_R^{abc}
  =2\operatorname{tr}_R\!\left(T_R^a\{T_R^b,T_R^c\}\right)
  =\mathcal D_R^{(abc)}.
  \tag{E.10}
\]
Its invariance follows by taking the trace of a commutator:
\[
  f^{dae}\mathcal D_R^{ebc}
  +f^{dbe}\mathcal D_R^{aec}
  +f^{dce}\mathcal D_R^{abe}=0.
  \tag{E.11}
\]
For type $A_r$ with $r\geq2$, the one-dimensional result (E.8) implies
\[
  \mathcal D_R^{abc}=A(R)d^{abc},
  \qquad d^{abc}=\mathcal D_F^{abc},
  \qquad A(F)=1.
  \tag{E.12}
\]
All other simple types give $\mathcal D_R^{abc}=0$ in every representation.
If $H$ is one Lie-algebra element with
$\operatorname{tr}_F(H_F^3)\ne0$, setting all three indices equal in (E.12)
yields Banks's one-generator rule,
\[
  A(R)=\frac{\operatorname{tr}_R(H_R^3)}
             {\operatorname{tr}_F(H_F^3)}.
  \tag{E.13}
\]
The same algebra element and generator normalization must be used in both
traces.

Banks's anomaly discussion in Chapter 8 has the group factor
\[
  D_\mu\langle J^{\mu a}\rangle
  \ \propto\ A(R)d^{abc}
  G^b{}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde G^{c\,\mu\nu}.
  \tag{E.14}
\]
The spacetime anomaly equation is four-dimensional, $D=4$.  With
$\epsilon^{0123}=+1$, the Hodge dual in (E.14) is
\[
  \widetilde G^{\mu\nu}
  :=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}G_{\rho\sigma}.
\]
This definition and the corresponding anomaly density are specific to
$D=4$; the group-theory tensor $d^{abc}$ above is independent of that
specialization.
With Hermitian generators, $\mathcal D_R^{abc}$ and $A(R)$ in (E.10)--(E.12)
are real, and the raw trace on printed page 258 is
$\tfrac12 A(R)d^{abc}$ in this normalization.  The factor $\ii$ printed in
that trace equation is incompatible with the Hermitian-generator convention
and with the reality of $A(R)$; it is a source error.  A Euclidean anomaly
equation may carry an overall $\ii$ from spacetime conventions, as the later
calculation in Chapter 8 does.  Its group-theory trace remains real.
Rescaling the reference tensor changes the real numerical factor only.

For the conjugate representation,
$T_{\overline R}^a=-(T_R^a)^*$, and (E.10) gives
\[
  A(\overline R)=-A(R).
  \tag{E.15}
\]
Every real or pseudoreal representation is equivalent to its conjugate, so
its coefficient vanishes.  The adjoint representation is real, which gives
$A(\mathrm{adj})=0$ even for $SU(N)$.  This also follows from its weights:
each root $\alpha$ is paired with $-\alpha$, and their contributions to
$\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{adj}}H^3$ cancel pairwise.
