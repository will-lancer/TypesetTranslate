\BanksSolution{7.1}
Let $\rho=\frac12\phi^a\phi^a$.  The most general parity-even
$O(n)$-invariant scalar Lagrangian with at most two derivatives can be
written
\[
 \mathcal L=-\frac12F(\rho)\,\partial_\mu\phi^a\partial^\mu\phi^a
 -\frac12G(\rho)
 (\phi^a\partial_\mu\phi^a)(\phi^b\partial^\mu\phi^b)-U(\rho).
\]
The particular functions $F$ and $G$ are immaterial for the current.  With
$\Pi_a{}^\mu=\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)$, an infinitesimal
rotation $\delta\phi^a=\omega^{ab}\phi^b$, with
$\omega^{ab}=-\omega^{ba}$, gives
\[
 J_{ab}{}^\mu=\Pi_a{}^\mu\phi_b-\Pi_b{}^\mu\phi_a
 =-F(\rho)\bigl(\partial^\mu\phi_a\,\phi_b
 -\partial^\mu\phi_b\,\phi_a\bigr).
\]
The $G$ term cancels because it is radial in field space.  The equations of
motion imply $\partial_\mu J_{ab}{}^\mu=0$.  This expression is also the
general Noether formula
\[
 J^\mu=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}
 \,\delta\phi^a.
\]

For $U(n)$ let $\phi$ be a complex fundamental and set
$\rho=\phi^\dagger\phi$.  A convenient way to display the general result is
to note that all scalar coefficients can be arbitrary functions of $\rho$,
while the two-derivative invariants are made from
$\partial_\mu\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\phi$,
$(\phi^\dagger\partial_\mu\phi)(\partial^\mu\phi^\dagger\phi)$, and the
real and imaginary parts of $(\phi^\dagger\partial_\mu\phi)^2$.  The second
real invariant,
\[
 \operatorname{Im}\bigl[(\phi^\dagger\partial_\mu\phi)^2\bigr]
 =\frac{1}{2\ii}\left[
 (\phi^\dagger\partial_\mu\phi)^2
 -(\partial_\mu\phi^\dagger\phi)^2\right],
\]
is odd under CP and is retained unless CP is imposed explicitly.  For a
Hermitian generator $T_A$ of $U(n)$, the transformation
$\delta\phi=\ii\omega^A T_A\phi$ gives the conserved current
\begin{equation*}
 J_A^\mu=
 \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}
 \,\ii(T_A)^i{}_{j}\phi_j
 -\ii\phi_j^*(T_A)^j{}_{i}
 \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i^*)}.
\end{equation*}
For the canonical kinetic term this reduces to
\[
 J_A^\mu=-\ii\left[(\partial^\mu\phi)^\dagger T_A\phi
 -\phi^\dagger T_A\partial^\mu\phi\right].
\]
The same formula, with the actual canonical momenta, applies to every
choice of the invariant functions.

Now take $N_f$ massless Dirac fields in a representation $R$ of a group
$G$.  In this chiral and anomaly discussion $D=4$; use
$\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=-2\eta^{\mu\nu}$,
$\gamma_5=\ii\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$, and
$P_{L,R}=(1\mp\gamma_5)/2$,
\[
 \mathcal L=-\ii\bar\psi_L^I\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi_L^I
 -\ii\bar\psi_R^I\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi_R^I .
\]
The two chiralities can be rotated independently:
\[
 \psi_L^I\longrightarrow (U_L)^I{}_{J}\psi_L^J,
 \qquad
 \psi_R^I\longrightarrow (U_R)^I{}_{J}\psi_R^J,
 \qquad U_L,U_R\in U(N_f).
\]
There is no mass term to link the two transformations.  The vector
interaction also respects them, because
\[
 A_\mu^a\bar\psi\gamma^\mu t_R^a\psi
 =A_\mu^a\left(\bar\psi_L\gamma^\mu t_R^a\psi_L
 +\bar\psi_R\gamma^\mu t_R^a\psi_R\right)
\]
and $t_R^a$ acts on $R$ while $U_{L,R}$ act on flavor.

The vector flavor current and all non-singlet chiral currents remain
conserved when the measure is regulated in a symmetry-preserving way.  The
singlet axial transformation $U_L=e^{\ii\alpha}$,
$U_R=e^{-\ii\alpha}$ has current
$j_5^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\psi$ and is anomalous.  In $D=4$, with
$\operatorname{tr}_R(t^at^b)=T(R)\delta^{ab}$ and
$\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}$ and
$\epsilon^{0123}=+1$,
\[
 \partial_\mu j_5^\mu
 =\frac{N_f g^2}{8\pi^2}\,
 T(R)\,F_{\mu\nu}{}^a\widetilde F^{a\mu\nu}.
\]
The coefficient is written in a convention in which $g$ is in
$D_\mu$; moving $g$ into the gauge field moves it out of this formula.
For a non-singlet flavor generator the flavor trace vanishes, which removes
this gauge anomaly.

\BanksSolution{7.2}
Let
\[
 D_\mu\phi_i=(\partial_\mu-\ii e q_iA_\mu)\phi_i,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L=-\sum_i(D_\mu\phi_i)^*D^\mu\phi_i
 -V(\phi,\phi^*)-\frac14F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}.
\]
For any fields $\Phi_A=(\phi_i,\phi_i^*,A_\rho)$, the passive translation
variation $\delta\Phi_A=-f^\nu\partial_\nu\Phi_A$ gives
\[
 T_{\rm can}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
 =\sum_i\left[(D^\mu\phi_i)^*\partial_\nu\phi_i
 +(D^\mu\phi_i)\partial_\nu\phi_i^*\right]
 +F^{\mu\rho}\partial_\nu A_\rho
 +\delta^\mu{}_\nu\mathcal L.
\]
The equations of motion imply
$\partial_\mu T_{\rm can}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=0$.  It is useful to replace
ordinary derivatives by covariant ones and use the Maxwell equation.  This
gives the gauge-invariant tensor
\[
 T_{\rm imp}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
 =\sum_i\left[(D^\mu\phi_i)^*D_\nu\phi_i
 +(D^\mu\phi_i)(D_\nu\phi_i)^*\right]
 +F^{\mu\rho}F_{\nu\rho}
 +\delta^\mu{}_\nu\mathcal L,
\]
which differs from the canonical tensor by a divergence and terms
proportional to the Maxwell equation.  The divergence integrates to a
surface term.  Consequently both tensors give the same translation charge
when the fields decrease sufficiently fast.

The standard metric prescription gives the symmetric Hilbert tensor
\[
 T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm H}
 =-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
 \qquad
 T_{\rm H}^{\mu\nu}
 =\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}},
\]
with the second equality understood after converting the metric variation.
The source's displayed definition has an overall factor-of-two omission:
its $1/\sqrt{-g}$ must be $2/\sqrt{-g}$ for the standard stress tensor used
below.  Varying
\[
 S=\int\dd^D x\sqrt{-g}\left[
 -\sum_i g^{\rho\sigma}(D_\rho\phi_i)^*D_\sigma\phi_i
 -V-\frac14g^{\rho\lambda}g^{\sigma\kappa}
 F_{\rho\sigma}F_{\lambda\kappa}\right]
\]
with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$ produces, after setting
$g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}$, the following expression:
\begin{align*}
 T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm H}
 ={}&\sum_i\left[(D_\mu\phi_i)^*D_\nu\phi_i
 +(D_\nu\phi_i)^*D_\mu\phi_i\right]\\
 &+F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho+\eta_{\mu\nu}\mathcal L.
\end{align*}
The canonical and Hilbert time components
obey, on shell,
\[
 T_{\rm can}^{0}{}_{\nu}-T_{\rm H}^{0}{}_{\nu}
 =\partial_i X^{i0}{}_{\nu}
 +\text{terms proportional to the field equations}
\]
for a local $X$ built from $A_\nu$, $F^{i0}$, and the scalar fields.
Therefore
\[
 P_\nu=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\,T_{\rm can}^{0}{}_{\nu}
 =\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\,T_{\rm H}^{0}{}_{\nu}
\]
under the stated boundary condition.  The index placement only changes the
corresponding metric signs.

Using the symmetric tensor, define
\[
 M_{\mu\nu\lambda}
 =x_\mu T_{\nu\lambda}^{\rm H}
 -x_\nu T_{\mu\lambda}^{\rm H}.
\]
Then
\[
 \partial^\lambda M_{\mu\nu\lambda}
 =T_{\nu\mu}^{\rm H}-T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm H}
 +x_\mu\partial^\lambda T_{\nu\lambda}^{\rm H}
 -x_\nu\partial^\lambda T_{\mu\lambda}^{\rm H}=0.
\]
The charges
 $J_{\mu\nu}=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\,M_{\mu\nu0}$ are time-independent.  Their
equal-time commutators generate Lorentz transformations.  On a scalar,
\[
 [J_{\mu\nu},\phi(x)]
 =\ii(x_\mu\partial_\nu-x_\nu\partial_\mu)\phi(x),
\]
and on $A_\rho$ there is in addition the vector-index term
$\ii(\eta_{\rho\mu}A_\nu-\eta_{\rho\nu}A_\mu)$.  Thus these are the
boost and rotation generators.

\BanksSolution{7.3}
Work in $D=4$ as in the problem.  For a symmetric conserved tensor, a current
of the required form is
$j_\nu=f^\mu T_{\mu\nu}$.  Its divergence is
\[
 \partial^\nu j_\nu=(\partial^\nu f^\mu)T_{\mu\nu}.
\]
Only the symmetric part of $\partial_\nu f_\mu$ contributes.  The linear
polynomial $f^\mu=Cx^\mu$ gives
\[
 \partial_\nu f_\mu=C\eta_{\mu\nu},
 \qquad
 \partial^\nu j_\nu=C\,T^\mu{}_\mu=0.
\]

Let $b^\mu$ be a constant vector and choose
\[
 f^\mu=b^\mu x^2-2x^\mu(b\cdot x).
\]
It has the quadratic coefficient
\[
 C^\mu{}_{\nu\kappa}
 =b^\mu\eta_{\nu\kappa}
 -b_\nu\delta^\mu{}_\kappa
 -b_\kappa\delta^\mu{}_\nu,
 \qquad C^\mu{}_{\nu\kappa}=C^\mu{}_{\kappa\nu}.
\]
The derivative obeys
\[
 \partial_\nu f_\mu+\partial_\mu f_\nu
 =-4(b\cdot x)\eta_{\mu\nu},
 \qquad
 \partial_\rho f^\rho=-8(b\cdot x).
\]
Hence
\[
 (\partial^\nu f^\mu)T_{\mu\nu}
 =-2(b\cdot x)T^\mu{}_\mu=0.
\]
The one constant $C$ and the four components of $b^\mu$ are the requested
five parameters.  The corresponding charges can be taken as
\[
 D=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\,x^\mu T_{0\mu},
 \qquad
 K_\rho=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\,
 \left(x^2\delta_\rho{}^\mu-2x_\rho x^\mu\right)T_{0\mu},
\]
up to an overall sign convention for the generators.  Along with
$P_\mu$ and $M_{\mu\nu}$, these are the dilatation and special-conformal
charges.  Their commutators close on $\mathfrak{so}(2,4)$; for example,
with one common Hermitian-generator convention,
\[
 [D,P_\mu]=\ii P_\mu,\qquad [D,K_\mu]=-\ii K_\mu,\qquad
 [K_\mu,P_\nu]=2\ii(\eta_{\mu\nu}D-M_{\mu\nu}).
\]

\BanksSolution{7.4}
The isospin transformation of the pion triplet is
\[
 \delta\pi^a=\omega^b\epsilon^{bac}\pi^c.
\]
The Ward identity obtained by making $\omega^b(x)$ local states that the
two-point function is an invariant rank-two tensor:
\[
 \langle0|T\pi^a(x)\pi^b(y)|0\rangle
 =\delta^{ab}G(x-y).
\]
The same scalar function $G$ multiplies every component.  Its
Lehmann representation therefore has the same pole location for every
$a$, and LSZ gives
\[
 m_{\pi^1}=m_{\pi^2}=m_{\pi^3}.
\]

For four triplets, the invariant rank-four tensors are the three pairwise
contractions.  Thus, before using permutations,
\begin{align*}
 G^{a_1a_2a_3a_4}(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
 ={}&\delta^{a_1a_2}\delta^{a_3a_4}F_1(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\\
 &+\delta^{a_1a_3}\delta^{a_2a_4}F_2(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\\
 &+\delta^{a_1a_4}\delta^{a_2a_3}F_3(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4).
\end{align*}
Permutation invariance relates the coefficient functions.  For example,
interchanging the second and third pion gives
$F_2(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=F_1(x_1,x_3,x_2,x_4)$, and the other relations follow
by the same operation.  Consequently one scalar amplitude function and its
crossed arguments describe the full tensor:
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal M_{ab;cd}(s,t,u)
 =\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}A(s,t,u)
 +\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}A(t,s,u)
 +\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}A(u,t,s).
\end{equation*}
The LSZ residues are common because the pion masses and wave-function
normalizations are common.  A convenient pair of independent physical
charge amplitudes is
\[
 \mathcal M(\pi^+\pi^-\to\pi^0\pi^0)=A(s,t,u),
 \qquad
 \mathcal M(\pi^+\pi^-\to\pi^+\pi^-)
 =A(s,t,u)+A(t,s,u).
\]
Every other charge channel is obtained from these by isospin, charge
conjugation, and crossing.  Equivalently, the full answer is one invariant
function with its required permutations; at fixed physical charge
kinematics the two displayed channels are a convenient independent pair.

\BanksSolution{7.5}
This chiral QCD calculation is four-dimensional, $D=4$, with
$\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=-2\eta^{\mu\nu}$,
$\gamma_5=\ii\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$, and
$\slashed p=\gamma^\mu p_\mu$.  Write $t^a=\tau^a/2$ and define the
Maurer--Cartan one-forms
\[
 L_\mu=g_L^{-1}\partial_\mu g_L,\qquad
 R_\mu=g_R^{-1}\partial_\mu g_R,\qquad
 B_\mu=L_\mu-R_\mu.
\]
Under a global transformation
$g_L\to V_L^\dagger g_L$, $g_R\to V_R^\dagger g_R$, both one-forms are
invariant.  Under the local identification
$g_{L,R}\to g_{L,R}h(x)$,
\[
 L_\mu\longrightarrow h^{-1}(L_\mu+\partial_\mu)h,
 \qquad
 R_\mu\longrightarrow h^{-1}(R_\mu+\partial_\mu)h.
\]
Thus either is an $SU_V(2)$ gauge potential.  The combinations
\[
 \mathcal V_\mu=\frac12(L_\mu+R_\mu),\qquad
 \mathcal A_\mu=\ii(R_\mu-L_\mu)
\]
are respectively an anti-Hermitian vector connection and a Hermitian axial
one-form.  Parity exchanges $L_\mu$ and $R_\mu$, so the parity-even nucleon
kinetic term uses $\mathcal V_\mu$, while the parity-odd one-form couples to
the axial current.  The leading Lagrangian is
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal L=
 +f_\pi^2\operatorname{Tr}(B_\mu B^\mu)
 +\bar N\left[-\ii\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu-\mathcal V_\mu)-m_N\right]N
 +g_A\bar N\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\mathcal A_\mu N.
\end{equation*}
The first term is the most general two-derivative pion term at this order;
terms with more derivatives are higher-order effective interactions.  The
only independent one-derivative nucleon coupling besides the connection is
the dimensionless axial coefficient $g_A$.

For global currents, set
$X_L^a=g_L^{-1}t^ag_L$ and $X_R^a=g_R^{-1}t^ag_R$.  With the convention
$\delta g_{L,R}=-\ii\alpha_{L,R}^at^ag_{L,R}$, one has
$\delta L_\mu=-\ii(\partial_\mu\alpha_L^a)X_L^a$ and the analogous
right-handed expression.  Reading the coefficient of
$\partial_\mu\alpha^a$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal J_L^{a\mu}
 ={}&-2\ii f_\pi^2\operatorname{Tr}(X_L^aB^\mu)
 +\frac12\bar N\gamma^\mu X_L^aN
 -g_A\bar N\gamma^\mu\gamma_5X_L^aN,\\
 \mathcal J_R^{a\mu}
 ={}&+2\ii f_\pi^2\operatorname{Tr}(X_R^aB^\mu)
 +\frac12\bar N\gamma^\mu X_R^aN
 +g_A\bar N\gamma^\mu\gamma_5X_R^aN.
\end{align*}
Changing the sign or normalization used to define the infinitesimal
generators changes these Noether currents by the corresponding common
convention.  In the weak-current formulas below, $J_{L,R}$ denotes the
conventional QCD half-current normalization.

Now make the local transformation $h=g_R^{-1}$.  Then
\[
 \Sigma=g_Lg_R^{-1},\qquad g_R'=1,\qquad g_L'=\Sigma,\qquad
 N'=h^{-1}N=g_RN,
\]
and
\[
 \Sigma\longrightarrow V_L^\dagger\Sigma V_R.
\]
The Lagrangian becomes
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L={}&-f_\pi^2\operatorname{Tr}(\partial_\mu\Sigma^\dagger\partial^\mu\Sigma)\\
 &+\bar N\left[-\ii\gamma^\mu
 \left(\partial_\mu-\frac12\Sigma^\dagger\partial_\mu\Sigma\right)
 -m_N\right]N\\
 &-\ii g_A\bar N\gamma^\mu\gamma_5
 \Sigma^\dagger\partial_\mu\Sigma\,N.
\end{align*}
The last line is understood as a matrix product acting on $N$.  In a
coordinate choice in which the compensating vector connection starts at
quadratic order, the linear pion terms are
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L_\pi&=-\frac12\partial_\mu\pi^a\partial^\mu\pi^a+\cdots,\\
 \mathcal L_{\pi N}
 &=\frac{g_A}{2f_\pi}\partial_\mu\pi^a\,
 \bar N\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\tau^aN+\cdots,\\
 \Sigma&=\exp\left(\frac{\ii\tau^a\pi^a}{2f_\pi}\right).
\end{align*}
The equivalent expression in the displayed gauge contains the same
coupling after the corresponding local field redefinition of $N$.

The leading pion--nucleon vertex in the nonrelativistic limit is
\[
 \mathcal V_{\pi NN}
 =-\frac{g_A}{f_\pi}\,t^a\,
 \boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf q,
\]
where $\mathbf q$ is the pion momentum entering the vertex.  Therefore
single-pion exchange between two nucleons has momentum-space amplitude
\[
 \mathcal M_\pi(\mathbf q)
 =-\frac{g_A^2}{f_\pi^2}
 \frac{(\boldsymbol\sigma_1\cdot\mathbf q)
 (\boldsymbol\sigma_2\cdot\mathbf q)\,
 (t_1^at_2^a)}
 {\mathbf q^2+m_\pi^2},
\]
up to the overall sign convention for the potential.  Its coordinate-space
form is
\[
 V_\pi(\mathbf r)
 =\frac{g_A^2}{f_\pi^2}\,
 (t_1^at_2^a)(\boldsymbol\sigma_1\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla)
 (\boldsymbol\sigma_2\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla)
 \frac{\ee^{-m_\pi r}}{4\pi r}.
\]
In the exact chiral limit $m_\pi=0$.  This shows that the long-range force
is fixed by $g_A$ and $f_\pi$; short-distance contact terms contain further
effective-theory coefficients.  Expanding the pion-only term fixes every
leading pion--pion vertex in terms of $f_\pi$, so pion scattering at this
order depends only on $f_\pi$.

Rescaling the global generator parameters only fixes the normalization of
the associated currents.  For the conventional QCD normalization, let $V$
and $A$ denote the full
vector and axial currents.  The chiral currents are their half-currents:
\[
 J_L^{a\mu}=\frac12\bigl(V^{a\mu}-A^{a\mu}\bigr),\qquad
 J_R^{a\mu}=\frac12\bigl(V^{a\mu}+A^{a\mu}\bigr).
\]
To the order needed here,
\[
 V^{a\mu}=\bar N\gamma^\mu t^aN
 +\epsilon^{abc}\pi^b\partial^\mu\pi^c+\cdots,
 \qquad
 A^{a\mu}=f_\pi\partial^\mu\pi^a
 +g_A\bar N\gamma^\mu\gamma_5t^aN+\cdots,
\]
with the overall signs fixed by the choice of broken-generator parameters.
Define $X^\pm=X^1\pm\ii X^2$.  The charged weak interaction is
\[
 \mathcal L_W=-\frac{g_W}{\sqrt2}
 \left(W_\mu^+J_L^{-\mu}+W_\mu^-J_L^{+\mu}\right),
 \qquad
 \frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}=\frac{g_W^2}{8m_W^2},
\]
where $W^\pm=(W^1\mp\ii W^2)/\sqrt2$.  Each chiral fermion current
contains $P_L=(1-\gamma_5)/2$, so the product of two $g_W/\sqrt2$
vertices gives the standard relation shown above.  Set the two-flavor CKM
factor to one.  Isospin gives
\[
 \langle p|J_L^{+\mu}|n\rangle
 =\frac12\bar u_p\gamma^\mu(1-g_A\gamma_5)u_n
 +O(q^\mu,\text{higher derivatives}).
\]
At energies below $m_W$, the neutron-decay amplitude is therefore
\[
 \mathcal M(n\to p e^-\bar\nu_e)
 =\frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}
 \bigl[\bar u_p\gamma^\mu(1-g_A\gamma_5)u_n\bigr]
 \bigl[\bar u_e\gamma_\mu(1-\gamma_5)v_{\nu}\bigr].
\]
Choose the charged-state convention
$\langle0|A^{+\mu}|\pi^-(p)\rangle=-\ii f_\pi p^\mu$; any $\sqrt2$ from
the definition of charged fields is included in this definition of
$f_\pi$.  Since the vector current has no vacuum-to-pion matrix element,
\[
 \langle0|J_L^{+\mu}|\pi^-(p)\rangle
 =-\frac12\langle0|A^{+\mu}|\pi^-(p)\rangle
 =\frac{\ii}{2}f_\pi p^\mu,
\]
up to the phase convention for the charged pion.  Hence
\[
 \mathcal M(\pi^-\to\mu^-\bar\nu_\mu)
 =\frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}f_\pi p_\mu
 \bigl[\bar u_\mu\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma_5)v_\nu\bigr]
 =\frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}f_\pi m_\mu
 \bigl[\bar u_\mu(1-\gamma_5)v_\nu\bigr].
\]
The last equality uses $p=k_\mu+k_\nu$ and the lepton Dirac equations.  As
a check, neglecting radiative corrections,
\[
 \Gamma(\pi^-\to\ell^-\bar\nu_\ell)
 =\frac{G_F^2f_\pi^2m_\ell^2m_\pi}{8\pi}
 \left(1-\frac{m_\ell^2}{m_\pi^2}\right)^2.
\]

\BanksSolution{7.6}
Let $T^a$ be generators with
$[T^a,T^b]=\ii f^{abc}T^c$, and define the source transformation
\[
 \delta_\omega A_\mu
 =\partial_\mu\omega+\ii[\omega,A_\mu],
 \qquad \omega=\omega^aT^a.
\]
Changing variables in the current functional integral gives
$\delta_\omega W(A)=0$.  In components this is
\[
0=\int\dd^D x\,
 \frac{\delta W}{\delta A_\mu^a(x)}
 \left[\partial_\mu\omega^a
 + f^{abc}A_\mu^b\omega^c\right].
\]
After integration by parts, the non-abelian Ward identities are
\begin{equation*}
 \left(D_\mu\frac{\delta W}{\delta A_\mu}\right)^a
 \equiv\partial_\mu\frac{\delta W}{\delta A_\mu^a}
 +f^{abc}A_\mu^b\frac{\delta W}{\delta A_\mu^c}=0,
\end{equation*}
with the sign of $f^{abc}$ following the convention in the first line.
Differentiating this identity with respect to $A$ generates the current
Ward identities and their contact terms.  Conversely, integrating all of
those identities against an arbitrary $\omega(x)$ gives the gauge
invariance of $W$.

Interpret the non-standard transform in the question as a functional
stationary relation,
\[
 W[A]=\Gamma[a]+M^2\int\dd^D x\,
 (A_\mu^a-a_\mu^a)(A^{a\mu}-a^{a\mu}),
\]
where $a$ is chosen by extremizing the right side with respect to $a$.  The
variation with respect to $A$ and $a$ gives
\[
 \frac{\delta W}{\delta A_\mu^a}
 =2M^2(A^{a\mu}-a^{a\mu}),
 \qquad
 \frac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta a_\mu^a}
 =2M^2(A^{a\mu}-a^{a\mu}).
\]
Thus
\[
 a_\mu^a=A_\mu^a-\frac1{2M^2}\frac{\delta W}{\delta A^{a\mu}}.
\]
The connected expansion follows directly from
$\exp(\ii W[A])=\langle\exp(\ii\int J\cdot A)\rangle$:
\[
 W[A]=\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{\ii^{\,n-1}}{n!}
 \int\prod_{r=1}^n\dd^D x_r\,
 G_{c\,\mu_1\cdots\mu_n}{}^{a_1\cdots a_n}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
 \prod_{r=1}^nA_{\mu_r}^{a_r}(x_r).
\]
The coefficient of $A^n$ is the connected $n$-current Green function.
Expanding the stationary equation recursively expresses $a[A]$ in these
connected functions.  Substitution into the transform removes one-particle
reducible chains, so the Taylor coefficients of $\Gamma$ are the
one-particle-irreducible current vertices with the external two-point
kernel fixed by $M^2$.

The Ward identity says that $\delta W/\delta A$ transforms covariantly as
the inhomogeneous part of a connection.  Hence
$a=A-(2M^2)^{-1}\delta W/\delta A$ transforms as a connection too, while
$A-a$ transforms homogeneously.  The transform is consequently invariant:
\[
 \Gamma[a^U]=\Gamma[a].
\]
At momenta much smaller than the heavy scales, locality and gauge invariance
give the Yang--Mills expansion
\[
 \Gamma[a]=-\frac1{4g_V^2}\int\dd^D x\,
 F_{\mu\nu}{}^a(a)F^{a\mu\nu}
 +\text{higher-derivative gauge invariants},
\]
where
\[
 F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu a_\nu-\partial_\nu a_\mu
 +\ii[a_\mu,a_\nu].
\]

If the symmetry is spontaneously broken to $H$, introduce a coset
representative $g(x)$ and gauge the left $G$ action.  With $a_\mu$ the
source connection and $A_\mu$ the compensating $H$ connection,
\[
 \mathcal D_\mu g=\partial_\mu g-a_\mu g+gA_\mu,
 \qquad
 J_\mu=(\mathcal D_\mu g)g^{-1}.
\]
The gauge-invariant NGB term is
\[
 \Gamma_{\rm NGB}[a,g]
 =f^2\int\dd^D x\,\operatorname{Tr}(J_\mu J^\mu),
\]
with $A_\mu$ eliminated algebraically or retained as the local $H$
redundancy.  For the chiral example it is equivalent, after choosing
$\Sigma$, to
\[
 \mathcal L_\pi
 =-f_\pi^2\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(D_\mu\Sigma^\dagger D^\mu\Sigma\bigr),
 \qquad
 D_\mu\Sigma=\partial_\mu\Sigma+\ii a_{L\mu}\Sigma
 -\ii\Sigma a_{R\mu}.
\]
At the vacuum $\Sigma=1$ this contains the mass term
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm NGB}^{(2)}
 =-f_\pi^2\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[(a_{L\mu}-a_{R\mu})
 (a_L^\mu-a_R^\mu)\bigr]
\]
up to the normalization of the gauge generators.  If
$a_{L,R}=a_{L,R}^at^a$ and the Yang--Mills fields are canonically
normalized, the induced mass matrix is
\[
 (\Delta M^2)_{ab}
 =2f_\pi^2\,\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[(T_L^a-T_R^a)
 (T_L^b-T_R^b)\bigr],
\]
with the factor of two absorbed if the mass term is written as
$-\frac12a_\mu^a(\Delta M^2)_{ab}a^{b\mu}$.  More invariantly, for a vacuum
vector $v$,
\[
 (\Delta M^2)_{ab}=f^2(T^av)\cdot(T^bv)
\]
in the normalization $\mathcal L\supset-\frac12(D_\mu v)^2$.  The complete
mass matrix is
\[
 (M_{\rm vector}^2)_{ab}=M^2_{ab}+(\Delta M^2)_{ab}.
\]
For the canonically normalized left and right triplets, the chiral
example makes the entries explicit:
\[
 \Delta M^2_{(L,a)(L,b)}=f_\pi^2\delta_{ab},\qquad
 \Delta M^2_{(L,a)(R,b)}=-f_\pi^2\delta_{ab},\qquad
 \Delta M^2_{(R,a)(R,b)}=f_\pi^2\delta_{ab}.
\]
Thus the vector eigencombination has zero NGB mass contribution and the
orthogonal axial combination has eigenvalue $2f_\pi^2$, before any gauge
coupling rescaling.
Only broken generators have a nonzero NGB contribution.  In
$SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\to SU(2)_V$, the axial combination
$a_A=(a_L-a_R)/2$ receives the pion contribution, while the vector
combination $a_V=(a_L+a_R)/2$ does not.  The latter can still have the
bare vector mass from $\Gamma$.  The isotriplet vector fields are the
$\rho$ mesons and the axial fields are the $A_1$ mesons.  Extending the
flavor group by the singlet vector factor adds the $\omega$, whose mass is
also controlled by its invariant bare term; the pions do not contribute to
that unbroken singlet direction.  This is the vector-dominance
approximation, with no exact low-energy theorem forcing the Yang--Mills
truncation for these heavy mesons.
