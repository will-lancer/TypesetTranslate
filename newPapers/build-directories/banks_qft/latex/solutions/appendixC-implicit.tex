\begin{bankssolutions}{Solutions to Implicit Exercises for Appendix C}

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-APPC-001}
The explicit gamma matrices, $\gamma_5$, and $\epsilon^{0123}$ below are
four-dimensional ($D=4$) objects with
$\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-,+,+,+)$; the antisymmetric contraction
identity is stated separately in general $D$, independent of the
dimensional-regularization parameter $d_{\mathrm{reg}}$.  The numerical trace
and $K_0$ formulas below use the same $D=4$ representation.

Hermiticity of the Pauli matrices gives
\[
 (\gamma^0)^\dagger
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf 1_2\\ \mathbf 1_2&0\end{pmatrix}
 =\gamma^0,
 \qquad
 (\gamma^i)^\dagger
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&-\sigma^i\\ \sigma^i&0\end{pmatrix}
 =-\gamma^i.
\]
Direct block multiplication also gives
\[
 (\gamma^0)^2=\mathbf 1_4,
 \qquad
 \gamma^0\gamma^i=-\gamma^i\gamma^0.
\]
The time component of the desired relation is therefore
$\gamma^0\gamma^0\gamma^0=\gamma^0=(\gamma^0)^\dagger$.  For a spatial
component,
\[
 \gamma^0\gamma^i\gamma^0
 =-\gamma^i(\gamma^0)^2
 =-\gamma^i
 =(\gamma^i)^\dagger.
\]
This verifies Banks's Eq.~(C.3) in the stated representation.

For the transformed matrices, adjunction and the original identity give
\begin{align*}
 (\gamma'^\mu)^\dagger
 &=\bigl(U^\dagger\gamma^\mu U\bigr)^\dagger
   =U^\dagger(\gamma^\mu)^\dagger U\\
 &=U^\dagger\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0U\\
 &=\bigl(U^\dagger\gamma^0U\bigr)
   \bigl(U^\dagger\gamma^\mu U\bigr)
   \bigl(U^\dagger\gamma^0U\bigr)
   =\gamma'^0\gamma'^\mu\gamma'^0.
\end{align*}
The insertion of $UU^\dagger=\mathbf 1_4$ between adjacent factors is the
step that uses unitarity.  It is also the reason Banks specifies unitary
changes of gamma-matrix basis when retaining this particular adjoint
formula.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-APPC-002}
Write $\eta^{ij}=\eta^{\mu_i\mu_j}$ and use a hat to omit an index.  Moving
the first gamma matrix through an even string gives
\[
 \gamma^{\mu_1}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n}}
 =-2\sum_{j=2}^{2n}(-1)^j\eta^{1j}
   \gamma^{\mu_2}\cdots\widehat{\gamma^{\mu_j}}\cdots
   \gamma^{\mu_{2n}}
 -\gamma^{\mu_2}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n}}\gamma^{\mu_1}.
\]
Cyclicity makes the trace of the final string equal to the trace on the
left.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 T_{2n}{}^{1\ldots 2n}
 =-\sum_{j=2}^{2n}(-1)^j\eta^{1j}
 T_{2n-2}{}^{2\ldots\widehat{j}\ldots 2n},
 \qquad T_0=4.
 \label{eq:appc-sol-trace-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Conjugation by $\gamma_5$ shows that every ordinary trace with an odd
number of gamma matrices vanishes.  Iterating
\eqref{eq:appc-sol-trace-recurrence} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \frac14T_6{}^{123456}={}&-\bigl\{
 \eta^{12}(\eta^{34}\eta^{56}-\eta^{35}\eta^{46}
                   +\eta^{36}\eta^{45})\\
 &-\eta^{13}(\eta^{24}\eta^{56}-\eta^{25}\eta^{46}
                   +\eta^{26}\eta^{45})\\
 &+\eta^{14}(\eta^{23}\eta^{56}-\eta^{25}\eta^{36}
                   +\eta^{26}\eta^{35})\\
 &-\eta^{15}(\eta^{23}\eta^{46}-\eta^{24}\eta^{36}
                   +\eta^{26}\eta^{34})\\
 &+\eta^{16}(\eta^{23}\eta^{45}-\eta^{24}\eta^{35}
                   +\eta^{25}\eta^{34})\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:appc-sol-six-trace}
\end{equation}
The eight-matrix answer is compactly and completely specified by
\[
 T_8{}^{12345678}
 =-\sum_{j=2}^{8}(-1)^j\eta^{1j}
   T_6{}^{2\ldots\widehat{j}\ldots8},
\]
with each $T_6$ replaced by \eqref{eq:appc-sol-six-trace}.  This contains the
$7!!=105$ signed pairings.  More generally, let $P$ run over pairings of
$\{1,\ldots,2n\}$.  Orient each pair $(a,b)$ by $a<b$, order pairs by
their first entries, and let $\operatorname{sgn}(P)$ be the parity of the
resulting sequence $(a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_n,b_n)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 T_{2n}{}^{1\ldots2n}
 =4(-1)^n\sum_P\operatorname{sgn}(P)
   \prod_{(a,b)\in P}\eta^{ab}.
 \label{eq:appc-sol-trace-pairings}
\end{equation}

For the chiral traces, cyclicity and
$\gamma_5\gamma^\mu=-\gamma^\mu\gamma_5$ imply
$T_{m,5}=(-1)^mT_{m,5}$, so the odd cases vanish.  The conventions in the
question give
\[
 \gamma_5=-\frac{\ii}{4!}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
 \gamma^\alpha\gamma^\beta\gamma^\gamma\gamma^\delta,
 \qquad \epsilon_{0123}=-1.
\]
The relation $\gamma^0\gamma_5\gamma^0=-\gamma_5$ gives
$T_{0,5}=0$.  The two-matrix trace is antisymmetric in its indices after
the Clifford relation is used.  Lorentz invariance supplies no constant
rank-two pseudotensor, so $T_{2,5}=0$.  Evaluating the ordered component
$0123$ fixes the four-matrix seed:
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3\gamma_5)
 =\ii\operatorname{tr}
  \bigl[(\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3)^2\bigr]
 =-4\ii,
\]
and Lorentz covariance yields
\[
 T_{4,5}{}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
 =-4\ii\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
\]
With $\epsilon^{ijkl}=\epsilon^{\mu_i\mu_j\mu_k\mu_l}$, the next order is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 T_{6,5}{}^{123456}=+4\ii\bigl(&
 \eta^{12}\epsilon^{3456}-\eta^{13}\epsilon^{2456}
 +\eta^{14}\epsilon^{2356}-\eta^{15}\epsilon^{2346}
 +\eta^{16}\epsilon^{2345}\\
 &+\eta^{23}\epsilon^{1456}-\eta^{24}\epsilon^{1356}
 +\eta^{25}\epsilon^{1346}-\eta^{26}\epsilon^{1345}\\
 &+\eta^{34}\epsilon^{1256}-\eta^{35}\epsilon^{1246}
 +\eta^{36}\epsilon^{1245}\\
 &+\eta^{45}\epsilon^{1236}-\eta^{46}\epsilon^{1235}
 +\eta^{56}\epsilon^{1234}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:appc-sol-six-chiral-trace}
\end{equation}
One all-orders construction follows immediately by substituting the
displayed formula for $\gamma_5$ and applying
\eqref{eq:appc-sol-trace-recurrence}:
\[
 T_{2n,5}{}^{1\ldots2n}
 =-\frac{\ii}{4!}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
 T_{2n+4}{}^{1\ldots2n\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.
\]
There is also a direct pairing form.  Partition
$\{1,\ldots,2n\}$ into $n-2$ pairs $(a,b)$, each oriented by $a<b$, and
an increasing remainder $c_1<c_2<c_3<c_4$.  Order the pairs by their
first entries and define
$\operatorname{sgn}(Q)$ as the parity of
$(a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_{n-2},b_{n-2},c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 T_{2n,5}{}^{1\ldots2n}
 =-4\ii(-1)^{n-2}\sum_Q\operatorname{sgn}(Q)
 \left(\prod_{(a,b)\in Q}\eta^{ab}\right)
 \epsilon^{c_1c_2c_3c_4}.
 \label{eq:appc-sol-chiral-pairings}
\end{equation}
For $2n=8$, this is the full reduction into two metrics and one
Levi-Civita tensor.  It has
$\binom84(4-1)!!=210$ signed terms.  Formula
\eqref{eq:appc-sol-six-chiral-trace} is its one-pair predecessor.

For the contractions, the first Clifford interchange gives Banks's
induction step directly:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 K_k(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_k)
 &=-\gamma_{\mu_1}K_{k-1}(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_k)
 -2\gamma_{\mu_2}\cdots\gamma_{\mu_k}\gamma_{\mu_1},\\
 K_0&=-4\mathbf 1_4\quad(D=4).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:appc-sol-contraction-recurrence}
\end{equation}
At $k=3$, this recurrence uses the corrected free-index structure
$-4\gamma_\mu\eta_{\nu\kappa}$ recorded in Erratum E-022, together with the
sign fixed by the mostly-plus Clifford relation. At $k=4$ it
gives
\begin{align*}
 K_4(\mu,\nu,\kappa,\alpha)={}&
 4\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\eta_{\kappa\alpha}
 +2\gamma_\mu\gamma_\kappa\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\nu
 -2\gamma_\nu\gamma_\kappa\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\mu.
\end{align*}
The printed line on p.~250 reverses $\gamma_\kappa\gamma_\alpha$ in the
three-gamma term. The induction fixes the order shown above; a
repeated-index substitution distinguishes the two expressions. For four
mutually distinct indices, direct Clifford reduction of the target
convention gives
$4\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\gamma_\kappa\gamma_\alpha$.

Anticommuting the final $\gamma_\lambda$ through an antisymmetrized string
gives
\begin{equation}
 \gamma^\lambda\gamma_{\mu_1\ldots\mu_r}\gamma_\lambda
 =(-1)^{r+1}(D-2r)\gamma_{\mu_1\ldots\mu_r}.
\label{eq:appc-sol-antisymmetric-contraction}
\end{equation}
The final passage through the string contributes
$D(-1)^{r+1}\gamma_{\mu_1\ldots\mu_r}$, while the $r$ anticommutator
terms contribute $2r(-1)^r\gamma_{\mu_1\ldots\mu_r}$. Their sum is the
coefficient in \eqref{eq:appc-sol-antisymmetric-contraction}. Here $D$ is the
physical spacetime dimension, independent of the dimensional-regularization
parameter $d_{\mathrm{reg}}$; the gamma-matrix representation and the later odd-string
specialization use $D=4$.

An odd ordered product in four dimensions contains only its rank-one and
rank-three Clifford components.  Reversal leaves the first component
fixed and changes the sign of the third.  Equation
\eqref{eq:appc-sol-antisymmetric-contraction} therefore implies the useful
odd-string identity
\[
K_{2s+1}(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_{2s+1})
 =+2\gamma_{\mu_{2s+1}}\cdots\gamma_{\mu_1}.
\]
The first requested continuation is
\[
K_5(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_5)
 =+2\gamma_{\mu_5}\gamma_{\mu_4}\gamma_{\mu_3}
       \gamma_{\mu_2}\gamma_{\mu_1}.
\]
Using this result once in \eqref{eq:appc-sol-contraction-recurrence} gives
\begin{align*}
 K_6(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_6)=-2\bigl(&
 \gamma_{\mu_1}\gamma_{\mu_6}\gamma_{\mu_5}\gamma_{\mu_4}
                 \gamma_{\mu_3}\gamma_{\mu_2}\\
 &+\gamma_{\mu_2}\gamma_{\mu_3}\gamma_{\mu_4}\gamma_{\mu_5}
                 \gamma_{\mu_6}\gamma_{\mu_1}\bigr).
\end{align*}
If
\[
 K_{k,5}=\gamma^\lambda\gamma_{\mu_1}\cdots\gamma_{\mu_k}
          \gamma_5\gamma_\lambda,
\]
then one anticommutation with the final gamma matrix gives
\[
 K_{k,5}=-K_k\gamma_5.
\]
Consequently
\begin{align*}
 K_{5,5}&=-2\gamma_{\mu_5}\gamma_{\mu_4}\gamma_{\mu_3}
              \gamma_{\mu_2}\gamma_{\mu_1}\gamma_5,\\
 K_{6,5}&=+2\bigl(
 \gamma_{\mu_1}\gamma_{\mu_6}\gamma_{\mu_5}\gamma_{\mu_4}
                 \gamma_{\mu_3}\gamma_{\mu_2}
 +\gamma_{\mu_2}\gamma_{\mu_3}\gamma_{\mu_4}\gamma_{\mu_5}
                 \gamma_{\mu_6}\gamma_{\mu_1}\bigr)\gamma_5.
\end{align*}

Several quick checks fix common sign errors.  Contracting the first two
indices of either six-matrix trace gives
\[
 \eta_{\mu_1\mu_2}T_6{}^{\mu_1\ldots\mu_6}=-4T_4{}^{\mu_3\ldots\mu_6},
 \qquad
 \eta_{\mu_1\mu_2}T_{6,5}{}^{\mu_1\ldots\mu_6}
 =-4T_{4,5}{}^{\mu_3\ldots\mu_6}.
\]
For four mutually distinct indices,
\eqref{eq:appc-sol-antisymmetric-contraction} gives
$K_4=+4\gamma_{\mu_1}\gamma_{\mu_2}\gamma_{\mu_3}\gamma_{\mu_4}$;
this tests the ordering in the four-matrix intermediate identity.  Taking
all indices equal to the timelike value gives $K_5=-2\gamma^0$ and
$K_6=-4\mathbf 1_4$, in agreement with direct cancellation of
$(\gamma^0)^2$.
