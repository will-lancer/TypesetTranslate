\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH02-001}
Let
\[
  F_n(\mathbb R^{D-1})=
  \{(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_n):\mathbf x_r\ne\mathbf x_s\}
\]
be the ordered configuration space. The coincidence sets have codimension
$D-1\geq3$. A loop in their complement can therefore be contracted after the
particle labels are held fixed. Thus $F_n(\mathbb R^{D-1})$ is simply connected.
The free quotient by $S_n$ is a covering
\[
  F_n(\mathbb R^{D-1})\longrightarrow\mathcal C_n(\mathbb R^{D-1})
\]
whose deck group is $S_n$, and hence
\[
  \pi_1(\mathcal C_n(\mathbb R^{D-1}))\simeq S_n.
\]
An exchange of adjacent particles represents a transposition $\tau_i$. In
particular,
\[
  \tau_i^2=1,
  \qquad
  \tau_i\tau_{i+1}\tau_i=\tau_{i+1}\tau_i\tau_{i+1}.
\]

For a species with a one-dimensional exchange space, put
$\rho(\tau_i)=\chi_i\in U(1)$. The first relation gives $\chi_i^2=1$. The braid
relation then gives $\chi_i=\chi_{i+1}$. Consequently every elementary exchange
has one common value
\[
  \chi=+1\quad\hbox{or}\quad\chi=-1.
\]
These are the trivial and sign representations of $S_n$.

The local-field version uses operators $F(f)$ and $F(g)$ supported in
space-like separated regions. Transporting one support around the other gives
\[
  F(f)F(g)=\chi F(g)F(f).
\]
A double exchange is homotopically trivial in $D-1\geq3$, so consistency gives
$\chi^2=1$. The value $+1$ is implemented by commuting creation fields and
gives Bose symmetry. The value $-1$ is implemented by anticommuting creation
fields and gives Fermi symmetry. Locality makes this sign independent of the
detailed paths, while cluster decomposition lets the exchange be performed far
from other excitations.

Ordinary particle quantum mechanics also allows a vector space $V$ and a
higher-dimensional unitary representation $\rho:S_n\to U(V)$. This is the
algebraic description of parastatistics. The local-QFT statement uses more
structure. For a net of local observables with positive energy, a unique
vacuum, and transportable sectors of finite statistics, the
Doplicher--Haag--Roberts reconstruction gives a compact gauge group $G$ and a
field algebra carrying $G$. A para-Bose sector of order $r$ can be represented by $r$-component
ordinary bosonic fields; a para-Fermi sector has the analogous fermionic
representation. Restricting to gauge-invariant states reproduces the original
para-sector. The extra matrix label is therefore an internal charge
multiplicity. Its space-time exchange sign remains $+1$ or $-1$. This is the
sense in which a local QFT has Bose and Fermi particle statistics, even though
its observable-sector description may display parastatistical multiplicities.

For two spatial dimensions, the coincidence sets have codimension two and exchange loops cannot
generally be contracted. The fundamental group of the unordered configuration
space is the braid group $B_n$, where the elementary braid obeys no relation
$\sigma_i^2=1$. A one-dimensional unitary representation may assign
$\rho(\sigma_i)=\ee^{\ii\theta}$ with arbitrary $\theta$, and matrix
representations give non-Abelian anyons. The missing order-two relation is the
topological opening for braid statistics.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH02-002}
Set $\varepsilon=+1$ for bosons and $\varepsilon=-1$ for fermions. Then the
graded oscillator relation gives
\[
  a(\mathbf p)a^\dagger(\mathbf p_1)\cdots
  a^\dagger(\mathbf p_k)\ket{0}
  =\sum_{s=1}^k\varepsilon^{s-1}
  \delta^{D-1}(\mathbf p-\mathbf p_s)
  a^\dagger(\mathbf p_1)\cdots
  \widehat{a^\dagger(\mathbf p_s)}\cdots
  a^\dagger(\mathbf p_k)\ket{0}.
\]
The hat denotes omission. Inserting this expression into $P^\mu$ creates one
factor $a^\dagger(\mathbf p_s)$ at the left of each term. Moving it back to its
original position supplies a second factor $\varepsilon^{s-1}$. Since
$\varepsilon^2=1$, the two signs cancel. The delta function performs the
momentum integral, leaving
\begin{align*}
  P^\mu\ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}
  &=\sum_{s=1}^k p_s^\mu
    a^\dagger(\mathbf p_1)\cdots a^\dagger(\mathbf p_k)\ket{0}\\
  &=\left(\sum_{s=1}^k p_s^\mu\right)
    \ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}.
\end{align*}
This works for either statistics and for any overall normalization of the
$k$-particle state.
Equivalently, the calculation establishes the useful distributional identity
\[
  [P^\mu,a^\dagger(\mathbf q)]=q^\mu a^\dagger(\mathbf q),
\]
whose ordinary Leibniz rule immediately gives the same result on every
finite-particle state.

The density $n_{\mathbf p}$ is operator-valued distribution. Its meaningful
form is smeared with a test function $f$:
\[
  N(f)=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p\,f(\mathbf p)n_{\mathbf p},
  \qquad
  N(f)\ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}
  =\sum_{s=1}^k f(\mathbf p_s)\ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}.
\]
On the finite-particle domain this formula extends from test functions to
$f=1$, giving $N\ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}=k\ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}$, and to
$f(\mathbf p)=p^\mu$, giving $P^\mu$. Smearing avoids the undefined attempt to
assign a finite operator value to $n_{\mathbf p}$ at one exact momentum.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH02-003}
Let $\Lambda$ be proper orthochronous, so it preserves the positive mass
shell. The invariant positive-shell measure follows from
\[
  \dd^D p\,\theta(p^0)\delta(p^2+m^2)
  =\frac{\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p}{2\omega_{\mathbf p}}.
\]
Write $\mathbf p'=\boldsymbol{\Lambda p}$. Its invariance gives
\[
  \frac{\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p'}{2\omega_{\mathbf p'}}
  =\frac{\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p}{2\omega_{\mathbf p}},
  \qquad
  J_\Lambda(\mathbf p)
  \equiv\left|\det\frac{\partial p'^i}{\partial p^j}\right|
  =\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda p}}}{\omega_{\mathbf p}}.
\]
The standard change-of-variables rule for a delta distribution therefore says
\[
  \delta^{D-1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda p}-\boldsymbol{\Lambda q})
  =\frac{\omega_{\mathbf p}}{\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda p}}}
    \delta^{D-1}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q).
\]
The coefficient may equally be evaluated at $q$, since the delta distribution
sets $q=p$.

Now transform two basis states:
\begin{align*}
  \braket{U(\Lambda)\mathbf p}{U(\Lambda)\mathbf q}
  &=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda p}}}{\omega_{\mathbf p}}}
    \sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda q}}}{\omega_{\mathbf q}}}
    \delta^{D-1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda p}-\boldsymbol{\Lambda q})\\
  &=\delta^{D-1}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q).
\end{align*}
Thus the map is an isometry on delta-normalized wave packets. The inverse
transformation $U(\Lambda^{-1})$ makes it onto, so it is unitary.

For composition, apply two transformations in succession:
\begin{align*}
  U(\Lambda_2)U(\Lambda_1)\ket{\mathbf p}
  &=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda_1p}}}{\omega_{\mathbf p}}}
    \sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda_2\Lambda_1p}}}
                 {\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda_1p}}}}
    \ket{\boldsymbol{\Lambda_2\Lambda_1p}}\\
  &=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda_2\Lambda_1p}}}
                 {\omega_{\mathbf p}}}
    \ket{\boldsymbol{\Lambda_2\Lambda_1p}}
   =U(\Lambda_2\Lambda_1)\ket{\mathbf p}.
\end{align*}
The square-root factor is precisely the half-density factor associated with
Banks's noncovariant $\delta^{D-1}$ normalization. Covariantly normalized states
$\ket{\mathbf p}_{\rm cov}=\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathbf p}}\ket{\mathbf p}$ transform without it.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH02-004}
Take $\braket{0}{0}=1$ and provisionally define
\[
  \ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}_c
  =c_k a^\dagger(\mathbf p_1)\cdots
  a^\dagger(\mathbf p_k)\ket{0}.
\]
Repeatedly moving the leftmost annihilator through
the creators gives one contraction for every permutation of the momenta. An
unpaired annihilator kills the ket vacuum; an unpaired creator kills the bra
vacuum. Hence sectors with different particle number are orthogonal, and
\[
  {}_c\!\braket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}{\mathbf q_1,\ldots,\mathbf q_l}_{c}
  =\delta_{kl}|c_k|^2
   \sum_{\sigma\in S_k}
   \varepsilon^{\operatorname{par}(\sigma)}
   \prod_{s=1}^k\delta^{D-1}(\mathbf p_s-\mathbf q_{\sigma(s)}).
\]
Here $\operatorname{par}(\sigma)$ is zero or one according to the parity of
$\sigma$. The choice
\[
  c_k=\frac{\ee^{\ii\alpha_k}}{\sqrt{k!}}
\]
produces Banks's factor $1/k!$; the phase $\alpha_k$ has no effect. With the
literal definition $c_k=1$, the oscillator algebra instead gives the same
permutation sum with coefficient one. Thus the displayed state definition,
commutation relation, and $1/k!$ inner product require the missing factor
$1/\sqrt{k!}$ in the state or a corresponding rescaling of its label. This
normalization issue does not affect the action of number-preserving operators.

Introduce a smooth one-particle wave function $f$ and
\[
  a^\dagger(f)=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p\,f(\mathbf p)a^\dagger(\mathbf p).
\]
For a multiplication or differential operator $h$ that is self-adjoint on the
chosen wave-packet domain, the graded relations and an integration by parts
give the ordinary commutator
\[
  [\dd\Gamma(h),a^\dagger(f)]=a^\dagger(hf),
  \qquad \dd\Gamma(h)\ket{0}=0.
\]
The commutator is ordinary because $\dd\Gamma(h)$ is even. Applying the product
rule now yields
\begin{align*}
  \dd\Gamma(h)a^\dagger(f_1)\cdots a^\dagger(f_k)\ket{0}
  =\sum_{r=1}^k
  a^\dagger(f_1)\cdots a^\dagger(hf_r)\cdots
  a^\dagger(f_k)\ket{0}.
\end{align*}
This is the second-quantized sum $h^{(1)}+\cdots+h^{(k)}$.

For $h=1$ one obtains
\[
  N=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p\,a^\dagger(\mathbf p)a(\mathbf p),
  \qquad N\ket{f_1,\ldots,f_k}=k\ket{f_1,\ldots,f_k}.
\]
For the multiplication operator $h=p^\mu$ the result is
\[
  P^\mu=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p\,p^\mu a^\dagger(\mathbf p)a(\mathbf p),
  \qquad P^\mu\ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}
  =\sum_r p_r^\mu\ket{\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_k}.
\]
Finally, take $h_{ij}=\ii(p_i\partial_j-p_j\partial_i)$. The vector field
$p_i\partial_j-p_j\partial_i$ has zero divergence, so boundary-free integration
by parts makes $h_{ij}$ self-adjoint. Its lift is
\[
  J_{ij}=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p\,a^\dagger(\mathbf p)
  \ii(p_i\partial_j-p_j\partial_i)a(\mathbf p),
\]
and it rotates every wave-packet argument by the one-particle orbital
generator. On a sharp creator the equivalent distributional identity is
\[
  [J_{ij},a^\dagger(\mathbf q)]
  =-\ii(q_i\partial_{q^j}-q_j\partial_{q^i})a^\dagger(\mathbf q).
\]
The derivative transfers to a wave function with the opposite sign, recovering
$h_{ij}$ above. These checks establish Eqs.~(2.1)--(2.5), with the explicit
normalization reconciliation for Eq.~(2.1).

For the remaining boost formulas, use the delta-normalized finite
transformation checked in the preceding exercise. An infinitesimal boost with
velocity $\mathbf v$ gives
\[
  p^i\longmapsto p^i+v^i\omega_{\mathbf p},
  \qquad
  \omega_{\mathbf p}\longmapsto
  \omega_{\mathbf p}+v^i p_i.
\]
Expanding its normalization factor and momentum label gives
\begin{align*}
  \sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda p}}}{\omega_{\mathbf p}}}
  \ket{\boldsymbol{\Lambda p}}
  &=\left(1+\frac{v^i p_i}{2\omega_{\mathbf p}}\right)
    \left(1+\omega_{\mathbf p}v^i\partial_i\right)\ket{\mathbf p}
    +O(v^2)\\
  &=\ket{\mathbf p}+\left(\frac{v^i p_i}{2\omega_{\mathbf p}}
    +\omega_{\mathbf p}v^i\partial_i\right)\ket{\mathbf p}+O(v^2).
\end{align*}
The differential operator in parentheses is anti-Hermitian on
$L^2(\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p)$: the divergence of $\omega_{\mathbf p}\mathbf v$ is
$v^i p_i/\omega_{\mathbf p}$, and the half-density term cancels half of the
integration-by-parts contribution on each side. Its Fock lift is therefore
\[
  v^i J_{0i}=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf p\,a^\dagger(\mathbf p)
  \left(\frac{v^i p_i}{2\omega_{\mathbf p}}
  +\omega_{\mathbf p}v^i\partial_i\right)a(\mathbf p),
\]
which is the boost formula in the surrounding text. Together with the
half-density and invariant-measure calculation of the preceding exercise,
this covers every displayed formula in the opening Fock-space discussion.
The source is using the anti-Hermitian Lie-algebra convention
$\delta\ket{\mathbf p}=v^i J_{0i}\ket{\mathbf p}$ in this display. With the unitary convention
$U=\exp(-\ii v^i K_i)$, the corresponding Hermitian boost generator is
$K_i=\ii J_{0i}$.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH02-005}
Work on a common invariant domain where the operator products and time
integrals exist, or read the following identities as a regulated formal power
series. Multiplying the evolution equation by $1/\ii=-\ii$ and integrating from $t_0$
to $t$ gives
\[
  U_D(t,t_0)=\mathbf1-\ii\int_{t_0}^{t}\dd t_1\,
  W(t_1)U_D(t_1,t_0).
\]
Substituting this equation for the $U_D$ inside its own integral produces
\begin{align*}
  U_D(t,t_0)
  &=\mathbf1-\ii\int_{t_0}^{t}\dd t_1\,W(t_1)\\
  &\quad+(-\ii)^2\int_{t_0}^{t}\dd t_1
    \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\dd t_2\,W(t_1)W(t_2)+\cdots.
\end{align*}
At order $n$ the integration domain is the simplex
\[
  t\geq t_1\geq t_2\geq\cdots\geq t_n\geq t_0,
\]
and iteration gives
\[
  U_D(t,t_0)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-\ii)^n
  \int_{t_0}^{t}\dd t_1\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\dd t_2\cdots
  \int_{t_0}^{t_{n-1}}\dd t_n\,W(t_1)\cdots W(t_n).
\]
The $n=0$ term is $\mathbf1$.
Differentiating the upper limit of the outermost integral sets $t_1=t$.
The remaining nested integrals are exactly the order-$(n-1)$ term, so the
series obeys $\partial_t U_D=-\ii W(t)U_D$ term by term and takes the value
$\mathbf1$ at $t=t_0$.

Apart from boundaries of measure zero, the cube $[t_0,t]^n$ is the disjoint
union of $n!$ simplexes, one for every ordering of its coordinates. The
time-ordering symbol places the latest operator at the left. Its integrand is
symmetric under a simultaneous permutation of the times and their operator
labels. Each simplex therefore gives the same ordered integral, so
\[
  U_D(t,t_0)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\ii)^n}{n!}
  \int_{t_0}^{t}\dd t_1\cdots\dd t_n\,
  T\!\left[W(t_1)\cdots W(t_n)\right].
\]
This series defines the shorthand
\[
  U_D(t,t_0)=T\exp\!\left[-\ii\int_{t_0}^{t}\dd s\,W(s)\right].
\]

Through second order, the full-cube form contains
\begin{align*}
  \frac{(-\ii)^2}{2}
  \int_{t_0}^{t}\dd t_1\dd t_2\,
  \bigl[&\theta(t_1-t_2)W(t_1)W(t_2)\\
        &+\theta(t_2-t_1)W(t_2)W(t_1)\bigr].
\end{align*}
Exchanging $t_1$ and $t_2$ in the second term turns the expression into the
ordered double integral above. If $[W(t),W(t')]=0$ for every pair of times,
time ordering has no effect and the ordinary exponential results:
\[
  U_D(t,t_0)=\exp\!\left[-\ii\int_{t_0}^{t}\dd s\,W(s)\right].
\]
For constant $W$ this becomes $\ee^{-\ii(t-t_0)W}$, which obeys both the
differential equation and the initial condition. Hermitian $W(t)$ also gives a
unitary evolution, as follows directly by differentiating $U_D^\dagger U_D$.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH02-006}
Assume a nonzero finite-component Wightman field on a positive-metric Hilbert
space, with a Poincar\'e-invariant cyclic vacuum, the spectrum condition, and
graded locality. These hypotheses exclude unphysical ghost fields and supply
the analytic and positivity steps used below.

In $D=4$, under spatial rotations, the Lorentz representation $(A,B)$ restricts to the
tensor product of spins $A$ and $B$. The Clebsch--Gordan series gives
\[
  (A,B)\big|_{SU(2)}
  =\bigoplus_{j=|A-B|}^{A+B}j.
\]
Successive values of $j$ differ by integers. Moreover,
$A+B-|A-B|=2\min(A,B)$ is an integer. It follows that
\[
  2j\equiv2(A+B)\pmod2,
  \qquad (-1)^{2j}=(-1)^{2(A+B)}\equiv s_\Phi.
\]
The number $s_\Phi$ is the action of the central $2\pi$ rotation on the field
multiplet.

Here is the analytic step behind the theorem. Translation covariance and the
spectrum condition write a two-point function schematically as
\[
  W_{ab}(z)=\bra{0}\Phi_a(z)\Phi_b{}^\dagger(0)\ket{0}
  =\int_{\overline V_+}\dd\mu(p)\,
    \ee^{\ii p\mathbin{\cdot}z}M_{ab}(p).
\]
It is the boundary value of a function analytic when the imaginary part of
$z$ lies in the appropriate open light cone. Reversing the operator order
produces the boundary value from the opposite tube. Real Lorentz covariance
extends to the connected complex Lorentz group on these analytic functions.
For a real space-like vector, the extended orbit contains both $z$ and $-z$.
A continuation that carries one to the other exchanges the two Jost points.
On vector coordinates this path closes, while its lift to the spin cover closes
with the central $2\pi$ rotation. After the covariant indices are paired with
the invariant intertwiner, the continuation therefore contributes $s_\Phi$.
The boundary values obey the spin-statistics lemma
\[
  W(z)=s_\Phi W^{\mathrm{op}}(z)
  \qquad (z^2>0),
\]
where $W^{\mathrm{op}}$ denotes the same invariantly contracted two-point
function with the fields reversed.

Graded locality gives independently
\[
  W(z)=\eta W^{\mathrm{op}}(z)
  \qquad (z^2>0).
\]
Here $\eta=+1$ is the commuting choice and $\eta=-1$ the anticommuting choice.
Together these relations imply
\[
  (\eta-s_\Phi)W^{\mathrm{op}}(z)=0
\]
throughout the space-like Jost region. If $\eta\ne s_\Phi$, analyticity makes
the two-point function vanish identically. Positivity then gives
\[
  \norm{\Phi^\dagger(f)\ket{0}}^2=0
\]
for every test function $f$, so $\Phi^\dagger(f)\ket{0}=0$. Cyclicity, or the
Reeh--Schlieder consequence of locality and the spectrum condition, makes the
local adjoint field identically zero; hence $\Phi$ is zero as well. This
contradicts the nonzero-field assumption. Therefore
\[
  \eta=s_\Phi=(-1)^{2j}.
\]

An integer-spin field has $\eta=+1$ and commutes at space-like separation. A
half-integer-spin field has $\eta=-1$ and anticommutes there. Scalars and
vectors give $+1$; Weyl and Dirac spinors give $-1$. Problem~\ref{problem:2.5}
checks the scalar case directly, while Problem~\ref{problem:2.8} constructs the
free fields of general spin. The general theorem uses the same ingredients for
interacting fields. Its assumptions have distinct jobs: covariance supplies
the central-rotation phase, positive energy supplies tube analyticity, locality
relates the two operator orders, and Hilbert-space positivity rules out the
vanishing-norm alternative. This is the content of the result cited by Banks in
Refs.~\cite{banks-ref-6,banks-ref-7,banks-ref-8}.

For $D>4$, the labels $(A,B)$ are replaced by representations of the higher
dimensional Lorentz spin group. Restriction to spatial rotations gives a
representation of $\operatorname{Spin}(D-1)$. Let $z_{2\pi}$ be the central
element covering a $2\pi$ rotation. It acts as $+1$ on tensorial, or
integer-spin, representations and as $-1$ on spinorial, or half-integer-spin,
representations. The complex-Lorentz continuation above inserts precisely this
element, so the comparison with graded locality reads
\[
  \eta=\rho(z_{2\pi}).
\]
The same positivity argument then assigns Bose statistics to tensorial
particles and Fermi statistics to spinorial particles in every $D\geq4$.

In two spatial dimensions, exchanges form the braid group and a double exchange
need not be contractible. The spin cover also allows general phases associated
with planar rotations. The relation between spin and exchange becomes a braid
spin-statistics relation, and the step restricting $\eta$ to the two central
signs no longer applies.
