\BanksSolution{9.1}
Put
\[
 A_\mu=A^3_\mu,
 \qquad W_\mu=\frac{A^1_\mu-\ii A^2_\mu}{\sqrt2},
 \qquad W_\mu^*=\frac{A^1_\mu+\ii A^2_\mu}{\sqrt2},
\]
and write $f_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$ and
$D_\mu W_\nu=(\partial_\mu-\ii gA_\mu)W_\nu$.  I use
$F^a{}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A^a_\nu-\partial_\nu A^a_\mu
+g\epsilon^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu$.  The three components of the
Yang--Mills curvature become
\begin{align*}
 F^3_{\mu\nu}&=f_{\mu\nu}-\ii g
 (W_\mu W_\nu^*-W_\nu W_\mu^*),\\
 F^+_{\mu\nu}&=D_\mu W_\nu-D_\nu W_\mu,
 \qquad F^-_{\mu\nu}=(F^+_{\mu\nu})^* .
\end{align*}
Using $-\frac14F^a{}_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu}$, one obtains
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L={}&-\frac14 f_{\mu\nu}f^{\mu\nu}
 -\frac12(D_\mu W_\nu-D_\nu W_\mu)^*
              (D^\mu W^\nu-D^\nu W^\mu)\\
 &+\ii g f_{\mu\nu}W^\mu W^{*\nu}
 +\frac{g^2}{4}(W_\mu W_\nu^*-W_\nu W_\mu^*)^2 .
\end{align*}
The first line is the Lagrangian of a charged spin-one field coupled to the
photon $A_\mu$.  Its second line contains the extra magnetic interaction
 which is fixed by the non-abelian curvature.  Indeed,
 $f^{\mu\nu}(W_{\mu}W^*_{\nu}-W_{\nu}W^*_{\mu})
 =2f^{\mu\nu}W_{\mu}W^*_{\nu}$, which accounts for the displayed
 cross-term normalization.  A general charged Proca field could have the
 same first line and an independent term
 $+\ii g\kappa f_{\mu\nu}W^\mu W^{*\nu}$.  Minimal Proca coupling gives
$\kappa=0$, whereas Yang--Mills theory gives $\kappa=1$.

To read the magnetic moment, take a slowly varying magnetic field and use
the spin-one generators $(S_k)_{ij}=-\ii\epsilon_{kij}$.  The quadratic
Hamiltonian contains
\[
 H_{\rm mag}=-\frac{q}{2m}(1+\kappa)\,
 \mathbf S\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf B
 \quad\text{for a vector of mass }m,
 \qquad D_\mu=\partial_\mu-\ii qA_\mu .
 \]
For the displayed convention $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-\ii gA_\mu$ one has
$q=g$.  Comparing with
$H_{\rm mag}=-(g_{\rm mag}q/2m)\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf B$ gives
$g_{\rm mag}=1+\kappa$.  Minimal Proca coupling has $g_{\rm mag}=1$,
whereas the Yang--Mills value is $g_{\rm mag}=2$.  The massless theory is
obtained by taking $m\to0$ after
projecting onto its physical helicities; the coefficient of the magnetic
operator is unchanged.

\BanksSolution{9.2}
I use $d_{\mathrm{reg}}=4-2\varepsilon$ and
$\Delta_\varepsilon=(16\pi^2\varepsilon)^{-1}$.  The factors of $\ii$ in a
pseudoscalar vertex depend on whether the calculation is written in
Minkowski or Euclidean signature.  I use the Hermitian Minkowski vertex
$\ii g_P\gamma _5$; after Wick rotation it is the $g_P\gamma _5$ vertex in
the question.  Set $G=g_S^2+g_P^2$.

The first step is to close the list of operators.  With no imposed parity or
chiral symmetry the most general renormalizable Lagrangian can be written
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L={}&-\frac12 Z_\phi(\partial\phi)^2
 -Z_\psi\bar\psi\ii\slashed\partial\psi-U(\phi)\\
 &-\bar\psi(M+\ii M_5\gamma _5)\psi
 -\phi\bar\psi(g_S+\ii g_P\gamma _5)\psi ,\\
 U(\phi)={}&\Lambda_{\rm vac}+t\phi+\frac12m_\phi^2\phi^2
       +\frac{h}{3!}\phi^3+\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4 .
\end{align*}
Thus the kinetic terms, vacuum energy, the two fermion mass structures, the
linear, quadratic, and cubic scalar terms, the quartic scalar term, and both
Yukawa structures all have to be allowed.  If one restricts to the symmetric
subsector $t=h=M=M_5=0$, the transformation
$\phi\mapsto-\phi$, $\psi\mapsto\gamma _5\psi$,
$\bar\psi\mapsto-\bar\psi\gamma _5$ preserves arbitrary $g_S$ and $g_P$.
It forbids precisely those four relevant odd or mass terms.  Without that
restriction the full list above is required by the regulator and the stated
symmetries.

For reference, the proper-time integrals which control the poles are
\begin{align*}
 I_2(\Delta)&=\int_{s_0}^{\infty}\frac{\dd s}{(4\pi s)^2}s\ee^{-s\Delta}
 =\frac{1}{16\pi^2}\left[
 \ln\frac{1}{\mu^2s_0}-\gamma_E-\ln\frac{\Delta}{\mu^2}
 +O(s_0\Delta)\right],\\
 I_1(\Delta)&=\int_{s_0}^{\infty}\frac{\dd s}{(4\pi s)^2}\ee^{-s\Delta}
 =\frac{1}{16\pi^2}\left[
 \frac1{s_0}+\Delta\left(\ln(s_0\Delta)+\gamma_E-1\right)+\cdots\right].
 \end{align*}
The first is logarithmically divergent and the second contains the power
divergence.  For later use put
\begin{align*}
 X(\phi)&=U''(\phi)=m_\phi^2+h\phi+\frac{\lambda}{2}\phi^2,\\
 A(\phi)&=M+g_S\phi,\qquad B(\phi)=M_5+g_P\phi,\\
 Y(\phi)&=A(\phi)^2+B(\phi)^2
 =q_0+q_1\phi+G\phi^2,
\end{align*}
where $q_0=M^2+M_5^2$ and
$q_1=2(Mg_S+M_5g_P)$.  The unrenormalized scalar and Dirac determinants
then have the following local proper-time expansion:
\begin{align}
 V_{s,\mathrm{raw}}(\phi)={}&-\frac{1}{64\pi^2s_0^2}
 +\frac{X}{32\pi^2s_0}-\frac{X^2}{64\pi^2}L_0
 +\frac{X^2}{64\pi^2}
 \left[\ln\frac{X}{\mu^2}-\frac32\right]+O(s_0X^3),\nonumber\\
 V_{f,\mathrm{raw}}(\phi)={}&+\frac{1}{16\pi^2s_0^2}
 -\frac{Y}{8\pi^2s_0}+\frac{Y^2}{16\pi^2}L_0
 -\frac{Y^2}{16\pi^2}
 \left[\ln\frac{Y}{\mu^2}-\frac32\right]+O(s_0Y^3),
 \label{eq:9.2-sol-raw-determinants}
\end{align}
with $L_0=\ln(1/(\mu^2s_0))-\gamma_E$.  The terms proportional to
$s_0^{-2}$ are vacuum-energy graphs.  The $X/s_0$ and $Y/s_0$ terms are
the scalar and fermion tadpole and self-energy power divergences.  The
$X^2L_0$ and $Y^2L_0$ terms contain the logarithmic tadpole, self-energy,
cubic, and four-point graphs after the background is differentiated.

Differentiating a graph with respect to an external momentum replaces $I_1$
by $I_2$ or a convergent integral, so every remaining pole is a local
polynomial.  The finite subtractions can be recorded before any graph is
combined:
\begin{align*}
 I_1^{\mathrm{fin}}(\Delta)&=
 \frac{\Delta}{16\pi^2}\left(\ln\frac{\Delta}{\mu^2}-1\right),\\
 I_2^{\mathrm{fin}}(\Delta)&=-\frac{1}{16\pi^2}\ln\frac{\Delta}{\mu^2}.
\end{align*}
Thus a scalar tadpole uses $I_1^{\mathrm{fin}}$, while a scalar self-energy,
Yukawa triangle, scalar fish, and fermion box use $I_2^{\mathrm{fin}}$
inside their Feynman-parameter integrals.  For example, the finite part of
every two-propagator subintegral is
\[
 B_{\mathrm{PT}}(p^2;m_1^2,m_2^2)
 =-\frac{1}{16\pi^2}\int_0^1\dd x\,
 \ln\frac{x m_1^2+(1-x)m_2^2+x(1-x)p^2}{\mu^2}.
\]
The determinant expressions in \eqref{eq:9.2-sol-raw-determinants} are the
zero-momentum evaluation of the same finite terms.

The counterterm which cancels the power pieces, including the two
field-independent vacuum terms, is
\begin{align}
 \delta U_{\mathrm{power}}={}&\frac{1}{64\pi^2s_0^2}
 -\frac{1}{16\pi^2s_0^2}-\frac{X}{32\pi^2s_0}
 +\frac{Y}{8\pi^2s_0},\nonumber\\
 \delta\Lambda_{\mathrm{power}}={}&\frac{1}{64\pi^2s_0^2}
 -\frac{1}{16\pi^2s_0^2}-\frac{m_\phi^2}{32\pi^2s_0}
 +\frac{q_0}{8\pi^2s_0},\nonumber\\
 \delta t_{\mathrm{power}}={}&-\frac{h}{32\pi^2s_0}
 +\frac{q_1}{8\pi^2s_0},\nonumber\\
 \delta m_{\phi,\mathrm{power}}^2={}&-\frac{\lambda}{32\pi^2s_0}
 +\frac{G}{4\pi^2s_0},\qquad
 \delta h_{\mathrm{power}}=\delta\lambda_{\mathrm{power}}=0.
 \label{eq:9.2-sol-power-counterterms}
\end{align}
The logarithmic residues are graph by graph.  We write
$\delta U=\delta\Lambda+\delta t\phi+\delta m_\phi^2\phi^2/2
+\delta h\phi^3/3!+\delta\lambda\phi^4/4!$:
\begin{align}
 \frac{\delta\Lambda^{(s)}_{\mathrm{vac}}}{L_0}&=\frac{m_\phi^4}{64\pi^2},
 &\frac{\delta t^{(s)}_{\mathrm{tad}}}{L_0}&=\frac{m_\phi^2h}{32\pi^2},\nonumber\\
 \frac{\delta m_\phi^{2\,(s)}}{L_0}&=\frac{h^2+\lambda m_\phi^2}{32\pi^2},
 &\frac{\delta h^{(s)}_{\mathrm{bub}}}{L_0}&=\frac{3\lambda h}{32\pi^2},\nonumber\\
 \frac{\delta\lambda^{(s)}_{\mathrm{fish}}}{L_0}&=\frac{3\lambda^2}{32\pi^2},
 &\frac{\delta\Lambda^{(f)}_{\mathrm{vac}}}{L_0}&=-\frac{q_0^2}{16\pi^2},\nonumber\\
 \frac{\delta t^{(f)}_{\mathrm{tad}}}{L_0}&=-\frac{q_0q_1}{8\pi^2},
 &\frac{\delta m_\phi^{2\,(f)}}{L_0}&=-\frac{q_1^2+2q_0G}{8\pi^2},\nonumber\\
 \frac{\delta h^{(f)}_{\mathrm{tri}}}{L_0}&=-\frac{3q_1G}{4\pi^2},
 &\frac{\delta\lambda^{(f)}_{\mathrm{box}}}{L_0}&=-\frac{3G^2}{2\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:9.2-sol-graph-residues}
\end{align}
The raw graph residues have the opposite signs to the entries in
\eqref{eq:9.2-sol-graph-residues}.  The scalar self-energy row combines the
$h^2$ bubble with the $\lambda m_\phi^2$ part of the quartic tadpole.  The
three scalar cubic bubbles and the three four-point channels are the three
pairings of a scalar loop.  The fermion triangle row is the zero-momentum
three-point graph, and the last row is the closed fermion box.  This displays
the tadpole, self-energy, cubic, triangle, and box terms before they are
assembled into the effective potential.

The momentum-dependent self-energies and the Yukawa vertex require the
wave-function residues as well.  In the proper-time prescription they are
\begin{align*}
 \delta Z_\phi&=-\frac{G}{8\pi^2}L_0,
 &\delta Z_\psi&=-\frac{G}{32\pi^2}L_0,\\
 \delta g\big|_{\rm external\ legs}&=-\frac{3gG}{32\pi^2}L_0,
 &\delta g\big|_{\rm triangle}&=+\frac{gG}{4\pi^2}L_0,
\end{align*}
where $g$ denotes either component of $g_S+\ii g_P$.  Their sum is
$5gG/(32\pi^2)L_0$ in the bare-coupling convention below.  The fermion
mass self-energy gives
\begin{align*}
 \delta M&=\frac{(3g_S^2-g_P^2)M+4g_Sg_PM_5}{32\pi^2}L_0,\\
 \delta M_5&=\frac{(3g_P^2-g_S^2)M_5+4g_Sg_PM}{32\pi^2}L_0.
\end{align*}
These equations give the finite proper-time remainder of each graph through
$I_1^{\mathrm{fin}}$ and $I_2^{\mathrm{fin}}$, rather than only its
aggregate pole.

Here is a useful diagram-by-diagram organization.  The scalar loop in the
four-scalar graph gives the three channels, the fermion loop gives the box,
and the scalar and fermion self-energies give the two field counterterms.
The triangle graphs correct each Yukawa vertex.  In the massless,
chirally-symmetric subsector, their complete pole subtraction is
\begin{align}
 Z_\phi^{1/2}&=1-\frac{G}{16\pi^2\varepsilon},
 &Z_\psi^{1/2}&=1-\frac{G}{64\pi^2\varepsilon}, \label{eq:9.2-sol-fields}\\
 (g_S)_0&=\mu^\varepsilon\left[g_S+
 \frac{5g_SG}{32\pi^2\varepsilon}\right],
 & (g_P)_0&=\mu^\varepsilon\left[g_P+
 \frac{5g_PG}{32\pi^2\varepsilon}\right], \label{eq:9.2-sol-yukawa}\\
 \lambda_0&=\mu^{2\varepsilon}\left[\lambda+
 \frac{3\lambda^2+8\lambda G-48G^2}{32\pi^2\varepsilon}\right],
 & (m_\phi^2)_0&=m_\phi^2\left[1+
 \frac{\lambda+4G}{32\pi^2\varepsilon}\right]. \label{eq:9.2-sol-poles}
\end{align}
The scalar self-energy is the $G$ term in $Z_\phi$, the fermion self-energy
is the $G$ term in $Z_\psi$, the triangle plus external-leg poles give
the two Yukawa relations, and the scalar fish diagrams plus the fermion box
give the quartic relation.  The last equation also follows from the scalar
tadpole graph after the massless fermion symmetry is imposed.  For
$g_P=0$, these residues reduce to the familiar one-real-scalar, one-Dirac-
fermion result.  In the Hermitian Minkowski convention used here, the
massless kinetic terms also admit an axial rephasing.  It rotates
$g_S+\ii g_P$ as a complex Yukawa coupling, so its pole residue must be
$5G(g_S+\ii g_P)$; this supplies a check on both pure and mixed limits.

For completeness, the same calculation with all relevant couplings retained
can be expressed without hiding the tadpole and cubic graphs.  Define
\[
 A(\phi)=M+g_S\phi,
 \qquad B(\phi)=M_5+g_P\phi,
 \qquad \mathcal M^2(\phi)=A(\phi)^2+B(\phi)^2 .
\]
The zero-derivative pole part of the one-loop effective action is generated
by the local polynomial
\begin{equation}
 \delta U_{\rm pole}(\phi)=\frac{1}{64\pi^2\varepsilon}[U''(\phi)]^2
 -\frac{1}{16\pi^2\varepsilon}[\mathcal M^2(\phi)]^2 .
 \label{eq:9.2-sol-potential-pole}
\end{equation}
The first term is the scalar determinant and the second is the Dirac
determinant.  Expanding it in powers of $\phi$ gives, in one expression,
the vacuum, tadpole, scalar mass, cubic, and quartic divergences.  For
$a_n\phi^n/n!$ in $U$, the corresponding one-loop beta function is
\[
 \beta_{a_n}=n\gamma_\phi a_n
 +2n! [\phi^n]\left\{
 \frac{[U'']^2}{64\pi^2}-\frac{[\mathcal M^2]^2}{16\pi^2}
 \right\},
 \qquad \gamma_\phi=\frac{G}{8\pi^2},
\]
where $[\phi^n]$ means the coefficient of $\phi^n$.  This gives the full
set of scalar relevant and marginal RG equations, including additive mass
terms when $M$ or $M_5$ is nonzero.  For an explicit form, put
\[
 q_0=M^2+M_5^2,\qquad q_1=2(Mg_S+M_5g_P).
\]
The coefficient extraction then gives
\begin{align*}
 \beta_{\Lambda_{\rm vac}}&=\frac{m_\phi^4}{32\pi^2}
 -\frac{q_0^2}{8\pi^2},\\
 \beta_t&=\gamma_\phi t+\frac{m_\phi^2h}{16\pi^2}
 -\frac{q_0q_1}{4\pi^2},\\
 \beta_{m_\phi^2}&=2\gamma_\phi m_\phi^2
 +\frac{h^2+\lambda m_\phi^2}{16\pi^2}
 -\frac{q_1^2+2q_0G}{4\pi^2},\\
 \beta_h&=3\gamma_\phi h
 +\frac{3\lambda h-24q_1G}{16\pi^2},\\
 \beta_\lambda&=4\gamma_\phi\lambda
 +\frac{3\lambda^2-48G^2}{16\pi^2}.
\end{align*}
The last line is the displayed massless result after inserting
$\gamma_\phi=G/(8\pi^2)$.  The fermion mass matrix obeys
\[
 \beta_{M+\ii M_5\gamma _5}
 =\frac{1}{16\pi^2}\left[G(M+\ii M_5\gamma _5)
 +2(g_S+\ii g_P\gamma _5)^2(M-\ii M_5\gamma _5)\right],
\]
or, after separating the two Dirac mass structures,
\begin{align*}
 \beta_M&=\frac{1}{16\pi^2}
 \bigl[(3g_S^2-g_P^2)M+4g_Sg_PM_5\bigr],\\
 \beta_{M_5}&=\frac{1}{16\pi^2}
 \bigl[(3g_P^2-g_S^2)M_5+4g_Sg_PM\bigr].
\end{align*}
Together with
\begin{align*}
 \beta_{g_S}&=\frac{5g_SG}{16\pi^2},
 &\beta_{g_P}&=\frac{5g_PG}{16\pi^2},\\
 \beta_\lambda&=\frac{3\lambda^2+8\lambda G-48G^2}{16\pi^2},
 &\beta_{m_\phi^2}&=\frac{\lambda+4G}{16\pi^2}m_\phi^2
 \quad\text{when }M=M_5=0,
\end{align*}
this is the complete one-loop RG system in the closed massless subsector and
its extension to the generic relevant deformations.

The finite prescription can be stated explicitly.  Let
\[
 L_0=\ln\frac{1}{\mu^2s_0}-\gamma_E
\]
and subtract, from every logarithmic graph, its $L_0$ term while retaining
the displayed momentum-dependent logarithm.  Subtract the $1/s_0$ terms in
the relevant operators and set the remaining finite parts of
$Z_\phi,Z_\psi$ and the couplings to zero.  In dimensional regularization
the same logarithmic integral is
\[
 I_2^{\rm DR}=\frac{1}{16\pi^2}
 \left[\frac1\varepsilon+\ln4\pi-\gamma_E
 -\ln\frac{\Delta}{\mu^2}\right]+O(\varepsilon).
\]
Thus the proper-time subtraction leaves $-\ln(\Delta/\mu^2)$, whereas
minimal subtraction leaves
$\ln4\pi-\gamma_E-\ln(\Delta/\mu^2)$.  If $r_q$ denotes the residue in
the square brackets of a bare-coupling relation, the definitions are related
at one loop by
\[
 q_{\rm PT}=q_{\rm MS}
 +(\ln4\pi-\gamma_E)r_q(q_{\rm MS})+O(\hbox{two loops}).
\]
For example, at $g_P=0$,
\begin{align*}
 g_{\rm PT}&=g_{\rm MS}
 +(\ln4\pi-\gamma_E)\frac{5g_{\rm MS}^3}{32\pi^2},\\
 \lambda_{\rm PT}&=\lambda_{\rm MS}
 +(\ln4\pi-\gamma_E)\frac{3\lambda_{\rm MS}^2
 +8\lambda_{\rm MS}g_{\rm MS}^2-48g_{\rm MS}^4}{32\pi^2}.
\end{align*}
Using $\overline{\rm MS}$ instead of MS absorbs this constant and makes the
one-loop finite redefinition vanish.  Any other finite subtraction changes
the displayed shift while leaving the one-loop beta functions unchanged.

\BanksSolution{9.3}
Let $r=x-y$ and let
\[
 G(r)=\langle T\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle_0
      =\frac{1}{4\pi^2r^2}
\]
in Euclidean four-dimensional notation.  Wick's theorem gives the operator
identity
\begin{equation}
 :\!\phi^4(x)\!::\!\phi^4(y)\!:
 =\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom4k^2 k!\,G(r)^k
 :\!\phi^{4-k}(x)\phi^{4-k}(y)\!: .
 \label{eq:9.3-sol-wick}
\end{equation}
The coefficient counts the choice of the $k$ fields at each point and the
$k!$ pairings between them.  Inserting this relation into a Green function
with any number of additional fields gives the same sum of contractions.
Contractions involving the additional fields occur after the two displayed
operators have been reduced, so their number changes matrix elements of the
local operators and never changes the coefficient in \eqref{eq:9.3-sol-wick}.
This proves that the OPE is an operator relation.

Taylor expand each of the uncontracted $x$ fields separately.  This keeps the
Leibniz descendants that are hidden when the fields are treated as one
composite.  For $\ell=4-k$, define
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal D_{k,n}(r;y)={}&
 \sum_{\substack{n_1,\ldots,n_\ell\geq0\\
                       n_1+\cdots+n_\ell=n}}
 \prod_{a=1}^{\ell}
 \frac{r^{\mu_{a,1}}\cdots r^{\mu_{a,n_a}}}{n_a!}\\[-2pt]
 &\qquad\times
 :\!\prod_{a=1}^{\ell}
 \left(\partial_{\mu_{a,1}}\cdots
       \partial_{\mu_{a,n_a}}\phi(y)\right)\phi^\ell(y)\!:,
 \end{align*}
where an empty derivative string is the field itself.  Taylor's theorem is
then
\[
 :\!\phi^{4-k}(x)\phi^{4-k}(y)\!:
 =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal D_{k,n}(r;y).
\]
For a fixed $k$, the coefficient of a term with $n$ derivatives scales as
$r^{n-2k}$. Consequently every singular term, with its exact numerical
coefficient, is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \bigl(:\!\phi^4(x)\!::\!\phi^4(y)\!:\bigr)_{\rm sing}
 =\sum_{k=1}^{4}\binom4k^2 k!\,G(r)^k
 \sum_{n=0}^{2k-1}\mathcal D_{k,n}(r;y).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:9.3-sol-singular}
 \end{equation}
The four leading terms are
\[
 24G^4+96G^3:\!\phi^2\!:
 +72G^2:\!\phi^4\!:+16G:\!\phi^6\!:,
\]
 followed in each case by the finite Taylor range displayed in
 \eqref{eq:9.3-sol-singular}.  The $n=1$ descendants are, for example,
 $96G^3r^\mu:\!\phi\partial_\mu\phi\!:$ and
 $144G^2r^\mu:\!\partial_\mu\phi\,\phi^3\!:$.  The second coefficient has
 two equal contributions, according to which of the two uncontracted fields
 at $x$ receives the derivative.  At $k=2$, the $n=2$
 term contains
 \[
 72G^2\frac{r^\mu r^\nu}{2}
 :\!\partial_\mu\partial_\nu(\phi^2)\,\phi^2\!:
 =72G^2r^\mu r^\nu
 :\!\bigl(\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi
 +\phi\,\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi\bigr)\phi^2\!: .
 \]
The first term on the right is the independent derivative-distributed
operator $:\!\phi^2(\partial\phi)^2\!:$, up to Lorentz contractions and
total derivatives.  The upper limit $n=2k-1$ is exactly the condition for a
negative power of $r$.

The full OPE is obtained by allowing $n$ to run to infinity in the same
formula and including $k=0$.  Its operator list is
\[
 \mathcal O_{p;\boldsymbol n,\boldsymbol\mu}(y)
 =:\!\prod_{a=1}^{p}
 \left(\partial_{\mu_{a,1}}\cdots
       \partial_{\mu_{a,n_a}}\phi(y)\right)\!: .
\]
Here $p>0$ takes the values $2,4,6,8$, with
\[
 n_a\geq0,\qquad s=\sum_{a=1}^{p}n_a,
 \]
 together with the identity $\mathcal O_{0}=1$.  Every distribution of the
 $s$ derivatives among the $p$ fields occurs.  Traces, equations-of-motion
 descendants, and total derivatives remain in this unreduced basis; a chosen
 quotient by those relations can be taken afterwards.  The associated Wilson
 coefficient has the functional form
\[
 C_{p;\boldsymbol n,\boldsymbol\mu}(r)=
 \text{tensor polynomial in }r\;[G(r)]^{(8-p)/2},
\]
\[
 C_{p;\boldsymbol n,\boldsymbol\mu}(\rho r)
 =\rho^{-8+p+s}C_{p;\boldsymbol n,\boldsymbol\mu}(r).
\]
The functional form is fixed by the $k=(8-p)/2$ contractions and the Taylor
factors; finite numerical factors for nonsingular terms depend on the chosen
operator basis.

\BanksSolution{9.4}
Expand $|D_\mu\phi|^2$ about the free theory.  With all external momenta
incoming, the scalar loop and the seagull loop are
\begin{align*}
 \Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\rm bub}(q)&=e^2\int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}p}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}
 \frac{(2p+q)^\mu(2p+q)^\nu}
 {(p^2+m_0^2)[(p+q)^2+m_0^2]},\\
 \Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sea}(q)&=-2e^2\delta^{\mu\nu}
 \int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}p}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}\frac1{p^2+m_0^2}.
\end{align*}
The sign of both expressions changes together with the convention for the
1PI effective action.  The relative factor of two is fixed by the two scalar
legs at the seagull vertex.

Feynman-parametrize the first integral and shift $p=l-xq$.  The numerator is
\[
 4l^\mu l^\nu+(1-2x)^2q^\mu q^\nu
\]
after the odd term is dropped.  The $l^\mu l^\nu$ term and the seagull
integral cancel the non-transverse terms.  Using
\[
 \int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}l}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}
 \frac1{(l^2+\Delta)^2}
 =\frac{\Gamma(2-d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2)}{(4\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}}
 \Delta^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2-2},
 \qquad \Delta=m_0^2+x(1-x)q^2,
\]
the sum is
\begin{equation}
 \Pi^{\mu\nu}(q)=
 (q^2\delta^{\mu\nu}-q^\mu q^\nu)
 \frac{e^2}{(4\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}}

\BanksSolution{9.5}
The covariant derivative contains the scalar--scalar--photon vertex and the
$A^2|\phi|^2$ seagull vertex.  Joining two photon propagators between scalar
lines gives a one-loop four-scalar 1PI graph.  At large loop momentum its
integrand behaves as
\[
e^4\int\frac{\dd^D p}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{1}{(p^2)^2},
\]
whose superficial degree is $D-4=0$ in the physical $D=4$ theory.  The
divergent part is
therefore a momentum-independent local polynomial with the U(1)-invariant
form $(\phi^*\phi)^2$.

For example, evaluating the crossed photon graphs in dimensional
regularization and adding the scalar diagrams produced after a quartic
coupling has been introduced gives, in the convention
$V=\lambda|\phi|^4/4$,
\[
 \lambda_0=\mu^{2\varepsilon}\left[
 \lambda+\frac{5\lambda^2-12e^2\lambda+24e^4}
 {32\pi^2\varepsilon}\right]+O(\text{two loops}).
\]
At the starting point $\lambda=0$, the $24e^4$ term is the pole from the
two-photon graph.  Adding
\[
 \delta\mathcal L=-\frac{\delta\lambda}{4}|\phi|^4,
 \qquad
 \delta\lambda=\frac{24e^4}{32\pi^2\varepsilon}
\]
cancels it.  The numerical coefficient changes under a rescaling of the
complex field or of the quartic coupling, while the local operator required
by the divergence does not.  This is the perturbative realization of the
general statement that scalar electrodynamics is not closed without the
marginal $|\phi|^4$ interaction.

 \operatorname{tr}(T_F^aT_F^b)=D_F\delta^{ab},

 C_F=T_F^aT_F^a .

 \Gamma_{\rm div}\supset
 -\frac{1}{16\pi^2\varepsilon}\int\dd^4x\,
 \frac14 F^a{}_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu}

16\pi^2(\beta_Y)_r={}&
 \frac12(Y_sY_s^\dagger Y_r+Y_rY_s^\dagger Y_s)
 +2Y_sY_r^\dagger Y_s\nonumber\\
 &+\frac12Y_s\operatorname{tr}(Y_s^\dagger Y_r+Y_r^\dagger Y_s)
 -3g^2\{C_F,Y_r\}. \label{eq:9.6-sol-yukawa}
\end{align}

 V=\frac12m^2_{rs}\phi_r\phi_s+\frac1{3!}h_{rst}\phi_r\phi_s\phi_t

The $a_1$ terms cancel.  The coefficient $a_2$ first contributes at order
$g_1^7$, so the first two beta coefficients are unchanged by every regular
power-series redefinition of the coupling.

\BanksSolution{9.10}
For a rectangular Wilson loop with temporal extent $T$ and spatial extent
$R$, define
\[
 V(R)=-\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\ln
 \frac{\langle W_R(T,R)\rangle}{d_R}.
\]
The two temporal Wilson-line vertices are $g_0T_R^a\delta_{\mu0}$ and
$-g_0T_R^a\delta_{\mu0}$.  At order $g_0^2$, the cross term between the
lines gives
\[
 V^{(0)}(R)=-\frac{g_0^2C_R}{4\pi R},
 \qquad T_R^aT_R^a=C_R\mathbf1_R.
\]
At order $g_0^4$ the self-energy pieces independent of $R$ are removed from
the potential.  The remaining graphs are the vacuum-polarization insertion,
the two three-gluon-line graphs, and the path-ordering commutator graph.  In
Feynman gauge the direct triple-gluon graph with all source indices temporal
vanishes.  Combining the other graphs before taking the ultraviolet limit
gives a transverse gluon self-energy
\[
 \Pi_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}(q)=\delta^{ab}(q^2\eta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu)
 \Pi(q^2).
\]
The disconnected two-gluon graphs exponentiate.  Explicitly, the connected
order-$g_0^4$ term in the logarithm is
\[
 \ln W\big|_{g_0^4,\,\mathrm{conn}}
 =W^{(4)}-\frac12\bigl(W^{(2)}\bigr)^2.
\]
Path ordering supplies the non-Abelian remainder through
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(T_R^aT_R^bT_R^aT_R^b)
 =\operatorname{tr}(T_R^aT_R^aT_R^bT_R^b)
 +\frac12\operatorname{tr}[T_R^a,T_R^b]^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}[T_R^a,T_R^b]^2=-C_A C_Rd_R.
\]
The first term is the square removed by the logarithm.  To evaluate the
remaining three-line and commutator graphs, use
\begin{align*}
 D(t,\mathbf R)&=\int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}p}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}
 \frac{\ee^{-\ii(tp_0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf R)}}{p^2-\ii0}\\
 &=\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt\pi}\right)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}
 \int_0^\infty\dd\alpha\,\alpha^{-d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}
 \ee^{-(t^2+R^2)/(4\alpha)}.
\end{align*}
The time orderings on the two orientations of the long sides reduce their
sum to the proper-time integral
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_{\mathrm{po}}(R,d_{\mathrm{reg}})={}&-
 \frac{g_0^4\mu^{2(4-d_{\mathrm{reg}})}C_A C_R d_R}
 {2^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}\pi^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}}\\[-2pt]
 &\qquad\times4\sqrt\pi\int_0^\infty\dd\alpha\,\dd\beta\,
 (\alpha\beta)^{-d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}\ee^{-R^2/(4\beta)}\alpha\sqrt\beta
 \nonumber\\[-2pt]
 &\qquad\times\left[1-\ee^{-R^2/(4\alpha)}
 \sqrt{1+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\right].
 \label{eq:9.10-sol-po-alpha}
\end{align}
With $\alpha=s(1-x)$ and $\beta=sx$, the Gaussian integrations give
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_{\mathrm{po}}(R,d_{\mathrm{reg}})={}&-
 \frac{g_0^4\mu^{2(4-d_{\mathrm{reg}})}C_A C_Rd_R}{2^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}\pi^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}}
 4\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma\!\left(d_{\mathrm{reg}}-\frac72\right)
 \left(\frac{R^2}{4}\right)^{7/2-d_{\mathrm{reg}}}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\times\int_0^1\dd x\,x^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2-3}
 \left[(1-x)^{1-d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}-(1-x)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2-3}\right].
 \label{eq:9.10-sol-po-beta}
\end{align}
The endpoint pole in the first term is the short-distance pole of one
propagator.  The second term is its path-ordering subtraction.  This is the
explicit Wilson-line graph integral, rather than a power-counting statement about it.  To
make the finite evaluation explicit, the remaining parameter integral is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 {\cal B}(d_{\mathrm{reg}})&=\int_0^1\dd x\,x^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2-3}
 \left[(1-x)^{1-d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}-(1-x)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2-3}\right]\\
 &=B\!\left(\frac {d_{\mathrm{reg}}}{2}-2,2-\frac {d_{\mathrm{reg}}}{2}\right)
  -B\!\left(\frac {d_{\mathrm{reg}}}{2}-2,\frac {d_{\mathrm{reg}}}{2}-2\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
under the same analytic continuation used for the loop integral.  Expanding
this expression at $d_{\mathrm{reg}}=4-2\varepsilon$, together with the transverse
polarization below, fixes both the pole and the finite Wilson-line constant.

For the vacuum-polarization insertion, the traces of the three pure-gauge
graphs in Feynman gauge are
\begin{align*}
 \Pi_{3g\,\mu}{}^{\mu\,ab}&=
 3(d_{\mathrm{reg}}-1)g_0^2\mu^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}-4}\delta^{ab}C_A
 \int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}p}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}
 \frac{p^2+q^2+p\mathbin{\cdot}q}{(p^2-\ii0)((p+q)^2-\ii0)},\\
 \Pi_{4g}{}^{\mu a\,\nu b}&=-C_A(d_{\mathrm{reg}}-1)g_0^2\mu^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}-4}\delta^{ab}\eta^{\mu\nu}
 \int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}p}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}
 \frac{p^2+q^2+2p\mathbin{\cdot}q}{(p^2-\ii0)((p+q)^2-\ii0)},\\
 \Pi_{\mathrm{gh}}^{\mu a\nu b}&=-g_0^2\mu^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}-4}C_A\delta^{ab}
 \int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}p}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}
 \frac{(p+q)^\mu p^\nu}{(p^2-\ii0)((p+q)^2-\ii0)}.
\end{align*}
After the common Feynman shift $r=p-xq$, their summed numerator is
\[
 N(d_{\mathrm{reg}},x,r,q)=-(d_{\mathrm{reg}}-2)^2(r^2+x^2q^2)
 +(2d_{\mathrm{reg}}^2-5d_{\mathrm{reg}}+4)xq^2.
\]
The factor $(d_{\mathrm{reg}}-2)^2$ removes the apparent quadratic pole, while the
logarithmic pole remains.  Using
\[
 \int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}r}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}
 \frac{1}{(r^2+\Delta)^n}
 =\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2}}
 \frac{\Gamma(n-d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2)}{\Gamma(n)}
 \Delta^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}/2-n}
\]
and expanding $d_{\mathrm{reg}}=4-2\varepsilon$, the renormalized sum of the transverse
polarization and \eqref{eq:9.10-sol-po-beta} is
\begin{align}
 {\cal K}_{00}^{ab}(\mathbf q)
 &\equiv\left[\frac{1}{\mathbf q^2}(-\Pi_{00}^{ab})
 \frac{1}{\mathbf q^2}\right]_{\rm YM}\nonumber\\
 &=-\frac{g^2(\mu)C_A\delta^{ab}}{16\pi^2\mathbf q^2}
 \left[-\frac{11}{3\bar\varepsilon}
 +\frac{11}{3}\ln\frac{\mathbf q^2}{\mu^2}-\frac{31}{9}\right],\\
 \frac1{\bar\varepsilon}&=\frac1\varepsilon-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi .
 \label{eq:9.10-sol-coulomb-finite}
\end{align}
Here the $-11/(3\bar\varepsilon)$ term is removed by the coupling
counterterm; the finite $31/9$ is the constant left by the evaluated
Beta-function and momentum integrals.
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde V_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(1)}(\mathbf q)
 =-\frac{C_Rg^4(\mu)}{16\pi^2\mathbf q^2}
 \left[\frac{11}{3}C_A\ln\frac{\mu^2}{\mathbf q^2}
       +\frac{31}{9}C_A\right].
 \label{eq:9.10-sol-finite-kernel}
\end{equation}
The $C_A$ pole in the same calculation is $11C_A/(3\varepsilon)$ and is
removed by the gauge-coupling counterterm.  Equation
\eqref{eq:9.10-sol-finite-kernel} records the finite constants from the
three-gluon, four-gluon, ghost, and path-ordering graphs after the
exponentiating square has been removed.
The matter contributions are
\[
 \Pi_{F+S}{}^{\mu a\,\nu b}\big|_{\rm div}
 =\ii(q^2\eta^{\mu\nu}-q^\mu q^\nu)\delta^{ab}
 \left(-\frac{g^2}{16\pi^2}\right)
 \left[\frac23D(R_F)+\frac13D(R_S)\right]\frac1\varepsilon,
\]
for Weyl fermions and complex scalars.  The pure gauge, ghost, and seagull
pieces add the $11C_2(G)/3$ term.  The one-loop coefficient is consequently
\[
 b_0=\frac{11}{3}C_2(G)-\frac{2}{3}D(R_F)-\frac{1}{3}D(R_S),
\]
where a real scalar contributes half the displayed complex-scalar amount.

After the UV pole is removed, the sum of the vacuum-polarization and
Wilson-line graphs can be organized in momentum space as
\[
 \widetilde V(\mathbf q)=-\frac{C_Rg^2(\mu)}{\mathbf q^2}
 \left[1+\frac{g^2(\mu)}{16\pi^2}
 \left(b_0\ln\frac{\mu^2}{\mathbf q^2}+a_1\right)\right]
 +O(g^6).
\]
The logarithmic Fourier transform is fixed by analytic continuation in the
power of $\mathbf q^2$.  In three spatial dimensions,
\[
 J(\alpha)=\int\frac{\dd^3\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}
 \frac{\ee^{\ii\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}}{(\mathbf q^2)^\alpha}
 =\frac{\Gamma(3/2-\alpha)}{4^\alpha\pi^{3/2}\Gamma(\alpha)}
 R^{2\alpha-3},
\]
so differentiating $J(\alpha)$ at $\alpha=1$ gives
% CONVENTION-SCOPE: fixed-D=4 reason=three-spatial-dimensional Fourier transform
\[
 \int\frac{\dd^3\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\ee^{\ii\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}}
 {\mathbf q^2}\ln\frac{\mu^2}{\mathbf q^2}
 =\frac{1}{4\pi R}\,2\ln(\mu R\ee^{\gamma_E}),
 \qquad
 \int\frac{\dd^3\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\ee^{\ii\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R}}
 {\mathbf q^2}=\frac{1}{4\pi R}.
\]

The Fourier transforms of the finite logarithms and the constant parts of the
three-gluon graphs give the MS-bar coordinate-space potential.  In the physical
three-spatial-dimensional slice,
\begin{equation}
 V(R)=-\frac{C_Rg^2(\mu)}{4\pi R}
 \left[1+\frac{g^2(\mu)}{16\pi^2}
 \left(2b_0\ln(\mu R\ee^{\gamma_E})+a_1\right)
 +O(g^4)\right].
 \label{eq:9.10-sol-potential}
\end{equation}
For pure Yang--Mills theory,
\[
 a_1=\frac{31}{9}C_2(G),
 \qquad b_0=\frac{11}{3}C_2(G).
\]
With Weyl fermions and complex scalars in representations with Dynkin indices
$D(R_F)$ and $D(R_S)$, the same finite polarization calculation gives
\[
 a_1=\frac{31}{9}C_2(G)-\frac{10}{9}D(R_F)-\frac89D(R_S),
 \qquad
 b_0=\frac{11}{3}C_2(G)-\frac23D(R_F)-\frac13D(R_S),
\]
and a real scalar contributes half of the complex-scalar term.  In a
minimal-subtraction convention rather than MS-bar, the constant is shifted by
the usual $\ln4\pi-\gamma_E$.

The explicit $\mu$ derivative of the logarithm in
\eqref{eq:9.10-sol-potential} is canceled by the running of the tree
coupling.  Thus
\[
 \mu\frac{\dd g}{\dd\mu}=\beta(g)
 =-\frac{g^3}{16\pi^2}\left[
 \frac{11}{3}C_2(G)-\frac{2}{3}D(R_F)-\frac{1}{3}D(R_S)\right]
 +O(g^5).
\]
For pure Yang--Mills this is
\[
 \boxed{\beta(g)=-\frac{11C_2(G)}{48\pi^2}g^3+O(g^5).}
\]
The negative sign makes the coupling decrease at short distance, and the
potential is the Coulomb form with the coupling evaluated at scale $1/R$.
