\BanksSolution{10.1}
Let the false vacuum be $\phi_f$, with $V(\phi_f)=0$, and let
$\phi_b(t)$ denote the bounce centered at the origin.  For an
$SO(D)$-invariant configuration the Euclidean action, with the angular volume
written as $\Omega_{D-1}$, is
\[
 S_E[\phi]=\Omega_{D-1}\int_0^\infty\dd t\,t^{D-1}
 \left[\frac12\dot\phi^{\,2}+V(\phi)\right].
\]
Varying this expression, with $\dot\phi(0)=0$ imposed by regularity, gives
\[
 \ddot\phi+\frac{D-1}{t}\dot\phi-V'(\phi)=0,
 \qquad
 \dot\phi(0)=0,\quad \phi(\infty)=\phi_f.
\]
Thus the radial variable plays the role of Euclidean time for a particle in
the inverted potential $-V$, with a friction coefficient $(D-1)/t$.

The mechanical energy for this radial problem is
\[
 {\cal E}(t)=\frac12\dot\phi^{\,2}-V(\phi),
 \qquad
 \dot{\cal E}(t)=-\frac{D-1}{t}\dot\phi^{\,2}\leq0.
\]
The value of $\phi(0)$ is free.  Initial values sufficiently close to the
true-vacuum side of the maximum of $-V$ give undershoot trajectories: friction
removes enough energy that the trajectory does not reach $\phi_f$.  Initial
values chosen so that the trajectory spends a long time near that maximum
leave the friction-dominated region with nearly conserved energy and give an
overshoot.  Dependence on the initial value is continuous, so the boundary
trajectory between these two sets approaches $\phi_f$ only at $t=\infty$.
This is the finite-action bounce.  Indeed, with
$m_f^2=V''(\phi_f)>0$, its large-$t$ tail obeys
\[
 \ddot\eta+\frac{D-1}{t}\dot\eta-m_f^2\eta=0,
 \qquad \eta=\phi_b-\phi_f,
\]
and the decaying solution has the asymptotics
\[
 \eta(t)=O\!\left(t^{-(D-1)/2}\ee^{-m_ft}\right).
\]
At the origin regularity gives $\phi_b(t)=\phi_b(0)+O(t^2)$, so both the
origin and infinity give convergent contributions to $S_E$.

Write a general small fluctuation as
$\phi(\mathbf x)=\phi_b(t)+\eta(\mathbf x)$, where $t=|\mathbf x|$.  The quadratic action is
\[
 S_E^{(2)}=\frac12\int\dd^D\mathbf x\,\eta\,{\cal M}\eta,
 \qquad
 {\cal M}=-\nabla^2+V''(\phi_b(t)).
\]
Using spherical harmonics on $S^{D-1}$,
\[
 \eta(t,\Omega)=\sum_{\ell,m}\eta_{\ell m}(t)Y_{\ell m}(\Omega),
 \qquad
 -\nabla_{S^{D-1}}^2Y_{\ell m}=\ell(\ell+D-2)Y_{\ell m},
\]
reduces the fluctuation problem to
\[
 {\cal M}_\ell=-\frac{\dd^2}{\dd t^2}
 -\frac{D-1}{t}\frac{\dd}{\dd t}
 +\frac{\ell(\ell+D-2)}{t^2}+V''(\phi_b(t)).
\]
The regular radial functions behave as $t^\ell$ at the origin and decay at
infinity.

Translations of the center produce the exact zero modes
\[
\eta_\mu(\mathbf x)=\frac{\partial}{\partial a_\mu}
 \phi_b(|\mathbf x-\mathbf a|)\bigg|_{\mathbf a=0}
 =-\frac{x_\mu}{t}\phi_b'(t),
 \qquad \mu=1,\ldots,D.
\]
They have $\ell=1$, and there are exactly $D$ of them for a generic
single-field bounce.  Their coefficients form the defining vector
representation of $SO(D)$.  Extra exact zero modes would require an extra
continuous symmetry, such as an internal symmetry or a scale symmetry, which
is absent for a generic two-minimum potential.

For the negative mode, assume the usual monotone single-field bounce and put
$u(t)=-\phi_b'(t)>0$ for $t>0$.  Differentiating the bounce equation shows
\[
 {\cal M}_1u=0.
\]
The function $u$ is nodeless, regular in the $\ell=1$ sector, and normalizable.
The Sturm--Liouville theorem therefore makes it the lowest state of
${\cal M}_1$, so ${\cal M}_1$ has no negative eigenvalue.  Since
\[
 {\cal M}_\ell={\cal M}_1+
 \frac{\ell(\ell+D-2)-(D-1)}{t^2},
 \qquad \ell\geq1,
\]
all sectors with $\ell\geq1$ are non-negative; the $\ell=1$ sector has the
translation zero modes and the sectors with $\ell\geq2$ are positive.

The $\ell=0$ sector has a negative direction.  For example, the quadratic
form of ${\cal M}_0$ evaluated on the nodeless function $u$ is lower than the
corresponding ${\cal M}_1$ form by
\[
 \langle u,({\cal M}_0-{\cal M}_1)u\rangle
 =-(D-1)\int_0^\infty\dd t\,t^{D-3}u(t)^2<0
 \]
for $D>1$.  The one-parameter shooting family also gives the upper bound on
the count.  If $\phi(t;\phi_0)$ is the regular trajectory with central value
$\phi_0$, then
\[
 w(t)=\frac{\partial\phi(t;\phi_0)}{\partial\phi_0}
\]
at the tuned value of $\phi_0$ solves ${\cal M}_0w=0$ with
$w(0)=1$ and $w'(0)=0$.  The undershoot/overshoot crossing makes this regular
zero-energy solution acquire exactly one node.  The Sturm oscillation theorem
then gives exactly one negative eigenvalue in the $\ell=0$ sector.  For $D=1$
the same conclusion is the standard one-dimensional bounce result, obtained
directly from the same shooting argument.  Hence the generic bounce has one
negative mode and $D$ translational zero modes.

\BanksSolution{10.2}
Let $d_{\rm s}$ denote the number of spatial coordinates in the static Derrick
argument.  Shift the potential so that the vacuum energy is zero and write the
static energy as
\[
 E[\phi]=T+U,
 \qquad
 T=\frac12\int\dd^{d_{\rm s}}\mathbf x\,\partial_a\phi^i\partial_a\phi^i,
 \qquad
 U=\int\dd^{d_{\rm s}}\mathbf x\,V(\phi).
\]
For a stable vacuum, $T\geq0$ and the finite-energy configurations under
consideration have $U\geq0$.  Define
$\phi_a(\mathbf x)=\phi(a\mathbf x)$.  After the change of variables
$\mathbf y=a\mathbf x$,
\[
 T[a]=a^{2-d_{\rm s}}T,\qquad U[a]=a^{-d_{\rm s}}U,\qquad
 E[a]=a^{2-d_{\rm s}}T+a^{-d_{\rm s}}U.
\]
For $d_{\rm s}>2$, every non-vacuum configuration has $E[a]<E[1]$ for $a>1$ close
enough to one.  Equivalently,
\[
 E'(1)=(2-d_{\rm s})T-d_{\rm s}U<0
\]
unless $T=U=0$.  The field shrinks under this variation and its energy falls,
so a finite, nonsingular static configuration cannot be a local energy minimum.
At $d_{\rm s}=2$ the gradient term is scale invariant and a nonzero potential
term still decreases under $a>1$.

Nothing in this argument uses there being only one scalar.  For $N$ scalars,
\[
 T=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int\dd^{d_{\rm s}}\mathbf x\,(\nabla\phi^i)^2
\]
has the same scaling.  More generally a positive target-space metric
$G_{ij}(\phi)$ only replaces the integrand by
$G_{ij}\partial_a\phi^i\partial_a\phi^j/2$, and the powers of $a$ are
unchanged.  Thus Derrick's conclusion applies to any finite collection of
canonical scalars without a further constraint.

For the time-dependent part, let $D$ be the Lorentzian spacetime dimension, so
$\mathbf x$ has $D-1$ components.  With mostly-plus signature, take
\[
 \mathcal L=-\partial_\mu\Phi^*\partial^\mu\Phi-V(\Phi^*\Phi).
\]
The positive-frequency ansatz is
$\Phi(t,\mathbf x)=\ee^{-\ii\omega t}\varphi(\mathbf x)$ with $\omega>0$.  With
the current convention in the problem,
\[
 Q=\ii\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\left(\Phi^*\dot\Phi-
 \Phi\dot\Phi^*\right)=2\omega I,
 \qquad I=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\,|\varphi|^2.
\]
The energy of the ansatz is
\[
 E_\omega=T+U+\omega^2I,
 \qquad
 T=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\,|\nabla\varphi|^2,
 \qquad
 U=\int\dd^{D-1}\mathbf x\,V(|\varphi|^2).
\]
For a fixed charge, eliminate $\omega$ to obtain
\[
 \mathcal H_Q[\varphi]=T+U+\frac{Q^2}{4I}.
\]
Under the spatial rescaling $\varphi_a(\mathbf x)=\varphi(a\mathbf x)$,
\[
 T[a]=a^{3-D}T,\qquad U[a]=a^{1-D}U,\qquad I[a]=a^{1-D}I,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \mathcal H_Q[a]=a^{3-D}T+a^{1-D}U+a^{D-1}\frac{Q^2}{4I}.
\]
The fixed-charge term changes the scaling of the energy, so Derrick's
unconstrained descent does not apply.  A stationary Q-ball obeys the virial
relation
\[
 (D-3)T+(D-1)U=(D-1)\frac{Q^2}{4I},
\]
which can have solutions.  The conserved charge supplies the constraint that
allows a localized charged soliton to be stable.

\BanksSolution{10.3}
Let $\widetilde\phi$ be a lift of the angular variable to the real line.  A
finite-action Euclidean trajectory approaches minima of $V$ at both ends and
has $\dot{\widetilde\phi}\to0$.  Since the minima repeat as
$\widetilde\phi_k=\widetilde\phi_0+2\pi k$,
\[
 N=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\tau\,
 \dot{\widetilde\phi}
 =\frac{\widetilde\phi(+\infty)-\widetilde\phi(-\infty)}{2\pi}
 \in\mathbb Z.
\]
On the circle the two endpoints represent the same point when $N\ne0$, while
on the real line they are distinct neighboring minima.  The ordinary
Euclidean action is finite: its first integral is
\[
 \frac12\dot{\widetilde\phi}^{\,2}-V(\widetilde\phi)=0,
\]
so an instanton connecting two adjacent minima has
\[
 S_{\rm inst}=\int\dd\widetilde\phi\,\sqrt{2V(\widetilde\phi)},
\]
with the integral taken across the barrier.  The exponential approach to the
minima makes this integral convergent, and an $N$-instanton sector has the
topological integral $2\pi N$.

With the literal normalization in the problem,
\[
 S_{\rm top}^{\rm L}=\theta\int\dd t\,\dot\phi=2\pi\theta N,
 \qquad
 \exp(-S_{\rm top}^{\rm E})=\ee^{\ii 2\pi\theta N}.
\]
It is customary to write the coefficient as $\vartheta/(2\pi)$, so that
$\vartheta$ is an angular Bloch parameter.  In that notation
\[
 \delta L=\frac{\vartheta}{2\pi}\dot\phi,
 \qquad
 \exp(-S_{\rm top}^{\rm E})=\ee^{\ii\vartheta N}.
\]
Thus the symbol called $\theta$ in the displayed Bloch condition is the
angular parameter $\vartheta$ in this normalized convention; the two formulas
are related by $\vartheta=2\pi\theta$ when the coefficient of $\dot\phi$ is
read literally.

The relation to Bloch sectors can be seen directly in the Hamiltonian.  Put
$\alpha=\vartheta/(2\pi)$.  The canonical momentum and Hamiltonian on the
circle are
\[
 p=\dot\phi+\alpha,
 \qquad
 H_\alpha=\frac12(p-\alpha)^2+V(\phi).
\]
For $U_\alpha(\phi)=\ee^{\ii\alpha\phi}$,
\[
 H_\alpha U_\alpha=U_\alpha H_0.
\]
If $u(\phi)$ is periodic, then
\[
 (U_\alpha u)(\phi+2\pi)=\ee^{\ii\vartheta}(U_\alpha u)(\phi).
\]
Therefore the periodic theory with the topological term is isospectral to
the theory on the line restricted to the Bloch sector
\[
 \psi(\phi+2\pi)=\ee^{\ii\vartheta}\psi(\phi).
\]

The same statement follows in the path integral.  Let $K_n$ be the ordinary
Euclidean kernel in the sector whose lifted endpoints differ by $2\pi n$.
Summing the sectors with the topological phase gives
\[
 Z_\vartheta(\beta)=\int_0^{2\pi}\dd\phi_0
 \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\ee^{\ii n\vartheta}K_n(\phi_0,\phi_0;\beta),
\]
up to the harmless choice of orientation for $n$ and for the translation
operator.  Translation of a Bloch wave multiplies it by
$\ee^{\ii\vartheta}$, so this sum is precisely the trace of
$\ee^{-\beta H_0}$ in that Bloch sector.  With operator insertions the same
sector sum gives the Bloch-sector Euclidean correlator.  Since
\[
 Z_\vartheta(\beta)\mathop{\sim}_{\beta\to\infty}
 \ee^{-\beta E_0(\vartheta)},
\]
normalized correlators tend to expectation values in the lowest-energy state
$|0,\vartheta\rangle$ with Bloch momentum $\vartheta$ (called $\theta$ in
the problem).

\BanksSolution{10.4}
Let $\phi_I(\tau)$ solve the one-dimensional Euclidean equation
\[
 \frac{\dd^2\phi_I}{\dd\tau^2}=V'(\phi_I),
 \qquad
 \phi_I(\tau)\longrightarrow\phi_\pm
 \quad\text{as }\tau\longrightarrow\pm\infty,
\]
where $\phi_\pm$ are degenerate vacua.  In a Lorentzian theory in one higher
dimension, take a static field depending on one spatial coordinate $z$:
\[
 \Phi(t,z)=\phi_I(z).
\]
Its static field equation is
\[
 -\frac{\dd^2\Phi}{\dd z^2}+V'(\Phi)=0,
\]
which is the same equation.  The solution approaches different vacua on the
two sides of $z=0$, so it is a kink, or domain wall.  In a theory with more
spatial coordinates, extend it uniformly along the wall,
$\Phi(t,z,\mathbf y)=\phi_I(z)$.  The energy is infinite only through the
area of the wall; its tension is finite and equals the instanton action per
unit parallel volume:
\[
 \sigma=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd z
 \left[\frac12(\phi_I')^2+V(\phi_I)-V(\phi_\pm)\right].
\]
The first integral gives
$\frac12(\phi_I')^2=V(\phi_I)-V(\phi_\pm)$, and hence
\[
 \sigma=\int_{\phi_-}^{\phi_+}\dd\phi\,
 \sqrt{2\bigl(V(\phi)-V(\phi_\pm)\bigr)}<\infty.
\]

For the two-dimensional Euclidean Higgs model, write the finite-action
instanton as $(\phi(x_1,x_2),A_1(x_1,x_2),A_2(x_1,x_2))$.  It has a regular core
where $\phi$ vanishes and a winding phase at infinity.  Set $A_0=0$ and make
the configuration static in the $(2+1)$-dimensional Lorentzian theory.  The
static energy is the two-dimensional Euclidean action, so the localized
configuration is a particle in the two spatial dimensions, with mass equal to
its instanton action.

The same fields can be extended along a third spatial coordinate $z$ by taking
them independent of $z$.  In $(3+1)$ dimensions this gives a string with
finite energy per unit length,
\[
 \frac{E}{L_z}=S_{E,2},
\]
and magnetic flux
\[
  \Phi_B=\int\dd^2\mathbf x\,F_{12}=\frac{2\pi n}{m}
\]
for a Higgs field of charge $m$.  The localized two-dimensional core and its
quantized flux are the Abrikosov--Nielsen--Olesen vortex data.  This is the
same instanton viewed successively as a particle and as a flux string.

\BanksSolution{10.5}
Write the Euclidean coordinate as a quaternionic matrix
$x=x_\mu\sigma^\mu$.  The unit vector in the source ansatz is
$\hat x=x/|x|$, and the winding-one map is
$g_1(\hat x)=\hat x_\mu\sigma^\mu$.  Every $R\in SO(4)$ acts on this matrix
by left and right $SU(2)$ multiplication.  Thus there are constant matrices
$L_R,V_R\in SU(2)$ such that
\[
 g_1(R\hat x)=L_Rg_1(\hat x)V_R^{-1}.
\]
The instanton one-form is
\[
 A=g_1^{-1}\dd g_1\,f(x^2),
 \qquad f(x^2)=\frac{x^2}{x^2+\rho^2}.
\]
Pulling it back by the rotation and using $(Rx)^2=x^2$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R^*A
 &=g_1(R\hat x)^{-1}\dd g_1(R\hat x)\,f(x^2)\\
 &=V_Rg_1(\hat x)^{-1}\dd g_1(\hat x)V_R^{-1}f(x^2)\\
 &=V_RA V_R^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The left factor $L_R$ cancels, while the right factor is a constant gauge
transformation.  Since $F=\dd A-A^2$ in the source convention,
\[
 R^*F=V_RF V_R^{-1}.
\]
The same covariance holds for every covariant derivative of $F$, because
$V_R$ is constant.  A gauge-invariant local function of the field is invariant
under conjugation, and all contractions of Euclidean indices with
$\delta_{\mu\nu}$ or $\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\kappa}$ are invariant under
$SO(4)$.  Therefore, for every such scalar observable ${\cal O}$,
\[
 {\cal O}[F](Rx)={\cal O}[F](x).
\]
The instanton is consequently rotationally invariant in the stated physical
sense: a rotation changes its gauge representative but leaves every
gauge-invariant function of the field unchanged.
