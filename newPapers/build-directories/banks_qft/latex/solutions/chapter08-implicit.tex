\begin{bankssolutions}{Solutions to Implicit Exercises for Chapter 8}

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-001}
In four dimensions the canonical dimensions are
\[
 [A_\mu]=[\phi]=1,\qquad [\psi]=\frac32,
 \qquad [D_\mu]=1,\qquad [F_{\mu\nu}]=2.
\]
With $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-\ii A_\mu$ and
$[D_\mu,D_\nu]=-\ii F_{\mu\nu}$,
\[
 F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu
 -\ii[A_\mu,A_\nu].
\]
We may therefore retain gauge-invariant operators of dimension at most four.
After constant field redefinitions put the kinetic matrices into canonical
form, the answer can be written
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L={}&-\sum_r\frac{1}{2g_r^2}
   \Tr(F_{r\mu\nu}F_r{}^{\mu\nu})\\
 &-\sum_r\frac{\theta_r}{32\pi^2}
   \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
   \Tr(F_{r\mu\nu}F_{r\rho\sigma})\\
 &-(D_\mu\phi)^\dagger D^\mu\phi
   -\psi_I^\dagger(\ii\bar\sigma^\mu D_\mu)\psi_I
   -V(\phi)\\
 &-\left[\frac12m_{IJ}\psi_I\psi_J
   +\frac12y_{aIJ}\phi_a\psi_I\psi_J+\mathrm{h.c.}\right].
 \tag{8.I.1}
\end{align*}
Equation (8.I.1) uses complex scalar notation.  A real scalar multiplet has
kinetic term $-\tfrac12(D_\mu\varphi)^T D^\mu\varphi$; the real-component
potential (8.I.2) uses this normalization.
For several abelian factors, the two gauge-field sums in (8.I.1) are replaced
by real symmetric kinetic and theta matrices.  Kinetic mixing between distinct
simple non-abelian factors is forbidden because their adjoint indices cannot
be contracted invariantly.

It is clearest to describe the general potential with real scalar components
$\varphi_A$.  It is
\[
 V=\Lambda+t_A\varphi_A+\frac12(m^2)_{AB}\varphi_A\varphi_B
 +\frac1{3!}\kappa_{ABC}\varphi_A\varphi_B\varphi_C
 +\frac1{4!}\lambda_{ABCD}\varphi_A\varphi_B\varphi_C\varphi_D.
 \tag{8.I.2}
\]
Each displayed tensor must be a singlet in the appropriate tensor product.
For example,
\[
 (T^a)_A{}^{A'}\lambda_{A'BCD}
 +(T^a)_B{}^{B'}\lambda_{AB'CD}
 +(T^a)_C{}^{C'}\lambda_{ABC'D}
 +(T^a)_D{}^{D'}\lambda_{ABCD'}=0,
 \tag{8.I.3}
\]
and analogous equations hold for $t,m^2,$ and $\kappa$.  A tadpole is thus
possible only for a gauge singlet.  Formula (8.I.2) includes holomorphic and
mixed monomials when rewritten in complex fields.

The Lorentz contraction of two left-handed Weyl fields is symmetric under
interchange of their species labels.  Hence $m_{IJ}=m_{JI}$ and
$y_{aIJ}=y_{aJI}$.  Gauge invariance says
\begin{align*}
 (T_\psi^a)_I{}^K m_{KJ}+(T_\psi^a)_J{}^K m_{IK}&=0,\\
 (T_\phi^a)_b{}^c y_{cIJ}
 +(T_\psi^a)_I{}^K y_{bKJ}
 +(T_\psi^a)_J{}^K y_{bIK}&=0.
\end{align*}
Thus a mass or Yukawa coupling exists exactly when the indicated tensor
product contains a singlet.  Banks's $M(\phi)$ packages the constant matrix
$m$ and the term linear in $\phi$.

A Pauli operator has the form
$\psi\sigma^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\psi$ and dimension five.  A four-fermion
operator has dimension six.  Their coefficients carry negative mass
dimension, so they belong to an effective-field-theory extension rather than
the perturbatively renormalizable action.  The theta term is retained even
though it is a total derivative locally, since topologically nontrivial gauge
fields make it physically relevant.  These observations reproduce the action
written immediately after Banks's cue.

For chiral fermions this operator list is only the classical answer.  Quantum
BRST invariance further requires cancellation of every local gauge anomaly,
including mixed anomalies among product-group factors; a global gauge anomaly
must also be absent.  The anomaly conditions are developed later in this
chapter.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-002}
Begin with a massive vector described without gauge redundancy.  Use
$\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-,+,\ldots,+)$, $p^2=-m^2$ on shell, and
$p\cdot x=-p^0x^0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x$ with positive-frequency factor
$\ee^{+\ii p\cdot x}$.  Its Proca propagator is
\[
 D_{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{-\ii}{k^2+m^2-\ii0}
 \left(\eta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{k_\mu k_\nu}{m^2}\right).
 \tag{8.I.4}
\]
The second term tends to a constant at large momentum.  Equivalently, an
external longitudinal polarization obeys
\[
 \epsilon_L^\mu(p)=\frac{p^\mu}{m}+O(m/E).
 \tag{8.I.5}
\]
Generic dimension-four cubic and quartic interactions therefore produce
$W_LW_L\to W_LW_L$ terms proportional to $E^4/m^4$ and $E^2/m^2$.  Loop
graphs made from (8.I.4) require counterterms of unbounded dimension.

Let $V_\mu{}^A$ denote all spin-one fields, including the connection of the
original group and the charged vectors.  The most general one-derivative
cubic vertex that can pass the leading longitudinal test can be put, by
integrations by parts and field redefinitions, in the form
\[
 \mathcal L_3=-\frac12 C_{ABC}
 (\partial_\mu V_\nu{}^A-\partial_\nu V_\mu{}^A)V^{B\mu}V^{C\nu}.
 \tag{8.I.6}
\]
Cancellation after replacing any polarization by its momentum first makes
$C_{ABC}$ totally antisymmetric.  Factorization of the four-point amplitude
then fixes its contact term to the one obtained by expanding
\[
 -\frac14\mathcal F_{\mu\nu}{}^A\mathcal F^{A\mu\nu},
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_{\mu\nu}{}^A=\partial_\mu V_\nu{}^A-\partial_\nu V_\mu{}^A
 +C^A{}_{BC}V_\mu{}^BV_\nu{}^C.
 \tag{8.I.7}
\]
At the next order, the amplitude with one longitudinal polarization vanishes
only when
\[
 C^A{}_{EB}C^E{}_{CD}+C^A{}_{EC}C^E{}_{DB}
 +C^A{}_{ED}C^E{}_{BC}=0.
 \tag{8.I.8}
\]
This is the Jacobi identity.  The constants $C^A{}_{BC}$ are consequently
the structure constants of a Lie algebra, and (8.I.7) is its Yang--Mills
field strength.  The original gauge group is the subalgebra left manifest;
the fields called ``charged vectors'' are connection components along the
remaining generators.

Decomposing (8.I.7) under the original gauge group also fixes the magnetic
moment of a charged massive component.  Its quadratic coupling to a background
connection is
\[
 -\frac12|D_\mu W_\nu-D_\nu W_\mu|^2
 -\ii g\,W_\mu^\dagger W_\nu F^{\mu\nu},
\]
up to generator conventions.  The second term is the Yang--Mills magnetic
coupling and gives the gyromagnetic ratio $g_{\rm mag}=2$.  An independently
chosen Pauli coefficient would spoil the longitudinal cancellations.

For massive connection components, the cancellation of the residual
$E^2/m^2$ terms requires scalar exchange with couplings determined by the
same generators.  Writing a scalar as a radial field times a gauge-orbit
matrix supplies the Goldstone mode, whose high-energy amplitude equals the
longitudinal-vector amplitude.  A vacuum expectation value then gives
\[
 (m_V^2)^{AB}=g_Ag_B(T^Av)\mathbin{\cdot}(T^Bv).
\]
This is the Higgs description.  A Stueckelberg form is its radial-mode-frozen
limit; in a non-abelian theory that limit alone has interactions with inverse
powers of the symmetry-breaking scale and is an effective theory.  Under the
locality, unitarity, and all-orders perturbative-renormalizability assumptions
in the question, the Yang--Mills completion is therefore forced.  This is the
claim Banks withholds in his footnote.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-003}
Use $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-,+,\ldots,+)$, $p^2=-m^2$ on shell, and
$p\cdot x=-p^0x^0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x$ with positive-frequency factor
$\ee^{+\ii p\cdot x}$.  The Lorentzian fermion operator is
$-\ii\slashed\partial-m$, with
$\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=-2\eta^{\mu\nu}$ and
$\slashed p=\gamma^\mu p_\mu$.
Write
\[
 F_{\mu\nu}{}^a=\partial_\mu A_\nu{}^a-\partial_\nu A_\mu{}^a
 +gf^{abc}A_\mu{}^bA_\nu{}^c.
\]
At a gauge-invariant vacuum with no vector mass, the gauge-field quadratic
action is
\[
 S_2=\frac12\int\frac{\dd^D k}{(2\pi)^D}
 A_\mu{}^a(-k)K^{\mu\nu}{}_{ab}(k)A_\nu{}^b(k),
 \qquad
 K^{\mu\nu}{}_{ab}=\delta_{ab}(-k^2\eta^{\mu\nu}+k^\mu k^\nu).
 \tag{8.I.9}
\]
It satisfies $K^{\mu\nu}{}_{ab}k_\nu\omega^b=0$ for every $\omega^b$.
Those are precisely the tangent directions
$\delta A_\mu{}^a=\partial_\mu\omega^a$ to the gauge orbit.  Since $K$ has
$\dim G$ zero eigenvectors at each nonzero momentum, it has no inverse.

For reference, with all momenta incoming, the pure-gauge vertices are
\begin{align*}
 V^{abc}{}_{\mu\nu\rho}(p,q,r)
 ={}&-\ii g f^{abc}\bigl[
 \eta_{\mu\nu}(p-q)_\rho+
 \eta_{\nu\rho}(q-r)_\mu+
 \eta_{\rho\mu}(r-p)_\nu\bigr],\\
 V^{abcd}{}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
 ={}&-\ii g^2\bigl[
 f^{abe}f^{cde}(\eta_{\mu\rho}\eta_{\nu\sigma}
                 -\eta_{\mu\sigma}\eta_{\nu\rho})\\
 &\quad+f^{ace}f^{bde}(\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta_{\rho\sigma}
                 -\eta_{\mu\sigma}\eta_{\nu\rho})\\
 &\quad+f^{ade}f^{bce}(\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta_{\rho\sigma}
                 -\eta_{\mu\rho}\eta_{\nu\sigma})\bigr].
 \tag{8.I.10}
\end{align*}
An overall sign changes with the $S$-matrix and metric conventions, while the
relative terms are fixed.

For a complex scalar, the $A_\mu{}^a\phi_j\phi_i^*$ vertex is
$-\ii g(T^a)_{ij}(p-q)_\mu$, where $p$ and $q$ are the incoming scalar and
conjugate-scalar momenta.  The seagull is
\[
 -\ii g^2\eta_{\mu\nu}\{T^a,T^b\}_{ij}.
 \tag{8.I.11}
\]
The fermion vertex is $-\ii g\gamma_\mu T^a$.  Scalar self-interactions and
Yukawa vertices are $-\ii$ times the corresponding field derivatives of
$V$ and $\mathcal L_{\rm Yuk}$.  The free scalar and fermion kernels are
$k^2+m_s^2$ and $\slashed k-m_f$ after their mass matrices are diagonalized.

If the vacuum $v$ breaks the group, the Hessian acquires a vector mass matrix
and vector--Goldstone mixing.  In real scalar coordinates its structure is
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L_2\supset{}&-\frac12\partial_\mu\eta_A\partial^\mu\eta_A
 -\frac12\eta_A H_{AB}\eta_B
 +A_\mu{}^a\mathcal M_{aA}\partial^\mu\eta_A
 -\frac12A_\mu{}^a(M_V^2)_{ab}A^{b\mu},\\
 \mathcal M_{aA}={}&g(t^av)_A,\qquad
 (M_V^2)_{ab}=\mathcal M_{aA}\mathcal M_{bA},
 \tag{8.I.11a}
\end{align*}
up to the correlated sign fixed by the convention for $t^a$.  The fermion
kernel uses the mass matrix $M(v)$.  With the Feynman prescription, its kernel
gives
\[
 S_F(p)=\frac{\ii}{\slashed p-m_f+\ii0}
 =-\frac{\ii(\slashed p+m_f)}{p^2+m_f^2-\ii0}.
 \tag{8.I.11b}
\]
Gauge invariance still gives a combined
zero direction,
\[
 \delta A_\mu{}^a=\partial_\mu\omega^a,
 \qquad \delta\phi=\ii g\omega^aT^av.
 \tag{8.I.12}
\]
Thus the complete unfixed Hessian, rather than its vector block alone, is
singular.  A gauge-fixing term lifts (8.I.12), supplies an inverse propagator,
and introduces the ghost vertices.  This is the obstruction that motivates
Banks's transition to BRST quantization.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-004}
Nilpotency immediately gives
\[
 B=\operatorname{im}Q\subset\ker Q=Z,
 \qquad Q(Q\ket t)=0.
\]
An exact vector $Q\ket t$ therefore represents $[0]$ in $H_Q=Z/B$.
Calling a closed representative physical means that its cohomology class is
nonzero, so it cannot also lie in $B$.  The two kinds are disjoint as
cohomology classes.  Exact vectors and closed vectors are not disjoint as
subsets, since every exact vector is closed.

The perturbative content can be shown without choosing a detailed gauge.
For every unphysical BRST doublet introduce coordinates $(u_i,v_i)$ obeying
\[
 su_i=v_i,\qquad sv_i=0.
\]
Here $s$ is the operation generated by $Q$.  Define
\[
 h=\sum_i u_i\frac{\partial}{\partial v_i},
 \qquad N=\sum_i\left(u_i\frac{\partial}{\partial u_i}
 +v_i\frac{\partial}{\partial v_i}\right).
\]
The graded anticommutator is
\[
 \{s,h\}=N. \tag{8.I.13}
\]
If a closed polynomial $X_n$ has positive doublet number $n$, then
\[
 X_n=\frac1nNX_n=\frac1n(sh+hs)X_n
 =s\left(\frac1nhX_n\right).
 \tag{8.I.14}
\]
Every closed local polynomial with positive doublet number is exact.  The
state-space statement uses the analogous oscillator homotopy $K$: on the
asymptotic Fock space, $\{Q,K\}=N_{\rm unphys}$.  A $Q$-closed state with
$N_{\rm unphys}=n>0$ equals $Q(K\ket\chi/n)$.  Longitudinal and time-like
vector modes, would-be Goldstone modes, ghosts, and antighosts arrange into
two linked pairs forming a quartet.  The quartet-free cohomology contains
$D-2$ transverse massless polarizations, $D-1$ on-shell polarizations of each
massive vector, Higgs directions orthogonal to $T^av$, and physical matter
states.

At nonzero coupling one filters the BRST differential by field number.  Its
lowest part has the homotopy (8.I.13); solving the closure equation order by
order removes every positive-doublet-number term.  The remaining local
ghost-number-zero expression is a gauge-invariant function of ordinary
fields, modulo equations of motion, plus an $s$-exact
term.  This is a statement about local operators.  Asymptotic states are
described by the quartet-free cohomology above and need not be represented by
local gauge-invariant polynomials.  For integrated local densities, total
derivatives are also identified.  The perturbative cohomology statements
assume an anomaly-free
BRST symmetry, admissible boundary conditions, and a gauge patch without a
Gribov obstruction.  Its Hilbert-space version also assumes a BRST-invariant
vacuum and a suitable nondegenerate inner product on cohomology.

Finally, a change of gauge fermion changes the action by $s\Delta\Psi$.
For closed insertions $O_i$, the Ward identity gives
\[
 \delta_\Psi\langle O_1\cdots O_n\rangle
 =\langle s(\Delta\Psi\,O_1\cdots O_n)\rangle=0.
\]
This proves the gauge-fixing independence used in Banks's local argument and
explains why exact states carry no physical matrix elements.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-005}
Use Euclidean momenta in dimensional regularization with
$d_{\mathrm{reg}}=4-2\varepsilon$ and scale $\mu$.  The scalar bubble and seagull combine
to
\[
 \Pi_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}(k)=g^2T(R)\delta^{ab}\mu^{2\varepsilon}
 \int\frac{\dd^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}\ell}{(2\pi)^{d_{\mathrm{reg}}}}
 \left[
 \frac{(2\ell+k)_\mu(2\ell+k)_\nu}
 {[(\ell+k)^2+m^2](\ell^2+m^2)}
 -\frac{2\delta_{\mu\nu}}{\ell^2+m^2}
 \right].
 \tag{8.I.15}
\]
The second term is essential.  Contracting the bubble numerator with $k^\mu$
uses
\[
 k\mathbin{\cdot}(2\ell+k)=(\ell+k)^2-\ell^2.
\]
After shifting the loop variable in one resulting tadpole, its remainder is
canceled by the seagull.  Hence $k^\mu\Pi_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}=0$ before doing the
integral.

Introduce a parameter $x$, shift $L=\ell+xk$, and use rotational symmetry for
$L_\mu L_\nu$.  In the convention
\[
 \Pi_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}=\delta^{ab}
 (k_\mu k_\nu-k^2\delta_{\mu\nu})\Pi_s(k^2),
\]
one obtains
\[
 \Pi_s(k^2)=\frac{g^2T(R)}{16\pi^2}
 \int_0^1\!\dd x\,(1-2x)^2
 \left[\frac1{\bar\varepsilon}
 -\ln\frac{m^2+x(1-x)k^2}{\mu^2}\right]+O(\varepsilon),
 \tag{8.I.16}
\]
where $1/\bar\varepsilon=1/\varepsilon-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi$.  Since
$\int_0^1(1-2x)^2\dd x=1/3$, the ultraviolet pole is
\[
 \Pi_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}\big|_{\rm div}
 =\frac{g^2T(R)}{48\pi^2\bar\varepsilon}\delta^{ab}
 (k_\mu k_\nu-k^2\delta_{\mu\nu}).
 \tag{8.I.17}
\]
This is the familiar $-\tfrac13T(R)$ contribution of one complex scalar to
the coefficient convention
$b_0=\tfrac{11}{3}C_A-\tfrac43T_Fn_f-\tfrac13T_sn_s$.

Subtracting at $k^2=0$ removes the local term.  At small momentum,
\[
 \Pi_s(k^2)-\Pi_s(0)
 =-\frac{g^2T(R)}{16\pi^2}\frac{k^2}{30m^2}
 +O(k^4/m^4),
\]
up to the sign chosen in the definition of $\Pi_{\mu\nu}$.  This is
decoupling.  At $k^2\gg m^2$, the renormalized form of (8.I.16) contains
$-g^2T(R)\ln(k^2/\mu^2)/(48\pi^2)$ in the displayed tensor convention.  No
gauge, Goldstone, or ghost propagator appears in
(8.I.15), so this scalar subset contains no $\alpha$.  In a broken model the
remaining one-loop graphs include physical Higgs fields, $\alpha$-dependent
Goldstones and ghosts, and gauge fields.  BRST identities cancel their
$\alpha$ dependence in pole positions and on-shell amplitudes.  Equation
(8.I.17) is the ultraviolet scalar contribution recovered from the general
$R_\alpha$ rules in Appendix D.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-006}
Put
\[
 f(r)=\phi_0+\delta f(r),\qquad |\delta f|\ll\phi_0,
 \qquad |a|\ll1.
\]
To first order, the BPS equations become
\[
 \delta f'=\frac{\phi_0}{r}a,
 \qquad a'=2\phi_0 r\,\delta f. \tag{8.I.18}
\]
Eliminating $a=r\delta f'/\phi_0$ gives
\[
 \delta f''+\frac1r\delta f'-2\phi_0^2\delta f=0. \tag{8.I.19}
\]
The two solutions are $I_0(\sqrt2\phi_0r)$ and
$K_0(\sqrt2\phi_0r)$.  The first grows at infinity, so the boundary condition
selects
\[
 \delta f=C K_0(\sqrt2\phi_0r),
 \qquad
 a=-\sqrt2Cr K_1(\sqrt2\phi_0r). \tag{8.I.20}
\]
The sign of $C$ is fixed by matching to the vortex core; for the usual
monotone solution $C<0$ because $f$ approaches $\phi_0$ from below.

Using
\[
 K_\nu(z)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\ee^{-z}
 \left[1+\frac{4\nu^2-1}{8z}+O(z^{-2})\right]
\]
shows explicitly that
\[
 f-\phi_0=O(r^{-1/2}\ee^{-\sqrt2\phi_0r}),
 \qquad a=O(r^{1/2}\ee^{-\sqrt2\phi_0r}).
\]
The BPS equation for the magnetic field gives
\[
 F_{12}=\phi_0^2-f^2=-2\phi_0\delta f+O(\delta f^2),
\]
so the flux density has the same exponential factor.  Its tail length is
$\ell=(\sqrt2\phi_0)^{-1}$ in Banks's normalization.  This is the flux tube
used in the surrounding monopole discussion: the monopole's flux is confined
to a Nielsen--Olesen tube rather than carried by a spherically symmetric
long-range field.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-007}
View $l$ as an integer-valued two-chain on the plaquettes of the dual lattice.
The lattice divergence is its boundary operator.  The constraint
\[
 \Delta_\nu l_{\mu\nu}=w_\mu(C)
\]
therefore says $\partial l=C$.  A particular solution is $l_p=1$ on an
oriented surface $\Sigma$ spanning $C$ and zero elsewhere.  Every other
solution differs from it by a closed integer two-chain.

For a charge-one loop, any solution must have nonzero $l_p$ on plaquettes
whose support spans $C$.  Since $l_p^2\ge1$ whenever $l_p\ne0$,
\[
 \sum_p l_p^2\ge A_{\min}(C), \tag{8.I.21}
\]
where $A_{\min}$ is the least number of plaquettes in a spanning surface.
Equality is achieved by assigning $l_p=\pm1$ with the orientation of a
minimal surface.  The denominator is dominated by $l=0$.  Consequently
\[
 \langle W(C)\rangle=\mathcal N_{\min}(C)
 \ee^{-g^2A_{\min}(C)}
 \left[1+O\!\left(A_{\min}(C)\ee^{-c g^2}\right)\right].
 \tag{8.I.22}
\]
$\mathcal N_{\min}$ counts degenerate minimal surfaces.  The constant $c$ is
the smallest increase of $\sum l_p^2$ produced by a local deformation.  On a
hypercubic lattice, replacing one plaquette by the other five faces of an
elementary cube raises the area by four, giving a typical
$A_{\min}\ee^{-4g^2}$ first correction.  Closed vacuum surfaces begin at
still higher order and their disconnected contributions cancel between the
numerator and denominator.  For a macroscopic surface, connected local
deformations exponentiate and shift the string tension by
$O(\ee^{-4g^2}/a^2)$.

For a rectangle of spatial length $R$ and Euclidean time length $T$,
$A_{\min}=RT/a^2$ after restoring the lattice spacing $a$.  The definition
$\langle W(R,T)\rangle\sim\ee^{-TV(R)}$ yields
\[
 V(R)=\sigma R+O(1),
 \qquad \sigma=\frac{g^2}{a^2} \tag{8.I.23}
\]
at leading strong coupling.  Perimeter and corner terms contribute to the
$O(1)$ piece.  The dimension of $\sigma$ is mass squared, as required.  For
charge $q$, $g^2$ in (8.I.23) is multiplied by the square of the least-magnitude
integer representative of $q$ modulo $N$.  This supplies the minimal-surface
step behind Banks's area-law statement.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-008}
The axial-current and $\gamma_5$ formulas here are four-dimensional.
For $q=(u,d)^T$, the nonsinglet axial current obeys
\[
 \partial_\mu A^{a\mu}
 =\bar q\,\ii\gamma_5\left\{M_q,\frac{\tau^a}{2}\right\}q,
 \qquad M_q=\operatorname{diag}(m_u,m_d). \tag{8.I.24}
\]
There is no gluonic anomaly for this traceless generator.  Insert (8.I.24)
between the vacuum and a one-pion state and use
$\langle0|A^{a\mu}|\pi^b(p)\rangle=\ii f_\pi p^\mu\delta^{ab}$.
The soft-pion Ward identity then gives
\[
 f_\pi^2m_\pi^2
 =-\frac{m_u+m_d}{2}
 \langle\bar uu+\bar dd\rangle+O(m_q^2). \tag{8.I.25}
\]
Thus an exact non-anomalous axial symmetry, broken by the QCD condensate,
would give an exactly massless Goldstone boson.  The observed
$m_\pi^2>0$ displays explicit breaking of the light-quark chiral symmetry and
rules out a symmetry that protects the full light direction required to make
that pion a Goldstone boson.

An attempted symmetry acting axially on only one flavor has
\[
 \partial_\mu j_{5,i}{}^\mu
 =2m_i\bar q_i\ii\gamma_5q_i
 +\frac{g_s^2}{16\pi^2}G^a{}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde G^{a\mu\nu}. \tag{8.I.26}
\]
The second term remains when $m_i=0$, so this $U(1)$ is not an exact symmetry
of the quantum standard model.  The instanton-induced interaction has the
flavor structure
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm inst}\sim
 \det_{ij}(\bar q_{Ri}q_{Lj})+\mathrm{h.c.}
\]
For nonzero masses of the other flavors, contracting their bilinears generates
an effective term $m_i^{\rm eff}\bar q_{Ri}q_{Li}$ with
$m_i^{\rm eff}\propto\prod_{j\ne i}m_j$.  An isolated perturbative zero is
therefore not protected from strong-QCD renormalization.

There is a useful logical qualification.  Equation (8.I.25) measures
$m_u+m_d$ at leading order; $m_\pi\ne0$ alone does not algebraically prove
$m_um_d\ne0$, and it says nothing by itself about a heavy Yukawa eigenvalue.
Hadron mass splittings, other pseudoscalar masses, and nonperturbative quark-
mass determinations supply that information.  What the pion establishes in
Banks's argument is the absence of the exact non-anomalous protecting chiral
symmetry, while (8.I.26) explains why a one-flavor zero cannot furnish a
standard-model symmetry solution to strong CP.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-009}
This electroweak calculation is four-dimensional Minkowski theory with the
mostly-plus metric.  Define the positive center-of-mass invariant by
$s\equiv-q^2>0$, and take the $\gamma_5$ projectors below in $D=4$.
At momenta much smaller than $m_W$, the propagator may be replaced by
$-\ii\eta_{\mu\nu}/m_W^2$.  Two charged-current vertices then give
\[
 \mathcal L_{\mu\text{-decay}}^{\rm eff}
 =-\frac{g_2^2}{2m_W^2}
 (\bar\nu_\mu\gamma^\rho P_L\mu)
 (\bar e\gamma_\rho P_L\nu_e)+\mathrm{h.c.} \tag{8.I.27}
\]
Comparing this with the convention
$-4G_F(\bar\nu_\mu\gamma^\rho P_L\mu)
(\bar e\gamma_\rho P_L\nu_e)/\sqrt2$ gives
\[
 \frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}=\frac{g_2^2}{8m_W^2}. \tag{8.I.28}
\]
The dimensions agree: $G_F$ has mass dimension $-2$.

For photon exchange in $e^-(p_1)e^+(p_2)\to
\mu^-(k_1)\mu^+(k_2)$,
\[
 \mathcal M=-\frac{e^2}{s+\ii0}
 [\bar v(p_2)\gamma^\mu u(p_1)]

 R\mathbin{\downarrow}_{n-1}
 =\bigoplus_\rho b^R_\rho r_\rho, \tag{8.I.67}
\]
where the label $\rho$ also records whether a component contains any heavy
flavor index.  Let $\mathsf R_n$ be the resulting integer restriction matrix,
and let $P_0$ and $P_h$ select the all-light and heavy-index components.  A
candidate sequence must obey
\begin{align*}
 \mathsf M_n\ell^{(n)}&=a_{\rm UV}^{(n)},
 &P_h\mathsf R_n\ell^{(n)}&=0,\\
 P_0\mathsf R_n\ell^{(n)}&=\ell^{(n-1)},
 &\mathsf M_{n-1}\ell^{(n-1)}&=a_{\rm UV}^{(n-1)}. \tag{8.I.68}
\end{align*}
The rows of $\mathsf M_n$ are the cubic and mixed anomaly coefficients.  The
second equation says that every heavy-flavor component is vectorlike and can
become massive.  The last two equations demand anomaly matching after the
mass deformation.  An integer solution of the first equation at one value of
$n$ can therefore fail either branching condition.

To complete Banks's all-$(N,N_F)$ no-go for odd $N$, one must show that

restrictions in the assumed confining regime.  The excerpt asserts that
representation-theoretic result without displaying the restriction matrices.
Neither the single massive-propagator factor nor the explicit

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-019}
The epsilon tensor and anomaly pole here are four-dimensional.  Define the
discontinuity by $\operatorname{disc}F=F(q^2-\ii0)-F(q^2+\ii0)$.
Vector-current conservation and the anomalous Ward identity fix the
longitudinal part of the amplitude:
\[
 \Gamma_L^{\lambda\mu\nu}(q,p_1,p_2)
 =C\frac{q^\lambda}{q^2-\ii0}
 \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{1\rho}p_{2\sigma}. \tag{8.I.69}
\]
Indeed, contraction with $q_\lambda$ reproduces the anomaly.  Transverse form
factors can be added, though none can remove (8.I.69) without introducing an
equal massless singularity.

The distribution identity
\[
 \frac1{q^2-\ii0}-\frac1{q^2+\ii0}
 =2\pi\ii\delta(q^2) \tag{8.I.70}
\]
then gives
\[
 \operatorname{disc}\Gamma_L^{\lambda\mu\nu}
 =2\pi\ii Cq^\lambda
 \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{1\rho}p_{2\sigma}\delta(q^2). \tag{8.I.71}
\]
If a Goldstone boson obeys
$\langle0|J_A^\lambda|\pi(q)\rangle=\ii f q^\lambda$ and its anomalous
two-current matrix element is $C/f$ times the epsilon tensor, Goldstone
exchange gives (8.I.69) directly.  The factor $f$ cancels, displaying anomaly
matching.

A massless fermion triangle realizes the same distribution without an
elementary scalar.  Cutting the two lines adjacent to the anomalous vertex
imposes
\[
 \ell^2=0,\qquad (\ell+q)^2=0.
\]
At $q^2=0$, these equations have the collinear solution
$\ell^\mu=xq^\mu$.  The propagator singularities compensate the shrinking
transverse phase space, leaving the finite distribution (8.I.71).  With a
positive fermion mass, the cut starts at $q^2=4m^2$ and no
$\delta(q^2)$ remains.

Each current has dimension three, and two Fourier integrations reduce the
three-point function to dimension one.  The tensor
$q^\lambda p_1p_2/q^2$ in (8.I.69) also has dimension one.  In an ordinary
local particle description with no anomaly-matching topological sector, a
strict positive mass gap puts the spectral support away from zero and makes
the correlator analytic at $q^2=0$.  Such a description cannot obey the
anomalous identity.  A Goldstone pole or a massless-fermion triangle supplies
the required zero-mass discontinuity in Banks's discussion; the latter shows
that the singularity need not be a one-particle pole.

\BanksImplicitSolution{I-CH08-020}
Choose a local coset representative.  Under a gauge transformation $U(x)$ it
has a compensating $h(x)\in H$,
\[
 \Omega\longmapsto U\Omega h^{-1},\qquad
 \Delta\longmapsto h\Delta,\qquad
 \Psi\longmapsto h_F\Psi.
\]
Using $D'=UDU^{-1}$ in the convention $D=\partial-gA$ gives
\[
 B_\mu\longmapsto hB_\mu h^{-1}+h\partial_\mu h^{-1}. \tag{8.I.71a}
\]
Thus $B_\mu$ is invariant when $G$ is fully broken.  For nontrivial $H$, its
broken component is $H$-covariant and its unbroken component is an
$H$ connection.

Write $\Omega=\exp\pi=1+\pi+O(\pi^2)$, with $\pi$ valued in the broken
generators.  Expanding in $\pi$, and counting $A$, $\Delta$, and $\pi$ as
semiclassical fluctuations, gives
\begin{align*}
 \phi&=v+\Delta+\pi v+O(\pi\Delta,\pi^2),\\
 B_\mu&=-gA_\mu+\partial_\mu\pi+O(\pi A,\pi\partial\pi),\\
 \Psi&=\psi-\pi_F\psi+O(\pi^2\psi). \tag{8.I.72}
\end{align*}
Thus the canonically normalized broken-vector combination is
\[
 \mathcal B_\mu^\perp=-g^{-1}P_\perp B_\mu
 =A_\mu^\perp-g^{-1}\partial_\mu\pi
 +O(\text{fluctuation}^2). \tag{8.I.73}
\]
Here $P_\perp$ projects onto the broken generators.  This field is
$H$-covariant, and it is gauge invariant when $H$ is trivial.  The scalar
$\Delta$ obeys $\langle T^av,\Delta\rangle=0$ for every broken generator in
the invariant scalar inner product, so it contains only physical Higgs
directions.  The last line of (8.I.72) is the gauge-dressed, $H$-covariant
fermion.

In $R_\kappa$ gauge, a broken-generator sector has
\[
 m_{\rm ghost}^2=m_{\rm Goldstone}^2=\kappa m_A^2,
\]
and the time-like vector pole sits at the same scale.  For fixed external
momenta near a physical pole, their propagators vanish as $\kappa^{-1}$ when
$\kappa\to\infty$.  The gauge condition enforces the unitary-gauge slice
$\pi=0$ at leading semiclassical order.  Equations (8.I.72)--(8.I.73) then
reduce to
\[
 \mathcal B_\mu^\perp=A_\mu^\perp,\qquad \phi=v+\Delta,
 \qquad \Psi=\psi,
\]
on the massive physical subspace, with $\mathcal B_\mu^\perp$ understood as its
broken projection.  The residues selected by LSZ therefore agree, to leading
semiclassical order, with those created by the dressed fields.  The abelian
check is
$\phi=(v+h)\ee^{\ii\theta}$ and
$\mathcal B_\mu=A_\mu-g^{-1}\partial_\mu\theta$, which becomes $A_\mu$ when
$\theta=0$.

The field $\Omega$ is defined only up to right multiplication by an element
of the unbroken group $H$.  Accordingly, the $H$ component of $B_\mu$ remains
an $H$ connection rather than a local gauge-invariant massive vector.  Its
asymptotic physical field still requires transverse projection.  Finally, the
large-$k$ part of the massive-vector propagator contains a longitudinal
$k_\mu k_\nu/m_A^2$ term after $\kappa\to\infty$.  Taking large momentum first
at finite $\kappa$ gives $1/k^2$ falloff instead.  The two limits do not
commute, which is Banks's reason for restricting the equality to physical
pole residues at leading semiclassical order.
