\subsection{Quantization of gauge theories in the Higgs phase}

We now leave the subject of anomalies and turn to the quantization of gauge
theories in the Higgs phase. We imagine a gauge group G, which, abusing
language, is broken down to a subgroup H by the VEV, $v_j$, of a multiplet of
scalar fields $\phi_j$. Define
\[
  f_i{}^a=(T_R^a)^j{}_i v_j
\]
and
\[
  \phi_i=v_i+\Delta_i .
\]

% BANKS-SOURCE: pdf=141 print=131 kind=prose
We use canonically normalized fields, whose covariant derivative is
\[
  D_\mu\phi_i=\partial_\mu\phi_i-g_a(T_R^a)^j{}_i\phi_j .
\]
The couplings $g_a$ are the same for all generators belonging to the same
simple factor of the gauge group.

We use the mostly-plus Lorentzian metric $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-,+,\ldots,+)$,
so a massive on-shell momentum obeys $k^2=-m_A^2$.

If we expand the classical Lagrangian around $v_i$, there will be kinetic
terms mixing gauge bosons and scalars. It is convenient to choose a
gauge-fixing term to eliminate this mixing. This defines the $R_\kappa$
gauges, to which we have alluded in our discussion of the abelian Higgs
model. After integrating out the Lagrange multiplier fields $N^a$ we have a
gauge-fixing Lagrangian
\[
  \mathcal L_{\rm GF}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa}
  \left(\partial^\mu A_\mu{}^a-\kappa g_af_i{}^a\Delta_i\right)^2
  +\bar c^d\left[\partial_\mu D_{ab}{}^{\mu}
  -\kappa g_ag_bf_i{}^a(T_b)^j{}_i(v_j+\Delta_j)\right]c^b .
\]
In the last term we modify the summation convention and consider the term
$g_ag_bf_i{}^a(T_b)^j{}_i(v_j+\Delta_j)$ as a matrix with two adjoint indices.

In the $R_\kappa$ gauges, all components of the vector fields propagate. The
physical components have mass matrix
\[
  (m_A^2)^{ab}=g_ag_bf_i{}^af_i{}^b,
\]
while the time-like components, as well as the ghosts, have mass matrix
$\kappa m_A^2$. The propagator is
\[
  \frac{-\ii}{k^2+m_A^2-\ii\epsilon}
  \left[\eta^{\mu\nu}-(1-\kappa)\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}
  {k^2+\kappa m_A^2-\ii\epsilon}\right] .
\]
For finite values of $\kappa$ this falls off at high momenta, but if we take
the $\kappa\to\infty$ limit it looks like a massive vector propagator. On
inspecting the gauge-fixing term, we see that in this limit we appear to get
the unitary gauge. The unphysical components of the $\Delta_i$ fields are
projected out. Indeed, the would-be NGBs have mass matrix
\[
  (m_{\rm NGB}^2)^{ij}=\kappa f_i{}^af_j{}^ag_a^2,
\]
because the full mass matrix for the fields $\Delta_i$ is
\[
  m_{\rm NGB}^2+M^2,
\]
where $M^2$ is $\kappa$-independent and operates in the subspace of fields
orthogonal to the would-be NGBs. $M$ is the mass matrix for physical
Higgs-boson excitations.

In Problem 8.3 you will show that the physical linearized excitations of a
gauge theory in $R_\kappa$ gauge are BRST-invariant states. As a consequence,
the fields of the model create these states from the vacuum to all orders in
perturbation theory. Furthermore, the linearized theory tells us precisely how
to project the fields $A_\mu{}^a$, $c^a$, and $\Delta_i$ onto physical states. We
simply take the $D-1$ physical polarization states of massive gauge bosons,
the $D-2$ transversely polarized states of massless gauge bosons, and components of
$\Delta_i$ perpendicular to the subspace of would-be Goldstone bosons (i.e.
satisfying $f_i{}^a\Delta_i=0$). No ghosts, time-like gauge fields, or would-be
NGBs are allowed.

% BANKS-SOURCE: pdf=142 print=132 kind=prose
In scattering theory, fields are evaluated along the trajectories of
asymptotic particles at infinity. Thus, if we make these projections on the
asymptotic fields, we are defining BRST-invariant operators. The LSZ formula
converts such asymptotic expressions into residues of poles in momentum-space
Green functions. We conclude that, if we evaluate the \emph{non-gauge-invariant}
Green functions of the fields $A_\mu{}^a$ and $\Delta_i$, but search for poles in
perturbation theory that are near the zeroth-order masses of physical particle
states, and project onto the proper subspaces of on-shell fields, then these
residues are gauge-invariant and independent of $\kappa$. In other words, the
S-matrix for physical states is a BRST-invariant, and therefore
$\kappa$-independent, quantity. As $\kappa\to\infty$, unphysical states go
off to infinite mass and the theory becomes manifestly unitary. Thus, the
scattering matrix satisfies perturbative unitarity.

Another way to understand Problem 8.3, and the remarks of the preceding
paragraph, is to introduce gauge-invariant fields via
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \phi&=\Omega(v+\Delta),\\
  B_\mu&=\Omega^\dagger(\partial_\mu-gA_\mu)\Omega,\\
  \Psi&=\Omega_F^\dagger\psi .
\end{aligned}
\]
$\Delta$ has fewer components than $\phi$. It parametrizes the space of field
configurations orthogonal to the space of gauge transforms of the classical
vacuum. $\psi$ is the multiplet of fermion fields in the theory, and
$\Omega_F$ is the action of the gauge group on that multiplet. It is easy to
see that, in $R_\kappa$ gauge, with $\kappa\to\infty$, the massive physical
fields are equal to these gauge-invariant fields to leading order in the
semi-classical expansion. For the massless vectors in the unbroken subgroup
$H$, we must still make a transverse projection. We may think of defining the
S-matrix directly in terms of these gauge-invariant operators.

The problem is that, as we have noted, the $\kappa\to\infty$ and
large-momentum limits do not commute for general Green functions. The
perturbation expansion describing Green functions of the fields is not
renormalizable in the $\kappa\to\infty$ limit. We will discuss this further
in Chapter 9.
