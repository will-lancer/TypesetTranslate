\BanksAppendix{Feynman rules}

In this section we list the Feynman rules in both Euclidean and Lorentzian
signature. The gauge and scalar rules retain their $D$-dimensional tensor
form, with $\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-,+,\ldots,+)$ and
$p^2=-m^2$ on a massive Lorentzian shell. The $\gamma_5$ couplings and the
explicit three-polarization basis below are four-dimensional specializations
($D=4$).

\subsection{Propagators}

For the Euclidean entries, set $x_E=(\tau,\mathbf x)$,
$p_{E4}=p_4$, and $\mathbf p_E=-\mathbf p$, so that
$p_E^2=p_4^2+\mathbf p_E^2$ and the continued positive-frequency mode is
$\exp(-\ii p_4\tau+\ii\mathbf p\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf x)
=\exp(-\ii p_E\mathbin{\cdot}x_E)$.  The Euclidean gamma matrices are
Hermitian and obey $\{\gamma_E^A,\gamma_E^B\}=2\delta^{AB}$ for
$A,B=1,\ldots,D$; in the four-dimensional spinor specialization one may take
$\gamma_E^4=-\gamma^0$ and $\gamma_E^i=\ii\gamma^i$.  We write
$\slashed p_E=\gamma_E^A p_{EA}$.  The Euclidean fermion entry below is the
inverse of $\slashed p_E+\ii m_F$ in this normalization.

\BanksDScalarPropagator

\subsubsection{Scalar propagator in $R_\kappa$ gauge}

The scalar mass-squared matrix is
\begin{equation*}
  m_S^2=\kappa T^a v(T^a v)^T+m_H^2,
\end{equation*}
where the first term is a sum of dyadics and operates in the subspace of
would-be NGBs, while the second term operates in the orthogonal subspace of
physical Higgs bosons. Everything is written in terms of real scalar fields.

\subsubsection{Dirac fermion propagator}

The fermion mass matrix can be an arbitrary combination of \(1\) and \(\gamma_5\),
with coefficients that are complex matrices in internal index space. The momentum
in the propagator goes in the direction of the particle number, which for charged
particles is minus the electric charge. Unpaired Weyl fermions are treated by using
a Dirac field with couplings such that the right-handed components are free. Closed
fermion loops get an extra minus sign.

\subsubsection{Scalar--scalar gauge vertex}

For purely scalar interactions one generally includes a \(1/k!\) in the Lagrangian,
for a term that contains \(k\) identical scalar fields. The vertices are then
\(\mathrm{i}g\) in Lorentzian signature and \(-g\) in Euclidean signature, where
\(g\) is the coefficient of the inverse factorials. Graphs are counted by their
symmetry numbers. To compute symmetry numbers one either figures out the order of
the group of geometrical symmetries of the graph, or goes through the combinatorics
of Wick's theorem. See D.4.

In our computation of Wilson loops, we have described Feynman rules for the
coupling to static non-abelian sources.

\subsection{External lines}

Here we give the rules for invariant amplitudes. Refer to Appendix B for the
additional factors for S-matrix elements and cross sections. The external lines in
a diagram look exactly like the internal lines except that they have one free end.
For scalars the rule that goes with an external line is just a 1. For vectors, we
have a factor of \(\epsilon^\mu(k)\) for incoming particles and
\((\epsilon^\mu(k))^*\) for outgoing particles. For massive particles with momentum
in the 3-direction, the (linear) polarization vectors are
\begin{equation*}
  \epsilon^\mu_{1,2}(k)=(0,\widehat{\mathbf e}_{1,2},0),
\end{equation*}
where \(\widehat{\mathbf e}_{1,2}\) are two-dimensional unit vectors in the
1- and 2-directions. Helicity (circular polarization) states are the complex
linear combinations with
\(2^{-1/2}(\widehat{\mathbf e}_1\pm \mathrm{i}\widehat{\mathbf e}_2)\).
The other polarization is called longitudinal
and has the form
\begin{equation*}
  \epsilon^\mu_3=\left(\frac{p_3}{m},0,0,\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{m}\right).
\end{equation*}
Here \(p^\mu=(E_{\mathbf p},0,0,p_3)\), so
\(p_\mu\epsilon^\mu_3=0\) and
\(\epsilon_{3\mu}\epsilon_3^\mu=1\) in the mostly-plus metric.
If we rotate the momentum to another direction, each of the three polarizations
rotates like a vector. We do not rotate the polarization indices, because we keep
the spin quantization axis along the 3-direction.

For external Dirac fermions we have the rules
\begin{align*}
  u(p,s) &\;--\; \text{incoming fermion},\\
  \bar u(p,s) &\;--\; \text{outgoing fermion},\\
  v(p,s) &\;--\; \text{outgoing anti-fermion},\\
  \bar v(p,s) &\;--\; \text{incoming anti-fermion}.
\end{align*}
The spinor solutions of the momentum-space Dirac equation were discussed in
Appendix C.
