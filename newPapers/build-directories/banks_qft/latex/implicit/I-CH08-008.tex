\begin{exercise}[Pion mass and a symmetry-protected zero Yukawa]{I-CH08-008}
In four-dimensional space-time, suppose an exact symmetry of the standard model were claimed to enforce
$\det(y_uy_d)=0$ by protecting a massless light-quark direction.  Write the
nonsinglet axial Ward identity for the $u,d$ sector and derive the leading
Gell-Mann--Oakes--Renner relation.  Explain why an exact, spontaneously broken
non-anomalous chiral symmetry would require a massless pseudoscalar, and use
$m_\pi\ne0$ to exclude that protecting symmetry in the observed vacuum.
Then analyze an axial rotation of only one quark.  Show why its QCD anomaly
prevents it from being an exact standard-model symmetry and how the
nonperturbative instanton determinant can generate the corresponding effective
mass when the other quark masses are nonzero.  State precisely what the pion
mass establishes and which further empirical input is needed to infer that
every Yukawa eigenvalue is nonzero.
\end{exercise}
