\begin{exercise}[The symmetric adjoint and the cubic anomaly tensor]{I-APPE-001}
Let $G$ be a connected compact group with simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, and
write $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ for its complexification.  Choose an invariant
inner product so that the adjoint representation is self-dual.  Decompose
\[
  \mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\otimes\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}
  =S^2\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\oplus\Lambda^2\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}.
\]

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Show that the Lie bracket supplies one adjoint summand in
$\Lambda^2\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$.  Relate an adjoint summand in
$S^2\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ to a totally symmetric invariant tensor
$d^{abc}$, and explain why its multiplicity equals the number of independent
invariant cubic polynomials on $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$.

\item For $G=SU(N)$, use Hermitian fundamental generators satisfying
\[
  [T^a,T^b]=\ii f^{abc}T^c,
  \qquad \operatorname{tr}_F(T^aT^b)=\frac12\delta^{ab}.
\]
Construct the symmetric equivariant map explicitly from the traceless part of
an anticommutator.  With
\[
  d^{abc}:=2\operatorname{tr}_F\!\left(T^a\{T^b,T^c\}\right),
\]
derive the complete expansion of $T^aT^b$, including the coefficient of the
identity matrix.  State the restriction on $N$, and account for the $SU(2)$
case.

\item Use the classification of basic invariant polynomials of simple Lie
algebras to show that a symmetric adjoint occurs only for type $A_r$ with
$r\geq2$.  Explain the low-rank accidental isomorphisms when this assertion
is expressed with the group names $SU(N)$, $SO(n)$, and $Sp(2n)$.  Check the
result against the explicit decomposition of
$\boldsymbol 8\otimes\boldsymbol 8$ for $SU(3)$.

\item For any finite-dimensional unitary representation $R$, prove that the
symmetrized cubic trace
\[
  \mathcal D_R^{abc}
  :=2\operatorname{tr}_R\!\left(T_R^a\{T_R^b,T_R^c\}\right)
\]
is an invariant symmetric tensor.  Determine its form for every compact
simple Lie algebra, define the anomaly coefficient $A(R)$ with an explicit
normalization, and connect the result to the group factor in Banks's ABJS
anomaly equation.  Include the conjugate-representation and
adjoint-representation checks.  Use Hermiticity and the commutator convention
to test the two displayed formulas on printed page 258, and state any source
correction needed before comparing normalizations with Chapter 8.
For the spacetime anomaly application, specialize to $D=4$ and use
$\epsilon^{0123}=+1$ with
$\widetilde G^{\mu\nu}:=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}G_{\rho\sigma}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}
