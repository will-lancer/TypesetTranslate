\chapterappendix{}{Assorted Integrals}

In order to combine the denominators of $N$ propagators, we need to replace a
product like $D_1^{-1}D_2^{-1}\cdots D_N^{-1}$ with an integral of a function
that involves a linear combination of $D_1,D_2,\ldots,D_N$. For this purpose
it is often convenient to make use of the formula
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{D_1D_2\cdots D_N}
={}&(N-1)!\int_0^1dx_1\int_0^{x_1}dx_2\cdots
\int_0^{x_{N-2}}dx_{N-1}\notag\\
&\cdot[D_1x_{N-1}+D_2(x_{N-2}-x_{N-1})+\cdots+D_N(1-x_1)]^{-N}.
\tag{11.A.1}\label{eq:11.A.1}
\end{align}
In this chapter we have used special cases of this formula for $N=2$ and
$N=3$.

After combining denominators, shifting the four-momentum variable of
integration, Wick rotating, and using four-dimensional rotational invariance,
we commonly encounter integrals of the form
\[
\int d^4k\,\frac{(k^2)^n}{(k^2+\nu^2)^m}
\]
with $(k^2+\nu^2)^m$ coming from the combined propagator denominators, and
$(k^2)^n$ coming from the propagator numerators and vertex momentum factors.
This is divergent for $2n+4\geq2m$, but the integral can be given a finite
value by analytically continuing the spacetime dimensionality from 4 to a
complex value $d$. To evaluate the resulting integral, we use the well-known
formula
\begin{equation}
\int_0^\infty d\kappa\,\frac{\kappa^{\ell-1}}{(\kappa^2+\nu^2)^m}
=\nu^{\ell-2m}\frac{\Gamma(\ell/2)\Gamma(m-\ell/2)}{2\Gamma(m)},
\tag{11.A.2}\label{eq:11.A.2}
\end{equation}
where $\ell=d+2n$. We used this formula in the special cases $n=0,m=2$ and
$n=1,m=2$ in Section 11.2.

Ultraviolet divergences manifest themselves in Eq.~\eqref{eq:11.A.2} as poles in the
factor $\Gamma(m-\ell/2)=\Gamma(m-n-d/2)$ as $d\to4$ with fixed integer $n$.
For $2+n=m$, this factor goes as
\begin{equation}
\Gamma\left(\frac{4-d}{2}\right)\to\frac{2}{d-4}+\gamma,
\tag{11.A.3}\label{eq:11.A.3}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma=0.5772157\cdots$ is the Euler constant. The limiting behavior for
$2+n>m$ can be obtained from \eqref{eq:11.A.3} and the recursion relation for Gamma
functions.
