\SupplementaryExercise{2}{Feynman Parameters}
  {(Adapted from Bernhard Mistlberger and Kevin Zhou, Stanford PHYS 330: QFT I, Problem Set 9 (2022), Problem 2, ``Feynman parameters,'' pp. 1--2.)}
  {zhou-mistlberger-phys330-f22-ps9-p2}
Loop integrals take a few generic forms.  For simplicity, consider a scalar
theory, so that the loop-integrand numerator contains no nontrivial factors.
In the volume's mostly-plus convention, define the tadpole, bubble, and
triangle families by
\[
 I_T(a;m^2)=\int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}
 \frac{1}{(k^2+m^2-i0)^a},
 \tag{3}
\]
\[
 I_B(a,b;p^2)=\int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}
 \frac{1}{(k^2-i0)^a((k+p)^2-i0)^b},
 \tag{4}
\]
\[
 I_\triangle(a,b,c;p_1,p_2)=\int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}
 \frac{1}{(k^2-i0)^a((k+p_1)^2-i0)^b
 ((k-p_2)^2-i0)^c}.
 \tag{5}
\]
They are named after the Feynman diagrams in which they appear.  The source
suppresses the displayed \(i0\) terms, but they are essential in the last
part and have therefore been restored here.  Regard \(a,b,c\) as general
real exponents (although in applications they are usually positive
integers), and regard \(d\) as a real parameter.  We use dimensional
regularization with \(d=4-2\varepsilon\) when taking the final check.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The Gamma function is defined by
\[
 \Gamma(z)=\int_0^\infty dx\,x^{z-1}e^{-x},
 \tag{6}
\]
and for a positive integer \(n\) it satisfies
\(\Gamma(n)=(n-1)!\).  Show, for real \(\nu\) in the convergence domain and
then by analytic continuation, that
\[
 \frac{1}{A^\nu}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu)}
 \int_0^\infty dx\,x^{\nu-1}e^{-xA}.
 \tag{7}
\]

\item
Show that for general \(\nu_i\),
\[
 \frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^n A_i^{\nu_i}}
 =\frac{\Gamma(\sum_{i=1}^n\nu_i)}
 {\prod_{i=1}^n\Gamma(\nu_i)}
 \int_0^1\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^n dx_i\right)
 \delta\!\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right)
 \frac{\prod_{i=1}^n x_i^{\nu_i-1}}
 {(\sum_{i=1}^n x_iA_i)^{\sum_{i=1}^n\nu_i}}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Here the \(x_i\) are Feynman parameters.

\emph{Hint.} Do not base the answer on the Peskin--Schroeder derivation,
which works only for integer \(\nu_i\).  Instead start from the result of
the preceding part.

\item
Apply (8) to \(I_B(a,b;p^2)\) to obtain an integrand whose denominator is a
single quantity raised to the \(a+b\) power.

\item
Complete the evaluation of \(I_B(a,b;p^2)\) by Wick rotating to Euclidean
signature and performing all remaining integrals.  The Euclidean measure
may be written
\(d^dk_E=|k_E|^{d-1}d|k_E|\,d\Omega_d\), where the area of the unit sphere
in \(d\) dimensions is
\[
 \Omega_d=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}.
 \tag{9}
\]
You may also use
\[
 \frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}
 =\int_0^1dx\,x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}.
 \tag{10}
\]
As a check, after translating the source's mostly-minus invariant to the
volume convention and setting \(d=4-2\varepsilon\), the answer must obey
\[
 I_B(1,1;p^2)
 =\left(\frac{p^2-i0}{4\pi}\right)^{-\varepsilon}
 \frac{i\,\Gamma(1-\varepsilon)^2\Gamma(1+\varepsilon)}
 {(4\pi)^2(1-2\varepsilon)\varepsilon
 \Gamma(1-2\varepsilon)}.
 \tag{11}
\]
\end{enumerate}

renormalization and are often called self-energy corrections.  The one-loop

Throughout this problem work in Feynman gauge and massless QED,
\(m_e=0\), using dimensional regularization with
\(d=4-2\varepsilon\).  In the volume convention an internal photon carries
the scalar denominator \((k^2-i0)^{-1}\), a massless internal fermion carries
the numerator \(-i\slashed q\) over \(q^2-i0\), a vertex contributes
\(e\gamma^\mu\), and a closed fermion loop contributes an additional minus
sign.

For self-containment, the complete scalar and tensor results needed from
Problems 2 and 3 of the source are supplied here.  With
\(I_B\equiv I_B(1,1;p^2)\),
\[
 I_B=\left(\frac{p^2-i0}{4\pi}\right)^{-\varepsilon}
 \frac{i\,\Gamma(1-\varepsilon)^2\Gamma(1+\varepsilon)}
 {(4\pi)^2(1-2\varepsilon)\varepsilon
 \Gamma(1-2\varepsilon)},
\]
\[
 \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}
 \frac{k^\mu}{(k^2-i0)((k+p)^2-i0)}
 =-\frac{p^\mu}{2}I_B,
\]
\[
 \int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}
 \frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{(k^2-i0)((k+p)^2-i0)}
 =\frac{d p^\mu p^\nu-p^2\eta^{\mu\nu}}
 {4(d-1)}I_B.
\]
The last two formulas use the dimensional-regularization rule that
scaleless tadpoles vanish.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Using the Feynman rules, write down \(\Sigma(p^2)\) and
\(\Pi^{\mu\nu}(p^2)\).  Because these are corrections to the electron and
photon propagators, do \emph{not} include external-leg factors such as
\(u_s(p)\) or \(\epsilon_\mu\) to form a scalar matrix element.  Instead,
\(\Sigma\) must remain a \(4\times4\) spinor matrix and \(\Pi^{\mu\nu}\) a
rank-two Lorentz tensor.

\item
Evaluate the loop integrals, expressing the final results in terms of
\(p^2\) and \(\varepsilon\).

\emph{Hint.} Once the gamma matrices have been handled, every integral that
remains is one of the supplied scalar or tensor integrals.

\item
The Euler--Mascheroni constant is defined by
\[
 \gamma_E=-\int_0^\infty dx\,e^{-x}\log x\simeq0.577.
 \tag{21}
\]
Show that
\[
 \Gamma(1-\varepsilon)=1+\gamma_E\varepsilon
 +O(\varepsilon^2).
 \tag{22}
\]

\item
Using (22), expand the results from the second part, dropping all terms that
vanish as \(\varepsilon\to0\).
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{5}{Euclidean Master Integrals and Shifted Tensor Integrals}

\item

\(\Omega_d=2\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2)\) and state its analytic continuation in

\item

\[
 J_n(m^2)=\int\frac{d^dq}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{1}{(q^2+m^2)^n}

for complex \(d\) and \(n\).  State its convergence domain before analytic

 \int\frac{d^dq}{(2\pi)^d}

the analytically continued result.

\(q^\mu\) and \(q^\mu q^\nu\).  Express the answers in terms of \(p^\mu\),
