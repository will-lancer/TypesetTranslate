\SupplementaryExercise{4}{Soft Photons}
  {(Adapted from John McGreevy, Physics 215B QFT, Winter 2025,
  Assignment 5, Problem 4.)}
  {mcgreevy-215b-w25-hw05-p4}
Let an electron be deflected from on-shell momentum \(p\) to \(p'\), with
\(p^2=p'^2=-m_e^2\), and write \(q=p'-p\).  Regulate the infrared region by
giving the photon a small mass \(m_\gamma\).  In the mostly-plus convention
of this volume a spacelike transfer has \(Q^2\equiv q^2>0\), and write
\(\overline{d^4k}\equiv d^4k/(2\pi)^4\).

Check that the contribution from a single virtual photon (Eq.~(2.60) of
McGreevy's lecture notes, with \(n=2\)) is
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\frac{e^2}{2}\int\overline{d^4k}\,
 \frac{-i\eta_{\rho\sigma}}{k^2+m_\gamma^2-i0}
 \left(\frac{p'}{p'\cdot k}-\frac{p}{p\cdot k}\right)^{\!\rho}
 \left(\frac{p'}{-p'\cdot k}-\frac{p}{-p\cdot k}\right)^{\!\sigma}
 \\
 &\hspace{35mm}={}-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}f_{\mathrm{IR}}(Q^2)
 \ln\!\left(\frac{Q^2}{m_\gamma^2}\right)+\text{finite},
 \end{split}
\]
where
\[
 f_{\mathrm{IR}}(Q^2)=\int_0^1dx\,
 \frac{m_e^2+Q^2/2}{m_e^2+x(1-x)Q^2}-1.
\]

Hints: Wick rotate.  Scale out the overall magnitude of \(k\), writing
\(k^\mu=k\widehat k^\mu\).  Use a Feynman parameter to combine
\((p\cdot\widehat k)(p'\cdot\widehat k)\).  Observe that this same
integral appears in the cross section involving the emission of one real
soft photon.

\emph{Editorial note.}  The source prints one dummy integration variable
in the measure and another in the integrand; the display above consistently
calls that variable \(k\).  The signs of \(Q^2\) and \(m_\gamma^2\) have
also been translated from the source's mostly-minus convention.

\SupplementaryExercise{7}{Equivalent-Photon Approximation}
  {(Adapted from John McGreevy, Physics 215B QFT, Winter 2025,
  Assignment 6, Problem 6.)}
  {mcgreevy-215b-w25-hw06-p6}
Consider a process in which very-high-energy electrons scatter from a
target.  At leading order in \(\alpha\), the electron line is connected to
the rest of the diagram by a single photon propagator.  If the initial and
final electron energies are \(E\) and \(E'\), the exchanged photon carries
momentum \(q=p-p'\) with
\[
 q^2=2EE'(1-\cos\theta)
\]
when the electron mass \(m\ll E\) is neglected.  Here and below the signs
have been translated to the mostly-plus convention, in which this
spacelike \(q\) has \(q^2>0\).  In the forward limit \(\theta\to0\), the
photon approaches its mass shell.  To what extent may it be treated as a
real photon?
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The matrix element for the scattering process can be written as
\[
 \mathcal M=(-ie)\bar u(p')\gamma^\mu u(p)
 \frac{-i\eta_{\mu\nu}}{q^2-i0}\,\widehat{\mathcal M}^{\nu}(q),
\]
where \(\widehat{\mathcal M}^{\nu}\) represents the coupling of the
virtual photon to the target.  Let \(q=(q^0,\mathbf q)\) and define
\(\widetilde q=(q^0,-\mathbf q)\).  Parametrize the electron current as
\[
 \bar u(p')\gamma^\mu u(p)
 =Aq^\mu+B\widetilde q^\mu+C\epsilon_1^\mu+D\epsilon_2^\mu,
\]
where \(\epsilon_\alpha\) are unit spacelike vectors transverse to
\(\mathbf q\), with \(\epsilon_\alpha^0=0\).  Show, by contracting with
\(q\), that \(B\) is at most of order \(\theta^2\), and hence may be
ignored at leading order about forward scattering.  Why is the coefficient
\(A\) irrelevant?
\item
Work in the frame \(p=(E,0,0,E)\), and compute
\[
 \bar u(p')\gamma\cdot\epsilon_\alpha u(p)
\]
explicitly for massless electrons in states of definite helicity.  The
vectors \(\epsilon_{\alpha=\parallel,\perp}\) are respectively parallel
and perpendicular to the scattering plane.  Keep terms through order
\(\theta\).  Note that the small \(\widehat{\mathbf 3}\) component of
\(\epsilon_\parallel\) matters.  For definiteness one may take
\(p'=(E',E'\sin\theta,0,E'\cos\theta)\).
\item
Write the electron--target cross section in terms of
\(\lvert\widehat{\mathcal M}^\mu\rvert^2\) and the target phase-space
integral.  This expression must be integrated over the final electron
momentum \(\mathbf p'\); the integral over \(p'^3\) is an integral over
the electron's energy loss.  Show that the integral over
\(\mathbf p'_\perp\) diverges logarithmically as
\(\mathbf p'_\perp\), or equivalently \(\theta\), tends to zero.
\item
The electron mass regulates the divergence that was exposed after setting
\(m=0\).  Reintroduce it in the mostly-plus form of the source's supplied
relation,
\[
 q^2=2(EE'-pp'\cos\theta)-2m^2,
 \qquad p=\lvert\mathbf p\rvert,\quad
 p'=\lvert\mathbf p'\rvert,
\]
and show that the forward divergence is replaced by a factor
\(\ln(s/m^2)\).
\item
Assemble all factors.  Assuming that the target cross sections are
independent of photon polarization, show that the leading part of the
electron--target cross section is obtained by treating the electron as a
source of real photons with energy distribution
\[
 N_\gamma(x)\,dx
 =\frac{dx}{x}\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}
 \bigl[1+(1-x)^2\bigr]\ln\!\left(\frac{s}{m^2}\right),
 \qquad x\equiv\frac{E_\gamma}{E}.
\]
This is the Weisz\"acker--Williams equivalent-photon approximation, a
precursor of the theory of jets and partons in QCD.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{12}{Soft Gravitons?}
  {(Adapted from John McGreevy, Physics 215B QFT, Winter 2025,
  Assignment 6, Problem 5.)}
  {mcgreevy-215b-w25-hw06-p5}
Photons are massless, so measured cross sections include soft photons that
the detector does not resolve; omitting them gives infrared-divergent
nonsense.  Gravitons are also massless.  Must they be treated in the same
way?  The following connected parts sketch how to answer this question.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Consider the schematic gauge-fixed action
\[
 S_0[h_{\mu\nu}]=\int d^4x\,\frac12h_{\mu\nu}\Box h^{\mu\nu}.
\]
This is a kinetic term for too many polarizations of a symmetric tensor
\(h_{\mu\nu}=h_{\nu\mu}\), regarded as a small fluctuation of the metric,
\(g_{\mu\nu}\simeq\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\).  As with the photon, rely
on the coupling to matter to prevent the production of unphysical
polarizations.  Write the propagator for \(h\), continuing to raise and
lower indices with \(\eta_{\mu\nu}\).\footnote{The source gives two
warnings.  First, the Einstein--Hilbert term is schematically
\(\int d^4x\,(8\pi G_N)^{-1}\sqrt{|g|}R
=\int d^4x\,(8\pi G_N)^{-1}(\partial h)^2+\cdots\): it contains a factor
of \(G_N^{-1}\).  Since \(R\) has dimensions of inverse length squared and
\(g\) is dimensionless, \(G_N\) has dimensions of length squared, with
\(8\pi G_N=M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{-2}\).  A factor of \(\sqrt{G_N}\) has been
absorbed into \(h\) so that its kinetic term has unit coefficient.
Second, \((\partial h)^2\) suppresses several index contractions, and de
Donder gauge fixing is needed to obtain the simple expression above.  The
field denoted by \(h_{\mu\nu}\) here is actually the trace-reversed field
\(\bar h_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12h\eta_{\mu\nu}\), with
\(h=\eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}\); the source deliberately drops the bar.
Details, which are not needed here, are in
\href{https://mcgreevy.physics.ucsd.edu/f13/225A-lectures.pdf}{Chapter 10
of McGreevy's GR notes}.}
\item
Couple the graviton to the electron field through
\[
 S_G=\int d^4x\,G h^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu},
 \qquad
 T_{\mu\nu}\equiv
 \bar\psi(\gamma_\mu\partial_\nu+\gamma_\nu\partial_\mu)\psi.
\]
What is the engineering dimension of \(G\)?  What is the new Feynman rule?
\item
Draw the tree-level diagrams for production of gravitational radiation
when an electron is accelerated by absorbing a photon,
\(e\gamma\to eh\).  Evaluate them if desired, and estimate or calculate
the cross section.  Hint: use dimensional analysis.
\item
Study the one-loop diagram in which a graviton corrects the QED vertex.
Is it infrared divergent?  If not, why not?
\item
If the preceding part is troublesome, replace the graviton by a massless
scalar \(\pi(x)\).  Compare derivative coupling
\(\lambda\,\partial_\mu\pi\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\) with direct Yukawa
coupling \(y\pi\bar\psi\psi\).  Warning: although this example resembles
the graviton case, its conclusion is different.
\item
Much of the coupling of gravity to matter is fixed by coordinate
invariance, which plays a role analogous to QED gauge invariance.  On the
small fluctuation the infinitesimal transformation is
\[
 h_{\mu\nu}(x)\longrightarrow h_{\mu\nu}(x)
 +\partial_\mu\lambda_\nu(x)+\partial_\nu\lambda_\mu(x).
\]
What condition does invariance impose on the \(T_{\mu\nu}\) appearing in
the coupling above?
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
