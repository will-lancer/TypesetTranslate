\begin{enumerate}
\SupplementarySolution{1}{Soft Power Counting in the QED Vertex and Why the Inclusive Rate Is Observable}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
In the soft region \(k^\mu\sim\rho\), the loop measure is \(\dd^4k\sim
\rho^4\), the photon propagator is \(k^{-2}\sim\rho^{-2}\), and
\[
 (p+k)^2+m^2=2p\cdot k+O(k^2)\sim\rho
\]
for each neighboring on-shell electron line.  Their product therefore
scales as \(\rho^4\rho^{-2}\rho^{-1}\rho^{-1}=\rho^0\).  Equivalently the
radial integral is \(\dd\rho/\rho\).  The soft part is logarithmic; neither
the numerator nor generic hard external invariants change this conclusion.
\item
A finite-time, finite-size detector projects onto an energy bin, not onto
an exact photon-number eigenstate.  Through order \(e^2\) the probability
assigned to the bin is
\[
 P_{\rm inc}(E)=P_0+2\Re(\mathcal M_0^*\mathcal M_{\rm virt})
 +\int_{\omega<E}\dd\Phi_\gamma\,|\mathcal M_\gamma|^2.
\]
The virtual interference contains \(-A\ln(\Lambda/\lambda)P_0\), while the
real term contains \(+A\ln(E/\lambda)P_0\).  Their \(\ln\lambda\) terms
cancel, leaving \(A\ln(E/\Lambda)P_0\).  An exclusive zero-photon
probability is thus regulator dependent, whereas the detector-defined
inclusive probability is finite.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{2}{The Eikonal Factor from an External Fermion}
For an outgoing fermion the relevant numerator is
\[
 \bar u(p)\gamma^\mu[-\ii(\sl p+\sl q)+m]
 =-2\ii p^\mu\bar u(p)+O(q),
\]
where the on-shell Dirac equation was used.  Its denominator is
\((p+q)^2+m^2-\ii0=2p\cdot q-\ii0+O(q^2)\).  Contracting with the photon
polarization leaves \(e\,p\cdot\epsilon/(p\cdot q-\ii0)\).  Reversing the
external attachment for an incoming line reverses both the momentum flow
and the pole prescription.  Together,
\[
 \mathcal M_\gamma=
 \eta e\,\frac{p\cdot\epsilon}{p\cdot q-\ii\eta0}\mathcal M_0+O(q^0),
\]
with \(\eta=+1\) outgoing and \(-1\) incoming.

\SupplementarySolution{3}{Gauge Terms in the Polarization Sum}
A general covariant polarization sum differs from the physical one by
\(q_\mu f_\nu+f_\mu q_\nu\).  Contracting \(q_\mu\) into the eikonal current
\[
 J^\mu_{\rm soft}=\sum_n\eta_ne_n\frac{p_n^\mu}{p_n\cdot q}
\]
gives \(q\cdot J_{\rm soft}=\sum_n\eta_ne_n\).  Charge conservation makes
this zero, so every gauge-dependent term drops out.  The physical
probability may consequently be evaluated with a convenient covariant
sum, provided the complete set of charged external legs is retained.

\SupplementarySolution{4}{Soft Photons}
Put
\[
 J^\mu(k)=\frac{p'^\mu}{p'\cdot k}-\frac{p^\mu}{p\cdot k}.
\]
The second eikonal bracket in the problem is \(-J^\mu(k)\).  After the
Wick rotation, write \(k_E=\kappa\widehat k\).  The photon propagator and
the two eikonal denominators separate the singular integral into a radial
factor \(\dd\kappa/\kappa\) and a finite angular factor.  For the mixed term
use
\[
 \frac{1}{(p\cdot\widehat k)(p'\cdot\widehat k)}
 =\int_0^1\dd x\,
 \frac{1}{\bigl[(xp+(1-x)p')\cdot\widehat k\bigr]^2}.
\]
The interpolating momentum obeys
\[
 \bigl[xp+(1-x)p'\bigr]^2
 =-m_e^2-x(1-x)Q^2,
 \qquad p\cdot p'=-m_e^2-\frac{Q^2}{2}.
\]
Consequently the angular integral of the mixed term, minus the two
self-terms, is proportional to
\[
 \int_0^1\dd x\left[
 \frac{m_e^2+Q^2/2}{m_e^2+x(1-x)Q^2}-1\right]
 =f_{\mathrm{IR}}(Q^2).
\]
All dependence on the photon mass is now in the radial integral.  Its
singular part is
\[
 \int^{O(Q)}_0\frac{\kappa\,\dd\kappa}
 {\kappa^2+m_\gamma^2}
 =\frac12\ln\!\left(\frac{Q^2}{m_\gamma^2}\right)+O(1).
\]
Keeping the measure and the prefactor \(e^2/2\), the angular normalization
then gives
\[
 I_{\mathrm{virt}}^{\mathrm{IR}}
 =-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}f_{\mathrm{IR}}(Q^2)
 \ln\!\left(\frac{Q^2}{m_\gamma^2}\right),
\]
with everything independent of \(m_\gamma\) included in ``finite.''  As a
check, \(f_{\mathrm{IR}}(0)=0\), because an undeflected charge does not
radiate.

Cutting the virtual photon replaces its propagator by the positive-energy
on-shell delta function.  The remaining eikonal current is the same
\(J^\mu(k)\), so precisely the same angular integral
\(f_{\mathrm{IR}}(Q^2)\) multiplies the real-emission logarithm.  The
opposite real--virtual signs are the origin of the inclusive cancellation.

\SupplementarySolution{5}{The Infrared Vertex Function}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
After combining the photon and two electron denominators, the singular
part has the form
\[
 F_1(q^2)\big|_{\rm IR}
 =-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\int_0^1\dd v\,c(v,q^2)
 \int_0^1\frac{\dd u}{(1-u)m^2+u\lambda^2+\cdots}.
\]
Only the corner in which the photon parameter tends to one, equivalently
the two electron parameters vanish together, can make all three
denominators soft.  Holding the ratio \(v\) fixed, the radial integral is
\(-\ln\lambda^2\) plus finite terms.  Therefore
\[
 F_1(q^2)\big|_{\rm IR}
 =\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\lambda^2}{m^2}
 \int_0^1\dd v\,c(v,q^2),
\]
which extracts the full infrared coefficient without computing the
regular region.
\item
The inverse map is \(u=x+y\), \(v=x/(x+y)\), and
\[
 \left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right|=u.
\]
Thus \(\dd x\,\dd y\,\dd z\,\delta(1-x-y-z)=u\,\dd u\,\dd v\).  If \(z\) is assigned to
the photon line, its soft corner is \(z\to1\), or \(u\to0\); with the
opposite conventional assignment it is \(u\to1\).  In either labeling set
\(r\) equal to the sum of the two small electron parameters.  The singular
piece separates as
\[
 \int_0^1\dd v\,C(v)\int_0\frac{r\,\dd r}{r^2m^2+\lambda^2},
\]
and the last integral is logarithmic.  The \(v\) integral is finite and
supplies the kinematic coefficient.
\item
For spacelike \(q^2=Q^2>0\), an economical representation is
\[
 c(Q^2)=\int_0^1\dd v\left[
 \frac{m^2+\tfrac12Q^2}{m^2+Q^2v(1-v)}-1\right].
\]
After symmetrizing \(v\leftrightarrow1-v\), the integrand can be rewritten
as a positive multiple of \(Q^2(1-2v)^2/
[m^2+Q^2v(1-v)]\).  Hence \(c(Q^2)\ge0\), and \(c(0)=0\), as required by
the charge subtraction \(F_1(0)=1\).  For \(Q^2\gg m^2\), the endpoint
regions \(v\sim m^2/Q^2\) and \(1-v\sim m^2/Q^2\) yield
\(c(Q^2)=\ln(Q^2/m^2)+O(1)\).
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{6}{Bremsstrahlung and Regulator Cancellation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The incoming and outgoing attachments give the current displayed in the
problem.  Put \(q=\omega\widehat q\).  Since each denominator is linear in
\(\omega\), the phase-space measure separates:
\[
 P_{\rm soft}=\frac{e^2}{2(2\pi)^3}
 \ln\frac E\lambda
 \int \dd\Omega\left(
 \frac{p'}{p'\cdot\widehat q}-\frac p{p\cdot\widehat q}\right)^2.
\]
Writing \(\beta\) for the relative speed of \(p\) and \(p'\), the angular
integral gives
\[
 P_{\rm soft}=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}
 \left[\frac1\beta\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-2\right]\ln\frac E\lambda .
\]
The bracket is Lorentz invariant and nonnegative.
\item
Define
\[
 C(\beta)=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}
 \left[\frac1\beta\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-2\right].
\]
The virtual interference is
\(-C(\beta)\ln(\Lambda/\lambda)P_0\), whereas unresolved real emission is
\(+C(\beta)\ln(E/\lambda)P_0\).  Their sum is
\[
 \Delta P_{\rm inc}=C(\beta)\ln\frac E\Lambda\,P_0+\text{finite}.
\]
The fictitious photon mass cancels.  The surviving logarithm involves the
detector resolution \(E\) and the hard matching scale \(\Lambda\), as a
physical inclusive observable must.
\item
Large loop momentum produces the ultraviolet endpoint.  Dimensional
continuation or a Pauli--Villars field suppresses that endpoint and fixes
local counterterms.  The infrared endpoint occurs when the photon momentum
vanishes while adjacent charged propagators go on shell; a photon mass
\(\lambda\) or \(d>4\) regulates it.  Dimensional regularization can label
both poles, but their momentum regions and signs remain distinct.  A local
UV counterterm cannot contain a detector threshold \(E\), so it cannot
remove \(\ln E\).  Only the real--virtual inclusive sum replaces the
unphysical infrared regulator by that physical resolution.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{7}{Equivalent-Photon Approximation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Take \(q=p-p'\).  For a massless electron the Dirac equations give the
electron Ward identity
\[
 q_\mu\bar u(p')\gamma^\mu u(p)
 =\bar u(p')(\slashed p-\slashed p')u(p)=0.
\]
The two transverse polarization vectors have zero contraction with
\(q\), so the parametrization in the problem implies
\[
 0=Aq^2+Bq\cdot\widetilde q,
 \qquad
 B=-A\frac{q^2}{q\cdot\widetilde q}.
\]
Now \(q^2=2EE'(1-\cos\theta)=O(EE'\theta^2)\), whereas
\(q\cdot\widetilde q=-(q^0)^2-\lvert\mathbf q\rvert^2\) is generically of
hard order.  Thus \(B/A=O(\theta^2)\).  Finally the target Ward identity
\(q_\nu\widehat{\mathcal M}^{\nu}=0\) removes the entire term
proportional to \(A\).
\item
One representation compatible with the volume conventions is obtained by
taking \(\gamma^\mu=\ii\gamma^\mu_{\mathrm W}\), where
\(\gamma^\mu_{\mathrm W}\) are the usual Weyl matrices for the opposite
metric signature.  A convenient phase choice gives
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 u_+(p)=\sqrt{2E}(0,0,1,0)^{\mathsf T},&
 u_-(p)=\sqrt{2E}(0,1,0,0)^{\mathsf T},\\[2mm]
 u_+(p')=\sqrt{2E'}(0,0,c,s)^{\mathsf T},&
 u_-(p')=\sqrt{2E'}(-s,c,0,0)^{\mathsf T},
 \end{array}
\]
where \(c=\cos(\theta/2)\) and \(s=\sin(\theta/2)\).  With
\(q=p-p'\), choose
\[
 \epsilon_\perp=(0,0,1,0),\qquad
 \epsilon_\parallel=
 \frac{(0,E-E'\cos\theta,0,E'\sin\theta)}
 {\sqrt{E^2+E'^2-2EE'\cos\theta}}.
\]
Both are unit spacelike vectors orthogonal to \(\mathbf q\).  For
\(E>E'\),
\[
 \epsilon_\parallel=
 \left(0,1,0,\frac{E'}{E-E'}\theta\right)+O(\theta^2).
\]
Using \(\bar u=u^\dagger\beta\), direct multiplication yields, for
helicity \(h=\pm1\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \bar u_h(p')\gamma\cdot\epsilon_\perp u_h(p)
 &=h\sqrt{EE'}\,\theta+O(\theta^2),\\
 \bar u_h(p')\gamma\cdot\epsilon_\parallel u_h(p)
 &=-\ii\sqrt{EE'}\,\frac{E+E'}{E-E'}\theta+O(\theta^2),\\
 \bar u_h(p')\gamma\cdot\epsilon_\alpha u_{-h}(p)&=0.
 \end{split}
\]
Individual phases change if the helicity spinors are rephased, but the
squared magnitudes and the essential conclusion do not: every nonzero
transverse matrix element is \(O(\theta)\).  The longitudinal component of
\(\epsilon_\parallel\) is required to obtain the factor
\((E+E')/(E-E')\).
\item
Define the inclusive target tensor
\[
 W^{\mu\nu}(q)=\sum_X\int \dd\Phi_X\,
 (2\pi)^4\delta^4(P+q-p_X)
 \widehat{\mathcal M}_X^\mu(q)
 \widehat{\mathcal M}_X^{\nu}(q)^*.
\]
Apart from the standard incident flux \(\mathcal F_{eT}\), the inclusive
cross section is
\[
 \dd\sigma_{eT}=
 \frac1{\mathcal F_{eT}}
 \frac{\dd^3p'}{(2\pi)^3\,2E'}
 \frac{e^2}{(q^2)^2}
 j_\mu j_\nu^*W^{\mu\nu}(q),
 \qquad j^\mu=\bar u(p')\gamma^\mu u(p).
\]
Only the transverse projection contributes at leading order.  Part (b)
gives \(j_\perp j_\perp^*=O(EE'\theta^2)\), while
\((q^2)^2=O(E^2E'^2\theta^4)\).  At fixed energy loss,
\(\dd^2\mathbf p'_\perp\simeq E'^2\theta\,\dd\theta\,\dd\phi\).  Hence the
singular part is
\[
 \int\frac{\theta\,\dd\theta\,\theta^2}{\theta^4}
 =\int\frac{\dd\theta}{\theta},
\]
which is logarithmic.
\item
Let \(x=(E-E')/E\), so \(E'=(1-x)E\) to leading power.  Expanding
\(p=\sqrt{E^2-m^2}\), \(p'=\sqrt{E'^2-m^2}\), and the supplied exact
kinematic relation at small angle gives
\[
 q^2=EE'\theta^2
 +m^2\frac{(E-E')^2}{EE'}+O(\theta^4,m^2\theta^2)
 =\frac{\mathbf p_\perp'^2+x^2m^2}{1-x}+\cdots.
\]
Thus \(q^2\) cannot fall below
\(q^2_{\min}=m^2x^2/(1-x)\).  With a hard upper limit of order \(s\),
\[
 \int_{q^2_{\min}}^{q^2_{\max}}\frac{\dd q^2}{q^2}
 =\ln\!\left(\frac{s}{m^2}\right)+O(1),
\]
where the omitted terms depend on \(x\) but are not enhanced by the large
collinear logarithm.
\item
Projecting the spin-averaged electron tensor onto the two transverse
polarizations, or equivalently squaring the bilinears in part (b), gives
the universal collinear splitting probability
\[
 \dd N_\gamma=
 \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}
 \frac{1+(1-x)^2}{x}\,\dd x\,\frac{\dd q^2}{q^2}
 +\text{terms without a collinear logarithm}.
\]
In the nearly on-shell region the target tensor is the same one that enters
the real-photon target cross section.  If that cross section is independent
of polarization, the electron--target result therefore factorizes as
\[
 \dd\sigma_{eT}=N_\gamma(x)\,\dd x\;
 \dd\sigma_{\gamma T}(xE)+\text{subleading terms}.
\]
Using the integral from part (d) gives
\[
 N_\gamma(x)\,\dd x
 =\frac{\dd x}{x}\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}
 \bigl[1+(1-x)^2\bigr]
 \ln\!\left(\frac{s}{m^2}\right),
\]
which is the Weisz\"acker--Williams spectrum requested.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{8}{Collinear Logarithms and KLN Safety}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
A small mass changes the singular denominator to
\(k_\perp^2+m^2h(z)\), with a positive \(h(z)\) of order unity away from
the endpoints.  The singular integral is
\[
 C\int_0^{z(1-z)s_{\rm res}}
 \frac{\dd\kappa^2}{\kappa^2+m^2h(z)}
 =C\left[\ln\frac{s_{\rm res}}{m^2}
 +\ln\frac{z(1-z)}{h(z)}+O(m^2/s_{\rm res})\right].
\]
Thus the logarithmic coefficient is
\[
 C=\mathcal N g^2\int_0^1\dd z\,z(1-z),
\]
where \(\mathcal N\) is the fixed phase-space normalization.  Regulator
details affect only the finite \(z\)-integral.
\item
Cutting the external self-energy graph gives exactly the unresolved
one-to-two phase space with the same squared splitting amplitude.  Unitarity
therefore fixes
\[
 2\Re\,\delta Z_{\rm coll}
 =-\int_{\rm \dd egenerate}\dd\Phi_2\,|\mathcal S_{1\to2}|^2.
\]
Multiplying the left side by the hard one-particle rate and adding the
right side as a real final state cancels
\(\ln(s_{\rm hard}/m^2)\) against
\(\ln(s_{\rm res}/m^2)\).  The completeness sum must include every
one- and two-particle state identified by the measurement; omitting the
degenerate state destroys the cancellation.
\item
Infrared safety requires
\[
 F_{n+1}(\ldots,zP,(1-z)P,\ldots)
 \longrightarrow F_n(\ldots,P,\ldots)
\]
in every collinear limit.  Then real and virtual singularities multiply
the same measurement weight and cancel.  Particle multiplicity fails:
it changes from \(n\) to \(n+1\).  Total energy flow into a fixed angular
cell passes because \(zP^0+(1-z)P^0=P^0\).  The same criterion is the
measurement-function form of the KLN degeneracy sum.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{9}{Gauge-Invariant Charged Dressings and Branch-Point Propagators}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Under \(A_i\to A_i+\partial_i\alpha\), the exponent changes by
\[
 -ie\int \dd^3y\,\frac{\nabla^2\alpha(\mathbf y)}
 {4\pi|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|}
 =+ie\,\alpha(\mathbf x),
\]
after integrating by parts and taking \(\alpha\) to vanish at spatial
infinity.  The exponential thus contributes \(e^{+ie\alpha(x)}\), while
\(\psi(x)\to e^{-ie\alpha(x)}\psi(x)\) in the stated convention.  The
phases cancel, so \(\Psi_D\) is gauge invariant.
\item
Using
\([E_i(\mathbf z),A_j(\mathbf y)]=-\ii\delta_{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf z-\mathbf y)\),
the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff series terminates after the first
commutator.  Acting on the vacuum,
\[
 E_i(\mathbf z)\Psi_D^\dagger(\mathbf x)\ket{0}
 =\frac{e(z-x)_i}{4\pi|\mathbf z-\mathbf x|^3}
 \Psi_D^\dagger(\mathbf x)\ket{0}
 +\text{vacuum fluctuations}.
\]
Its divergence is
\(e\delta^3(\mathbf z-\mathbf x)\), so the dressing supplies exactly the
Coulomb flux demanded by Gauss's law.
\item
Near threshold set \(\Delta=p^0-m\).  The long-time contribution is
\[
 \int_0^\infty \dd t\,e^{\ii(\Delta+\ii0)t}t^{-\gamma}
 =e^{\ii\pi(1-\gamma)/2}\Gamma(1-\gamma)
 (\Delta+\ii0)^{\gamma-1}.
\]
For \(\gamma=0\) this reduces to a simple pole.  Any positive noninteger
\(\gamma\) instead gives a branch point and a continuum spectral density
starting at \(m\).  The charged excitation is inseparable from arbitrarily
soft photons, so a bare charged Fock state is not an isolated eigenstate.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{10}{Soft Photon Clouds, Dressed States, and Inclusive Rates}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
For \(q=\omega\widehat q\), the coherent occupation is
\[
 N=\sum_\lambda\int\frac{\dd^3q}{(2\pi)^3\,2\omega}
 |\alpha_\lambda(\mathbf q)|^2
 =A\int_\lambda^E\frac{\dd\omega}{\omega}
 =A\ln\frac E\lambda ,
\]
where the finite angular integral is denoted by \(A\).  Its energy is
\[
 E_{\rm cloud}=A\int_\lambda^E \dd\omega=A(E-\lambda).
\]
Thus the dressing leaves every finite-energy measurement well defined
while containing infinitely many zero-energy quanta.
\item
Both constructions use
\[
 J^\mu_{\rm as}(q)=\sum_n\eta_ne_n\frac{p_n^\mu}{p_n\cdot q}.
\]
Exponentiating \(J_{\rm as}\) as a displacement operator builds the coherent
photon cloud into the incoming and outgoing states; exponentiating its
squared norm in a real-emission sum traces instead over unresolved photon
states.  The first defines an infrared-finite amplitude between specified
dressed charges.  The second predicts the probability assigned by a
finite-resolution detector.  Their identical eikonal current explains the
same cancellation of the regulator, though the observables are not
identical.
\item
For \(k\to0\),
\[
 \frac{-\ii(\sl p+\sl k)+m}{(p+k)^2+m^2-\ii0}
 \longrightarrow \frac{\slashed p-\ii m}{2p\cdot k-\ii0},
\]
and similarly on the other external line.  The Dirac equations reduce the
numerator to \(p\cdot p'\), yielding precisely the integral in the problem.
Its radial part is logarithmic.  Multiple soft virtual photons attach to
the same classical external-line current; disconnected attachments carry
the factor \(1/N!\), so their sum is the exponential of this one-loop soft
integral.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{11}{The Leading Soft-Photon Theorem and Crossing}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Only attachments to external charged lines possess a propagator that goes
on shell as \(q\to0\).  Expanding that propagator and using the appropriate
external wave equation gives
\[
 \mathcal M_{n+1}=
 \left[\sum_k\eta_ke_k
 \frac{p_k\cdot\epsilon}{p_k\cdot q-\ii\eta_k0}\right]\mathcal M_n
 +O(q^0).
\]
Here \(p_k\) is a positive-energy physical momentum and
\(\eta_k=+1\) for an outgoing state and \(-1\) for an incoming state.
Internal attachments remain off shell and therefore contribute only to
the \(O(q^0)\) remainder.
\item
The replacement \(\epsilon^\mu\to\epsilon^\mu+cq^\mu\) changes the soft
factor by
\[
 c\sum_k\eta_ke_k\frac{p_k\cdot q}{p_k\cdot q}
 =c\sum_k\eta_ke_k.
\]
It vanishes for arbitrary \(c\) exactly when total outgoing charge equals
total incoming charge.  This is the global \(U(1)\) Ward identity, now
visible as the gauge invariance of the leading soft theorem.
\item
An outgoing particle contributes
\(+e\,p\cdot\epsilon/(p\cdot q)\).  Crossing sends its all-outgoing momentum
\(p\to-p\), replaces it by an incoming antiparticle of charge \(-e\), and
changes \(\eta:+1\to-1\).  The product becomes
\[
 (-1)(-e)\frac{(-p)\cdot\epsilon}{(-p)\cdot q}
 =e\frac{p\cdot\epsilon}{p\cdot q}.
\]
Thus the soft factor is crossing covariant; apparent sign changes cancel
when momentum, charge, and in/out assignment are transformed together.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{12}{Soft Gravitons?}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
For a symmetric tensor the identity operator is
\[
 I_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}
 =\frac12\left(
 \eta_{\mu\rho}\eta_{\nu\sigma}
 +\eta_{\mu\sigma}\eta_{\nu\rho}\right).
\]
The inverse of the schematic quadratic kernel in the problem is therefore
\[
 D_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}(k)
 =\frac{-\ii}{k^2-\ii0}I_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}.
\]
This is the propagator for the trace-reversed, gauge-fixed variable used in
the statement.  If one rewrites it for the unreversed metric perturbation,
the familiar numerator is
\(I_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}-\tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta_{\rho\sigma}\), up to
gauge-dependent momentum terms.  Conserved sources remove those momentum
terms.
\item
In four dimensions the canonical fields have
\([h]=1\), \([\psi]=3/2\), and hence \([T_{\mu\nu}]=4\).  Since the
Lagrangian density has dimension four,
\[
 [G]=-1,
\]
in agreement with \(G\sim\sqrt{G_N}\).  If momentum \(p\) flows into the
vertex along the differentiated \(\psi\) line, the interaction as written
gives, up to the overall Fourier-transform phase convention,
\[
 \mathcal V_{\mu\nu}(p)
 =G(\gamma_\mu p_\nu+\gamma_\nu p_\mu).
\]
For the Hermitian, symmetrized stress tensor the corresponding rule has
\(p\) replaced by one half the sum of the incoming and outgoing fermion
momenta.  Either form displays the crucial derivative momentum and the
dimensionful coupling.
\item
Let the incoming photon and outgoing graviton momenta be \(k\) and \(r\),
so \(p+k=p'+r\).  There are two Compton-like orderings on the electron
line: absorb \(k\) before emitting \(r\), or emit \(r\) before absorbing
\(k\).  The graviton is the doubled wavy line:

  With \(\mathcal V_{\mu\nu}\) denoting the preceding graviton
vertex, their sum is schematically
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal M_{e\gamma\to eh}=e\bar u(p')\bigl[&
 \epsilon_h^{*\mu\nu}\mathcal V_{\mu\nu}
 S_F(p+k)\gamma\cdot\epsilon_\gamma\\
 &+\gamma\cdot\epsilon_\gamma S_F(p-r)
 \epsilon_h^{*\mu\nu}\mathcal V_{\mu\nu}\bigr]u(p).
 \end{split}
\]
At energy \(E\gg m\), dimensional analysis gives
\(\mathcal M\sim eGE\).  The two-body cross section contains a factor
\(1/E^2\), and therefore
\[
 \sigma(e\gamma\to eh)\sim e^2G^2\sim\alpha G^2
\]
up to numerical constants and angular dependence.  This has the correct
dimension of area and is energy independent at leading high-energy power.
Gauge-dependent terms in the graviton polarization sum vanish against the
conserved stress tensor.
\item
It \emph{is} infrared divergent.  The derivatives in the matter stress
tensor act on the hard electron fields, so a soft graviton attachment
supplies hard momenta rather than powers of the soft loop momentum \(k\).
The eikonal region of the vertex correction therefore contains
\[
 G^2\int\frac{\dd^4k}{(2\pi)^4}
 \frac{\mathcal N(p,p')}
 {(k^2-\ii0)(p\cdot k-\ii0)(p'\cdot k+\ii0)},
\]
where \(\mathcal N(p,p')\) is nonzero as \(k\to0\).  Radially this behaves
as \(k^3\dd k/(k^2kk)=\dd k/k\).  Equivalently the leading soft-graviton factor
is proportional to \(Gp^\mu p^\nu/(p\cdot k)\).  Thus virtual graviton
corrections have a logarithmic infrared divergence, and a physical rate
must include unresolved real soft gravitons.  The conditional wording in
the source prompt should not be read as asserting the opposite conclusion.
\item
For derivative scalar coupling each soft-scalar vertex contains a factor
of its momentum \(k_\mu\).  Two such vertices change the preceding soft
power count by \(k^2\), making the origin integrable.  More strongly,
integration by parts gives
\[
 \lambda\,\partial_\mu\pi\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi
 =-\lambda\pi\,\partial_\mu(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi)
 +\text{a total derivative},
\]
which vanishes for the conserved vector current, subject to the usual
boundary conditions.  Direct Yukawa coupling has no soft momentum in its
vertex, so its two eikonal denominators remain and the virtual correction
is logarithmically infrared divergent for a massive fermion.  This is why
the derivative-scalar analogy does not reproduce the graviton answer: the
graviton derivative acts on a hard matter field, not on the soft graviton.
\item
Using the symmetry of \(T^{\mu\nu}\) and discarding a boundary term,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \delta S_G
 &=G\int \dd^4x\,
 (\partial_\mu\lambda_\nu+\partial_\nu\lambda_\mu)T^{\mu\nu}\\
 &=-2G\int \dd^4x\,\lambda_\nu\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}.
 \end{split}
\]
Invariance for arbitrary \(\lambda_\nu(x)\) therefore requires
\[
 \boxed{\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0}.
\]
Thus the object coupled to the graviton must be a conserved stress tensor.
\end{enumerate}
