\chapterbackmatter{Supplementary Exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
\SupplementaryExercise{1}{One Phase-Space Time Slice}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 5,
  Problem 1)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps5-ch9}
For \(H=P^2/(2m)+V(Q)\), insert a momentum resolution of the identity
between two nearby position eigenstates.  Derive the short-time
phase-space kernel through first order in the time step, including its
normalization and the endpoint at which \(V\) is evaluated.

\SupplementaryExercise{2}{Integrating Out a Quadratic Momentum}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 5,
  Problem 1)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps5-ch9}
Starting with the time-sliced phase-space path integral for
\(H=P^2/(2m)+V(Q)\), perform every momentum integral.  Derive the
configuration-space action and explain why the resulting determinant is
field independent in this example.

\SupplementaryExercise{3}{The Schrödinger Equation from One Step}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 5,
  Problem 2)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps5-ch9}
Propagate a smooth wavefunction through one infinitesimal
configuration-space time slice.  Expand consistently in the short time and
prove that the path-integral kernel generates the Schrödinger equation with
the correct kinetic and potential terms.

\SupplementaryExercise{4}{The Free-Particle Kernel}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 5,
  Problem 3)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps5-ch9}
Evaluate the time-sliced path integral of a free particle by successive
Gaussian integration.  Obtain the exact kernel, and check both its
short-time delta-function limit and its physical dimensions.

\SupplementaryExercise{5}{Composition Fixes the Normalization}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 5,
  Problems 3--4)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps5-ch9}
Assume the free kernel has its classical exponential but an unknown
time-dependent prefactor.  Use the composition law at an intermediate time
to determine the functional dependence of the prefactor and fix its phase
from the short-time limit.

\SupplementaryExercise{6}{The Dirichlet Fluctuation Spectrum}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 5,
  Problem 4)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps5-ch9}
Expand a free-particle path with fixed endpoints about its classical path.
Find the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of the quadratic fluctuation
operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions, and explain how its determinant
produces the \(T^{-1/2}\) kernel scaling.

\SupplementaryExercise{7}{The Oscillator Feynman Green Function}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapter 7, Problems 7.1--7.2)}
  {srednicki-qft-path-integrals-ch9}
Evaluate the energy contour integral for the harmonic-oscillator Green
function with the Feynman prescription.  Verify explicitly that applying
the oscillator wave operator gives a delta function and identify the
time-ordering encoded by the poles.

\SupplementaryExercise{8}{Vacuum Survival under an External Force}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapter 7, Problem 7.4)}
  {srednicki-qft-path-integrals-ch9}
A ground-state oscillator is acted on by a real force \(f(t)\) of compact
support.  Evaluate the Gaussian path integral and express its vacuum
survival probability entirely in terms of the on-shell Fourier component
of the force.

\SupplementaryExercise{9}{The Free Scalar Source Functional}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapter 8, Sections 8.1--8.2)}
  {srednicki-qft-path-integrals-ch9}
For a real massive scalar coupled linearly to a source \(J\), complete the
functional square and derive \(Z_0[J]/Z_0[0]\).  State the momentum-space
kernel and its \(i\epsilon\) prescription in the mostly-plus convention.

\SupplementaryExercise{10}{Wick Pairings by Differentiation}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapter 8, Eqs. 8.14--8.17)}
  {srednicki-qft-path-integrals-ch9}
Differentiate the free scalar source functional four times and then set
the source to zero.  Derive the three pairings of the four-point function
and generalize the counting to \(2n\) insertions.

\SupplementaryExercise{11}{The Contact Term in the Green Equation}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapter 8, Problems 8.1--8.4)}
  {srednicki-qft-path-integrals-ch9}
Act with the Klein--Gordon operator on the time-ordered scalar two-point
function.  Track derivatives of the step functions and show how the
equal-time canonical commutator generates the four-dimensional delta
function.

\SupplementaryExercise{12}{The Scalar Vacuum Wave Functional}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapter 8, Problem 8.8)}
  {srednicki-qft-path-integrals-ch9}
Treat each spatial Fourier mode of a free real scalar as an oscillator.
Derive the vacuum wave functional, its nonlocal position-space kernel, and
the equal-time covariance of two field modes.

\SupplementaryExercise{13}{Vacuum Boundaries and the Normalized Ratio}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 6,
  introductory notes)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps6-ch9}
Derive the normalized path-integral formula for a scalar time-ordered
\(n\)-point function by retaining the initial and final vacuum
wavefunctionals.  Explain how their large-time limit supplies convergence
and why the denominator is necessary.

\SupplementaryExercise{14}{No-Production Probability from a Source}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 6,
  Problem 1)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps6-ch9}
For a free real scalar coupled to a real classical source of compact
support, evaluate the normalized functional integral and derive the
probability that the \(\InKet{\Omega}\) state evolves into
\(\OutKet{\Omega}\).

\SupplementaryExercise{15}{Connected Two-Point Graphs from the Path Integral}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 6,
  Problem 2(a))}
  {mit-823-s23-ps6-ch9}
In \(\lambda\phi^4\) theory, expand the normalized path integral for the
two-point function through first order in \(\lambda\).  Determine the
contraction multiplicities and isolate the connected correction after
vacuum bubbles cancel.

\SupplementaryExercise{16}{The Tree Four-Point Function}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 6,
  Problem 2(b))}
  {mit-823-s23-ps6-ch9}
Use the functional integral of \(\lambda\phi^4\) theory to calculate the
connected four-point function at first order.  Count the Wick contractions
and show why the \(4!\) in the interaction leaves a unit vertex
multiplicity.

\SupplementaryExercise{17}{The Vacuum Cumulant Expansion}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 6,
  Problem 3)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps6-ch9}
Organize the vacuum functional as a sum over products of connected vacuum
graphs.  Prove combinatorially that taking its logarithm leaves exactly one
copy of every connected vacuum graph with its symmetry factor.

\SupplementaryExercise{18}{Cancellation of Arbitrary Vacuum Bubbles}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 6,
  Problem 4)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps6-ch9}
For a general polynomial scalar interaction, factor diagrams contributing
to an unnormalized \(n\)-point function into vacuum and source-connected
components.  Prove to all perturbative orders that normalization by
\(Z[0]\) cancels every vacuum component.

\SupplementaryExercise{19}{A Wave Functional as a Euclidean Boundary}
  {(Adapted from David Skinner, Advanced Quantum Field Theory,
  Sections 1.2.3 and 3.1)}
  {skinner-aqft-path-integrals-2018-ch9}
Represent \(\braket{\phi_f}{e^{-TH}\psi_i}\) as a Euclidean path integral
with one fixed boundary and one boundary weighted by an arbitrary initial
wave functional.  Show how the ground-state functional is projected out as
\(T\to\infty\).

\SupplementaryExercise{20}{A Parity-Twisted Euclidean Trace}
  {(Adapted from David Skinner, Advanced Quantum Field Theory,
  Example Sheet 1, Problem 6)}
  {skinner-aqft-path-integrals-2018-ch9}
Find the Euclidean path-integral boundary condition that computes
\(\operatorname{Tr}(Pe^{-TH})\) for a particle on the line, where \(P\)
is parity.  Evaluate the trace for the harmonic oscillator and compare it
with the energy-basis answer.

\SupplementaryExercise{21}{Pfaffians under a Grassmann Change of Basis}
  {(Adapted from David Skinner, Advanced Quantum Field Theory,
  Example Sheet 1, Problem 3)}
  {skinner-aqft-path-integrals-2018-ch9}
For \(2N\) real Grassmann variables and an invertible antisymmetric matrix
\(A\), derive the transformation of the Berezin measure under
\(\theta=N\theta'\).  Use it to prove
\(\operatorname{Pf}(N^{\mathsf T}AN)=\det(N)\operatorname{Pf}(A)\).

\SupplementaryExercise{22}{An Exact Four-Grassmann Interaction}
  {(Adapted from David Skinner, Advanced Quantum Field Theory,
  Example Sheet 1, Problem 4)}
  {skinner-aqft-path-integrals-2018-ch9}
Evaluate exactly the partition function of four real Grassmann variables
with an antisymmetric quadratic kernel and a completely antisymmetric
quartic coupling.  Reproduce the same answer by enumerating the allowed
vacuum contractions.

\SupplementaryExercise{23}{Bosonic and Fermionic Determinant Powers}
  {(Adapted from David Skinner, Advanced Quantum Field Theory,
  Sections 2.2 and 2.4)}
  {skinner-aqft-path-integrals-2018-ch9}
Compare a finite-dimensional real bosonic Gaussian, a complex Grassmann
Gaussian, and a real Grassmann Gaussian.  Derive respectively the
determinant powers \(-1/2\), \(+1\), and the Pfaffian, including the
assumptions on their kernels.

\SupplementaryExercise{24}{Fermionic Wick's Theorem from a Pfaffian}
  {(Adapted from David Skinner, Advanced Quantum Field Theory,
  Sections 2.4 and 3.2)}
  {skinner-aqft-path-integrals-2018-ch9}
Introduce Grassmann sources for a real fermionic Gaussian.  Complete the
square, differentiate four times, and derive the signed sum over the three
pairings.  Explain how the result generalizes to \(2N\) insertions.

\SupplementaryExercise{25}{The One-Pair Berezin Gaussian}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 10,
  Problem 3)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps10-grassmann-ch9}
With a stated order for both variables and differentials, evaluate
\(\int d\bar\theta\,d\theta\,e^{-\bar\theta a\theta}\) directly by
expansion.  Check translation invariance under independent Grassmann
shifts.

\SupplementaryExercise{26}{The Complex Grassmann Determinant}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 10,
  Problem 3)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps10-grassmann-ch9}
For \(N\) independent pairs \(\theta_i,\bar\theta_i\), diagonalize or
triangularize the kernel and prove that their Gaussian integral equals
\(\det A\).  Explain why the determinant is not inverted as it is for
commuting variables.

\SupplementaryExercise{27}{An Inverse Matrix from a Fermionic Insertion}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 10,
  Problem 3)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps10-grassmann-ch9}
Insert \(\theta_k\bar\theta_l\) into the complex Grassmann Gaussian.
Derive the result by differentiating the determinant and identify carefully
the ordering of the indices of \(A^{-1}\).

\SupplementaryExercise{28}{The Four-Fermion Gaussian Sign}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 10,
  Problem 3)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps10-grassmann-ch9}
Evaluate the normalized Gaussian expectation of
\(\theta_i\bar\theta_j\theta_k\bar\theta_l\).  Derive the relative minus
sign between its two pairings directly from Grassmann source
differentiation.

\SupplementaryExercise{29}{The Free Dirac Source Functional}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 10,
  Problem 3 and path-integral notes)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps10-grassmann-ch9}
Add independent Grassmann sources to the free Dirac action.  Shift
\(\psi,\bar\psi\), derive the normalized generating functional, and recover
the momentum-space Dirac propagator in the volume's gamma-matrix
convention.

\SupplementaryExercise{30}{Fermion Determinants and Cyclic Signs}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 10,

  {mit-823-s23-ps10-grassmann-ch9}
Integrate out a Dirac field coupled bilinearly to a background matrix
\(\Gamma(x)\).  Expand the resulting determinant and show explicitly why
each connected closed fermion loop carries one additional minus sign.
