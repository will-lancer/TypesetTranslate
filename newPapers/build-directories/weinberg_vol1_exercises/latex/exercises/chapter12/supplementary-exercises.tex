\SupplementaryExercise{1}{Canonical Dimensions in Arbitrary Dimension and A \(Z_2\)-Even Scalar Operator Census}
  {(Inspired by Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory, Lectures 1--2 | Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory, Lecture 2.)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12-curated-parent-1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
In \(D\) space-time dimensions, determine the canonical dimensions of a
real scalar, a Dirac spinor, an Abelian gauge potential, and their free
kinetic operators.  For a local operator containing \(n_\phi\) scalars,
\(n_\psi\) spinors, \(n_F\) field strengths, and \(r\) derivatives, derive
the mass dimension of its Wilson coefficient and classify it as relevant,
marginal, or irrelevant.
\item
For a real scalar in four dimensions with \(\phi\mapsto-\phi\), list a
nonredundant Lorentz-invariant operator basis through dimension six.  Work
modulo total derivatives and the leading equation of motion.  Explain why
\(\phi^3\Box\phi\), \(\phi^2(\partial\phi)^2\), and \(\phi^6\) do not all
have to be retained as independent operators.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{2}{The Jacobian of a Local Field Coordinate}
  {(Inspired by Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory, Lecture 3.)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12-curated-parent-2}
In the scalar path integral make the local change of variable
\(\phi(x)=\chi(x)+a\chi^3(x)/M^2\).  Compute its functional Jacobian.
Show why the Jacobian is unity in dimensional regularization, describe the
local terms it produces with a hard cutoff, and track how a linear source
\(J\phi\) changes.  Reconcile the resulting change of off-shell Green
functions with invariance of on-shell amplitudes.

\item
Let a heavy real field \(H\) have Euclidean quadratic operator
\(M^2-\Box\) and interaction \(\kappa H\phi^2/2\).  Integrate out \(H\)

through \(M^{-4}\).  Verify the coefficients by expanding the full-theory
four-\(\phi\) exchange amplitude at small external momentum.
\item
For the heavy-scalar model of Exercise S.12.4, compare three low-energy
descriptions: setting \(H=0\), retaining only the induced \(\phi^4\)
operator, and retaining the first derivative correction.  Determine the
relative error in the four-point amplitude at momentum scale \(E\ll M\)
in each case and state the energy at which the local expansion fails.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{4}{Chiral Spurions and Dipole Suppression}
  {(Inspired by Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory, Lectures 1--2.)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12-curated-parent-4}
Massless QED is invariant under independent phase rotations of
\(\psi_L\) and \(\psi_R\).  Treat the fermion mass as a spurion and show
that a dipole operator connecting \(\psi_L\) and \(\psi_R\) must carry one
chiral-symmetry-breaking insertion.  Compare the natural estimates

contributions to a magnetic moment.

\SupplementaryExercise{5}{Massless \(\phi^4\) Theory at a Symmetric Subtraction Point}
  {(Adapted from University of Cambridge, Mathematical Tripos Part III, 2023, Paper 304: Advanced Quantum Field Theory, Question 2.)}
  {cambridge-partiii-2023-aqft-paper304-q2}
In the volume's mostly-plus convention, consider the massless real scalar
theory in four-dimensional Minkowski space with Lagrangian
\[
 \mathcal L=-\frac12\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi
 -\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Without derivation, write down the momentum-space Feynman rules for the
theory, including counterterms.  Draw the diagrams that contribute to the
renormalized 1PI four-point function \(\Gamma_4\) through order
\(\lambda^2\).
\item
The function \(\Gamma_4\) may be written in terms of the Mandelstam
variables \(s,t,u\), defined as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.2.23}.  Define the
effective coupling \(\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\) at an energy scale \(M\) by
\[
 -\ii\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=\Gamma_4
 \qquad\text{when}\qquad s=t=u=-M^2.
\]
Using dimensional regularization in \(d=4-\epsilon\), evaluate the relevant
integrals and show that
\[
 -\ii\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}
 =-\ii\lambda
 -\frac{\ii(-\ii\lambda)^2}{32\pi^2}\int_0^1\dd x
 \left(\frac6\epsilon-3\gamma+\cdots\right)
 -\ii\delta\lambda+\cdots.
\]
Here \(\gamma\) is the Euler--Mascheroni constant, and the ellipses inside
the parentheses contain the finite scale- and Feynman-parameter-dependent
terms.  Find a suitable choice of \(\delta\lambda\) such that
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=\lambda\) at the renormalization scale \(M\).
\item
State, without derivation, the Callan--Symanzik equation for this theory.
Assuming that there is no wavefunction renormalization at this order in
\(\lambda\), show that
\[
 \beta(\lambda)=\frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2}+O(\lambda^3).
\]
\end{enumerate}
You may use, for integers \(a,b\),
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\frac{\dd^d\ell}{(2\pi)^d}
 \frac{(\ell^2)^a}{(\ell^2+\Delta)^b}
 =\frac{\Gamma(b-a-d/2)\Gamma(a+d/2)}
 {(4\pi)^{d/2}\Gamma(b)\Gamma(d/2)}
 \Delta^{-(b-a-d/2)},
\]
as well as
\[
 \Gamma(z)=\frac1z-\gamma+O(z)
 \qquad (z\to0).
\]

\SupplementaryExercise{6}{Wilsonian Shell Integration in \(\Phi^3\) Theory}
  {(Adapted from University of Cambridge, Mathematical Tripos Part III, 2024, Paper 304: Advanced Quantum Field Theory, Question 3.)}
  {cambridge-partiii-2024-aqft-paper304-q3}
Consider a scalar field theory in \(d\)-dimensional Euclidean space with
action
\[
 S[\Phi]=\int \dd^dx\left(
 \frac12\partial_\mu\Phi\,\partial^\mu\Phi+\frac{g}{3!}\Phi^3
 \right).
\]
The quantum field \(\Phi(x)\) is defined with a momentum cutoff \(\Lambda\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Decompose its Fourier transform into low- and high-momentum modes at an
arbitrary scale \(b\Lambda<\Lambda\), where \(0<b<1\), by
\[
 \widetilde\Phi(p)=
 \begin{cases}
  \widetilde\phi(p),&p^2<b^2\Lambda^2,\\
  \widetilde\chi(p),&b^2\Lambda^2<p^2<\Lambda^2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Show that
\[
 \Phi(x)=\phi(x)+\chi(x),
\]
where \(\widetilde\phi(p)\) and \(\widetilde\chi(p)\) are the Fourier
transforms of \(\phi(x)\) and \(\chi(x)\), respectively.
\item
Define the effective action by
\[
 e^{-W[\phi]}=\int\mathcal D\chi\,e^{-S[\Phi]}.
\]
Show that it may be written as
\[
 e^{-W[\phi]}=e^{-S[\phi]}
 \left[
  \exp\left(-S_{\mathrm{int}}
  \left[\phi,-\frac{\delta}{\delta J}\right]\right)Z_\chi[J]
 \right]_{J=0},
\]
where \(Z_\chi[J]\) is the generating functional of the free massless
high-momentum field \(\chi\).  Find \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\), define the source
sign in \(Z_\chi[J]\) so that the displayed identity holds, and briefly
explain how to evaluate the action perturbatively.
\item
Assume that, to the order of interest, the effective action has the form
\[
 W[\phi]=\int \dd^dx\left(
 \frac12\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi
 +\frac{g_{\mathrm{eff}}}{3!}\phi^3+\cdots\right),
\]
where the omitted effective-Lagrangian terms may be neglected.  Draw all
Feynman diagrams needed to determine \(g_{\mathrm{eff}}\) through order
\(g^3\).  Find \(g_{\mathrm{eff}}\) to this order and show that for \(d=6\)
\[
 g_{\mathrm{eff}}=g+Cg^3\ln b+\cdots,
\]
specifying \(C\).  You may neglect wavefunction rescaling.
\end{enumerate}
You may use
\[
 \Omega_{d-1}=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)},
 \qquad \Gamma(n)=(n-1)!\quad(n\in\mathbb Z_+),
\]
where \(\Omega_{d-1}\) is the area of the unit \((d-1)\)-sphere.

\SupplementaryExercise{7}{The One-Loop Yukawa Beta Function}
  {(Adapted from Daniel Harlow, Lecture Notes for Physics 8.324: Relativistic Quantum Field Theory II, Section 12.8, Problem 2, with the Yukawa Lagrangian from Eq. (4.34).)}
  {harlow-qft2-sec12p8-problem2-yukawa-beta}
Consider the Yukawa theory used in the source problem: a real scalar
\(\phi\) and a Dirac field \(\psi\) with the mostly-plus Lagrangian
\[
 \mathcal L=-\frac12\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi
 -\frac{m_\phi^2}{2}\phi^2
 -\ii\left(\bar\psi\slashed\partial\psi+m_\psi\bar\psi\psi\right)
 -ig\phi\bar\psi\psi.
\]
Using dimensional regularization, compute the complete one-loop
renormalization of the Yukawa coupling \(g\), including the field-strength
renormalizations required when the interaction is expressed in canonically
normalized fields, and extract the beta function.  You may take the masses
to zero after choosing nonexceptional external momenta, because the
ultraviolet pole and beta function are mass-independent.  State whether
additional scalar interactions needed to close the full theory under
renormalization affect this one-loop result.

\SupplementaryExercise{8}{Euler--Heisenberg, Rayleigh Scattering, and Fermi Theory}
  {(Inspired by Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field Theories, Chapter 3 | Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field Theories, Chapter 3, Section 7 | Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field Theories, Chapter 3, Sections 8--9.)}
  {manohar-eft-2018-ch12-curated-parent-1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Below the electron mass, use Lorentz and gauge invariance to show that the
first local photon self-interactions occur at dimension eight.  Construct
the two parity-even invariants, estimate their one-loop coefficients, and
derive the low-energy scaling of the photon--photon amplitude and total
cross-section.
\item
Represent a neutral spinless atom by a nonrelativistic field \(h\) and
write its leading rotationally invariant couplings to
\(\mathbf E^2\) and \(\mathbf B^2\).  If the atomic size is \(a_0\), use
dimensional analysis to obtain the frequency dependence of the elastic
photon-scattering amplitude and cross-section.  Explain why no
frequency-independent term is allowed.
\item
Expand the propagator of a vector boson of mass \(M\) exchanged between two
approximately conserved currents.  Match the first two local operators,
determine their dimensions, and show that the ratio of the derivative
correction to the leading Fermi interaction is \(O(p^2/M^2)\).  Distinguish
the Wilson coefficient scale from the actual radius of convergence.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{9}{Loop Power Counting and Expansion by Regions}
  {(Inspired by Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field Theories, Chapters 3--4 | Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field Theories, Chapter 4, Section 4 | Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field Theories, Chapter 4.)}
  {manohar-eft-2018-ch12-curated-parent-2}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Consider a graph containing vertices with operator dimensions \(d_i\) and
multiplicities \(V_i\).  In dimensional regularization, prove that loops
cannot generate positive powers of the heavy scale that compensate the
factors \(M^{4-d_i}\).  Derive a formula for the net inverse power of \(M\)
and explain why counterterms at a fixed EFT order form a finite set.
\item
For \(m\ll M\), study
\[
 I(m,M)=\mu^{2\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^{4-2\varepsilon}k_E}{(2\pi)^{4-2\varepsilon}}
 \frac1{(k_E^2+m^2)(k_E^2+M^2)}.
\]
Evaluate it exactly and expand in \(m^2/M^2\).  Then expand only the heavy
propagator before integrating and show which nonanalytic light-mass terms
the EFT loops reproduce.  Identify the analytic difference as matching
data.
\item
Prove by a scaling argument that a dimensionally regulated integral with no
mass or external-momentum scale vanishes.  Separate a logarithmic example
formally into ultraviolet and infrared poles, explain why its total is zero,
and show how the same zero permits matching calculations to discard common
infrared contributions without losing ultraviolet information.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{10}{Heavy-Vector Matching and Infrared Cancellation}
  {(Inspired by Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 2--3 | Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 3--4.)}
  {skiba-tasi-eft-2010-ch12-curated-parent-1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
A massive vector \(X_\mu\) with mass \(M\) couples as
\(g_X X_\mu(J_1^\mu+J_2^\mu)\) to conserved light currents.  Integrate it
out at tree level and determine the coefficients of the three induced
current--current operators.  Show how the relative sign can be checked
from the low-momentum full-theory exchange amplitude.
\item
Compare an operator generated by single heavy-particle exchange with one
first generated by a heavy one-loop graph.  Derive their natural coefficient
estimates, including couplings and the \(16\pi^2\) loop factor.  Determine
when a loop-generated dimension-six effect can dominate a tree-generated
dimension-eight effect at energy \(E\).
\item
Suppose a one-loop full-theory amplitude contains both
\(\ln(M^2/\mu^2)\) and \(\ln(m^2/\mu^2)\), with \(m\) a light mass.
Explain, using equality of low-energy amplitudes, why the same
\(\ln(m^2/\mu^2)\) must occur in the EFT loop.  Show algebraically that the
Wilson coefficient obtained by subtraction is infrared finite and depends
only on short-distance data.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{11}{Dipoles, Anapoles, and Threshold Decoupling}
  {(Inspired by Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 2--3 | Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 3--4.)}
  {skiba-tasi-eft-2010-ch12-curated-parent-2}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Without imposing C, P, or T, classify all gauge- and Lorentz-invariant
dimension-five operators made from one Dirac field and the electromagnetic
field.  Reduce derivative bilinears with the equations of motion, impose
Hermiticity, and identify separately the magnetic and electric dipoles.
\item
At dimension six consider
\[
 (\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\psi)\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu},\qquad
 (\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\gamma_5\psi)\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu},\qquad
 (\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu})^2.
\]
Use the leading Maxwell equation to trade these operators for
current--current terms.  Explain why either off-shell basis is valid but
why combining both lists overcounts physical parameters.
\item
A heavy charged Dirac fermion contributes to the photon vacuum
polarization.  Expand its renormalized one-loop result for
\(|q^2|\ll M^2\), identify the local \(F_{\mu\nu}\Box F^{\mu\nu}\)

\SupplementaryExercise{12}{Asymptotic Regions, the Hierarchy Problem, and Shift Symmetry}
  {(Inspired by Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated, Sections 2--4 | Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated, Sections 5--6 | Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated, Sections 2 and 6.)}
  {cohen-tasi-eft-2019-ch12-curated-parent-1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
For \(0<m\ll M\), evaluate
\[
 J(m,M)=\int_0^\infty\frac{\dd k}{(k+m)(k+M)}.
\]
Recover its small-\(m/M\) expansion by adding a soft-region expansion
\(k\sim m\) and a hard-region expansion \(k\sim M\).  Introduce an
intermediate separator, show that it cancels, and identify which region
contains the nonanalytic \(\ln m\).
\item
Let light and heavy real scalars interact through
\(-\lambda_{\ell h}\phi^2H^2/4\), with \(M\gg m\).  Compute the heavy
one-loop contribution to the renormalized light mass in
\(\overline{\mathrm{MS}}\), keeping its \(M^2\) dependence.  Match at
\(\mu=M\), and quantify the tuning required to maintain \(m^2\ll M^2\)
when no symmetry forbids the correction.
\item
Assume a real scalar has an exact shift symmetry
\(\phi\mapsto\phi+\epsilon\).  Classify its lowest local interactions,
prove that every external scalar enters an amplitude with at least one
power of its momentum, and explain why a mass counterterm is forbidden.
Then introduce a small spurion that breaks the symmetry and determine how
radiative mass corrections must depend on it.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{13}{Symmetry Is Attractive: Two-Scalar Renormalization}
  {(Adapted from John McGreevy, Physics 215B Quantum Field Theory, Winter 2025, Assignment 6, Problem 3.)}
  {mcgreevy-215b-w25-hw06-p3}
In \(D=3+1\), consider two real scalar fields of equal mass with the
mostly-plus interaction
\[
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}
 =-\frac{g}{4!}\left(\phi_1^4+\phi_2^4\right)
 -\frac{2\lambda}{4!}\phi_1^2\phi_2^2.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Show that the model has an \(O(2)\) symmetry when \(\lambda=g\).
\item
For general \(g,\lambda\), determine whether counterterms of the form
\(\phi_1\phi_2\) or \(\phi_1\Box\phi_2\) are required.  If not, explain
which symmetry forbids them.
\item
Renormalize the theory through one loop using, for example, a Euclidean
momentum cutoff or Pauli--Villars regularization.  Add all necessary
counterterms and impose renormalization conditions on the propagators in
the case where both scalars are massless and on the independent
\(2\to2\) amplitudes at nonexceptional kinematic values
\(s_0,t_0,u_0\).  You may reuse the ordinary \(\phi^4\) bubble integral.
\item
In the low-energy limit \(s_0,t_0,u_0\ll\Lambda^2\), where \(\Lambda\) is
the cutoff, tune both propagator poles to \(p^2=0\).  Show that for positive
couplings with \(\lambda/g<3\) the low-energy coupling ratio flows toward
the \(O(2)\)-symmetric value when it begins nearby.  Equivalently, analyze
the beta function of \(r=\lambda/g\) and relate the flow to the source's
fixed-physical-coupling, large-cutoff formulation.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{14}{Scale Symmetry, the Dilatation Current, and Its Generator}
  {(Adapted from John McGreevy, Physics 215A, Fall 2023, Assignment 4, Problem 1.)}
  {mcgreevy-215a-f23-hw04-p1}
In \(D>2\) space-time dimensions consider a real scalar with action
\[
 S=\int d^Dx\left[
 \frac12\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi-g\phi^p
 \right].
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Under the active transformation
\(\phi_\lambda(x)=\lambda^\Delta\phi(\lambda x)\), determine
\(\Delta\) and \(p\) for which the classical action is invariant.  Relate
\(\Delta\) to the engineering dimension of the field.
\item
Find the infinitesimal field transformation.  Construct an improved,
conserved stress tensor whose on-shell trace vanishes, and use it to obtain
the conserved dilatation current and charge.
\item
Using the equal-time canonical commutator, verify that the charge generates
the infinitesimal scale transformation of \(\phi\).  Finally, state how the
dilatation current of a general first-derivative Lagrangian differs from
\(x_\nu T^{\mu\nu}\) when a nontrivial virial current is present.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
