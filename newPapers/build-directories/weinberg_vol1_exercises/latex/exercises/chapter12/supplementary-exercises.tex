\SupplementaryExercise{1}{Canonical Dimensions in Arbitrary Dimension}
  {(Adapted from Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory,
  Lectures 1--2)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12}
In \(D\) space-time dimensions, determine the canonical dimensions of a
real scalar, a Dirac spinor, an Abelian gauge potential, and their free
kinetic operators.  For a local operator containing \(n_\phi\) scalars,
\(n_\psi\) spinors, \(n_F\) field strengths, and \(r\) derivatives, derive
the mass dimension of its Wilson coefficient and classify it as relevant,
marginal, or irrelevant.

\SupplementaryExercise{2}{A \(Z_2\)-Even Scalar Operator Census}
  {(Adapted from Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory,
  Lecture 2)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12}
For a real scalar in four dimensions with \(\phi\mapsto-\phi\), list a
nonredundant Lorentz-invariant operator basis through dimension six.  Work
modulo total derivatives and the leading equation of motion.  Explain why
\(\phi^3\Box\phi\), \(\phi^2(\partial\phi)^2\), and \(\phi^6\) do not all
have to be retained as independent operators.

\SupplementaryExercise{3}{Removing an Equation-of-Motion Operator}
  {(Adapted from Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory,
  Lecture 3)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12}
Start from the Euclidean leading Lagrangian
\[
 \mathcal L_0=\frac12(\partial\phi)^2+\frac12m^2\phi^2
 +\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4
\]
and add \(c\,\phi^3\Box\phi/M^2\).  Construct a local field redefinition
that removes this operator through order \(M^{-2}\), determine the induced
shifts of the \(\phi^4\) and \(\phi^6\) coefficients, and explain why
on-shell amplitudes are unchanged.

\SupplementaryExercise{4}{Tree-Level Matching of a Heavy Scalar}
  {(Adapted from Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory,
  Lectures 1 and 4)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12}
Let a heavy real field \(H\) have Euclidean quadratic operator
\(M^2-\Box\) and interaction \(\kappa H\phi^2/2\).  Integrate out \(H\)
classically, obtain the exact nonlocal light-field action, and expand it
through \(M^{-4}\).  Verify the coefficients by expanding the full-theory
four-\(\phi\) exchange amplitude at small external momentum.

\SupplementaryExercise{5}{Matching Is Not Merely Dropping the Heavy Field}
  {(Adapted from Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory,
  Lectures 1 and 4)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12}
For the heavy-scalar model of Exercise S.12.4, compare three low-energy
descriptions: setting \(H=0\), retaining only the induced \(\phi^4\)
operator, and retaining the first derivative correction.  Determine the
relative error in the four-point amplitude at momentum scale \(E\ll M\)
in each case and state the energy at which the local expansion fails.

\SupplementaryExercise{6}{Chiral Spurions and Dipole Suppression}
  {(Adapted from Iain W. Stewart, MIT 8.851 Effective Field Theory,
  Lectures 1--2)}
  {mit-8851-s13-foundations-ch12}
Massless QED is invariant under independent phase rotations of
\(\psi_L\) and \(\psi_R\).  Treat the fermion mass as a spurion and show
that a dipole operator connecting \(\psi_L\) and \(\psi_R\) must carry one
chiral-symmetry-breaking insertion.  Compare the natural estimates
\(c/M\) and \(cm/M^2\) for its coefficient and the corresponding
contributions to a magnetic moment.

\SupplementaryExercise{7}{Superficial Divergence in \(\phi^4\) Theory}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapters 18--19 and 29)}
  {srednicki-qft-wilsonian-ch12}
For a connected graph in four-dimensional \(\phi^4\) theory, use the
topological identities relating loops, internal lines, vertices, and
external lines to prove that its superficial degree of divergence is
\(D=4-E\).  Identify every primitively divergent amplitude and its allowed
local counterterm.

\SupplementaryExercise{8}{A Momentum-Subtracted Scalar Bubble}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapters 18--19)}
  {srednicki-qft-wilsonian-ch12}
For
\[
 B(p^2)=\int\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}
 \frac1{(k_E^2+m^2)((k_E+p_E)^2+m^2)},
\]
form the momentum-subtracted difference \(B(p^2)-B(-\mu^2)\).  Show
directly at the integrand level that it is ultraviolet convergent, evaluate
it as a Feynman-parameter integral, and identify the local counterterm that
implements the subtraction.

\SupplementaryExercise{9}{A Nested BPHZ Forest}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory,
  Chapters 18--19)}
  {srednicki-qft-wilsonian-ch12}
Let a graph \(G\) contain one logarithmically divergent proper subgraph
\(\gamma\), while \(G\) itself has superficial degree two.  Write the
BPHZ forest formula explicitly in terms of \(t_\gamma\) and \(t_G\).
Explain the order of the Taylor operations and show how the four resulting
terms are represented by the original graph, a subgraph counterterm, an
overall counterterm, and a counterterm graph with a further overall
subtraction.

\SupplementaryExercise{10}{Integrating Out a Thin Momentum Shell}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory, Chapter 29)}
  {srednicki-qft-wilsonian-ch12}
In Euclidean \(\lambda\phi^4/4!\) theory, split the field into modes below
\(\Lambda-\delta\Lambda\) and modes in the shell
\(\Lambda-\delta\Lambda<|k|<\Lambda\).  To first order in \(\lambda\),
integrate the shell and obtain the induced change of the light-field mass.
Give the leading result for \(\Lambda\gg m\).

\SupplementaryExercise{11}{The Shell Correction to the Quartic Coupling}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory, Chapter 29)}
  {srednicki-qft-wilsonian-ch12}
Continue Exercise S.12.10 to second order in \(\lambda\).  Evaluate the
connected contraction with two quartic vertices that is local for external
momenta much smaller than the shell momentum.  Include the three channels
and derive the leading logarithmic change in the quartic coupling.

\SupplementaryExercise{12}{Why an Infinite Action Can Still Predict}
  {(Adapted from Mark Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory, Chapter 29)}
  {srednicki-qft-wilsonian-ch12}
Write a Wilson action at cutoff \(\Lambda\) as a sum of operators
\(\mathcal O_d\) with coefficients \(c_d/\Lambda^{d-4}\).  For an amplitude
at scale \(E\ll\Lambda\), prove that only finitely many operators contribute
through relative order \((E/\Lambda)^N\).  State precisely which assumption
about the dimensionless coefficients is required, and exhibit how the
conclusion can fail under fine tuning.

\SupplementaryExercise{13}{Euler--Heisenberg Power Counting}
  {(Adapted from Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field
  Theories, Chapter 3)}
  {manohar-eft-2018-ch12}
Below the electron mass, use Lorentz and gauge invariance to show that the
first local photon self-interactions occur at dimension eight.  Construct
the two parity-even invariants, estimate their one-loop coefficients, and
derive the low-energy scaling of the photon--photon amplitude and total
cross-section.

\SupplementaryExercise{14}{The Frequency Law of Rayleigh Scattering}
  {(Adapted from Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field
  Theories, Chapter 3, Section 7)}
  {manohar-eft-2018-ch12}
Represent a neutral spinless atom by a nonrelativistic field \(h\) and
write its leading rotationally invariant couplings to
\(\mathbf E^2\) and \(\mathbf B^2\).  If the atomic size is \(a_0\), use
dimensional analysis to obtain the frequency dependence of the elastic
photon-scattering amplitude and cross-section.  Explain why no
frequency-independent term is allowed.

\SupplementaryExercise{15}{Fermi Theory from a Massive Vector}
  {(Adapted from Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field
  Theories, Chapter 3, Sections 8--9)}
  {manohar-eft-2018-ch12}
Expand the propagator of a vector boson of mass \(M\) exchanged between two
approximately conserved currents.  Match the first two local operators,
determine their dimensions, and show that the ratio of the derivative
correction to the leading Fermi interaction is \(O(p^2/M^2)\).  Distinguish
the Wilson coefficient scale from the actual radius of convergence.

\SupplementaryExercise{16}{Loop Power Counting in an EFT}
  {(Adapted from Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field
  Theories, Chapters 3--4)}
  {manohar-eft-2018-ch12}
Consider a graph containing vertices with operator dimensions \(d_i\) and
multiplicities \(V_i\).  In dimensional regularization, prove that loops
cannot generate positive powers of the heavy scale that compensate the
factors \(M^{4-d_i}\).  Derive a formula for the net inverse power of \(M\)
and explain why counterterms at a fixed EFT order form a finite set.

\SupplementaryExercise{17}{Expansion Before Integration}
  {(Adapted from Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field
  Theories, Chapter 4, Section 4)}
  {manohar-eft-2018-ch12}
For \(m\ll M\), study
\[
 I(m,M)=\mu^{2\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^{4-2\varepsilon}k_E}{(2\pi)^{4-2\varepsilon}}
 \frac1{(k_E^2+m^2)(k_E^2+M^2)}.
\]
Evaluate it exactly and expand in \(m^2/M^2\).  Then expand only the heavy
propagator before integrating and show which nonanalytic light-mass terms
the EFT loops reproduce.  Identify the analytic difference as matching
data.

\SupplementaryExercise{18}{Scaleless Integrals and Power Counting}
  {(Adapted from Aneesh V. Manohar, Introduction to Effective Field
  Theories, Chapter 4)}
  {manohar-eft-2018-ch12}
Prove by a scaling argument that a dimensionally regulated integral with no
mass or external-momentum scale vanishes.  Separate a logarithmic example
formally into ultraviolet and infrared poles, explain why its total is zero,
and show how the same zero permits matching calculations to discard common
infrared contributions without losing ultraviolet information.

\SupplementaryExercise{19}{Heavy-Vector Current Matching}
  {(Adapted from Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory
  and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 2--3)}
  {skiba-tasi-eft-2010-ch12}
A massive vector \(X_\mu\) with mass \(M\) couples as
\(g_X X_\mu(J_1^\mu+J_2^\mu)\) to conserved light currents.  Integrate it
out at tree level and determine the coefficients of the three induced
current--current operators.  Show how the relative sign can be checked
from the low-momentum full-theory exchange amplitude.

\SupplementaryExercise{20}{Tree-Generated and Loop-Generated Coefficients}
  {(Adapted from Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory
  and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 2--3)}
  {skiba-tasi-eft-2010-ch12}
Compare an operator generated by single heavy-particle exchange with one
first generated by a heavy one-loop graph.  Derive their natural coefficient
estimates, including couplings and the \(16\pi^2\) loop factor.  Determine
when a loop-generated dimension-six effect can dominate a tree-generated
dimension-eight effect at energy \(E\).

\SupplementaryExercise{21}{Infrared Physics Cancels in Matching}
  {(Adapted from Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory
  and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 3--4)}
  {skiba-tasi-eft-2010-ch12}
Suppose a one-loop full-theory amplitude contains both
\(\ln(M^2/\mu^2)\) and \(\ln(m^2/\mu^2)\), with \(m\) a light mass.
Explain, using equality of low-energy amplitudes, why the same
\(\ln(m^2/\mu^2)\) must occur in the EFT loop.  Show algebraically that the
Wilson coefficient obtained by subtraction is infrared finite and depends
only on short-distance data.

\SupplementaryExercise{22}{The Complete QED Dipole Pair}
  {(Adapted from Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory
  and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 2--3)}
  {skiba-tasi-eft-2010-ch12}
Without imposing C, P, or T, classify all gauge- and Lorentz-invariant
dimension-five operators made from one Dirac field and the electromagnetic
field.  Reduce derivative bilinears with the equations of motion, impose
Hermiticity, and identify separately the magnetic and electric dipoles.

\SupplementaryExercise{23}{Anapoles, Charge Radii, and Equations of Motion}
  {(Adapted from Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory
  and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 2--3)}
  {skiba-tasi-eft-2010-ch12}
At dimension six consider
\[
 (\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\psi)\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu},\qquad
 (\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\gamma_5\psi)\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu},\qquad
 (\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu})^2.
\]
Use the leading Maxwell equation to trade these operators for
current--current terms.  Explain why either off-shell basis is valid but
why combining both lists overcounts physical parameters.

\SupplementaryExercise{24}{Decoupling Across a Mass Threshold}
  {(Adapted from Witold Skiba, TASI Lectures on Effective Field Theory
  and Precision Electroweak Measurements, Sections 3--4)}
  {skiba-tasi-eft-2010-ch12}
A heavy charged Dirac fermion contributes to the photon vacuum
polarization.  Expand its renormalized one-loop result for
\(|q^2|\ll M^2\), identify the local \(F_{\mu\nu}\Box F^{\mu\nu}\)
correction, and explain why a mass-independent subtraction scheme still
requires an explicit threshold matching of the electric charge.

\SupplementaryExercise{25}{A Two-Region Asymptotic Integral}
  {(Adapted from Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated,
  Sections 2--4)}
  {cohen-tasi-eft-2019-ch12}
For \(0<m\ll M\), evaluate
\[
 J(m,M)=\int_0^\infty\frac{dk}{(k+m)(k+M)}.
\]
Recover its small-\(m/M\) expansion by adding a soft-region expansion
\(k\sim m\) and a hard-region expansion \(k\sim M\).  Introduce an
intermediate separator, show that it cancels, and identify which region
contains the nonanalytic \(\ln m\).

\SupplementaryExercise{26}{A Heavy Scalar and the Hierarchy Problem}
  {(Adapted from Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated,
  Sections 5--6)}
  {cohen-tasi-eft-2019-ch12}
Let light and heavy real scalars interact through
\(-\lambda_{\ell h}\phi^2H^2/4\), with \(M\gg m\).  Compute the heavy
one-loop contribution to the renormalized light mass in
\(\overline{\mathrm{MS}}\), keeping its \(M^2\) dependence.  Match at
\(\mu=M\), and quantify the tuning required to maintain \(m^2\ll M^2\)
when no symmetry forbids the correction.

\SupplementaryExercise{27}{Shift Symmetry as Radiative Protection}
  {(Adapted from Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated,
  Sections 2 and 6)}
  {cohen-tasi-eft-2019-ch12}
Assume a real scalar has an exact shift symmetry
\(\phi\mapsto\phi+\epsilon\).  Classify its lowest local interactions,
prove that every external scalar enters an amplitude with at least one
power of its momentum, and explain why a mass counterterm is forbidden.
Then introduce a small spurion that breaks the symmetry and determine how
radiative mass corrections must depend on it.

\SupplementaryExercise{28}{Estimating the EFT Unitarity Scale}
  {(Adapted from Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated,
  Sections 2--3)}
  {cohen-tasi-eft-2019-ch12}
For a massless scalar with
\(\mathcal L_{\rm int}=c(\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi)^2/M^4\),
compute the tree-level \(2\to2\) amplitude in terms of \(s,t,u\).
Project its angle-independent part onto the \(J=0\) partial wave and
estimate the energy at which perturbative unitarity fails.  Explain what
this bound does and does not say about the ultraviolet completion.

\SupplementaryExercise{29}{Separating Matching from Large Logarithms}
  {(Adapted from Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated,
  Sections 3--5)}
  {cohen-tasi-eft-2019-ch12}
A Wilson coefficient satisfies
\(\mu\,dC/d\mu=\gamma C\) with constant \(\gamma\) at the order considered
and is matched as \(C(M)=C_0\).  Solve for \(C(\mu)\), expand the result,
and show how choosing \(\mu\sim E\ll M\) sums the powers of
\(\gamma\ln(M/E)\).  Explain why matching at \(M\) and evaluating directly
at \(E\) are logically distinct steps.

\SupplementaryExercise{30}{Finite Predictivity at Fixed Accuracy}
  {(Adapted from Timothy Cohen, As Scales Become Separated,
  Sections 2--4)}
  {cohen-tasi-eft-2019-ch12}
Consider a four-dimensional EFT with all operators allowed by its exact
symmetries and a single heavy scale \(M\).  Give a proof that a calculation
of an \(E\)-scale observable through \(O((E/M)^N)\) requires only finitely
many Wilson coefficients and loop graphs.  Your argument must include
counterterm closure, multiple insertions, loop counting, and the role of
relevant parameters.  State two circumstances in which naive dimensional
power counting needs refinement.
\end{enumerate}
