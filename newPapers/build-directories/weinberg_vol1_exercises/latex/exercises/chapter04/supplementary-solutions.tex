\SupplementarySolution{1}{A Complex Gaussian and Its Pairings}
Because \(A\) is positive and symmetric, an orthogonal matrix diagonalizes
it: \(A=O^{\mathsf T}DO\), with \(D=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) and
\(a_r>0\).  The simultaneous change of variables
\(x\mapsto Ox\), \(x^*\mapsto Ox^*\) has unit Jacobian.  For one complex
component,
\[
 \int \dd x\,\dd x^*\,e^{-a x^*x}
 =-2\ii\int_{\mathbb R^2}\dd u\,\dd v\,e^{-a(u^2+v^2)}
 =-\frac{2\pi \ii}{a}=\frac{2\pi}{ia}.
\]
The product of these one-component integrals is therefore
\[
 Z_A[0,0]
 =\prod_{r=1}^n\frac{2\pi}{ia_r}
 =\frac{1}{\det(\ii A/2\pi)}.
\]

Since \(A=A^{\mathsf T}\), completing the square gives
\[
\begin{split}
 -x^{*\mathsf T}Ax+B^{\mathsf T}x+\widetilde B^{\mathsf T}x^*
 ={}&-\bigl(x^*-A^{-1}B\bigr)^{\mathsf T}
 A\bigl(x-A^{-1}\widetilde B\bigr)\\
 &+\widetilde B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B.
\end{split}
\]
The contours may be translated because the Gaussian is entire and
decays in the required directions.  Thus
\[
 Z_A[B,\widetilde B]
 =\frac{1}{\det(\ii A/2\pi)}
 e^{\widetilde B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B},
\]
as claimed.

After division by \(Z_A[0,0]\), insertions are generated by
\[
 \left.
 \frac{\partial}{\partial B_{i_1}}\cdots
 \frac{\partial}{\partial B_{i_M}}
 \frac{\partial}{\partial\widetilde B_{j_1}}\cdots
 \frac{\partial}{\partial\widetilde B_{j_N}}
 e^{\widetilde B^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}B}
 \right|_{B=\widetilde B=0}.
\]
Every term in the exponential contains one \(B\) and one
\(\widetilde B\).  Consequently the derivative vanishes unless \(M=N\).
For \(M=N=r\), it is
\[
 \boxed{
 \left\langle
 x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}
 x^*_{j_1}\cdots x^*_{j_r}
 \right\rangle_A
 =\sum_{\pi\in S_r}\prod_{a=1}^r
 (A^{-1})_{i_a j_{\pi(a)}} }.
\]
Thus an arrow joining \(x_i\) to \(x_j^*\) contributes
\((A^{-1})_{ij}\), while \(xx\) and \(x^*x^*\) contractions vanish.
For two pairs the formula gives
\[
 (A^{-1})_{i_1j_1}(A^{-1})_{i_2j_2}
 +(A^{-1})_{i_1j_2}(A^{-1})_{i_2j_1},
\]
corresponding to the direct and crossed arrow patterns in the question.

The source's final \(j_M\) is harmless only after it has established
\(M=N\); as a purported formula before that restriction, it is an index
mismatch.  The corrected general left-hand side ends at \(j_N\), and the
nonzero formula above then sets \(M=N=r\).

\SupplementarySolution{2}{Generating Connected Contributions and Their Volume Scaling}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
If a disconnected graph contains \(m_C\) indistinguishable copies of
component \(C\), permutations of these copies do not produce a new graph.
Its value is therefore
\[
 V_D=\prod_C\frac{V_C^{m_C}}{m_C!}.
\]
Summing independently over every nonnegative \(m_C\),
\[
 \sum_DV_D
 =\prod_C\sum_{m_C=0}^\infty\frac{V_C^{m_C}}{m_C!}
 =\prod_Ce^{V_C}
 =\exp\!\left(\sum_CV_C\right).
\]
Taking the logarithm removes all products and leaves exactly one copy of
each connected component value.
\item
Use condensed indices and write
\[
 Z[J]=1+Z_1+\frac1{2!}Z_2+\frac1{3!}Z_3+\frac1{4!}Z_4+\cdots,
\]
where \(Z_n\) is homogeneous of degree \(n\) in \(J\).  Expanding
\(\log Z\) gives
\[
 W_1=Z_1,\qquad
 W_2=Z_2-Z_1^2,
\]
\[
 W_3=Z_3-3Z_2Z_1+2Z_1^3,
\]
with the products understood as sums over assignments of labeled source
indices.  At fourth order,
\[
 W_4=Z_4-4Z_3Z_1-3Z_2^2+12Z_2Z_1^2-6Z_1^4.
\]
Functional differentiation assigns the external labels to every
displayed factor and reproduces the partition formulas of S.4.1.
Consequently
\[
 \left.\frac{\delta^nW}{\delta J_1\cdots\delta J_n}\right|_{J=0}
\]
is the connected \(n\)-point function, while the same derivative of
\(Z\) is the full one.
\item
In a periodic box,
\[
 \int_Vd^3x\,e^{\ii(\mathbf P_f-\mathbf P_i)\cdot\mathbf x}
 =V\,\delta_{\mathbf P_f,\mathbf P_i}.
\]
A connected component has one freely translatable center and therefore
one such factor.  Its remaining integrals involve relative coordinates
and do not acquire another overall power of \(V\).  A graph with \(C\)
disconnected components has \(C\) independently translatable centers,
so its leading volume dependence is \(V^C\).

Since the full generating object is the exponential of its connected
pieces, its logarithm contains only one connected component at a time and
is proportional to \(V\).  This is the linked-cluster origin of
extensivity.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{3}{Vacuum Cancellation, Connectedness, and Momentum Delta Functions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Let \(B\) be the sum of connected vacuum bubbles.  Diagram
exponentiation gives
\[
 \bra0S\ket0=e^B.
\]
In the numerator with external fields, every diagram uniquely splits
into vacuum components and a remainder in which each component touches
at least one external point.  Hence
\[
 \bra0T\{\phi_1\cdots\phi_nS\}\ket0
 =e^B\,\mathcal N_n.
\]
The Gell-Mann--Low ratio is therefore \(\mathcal N_n\): the factor \(e^B\)
cancels exactly.

A vacuum bubble touches no external point.  By contrast, a diagram may
have two separate components, each attached to some external points.
Such a diagram survives vacuum normalization but is removed when one
passes from the full correlator to its connected part.
\item
A graph is connected if every pair of vertices is joined by some path.  It
is one-particle irreducible if it remains connected after any one
internal line is cut.  For example, take two interaction subgraphs joined
by a single internal propagator, with one external leg attached at each
end.  The complete graph is connected, but cutting the joining propagator
separates it into two pieces.

Connected correlation functions sum all connected graphs, including this
one-particle-reducible example.  One-particle-irreducible graphs form a
smaller set used to build effective vertices; chains of them reconstruct
general connected graphs.
\item
Choose a spanning forest, one spanning tree in each connected component.
It contains \(V-C\) edges.  Each of the remaining
\[
 L=I-(V-C)
\]
internal edges creates one independent cycle.  Rearrangement gives
\[
 V-I+L=C.
\]
Every vertex initially supplies one momentum delta function.  Of the
\(I\) internal momenta, \(I-L\) are fixed by these constraints; the
\(L\) loop momenta remain to be integrated.  The number of unused delta
functions is therefore
\[
 V-(I-L)=V-I+L=C.
\]
They impose conservation of the total momentum entering each connected
component.  A connected graph has \(C=1\) and hence only the overall
conservation delta function.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{4}{Microcausality, Statistics, and Cluster Decomposition}
Let \(\sigma=\pm1\) denote the sign in the mixed relation, so that
\[
 \Phi(x)\Phi^\dagger(y)
 +\sigma\Phi^\dagger(y)\Phi(x)=0
\]
at spacelike separation.  Suppose provisionally that
\[
 \Phi(x)\Phi(y)+s\Phi(y)\Phi(x)=0,
 \qquad s=\pm1.
\]
Taking the adjoint shows that the same provisional sign \(s\) occurs in
the \(\Phi^\dagger\Phi^\dagger\) relation.

For rigor one smears the fields in two spacelike-separated regions; we use
point notation to keep the algebra readable.  Positivity gives
\[
\begin{split}
 0\leq N(x,y)
 &\equiv\left\|\Phi(x)\Phi(y)\ket\Omega\right\|^2\\
 &=\left\langle
 \Phi^\dagger(y)\Phi^\dagger(x)\Phi(x)\Phi(y)
 \right\rangle.
\end{split}
\]
Move \(\Phi^\dagger(y)\) first past \(\Phi^\dagger(x)\), using the
adjoint same-field relation, and then past \(\Phi(x)\), using the mixed
relation.  The two exchanges give
\[
 N(x,y)=s\sigma
 \left\langle
 \Phi^\dagger(x)\Phi(x)\Phi^\dagger(y)\Phi(y)
 \right\rangle.
\]
At large spacelike separation, cluster decomposition makes the last
expectation value tend to
\[
 \left\langle\Phi^\dagger(x)\Phi(x)\right\rangle
 \left\langle\Phi^\dagger(y)\Phi(y)\right\rangle.
\]
Each factor is a nonnegative norm.  It is strictly positive for a suitable
smearing of any nontrivial local field: if every such
\(\Phi(f)\ket\Omega\) vanished, the separating property supplied by
Reeh--Schlieder would make \(\Phi\) the zero operator.  Hence the
large-separation limit is positive.  Since \(N(x,y)\geq0\), it follows
that
\[
 s\sigma=+1,
 \qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad s=\sigma.
\]
The same conclusion for the adjoint relation follows by Hermitian
conjugation.  Thus the mixed, unstarred, and starred spacelike relations
all necessarily carry the same statistics sign.

\SupplementarySolution{5}{Wick's Theorem for Three Fields}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
For bosonic fields, interchanging \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) merely interchanges
the two terms
\[
 \theta(x_1^0-x_2^0)\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \theta(x_2^0-x_1^0)\phi(x_2)\phi(x_1)
\]
in the time-ordered product.  The equal-time ambiguity is harmless
because scalar fields commute at equal time.  In the normal-ordered
product, creation parts commute with creation parts and annihilation
parts commute with annihilation parts; the two mixed terms exchange
places.  Thus both products are symmetric.  Subtracting them in
\[
 D_F(x_1-x_2)
 =T\{\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\}
 -:\!\phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\!:
\]
proves \(D_F(x_1-x_2)=D_F(x_2-x_1)\).
\item
Write \(\phi_i=\phi_i^{(+)}+\phi_i^{(-)}\), with the positive-frequency
part annihilating the vacuum and the negative-frequency part creating
quanta.  Moving every annihilation part to the right produces one
commutator for each interchange with a creation part.  Since three fields
cannot be completely paired, the possibilities are no contraction or one
contraction:
\[
\begin{split}
 T\{\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\}={}&:\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3:
 +D_F(x_1-x_2)\phi_3\\
 &+D_F(x_1-x_3)\phi_2
 +D_F(x_2-x_3)\phi_1.
\end{split}
\]
The propagators are c-numbers, so their position relative to the
uncontracted field is immaterial.  The result is symmetric in all three
points, as required by time ordering for a scalar field.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{6}{Cubic-Theory Vacuum Graphs and Normalized Correlators}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The zeroth-order term is one, and the first-order term vanishes because
it contains three free fields.  With the vertex factor denoted \(\ii\lambda\),
the second-order term is
\[
 \frac{(\ii\lambda)^2}{2(3!)^2}
 \int \dd^4x\,\dd^4y\,
 \langle0|T\{\phi(x)^3\phi(y)^3\}|0\rangle.
\]
There are \(3!=6\) contractions joining every field at \(x\) to one at
\(y\), and \(3\cdot3=9\) contractions with one joining line and one
tadpole at each vertex.  Thus
\[
 \bra0S\ket0
 =1+(\ii\lambda)^2\int \dd^4x\,\dd^4y
 \left[\frac{D_F(x-y)^3}{12}
 +\frac{D_F(x-y)D_F(0)^2}{8}\right]+O(\lambda^4).
\]
The two connected vacuum graphs have symmetry factors \(12\) and \(8\).
\item
At order \(n\), cubic theory contains \(3n\) free fields.  A vacuum
expectation value is a sum over complete pairings and hence vanishes when
\(3n\) is odd.  Since \(3n\) has the parity of \(n\), every odd
perturbative order vanishes.

\SupplementarySolution{7}{A Classical Source and Coherent In--Out States}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The in--out relation gives
\[
 F'=S^{-1}FS,\qquad G'=S^{-1}GS,
\]
and therefore
\[
 e^{F'}=S^{-1}e^FS,\qquad e^{G'}=S^{-1}e^GS.
\]
Because \(\mu\) is a c-number,
\[
 e^{-\mu/2}e^{F'}e^{G'}
 =S^{-1}\!\left(e^{-\mu/2}e^Fe^G\right)S
 =S^{-1}SS=S.
\]
With the convention \(\InKet0=S\OutKet0\), and since
\(G'\OutKet0=0\),
\[
 \InKet0=S\OutKet0
 =e^{-\mu/2}e^{F'}\OutKet0.
\]
Thus the in-vacuum is a normalized multimode coherent state of
out-particles.
\item
Since \([a,za^\dagger]=z\),
\(ae^{za^\dagger}=e^{za^\dagger}(a+z)\), so
\(a\ket z=z\ket z\).  Equivalently,
\[
 \ket z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket n,
\]
so the eigenvalue is \(z\).
\item
Using the number-state expansion,
\[
 \braket{z_2}{z_1}
 =\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(z_2^*z_1)^n}{n!}
 =e^{z_2^*z_1}.
\]
In particular, \(\braket z z=e^{|z|^2}\); the state in this problem is
deliberately unnormalized.
\item
Since \(a\ket z=z\ket z\) and \(\bra z a^\dagger=z^*\bra z\),
\[
 \langle q\rangle=\frac{z+z^*}{\sqrt2}
 =\sqrt2\,\operatorname{Re}z,\qquad
 \langle p\rangle=\frac{z-z^*}{\ii\sqrt2}
 =\sqrt2\,\operatorname{Im}z.
\]
\item
Normal ordering \(q^2\) and \(p^2\) and using
\([a,a^\dagger]=1\) gives
\[
 \langle q^2\rangle=\langle q\rangle^2+\frac12,\qquad
 \langle p^2\rangle=\langle p\rangle^2+\frac12.
\]
Hence
\[
 \Delta q=\Delta p=\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad
 \Delta q\,\Delta p=\frac12.
\]
The displacement changes the means but leaves the vacuum fluctuations
unchanged.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{8}{Recovering a Classical Field from an Impulse}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The equal-time algebra and the free Hamiltonian give
\[
 \dot\phi=\ii[H,\phi]=\pi,\qquad
 \dot\pi=\ii[H,\pi]=(\boldsymbol\nabla^2-m^2)\phi+J.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \ddot\phi=(\boldsymbol\nabla^2-m^2)\phi+J,
\]
which is precisely
\((\Box-m^2)\phi=-J\) for
\(\Box=\boldsymbol\nabla^2-\partial_t^2\).
\item
Integrating the equation across an infinitesimal interval containing
\(t=0\), the finite spatial and mass terms vanish in the limit, while
\(\int \dd t\,J=j\).  Hence
\[
 \dot\phi(0^+)-\dot\phi(0^-)=j.
\]
The field itself cannot jump without producing a derivative of a delta
function, so
\[
 \phi(0^+)=\phi(0^-),\qquad
 \pi(0^+)=\pi(0^-)+j.
\]
\item
The inverse of the displayed mode expansion is
\[
 a(\mathbf p,0)=\int \dd^3x\,e^{-\ii\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}
 \left[
 \sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\mathbf p}}2}\,\phi(\mathbf x,0)
 +\frac{\ii}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathbf p}}}\,\pi(\mathbf x,0)
 \right].
\]
Only the momentum jump contributes, giving
\[
 a(\mathbf p,0^+)-a(\mathbf p,0^-)
 =\frac{\ii\,\widetilde j(\mathbf p)}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathbf p}}}.
\]
\item
Let
\(\alpha(\mathbf p)=\ii\widetilde j(\mathbf p)/
\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathbf p}}\).  Since the pre-impulse vacuum obeys
\(a(\mathbf p,0^-)\ket0=0\),
\[
 a(\mathbf p,0^+)\ket0=\alpha(\mathbf p)\ket0.
\]
Thus the same state is a multimode coherent state for the post-impulse
annihilation operators.  Its one-particle density matrix is
\[
 \bra0a^\dagger(\mathbf p,0^+)a(\mathbf q,0^+)\ket0
 =\alpha(\mathbf p)^*\alpha(\mathbf q),
\]
so the diagonal momentum density is
\(|\widetilde j(\mathbf p)|^2/(2\omega_{\mathbf p})\), with the
\((2\pi)^3\) measure fixed by the mode convention in the question.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{9}{Relativistic Propagators and Causal Support}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The positive-frequency contraction is
\[
 D(z)=\int\frac{\dd^3\mathbf p}{(2\pi)^3\,2\omega_{\mathbf p}}e^{\ii p\cdot z}
 =\int\frac{\dd^4p}{(2\pi)^3}
 \delta(p^2+m^2)\theta(p^0)e^{\ii p\cdot z}.
\]
\item
The measure, mass-shell delta, positive-energy sheet, and scalar product
are invariant under the proper orthochronous Lorentz group; hence
\(D(\Lambda z)=D(z)\).
\item
For spacelike \(z\), choose \(z^0=0\) and set \(r=\sqrt{z^2}\).  The
radial integral gives
\[
 D(z)=\frac{m}{4\pi^2r}K_1(mr)
 \sim \frac{\sqrt m}{2(2\pi)^{3/2}r^{3/2}}e^{-mr}.
\]
\item
\([\phi(x),\phi(y)]=D(x-y)-D(y-x)\).  Spacelike-separated points have
a frame in which they are simultaneous, so the two terms coincide and
the vacuum expectation value vanishes.  For a free field the commutator
is a c-number, so the operator commutator vanishes as well.
\item
For \(\mathcal L=\mathcal L_0+J\phi\), variation gives
\((\Box-m^2)\phi=-J\).
\item
Applying the Klein--Gordon operator gives
\[
 (\Box-m^2)\left[\ii\int \dd^4y\,G(x-y)J(y)\right]
 =\ii^2J(x)=-J(x).
\]
Thus
\[
 \phi(x)=\phi_{\rm hom}(x)
 +\ii\int \dd^4y\,G(x-y)J(y),
\qquad
(\Box-m^2)\phi_{\rm hom}=0.
\]
\item
For \(z^0>0\), close the \(p^0\) contour in the lower half-plane; for
\(z^0<0\), close it in the upper half-plane.  The retarded prescription
places both poles below the real axis, the advanced prescription places
both above it, and the Feynman prescription places the positive-energy
pole below and the negative-energy pole above.  Summing residues gives
\[
\begin{split}
 G_R(z)&=\theta(z^0)[D(z)-D(-z)],\\
 G_A(z)&=\theta(-z^0)[D(-z)-D(z)],\\
 G_F(z)&=\theta(z^0)D(z)+\theta(-z^0)D(-z).
\end{split}
\]
\item
Since \(G_R(z)=\theta(z^0)[D(z)-D(-z)]\), part 4 makes it vanish
outside the future light cone.  The retarded source response is causal.
\item
At spacelike separation \(G_F=D\), which is nonzero but exponentially
small.  It encodes vacuum correlation; signaling is governed by the
retarded commutator and therefore remains causal.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementarySolution{10}{The Force Between Static Sources}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Expand the exponential and use Wick's theorem.  Odd terms vanish.  At
order \(2n\), complete pairings contribute
\((2n)!/(2^n n!)\) copies of the same product, so
\[
\begin{split}
 Z[J]
 &=\sum_{n=0}^\infty
 \frac{i^{2n}}{(2n)!}\frac{(2n)!}{2^n n!}
 \left[\int \dd^4x\,\dd^4y\,
 J(x)D_F(x-y)J(y)\right]^n\\
 &=\exp\!\left[-\frac12\int \dd^4x\,\dd^4y\,
 J(x)D_F(x-y)J(y)\right].
\end{split}
\]
This is the exact Gaussian result.
\item
Write \(J=J_1+J_2\).  The terms \(J_1D_FJ_1\) and \(J_2D_FJ_2\)
are the discarded point-source self-energies.  The factor \(1/2\)
cancels the two equal cross terms, leaving
\[
 \log Z_{\rm int}
 =-g^2\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}\dd t\,\dd t'\,
 D_F(t-t',\mathbf R).
\]
For \(T\) much longer than every other scale, set
\(\tau=t-t'\) and replace the overlap length by \(T\):
\[
 \log Z_{\rm int}
 =-g^2T\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\tau\,
 D_F(\tau,\mathbf R)+O(T^0).
\]
With the mostly-plus propagator convention,
\[
 D_F(x)=\int\frac{\dd^4p}{(2\pi)^4}
 \frac{-\ii\,e^{\ii p\cdot x}}{p^2+m^2-\ii\epsilon},
\]
the time integral sets \(p^0=0\), and therefore
\[
 \int \dd\tau\,D_F(\tau,\mathbf R)
 =-\ii\int\frac{\dd^3p}{(2\pi)^3}
 \frac{e^{\ii\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf R}}{\mathbf p^2+m^2}
 =-\frac{\ii}{4\pi R}e^{-mR}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 Z_{\rm int}
 =\exp\!\left[\frac{ig^2T}{4\pi R}e^{-mR}\right]
\]
up to the omitted self-energy phase and endpoint corrections.
\item
Comparison with \(Z_{\rm int}=e^{-\ii V(R)T}\) gives the Yukawa potential
\[
 V(R)=-\frac{g^2}{4\pi R}e^{-mR}.
\]
Since
\[
 -\frac{\dd V}{\dd R}
 =-\frac{g^2e^{-mR}}{4\pi}
 \left(\frac{m}{R}+\frac1{R^2}\right)<0
\]
when the positive radial direction points from one source away from the
other, the force is attractive.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
