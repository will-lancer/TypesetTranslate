\SupplementaryExercise{1}{The Proca Field}
  {(Adapted from University of Cambridge, Mathematical Tripos Part III, Quantum Field Theory, Paper 301, 2024, Question 1.)}
  {cambridge-partiii-2024-qft-paper301-q1}
In the volume's mostly-plus convention, the Proca Lagrangian is
\[
 \mathcal L=-\frac14F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}
 -\frac12m^2A^\mu A_\mu,
 \qquad
 F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu,
 \qquad m^2>0.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Verify that this theory is not invariant under the standard gauge
transformation of \(A_\mu\).  Derive the equations of motion and show that
they imply
\[
 (\Box-m^2)A^\nu=0,
 \qquad
 \partial_\mu A^\mu=0.
\]
\item
Evaluate the energy-momentum tensor of the Proca theory.  Show that its
on-shell energy density is positive definite, up to a total spatial
derivative.
\item
Find the plane-wave solutions of the equations from part (a), and show that
the massive vector has three polarization states.
\item
Construct the Proca propagator, giving an integral expression for
\[
 \Delta_{\mu\nu}(x-y)
 =\bra{\Omega}T\!\left(A_\mu(x)A_\nu(y)\right)\ket{\Omega}.
\]
\emph{Hint:} Regard the propagator as a Green function.  Write the equation
of motion as \(\Pi_{\mu\nu}A^\nu=0\), then invert the operator
\(\Pi_{\mu\nu}\).
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{3}{Electron--Positron Annihilation to Muons}
  {(Adapted from Bernhard Mistlberger and Kevin Zhou, Stanford PHYS 330, Fall 2022, Problem Set 8, Problem 3.)}
  {zhou-mistlberger-phys330-f22-ps8-p3}
One of the key successes of quantum electrodynamics is its description of
particle creation and annihilation at relativistic energies.  Consider
\[
 e^+(p_{e^+})+e^-(p_{e^-})
 \longrightarrow
 \mu^+(p_{\mu^+})+\mu^-(p_{\mu^-}),
\]
where the electron and muon are Dirac fields of charge magnitude \(e\), with
masses \(m_e\) and \(m_\mu\).  In the volume's mostly-plus convention define
\[
 \begin{split}
 s&=-(p_{e^+}+p_{e^-})^2,\\
 t&=-(p_{e^-}-p_{\mu^-})^2,\\
 u&=-(p_{e^-}-p_{\mu^+})^2.
 \end{split}
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Find the scattering amplitude \(\mathcal M\) to leading nonvanishing order
in \(e\).
\item
Let \(\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}\) denote the squared amplitude summed over
final spins and averaged over initial spins.  Compute it in terms of
\(e,m_e,m_\mu,s,t,u\), without neglecting the electron mass.
\item
For the remainder of the problem work in the center-of-mass frame.  Rewrite
\(\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}\) in terms of
\(e,m_e,m_\mu,s\) and the angle \(\theta\) between
\(\mathbf p_{e^-}\) and \(\mathbf p_{\mu^-}\).
\item
Use
\[
 \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}
 =\frac{1}{64\pi^2s}
 \frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{|\mathbf p_i|}
 \overline{|\mathcal M|^2}
\]
to compute the differential and total cross sections.  The displayed
two-body formula applies to distinct final particles; for \(n\) identical
final particles the fully labelled phase-space integral must be divided by
\(n!\).
\end{enumerate}
For orientation or to check the result, you may consult Peskin and Schroeder,
Section 5.1.  That discussion neglects the electron mass, whereas every part
of this problem requires \(m_e\) to be retained.

\SupplementaryExercise{4}{Gauge Symmetry, QED Rules, and Three Tree Processes}
  {(Adapted from University of Cambridge, Mathematical Tripos Part III, Quantum Field Theory, Paper 301, 2023, Question 4.)}
  {cambridge-partiii-2023-qft-paper301-q4}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
What is meant by a gauge symmetry?  Write the Lagrangian density for quantum
electrodynamics and demonstrate its gauge invariance explicitly.  Use the
volume's mostly-plus metric and gamma-matrix conventions.
\item
Write down, without derivation, a complete set of momentum-space Feynman
rules for calculating QED scattering amplitudes in a covariant gauge, such
as Feynman gauge.  State the arrow, external-state, and relative
fermion-sign prescriptions as well as the vertex and propagators.
\item
Draw every tree-level diagram contributing to each of the following
two-particle processes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item
\(e^-(p_1,s_1)+e^+(p_2,s_2)\to
e^-(p_3,s_3)+e^+(p_4,s_4)\);
\item
\(e^-(p_1,s_1)+e^+(p_2,s_2)\to
\gamma(k_1,\lambda_1)+\gamma(k_2,\lambda_2)\);
\item
\(\gamma(k_1,\lambda_1)+e^+(p_1,s_1)\to
\gamma(k_2,\lambda_2)+e^+(p_2,s_2)\).
\end{enumerate}
For every initial and final particle, identify the momentum together with
the spin component or photon helicity that characterizes its state, and
label those quantum numbers on the diagrams.
\item
Use the rules from part (b) to evaluate the complete tree-level scattering
amplitude for each of the three processes.  The two diagrams for a given
process must be combined with their correct relative sign.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{5}{Canonical Structure of Scalar Electrodynamics}
  {(Adapted from Daniel Harlow, Lecture Notes for Physics 8.324: Relativistic Quantum Field Theory II, Fall 2024, Section 9.8, Problem 5.)}
  {harlow-qft2-fall2024-sec9-8-p5}
Scalar electrodynamics couples a complex scalar field \(\Phi\) to a Maxwell
field \(A_\mu\).  In the volume's mostly-plus convention its Lagrangian
density is
\[
 \mathcal L=
 -\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
 -(\partial_\mu\Phi^*+iqA_\mu\Phi^*)
  (\partial^\mu\Phi-iqA^\mu\Phi)
 -m^2|\Phi|^2 .
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Determine the current density that appears in Maxwell's equations.
\item
Derive the equation of motion for \(\Phi\).
\item
Find the canonical conjugates of \(\Phi\) and \(\Phi^*\), and express the
full current density in terms of canonical coordinates and momenta.
\item
Show directly that the action is invariant under the local transformation
\[
 A'_\mu=A_\mu+\partial_\mu\Omega,
 \qquad
 \Phi'=e^{iq\Omega}\Phi .
\]
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{7}{The Gupta--Bleuler Physical Space and Residual Gauge Modes}
  {(Adapted from Hong Liu and MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.323 Assignment 11, Problem 1.)}
  {mit-823-s23-ps11-ch8-curated-parent-1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
In covariantly gauge-fixed free electrodynamics, calculate an equal-time
commutator of \(A_0\) with \(\partial_\mu A^\mu\).  Explain why its nonzero
value forbids imposing the Lorenz condition as an operator identity.
\item
Separate \(\partial_\mu A^\mu\) into annihilation and creation parts.
Formulate the Gupta--Bleuler condition, express it in terms of time-like and
longitudinal photon operators, and show why the vacuum satisfies it.
\item
Form the sum and difference of the time-like and longitudinal photon
annihilation operators.  Compute all four mixed commutators and identify
which creation operator generates the null excitations allowed by the
physical-state condition.
\item
Prove that every Gupta--Bleuler state is a transverse state plus a vector
orthogonal to every physical state.  Explain why quotienting by these null
vectors leaves a positive-definite two-polarization Hilbert space.
\item
Evaluate the one-particle wavefunction
\(\bra{\Omega}A_\mu(x)\ket{\mathbf k,\mathrm{null}}\).
Show that it is a pure gradient \(\partial_\mu\lambda\), with
\(\Box\lambda=0\), and interpret the result.
\item
Replace the null photon of definite momentum by a normalizable wave packet.
Construct the corresponding scalar gauge parameter explicitly and prove
that the complete wavefunction remains a residual Lorenz-gauge
transformation.
\end{enumerate}

\SupplementaryExercise{12}{Nonminimal Electromagnetic Couplings}
  {(Adapted from Bernhard Mistlberger and Kevin Zhou, Stanford PHYS 330, Fall 2022, Problem Set 8, Problem 4.)}
  {zhou-mistlberger-phys330-f22-ps8-p4}
Use the one-particle background-field formalism of the source's Problem 1.
In the volume convention, the minimally coupled interaction Hamiltonian for
a Dirac field of charge \(q\) is
\[
 H_I=-iq\int d^3x\,\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\Psi A_\mu .
\]
Take relativistically normalized states
\[
 \ket{\mathbf p,s}
 =\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,a_s^\dagger(\mathbf p)\ket{\Omega},
\]
and assume that the electromagnetic field has negligible uncertainty, so
that \(A_\mu\) may be replaced by a time-independent classical expectation
value \(A_\mu^{\rm cl}(\mathbf x)\).  Then
\[
 \bra{\mathbf p,s}H_I\ket{\mathbf p',s'}
 =-iq\int d^3x\,
 e^{-i(\mathbf p-\mathbf p')\cdot\mathbf x}
 \bar u_s(p)\gamma^\mu u_{s'}(p')A_\mu^{\rm cl}(\mathbf x).
\]
This displayed matrix-element formula is to be used for
\(\ket{\mathbf p',s'}\ne\ket{\mathbf p,s}\), as in the source setup.
For a nonrelativistic particle of charge \(q'\) and magnetic moment
\(\boldsymbol\mu=gq'\mathbf S/(2m)\),
\[
 H_I^{\rm nr}=q'A_0(\mathbf x)-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B(\mathbf x),
\]
and matching relativistic to nonrelativistic normalization requires
\[
 \bra{\mathbf p,s}H_I\ket{\mathbf p',s'}
 =2m\,
 \bra{\mathbf p,s}H_I^{\rm nr}\ket{\mathbf p',s'}
 \qquad
 (|\mathbf p|,|\mathbf p'|\ll m).
\]
With
\(\gamma^{\mu\nu}=\frac12[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]\), the Gordon identity in
the volume convention is
\[
 -i\bar u(p')\gamma^\mu u(p)
 =\frac{1}{2m}\bar u(p')
 \left[(p'+p)^\mu+\gamma^{\mu\nu}(p'-p)_\nu\right]u(p).
\]
Detailed calculations are not required below; qualitative answers with
justification are sufficient.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The proton and neutron magnetic moments are not given by minimal Dirac
coupling.  Show that adding a term proportional to
\[
 i\bar\Psi[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]\Psi F_{\mu\nu}
\]
shifts the magnetic dipole moment.
\item
What is the physical effect of a term proportional to
\[
 \bar\Psi[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]\gamma_5\Psi F_{\mu\nu}?
\]
These are the two dimension-five nonminimal couplings.
\item
What is the physical effect of the dimension-six term
\[
 \bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\Psi\,\partial^\nu F_{\mu\nu}?
\]
\item
What is the physical effect of the dimension-six term
\[
 \bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\Psi\,\partial^\nu F_{\mu\nu}?
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
