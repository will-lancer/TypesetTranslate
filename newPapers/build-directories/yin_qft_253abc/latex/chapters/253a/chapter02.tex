\subsection{From the action to the transition kernel}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U002; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A01; notes=253a:10-11; pdf=20-22; video=96lN2omwit4:00:01:26.700-01:20:25.500; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000003..YIN253A-C02-T000039; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ001,YIN253A-C02-EQ002; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Classical mechanics admits a Lagrangian description in terms of $q$ and $\dot q$. Its equations follow by extremizing the action. The Hamiltonian description uses $q$ and the conjugate momentum $p$, with time evolution generated by the Poisson bracket. Hopefully this is familiar from classical mechanics.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U003; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A01; notes=253a:10; pdf=21; video=96lN2omwit4:00:02:35.879-00:08:14.060; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000005..YIN253A-C02-T000013; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ001; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  S[q]&=\int dt\,L(q,\dot q),
  &\dot f&=\{H,f\}_{\mathrm{Poisson}},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-classical-descriptions}\\
  \dot q&=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p},
  &\dot p&=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-hamilton-equations}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U004; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A01; notes=253a:10; pdf=21; video=96lN2omwit4:00:06:55.139-00:08:14.060; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000011..YIN253A-C02-T000013; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ001; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The Legendre transform relates these variables. Fixing $p=\partial L/\partial\dot q$ turns $p\dot q-L$ into the Hamiltonian as a function of $p$ and $q$.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U005; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A01; notes=253a:10; pdf=21; video=96lN2omwit4:00:06:55.139-00:08:14.060; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000011..YIN253A-C02-T000013; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ001; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{equation}
  p=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q},
  \qquad
  H(p,q)=\left.p\dot q-L(q,\dot q)\right|_{\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}=p}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-legendre-transform}
\end{equation}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U006; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A01; notes=253a:10-11; pdf=21-22; video=96lN2omwit4:00:10:19.380-00:17:06.020; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000019..YIN253A-C02-T000031; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ001,YIN253A-C02-EQ002; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Quantization supplies a Hilbert space and a Hamiltonian operator $\hat H$. Of course, there are two different ways to describe time evolution. Schr\"odinger states evolve under $U(t)$, while Heisenberg observables evolve by conjugation.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U007; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A01; notes=253a:10-11; pdf=21-22; video=96lN2omwit4:00:10:44.579-00:15:00.420; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000021..YIN253A-C02-T000028; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ001,YIN253A-C02-EQ002; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  &\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal H,
  \qquad H(p,q)\longrightarrow\hat H,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-quantum-bridge}\\
  &\ket{\psi}\longmapsto U(t)\ket{\psi},
  \qquad U(t)=e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\hat Ht},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-schrodinger-evolution}\\
  &O(t)=U(t)^{-1}OU(t),
  \qquad \dot O(t)=\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat H,O(t)].
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-heisenberg-evolution}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U008; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A01; notes=253a:11; pdf=22; video=96lN2omwit4:00:14:59.220-00:23:41.120; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000029..YIN253A-C02-T000039; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ002; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
There is no operator corresponding to the Lagrangian. Its route into quantum mechanics passes through the transition kernel. Coordinate eigenstates form a complete basis, so the matrix elements of $U(T)$ determine time evolution. The formal answer is a sum over paths with fixed endpoints.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U009; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A01; notes=253a:11; pdf=22; video=96lN2omwit4:00:18:53.220-00:23:41.120; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000034..YIN253A-C02-T000039; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ002; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{equation}
  \bra{q_f}U(T)\ket{q_i}
  =\int_{q(0)=q_i}^{q(T)=q_f}[Dq]\,
  e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}S[q]},
  \qquad
  S[q]=\int_0^Tdt\,L(q,\dot q).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-formal-kernel}
\end{equation}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U010; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A02; notes=253a:12-14; pdf=23-25; video=96lN2omwit4:00:23:53.000-01:20:19.219; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000040..YIN253A-C02-T000139; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ003,YIN253A-C02-EQ004; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{Time slicing and the regulated measure}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U011; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A02; notes=253a:12-13; pdf=23-24; video=96lN2omwit4:00:23:53.000-00:58:18.680; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000040..YIN253A-C02-T000100; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ003,YIN253A-C02-EQ004; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
There is an elephant in the room: what the heck is this measure? Divide the interval into $N$ steps of length $T/N$. Insert a coordinate resolution of the identity at every intermediate time and a momentum resolution inside every short-time kernel. The resulting object is an ordinary finite-dimensional integral.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U012; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A02; notes=253a:12; pdf=23; video=96lN2omwit4:00:29:45.000-00:46:53.240; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000051..YIN253A-C02-T000079; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ003; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  \bra{q_f}U(T)\ket{q_i}
  ={}&\int\prod_{n=1}^{N-1}d^Dq_n
  \prod_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{d^Dp_n}{(2\pi\hbar)^D}
  \nonumber\\[-1mm]
  &\times\exp\!\left\{\frac{i}{\hbar}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
  \left[p_n\cdot(q_{n+1}-q_n)
  -H(q_n,p_n)\frac{T}{N}
  +O\!\left(\hbar,\left(\frac{T}{N}\right)^2\right)\right]\right\},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-finite-phase-space-kernel}\\
  q_f&\equiv q_N,
  \qquad q_i\equiv q_0.
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U013; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A02; notes=253a:12; pdf=23; video=96lN2omwit4:00:33:27.299-00:35:58.160; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000057..YIN253A-C02-T000060; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ003; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf 1=\int\frac{d^Dp_n}{(2\pi\hbar)^D}\ket{p_n}\bra{p_n},
  \qquad
  \braket{q_{n+1}}{p_n}=e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}p_n\cdot q_{n+1}}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-momentum-resolution}
\end{equation}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U014; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A02; notes=253a:13; pdf=24; video=96lN2omwit4:00:39:18.260-00:56:57.740; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000067..YIN253A-C02-T000097; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ004; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The short-time symbol remembers an operator-ordering prescription, and there is no sacred ordering. Placing the momentum operators on one side gives $H$; reversing the construction gives $H'$. Their difference can begin at order $\hbar$. Drop that term and you change the answer, because a contribution small on one slice can accumulate across the product. Its effect must be determined before the continuum limit is taken.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U015; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A02; notes=253a:13; pdf=24; video=96lN2omwit4:00:53:02.520-00:56:57.740; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000090..YIN253A-C02-T000097; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ004; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  \bra{p_n}\hat H\ket{q_n}
  &=H(p_n,q_n)\braket{p_n}{q_n},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-left-ordering}\\
  \bra{q_n}\hat H\ket{p_n}
  &=H'(p_n,q_n)\braket{q_n}{p_n},
  \qquad H'-H=O(\hbar).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-right-ordering}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U016; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A02; notes=253a:13-14; pdf=24-25; video=96lN2omwit4:00:46:55.680-01:20:19.219; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000080..YIN253A-C02-T000139; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ004; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The continuum expression records the limiting prescription supplied by time slicing. When $H$ is quadratic in $p$, each momentum integral is Gaussian. Completing the square leaves the Legendre transform in the exponent. If the quadratic coefficient depends on the coordinate, the Gaussian determinant contributes to the configuration-space measure.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U017; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A02; notes=253a:13-14; pdf=24-25; video=96lN2omwit4:01:04:32.819-01:11:21.260; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000113..YIN253A-C02-T000124; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ004; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  \bra{q_f}U(T)\ket{q_i}
  &=\int\mathcal Dq\,\mathcal Dp\,
  \exp\!\left\{\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_0^Tdt\,
  \bigl(p\cdot\dot q-H(q,p)\bigr)\right\},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-continuum-phase-space}\\
  L(\xi,\dot\xi)
  &=\frac12G_{\alpha\beta}(\xi)\dot\xi^\alpha\dot\xi^\beta-V(\xi),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-generalized-lagrangian}\\
  H(p,\xi)
  &=\frac12\bigl(G^{-1}(\xi)\bigr)^{\alpha\beta}p_\alpha p_\beta+V(\xi)+O(\hbar),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-generalized-hamiltonian}\\
  [D\xi]
  &\leadsto\prod_{n=1}^{\widetilde N}
  \frac{d\xi_n^\alpha}{\sqrt{2\pi i\hbar\,\frac{T}{N}}}
  \sqrt{\det G_{\alpha\beta}(\xi_n)}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-generalized-measure}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U018; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A03; notes=253a:15-16; pdf=26-27; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:02:50.879-00:38:28.460; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000158..YIN253A-C02-T000218; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ005; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{Spectral correlators and Euclidean time}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U019; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A03; notes=253a:15-16; pdf=26-27; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:02:50.879-01:19:02.520; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000158..YIN253A-C02-T000218; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ005; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The first physical quantity to extract from this formalism is the energy spectrum. A ground-state two-point function contains that spectrum. Insert a complete basis of eigenstates between the operators. Each contributing state supplies an oscillation whose frequency is the gap $E_n-E_0$.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U020; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A03; notes=253a:15; pdf=26; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:04:05.159-00:10:52.579; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000160..YIN253A-C02-T000170; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ005; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  \hat H\ket{n}&=E_n\ket{n},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-energy-eigenstates}\\
  G(t)&=\bra{0}\hat q_\beta(t)\hat q_\beta(0)\ket{0}
  =\sum_n\bra{0}\hat q_\beta(0)\ket{n}
  \bra{n}\hat q_\beta(0)\ket{0}
  e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}(E_n-E_0)t}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-spectral-correlator}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U021; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A03; notes=253a:15-16; pdf=26-27; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:11:09.000-00:28:27.260; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000172..YIN253A-C02-T000200; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ005; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
There is nothing wrong with the spectral sum, though one must ask where the infinite sum converges. I do not like the word rotation here: nothing is being rotated; this is an analytic continuation. Continue through the lower half-plane, where $\operatorname{Im}t<0$. Writing $t=-i\tau$ with $\tau>0$ turns every positive gap into exponential suppression. Long Euclidean evolution kills every excited-state component exponentially, leaving only the ground state when the initial overlap is nonzero.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U022; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A03; notes=253a:15-16; pdf=26-27; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:12:43.740-00:28:27.260; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000175..YIN253A-C02-T000200; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ005; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  G(-i\tau)
  &=\sum_n\bra{0}\hat q(0)\ket{n}\bra{n}\hat q(0)\ket{0}
  e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(E_n-E_0)\tau},
  \qquad \tau>0,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-euclidean-spectral-correlator}\\
  e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(\hat H-E_0)T}\ket{\psi}
  &=c_0\ket{0}+\sum_{n>0}c_n
  e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}(E_n-E_0)T}\ket{n}
  \xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}c_0\ket{0}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-ground-state-projection}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U023; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A04; notes=253a:17-18; pdf=28-29; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:38:28.680-01:07:17.000; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000219..YIN253A-C02-T000282; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ006; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{The normalized Euclidean path integral}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U024; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A04; notes=253a:17; pdf=28; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:38:28.680-00:47:03.480; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000219..YIN253A-C02-T000238; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ006; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Place the operator insertions between long Euclidean evolution factors, then divide by the same matrix element without insertions. The ground-state overlap cancels in this ratio. Time slicing turns $\hat q$ into multiplication by the coordinate at the corresponding slice, which explains why path-integral insertions are ordinary variables.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U025; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A04; notes=253a:17-18; pdf=28-29; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:38:28.680-00:57:30.800; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000219..YIN253A-C02-T000261; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ006; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{equation}
  G_E(\tau)=\lim_{T\to\infty}
  \frac{\displaystyle\int[Dq]\,e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}S^E[q]}q(\tau)q(0)}
  {\displaystyle\int[Dq]\,e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}S^E[q]}},
  \qquad
  S^E[q]=\int d\tau\,L^E(q,\partial_\tau q).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-normalized-euclidean-path-integral}
\end{equation}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U026; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A04; notes=253a:17-18; pdf=28-29; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:47:04.200-00:55:31.460; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000239..YIN253A-C02-T000257; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ006; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Under $t=-i\tau$, one has $dt=-i\,d\tau$ and $\dot q=i\partial_\tau q$. The Euclidean Lagrangian is defined so that the Lorentzian phase becomes a decaying weight. For an ordinary kinetic term, rapidly varying paths carry a large Euclidean action. The whole point is that analytic continuation is unambiguous once the function is known in imaginary time. The path integral is therefore dominated by paths with small Euclidean action. In quantum field theory, locality will resolve some of the measure ambiguities that remain here.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U027; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A04; notes=253a:18; pdf=29; video=uzixOflp0tY:00:47:04.200-00:55:31.460; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000239..YIN253A-C02-T000257; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ006; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  q(t)&\mathrel{\overset{t=-i\tau}{\rightsquigarrow}}q(\tau),
  &\dot q&=i\partial_\tau q,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-wick-map}\\
  e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\int L\,dt}
  &\rightsquigarrow e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}S^E[q]},
  &L^E&=\frac12(\partial_\tau q)^2+V(q)
  \quad\text{for}\quad L=\frac12\dot q^{\,2}-V(q).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-euclidean-weight}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U028; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A05,YIN253A-C02-A06; notes=253a:19-23; pdf=30-34; video=uzixOflp0tY:01:07:17.280-01:19:04.800,M0py5a4RWhE:00:00:02.340-00:41:30.140; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000283..YIN253A-C02-T000396; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ007,YIN253A-C02-EQ008,YIN253A-C02-EQ009; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{The harmonic oscillator and its Green function}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U029; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A05; notes=253a:19-20; pdf=30-31; video=uzixOflp0tY:01:07:17.280-01:19:04.800; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000283..YIN253A-C02-T000304; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ007; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
For the harmonic oscillator, we will go through this painful process explicitly. Take the oscillator on the finite interval $[-T,T]$ with Dirichlet endpoints. Expand each path in a sine basis adapted to those endpoints. The action becomes a sum of independent positive Gaussian terms.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U030; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A05; notes=253a:19-20; pdf=30-31; video=uzixOflp0tY:01:08:15.480-01:18:37.400; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000284..YIN253A-C02-T000303; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ007; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  S^E[q]&=\int_{-T}^{T}d\tau\,
  \left[\frac12(\partial_\tau q)^2+\frac12\omega^2q^2\right],
  \qquad q(-T)=q(T)=0,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-oscillator-action}\\
  q(\tau)&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q_nf_n(\tau),
  \qquad f_n(\tau)=\frac1{\sqrt T}\sin\frac{n\pi(\tau+T)}{2T},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-dirichlet-modes}\\
  S^E[q]&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
  \frac12\left[\left(\frac{n\pi}{2T}\right)^2+\omega^2\right]q_n^2.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-diagonal-mode-action}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U031; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A06; notes=253a:21-22; pdf=32-33; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:00:02.340-01:23:02.640; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000321..YIN253A-C02-T000396; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ008,YIN253A-C02-EQ009; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The regulated functional integral is now a product of ordinary Gaussian integrals. Different modes are independent, so their covariance is diagonal. Reconstructing the coordinate-space correlator gives a sine sum. As $T$ grows, $k=n\pi/(2T)$ becomes continuous. Nowhere did we use the classical equation of motion; each $q_n$ is an ordinary integration variable.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U032; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A06; notes=253a:21-22; pdf=32-33; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:03:31.040-00:26:25.460; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000325..YIN253A-C02-T000364; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ008; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  \langle q_nq_m\rangle
  &=\delta_{nm}\frac{\hbar}{\left(\frac{n\pi}{2T}\right)^2+\omega^2},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-mode-covariance}\\
  \langle q(\tau_1)q(\tau_2)\rangle_T
  &=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{\hbar}{\left(\frac{n\pi}{2T}\right)^2+\omega^2}
  \frac1T
  \sin\frac{n\pi(\tau_1+T)}{2T}
  \sin\frac{n\pi(\tau_2+T)}{2T},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-finite-interval-covariance}\\
  \sum_n\frac{\pi}{2T}F\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2T}\right)
  &\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}\int_0^\infty dk\,F(k).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-sum-to-integral}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U033; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A06; notes=253a:22-23; pdf=33-34; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:24:02.220-00:40:23.359; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000360..YIN253A-C02-T000394; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ008,YIN253A-C02-EQ009; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Writing the sine functions as exponentials separates the direct propagation from the endpoint image. The elementary Fourier integral closes in the upper half-plane for positive $\tau$ and in the lower half-plane for negative $\tau$. This contour deformation is valid provided the integrand is analytic between the two contours. Write $\tau_{12}:=\tau_1-\tau_2$. The two cases combine through the absolute value of the Euclidean-time separation.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U034; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A06; notes=253a:22-23; pdf=33-34; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:27:59.640-00:40:23.359; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000368..YIN253A-C02-T000394; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ009; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\,\frac{e^{ik\tau}}{k^2+\omega^2}
  &=2\pi i\underset{k\to i\omega}{\operatorname{Res}}
  \frac{e^{ik\tau}}{k^2+\omega^2}
  =\frac{\pi}{\omega}e^{-\omega\tau},
  \qquad \tau>0,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-positive-time-residue}\\
  \langle q(\tau_1)q(\tau_2)\rangle_T
  &=\frac{\hbar}{\omega}\left[
  \frac12e^{-\omega|\tau_{12}|}
  -\frac12e^{-\omega(\tau_1+\tau_2+2T)}\right],
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-finite-T-propagator}\\
  \lim_{T\to\infty}\langle q(\tau_1)q(\tau_2)\rangle_T
  &=\frac{\hbar}{2\omega}e^{-\omega|\tau_{12}|}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-oscillator-propagator}
\end{align}
If the calculation becomes confusing, return to the spectral representation: the exponential decay records the oscillator's energy gap.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U035; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A07; notes=253a:24-27; pdf=35-38; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:41:31.940-01:23:04.739; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000397..YIN253A-C02-T000455; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ010,YIN253A-C02-EQ011; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{Gaussian identities and Wick contractions}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U036; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A07; notes=253a:24-25; pdf=35-36; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:41:31.940-01:06:19.880; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000397..YIN253A-C02-T000428; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ010; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The mode calculation was a pain. We can perform the Gaussian algebra once and for all. Let $A$ be symmetric, with the Gaussian understood in a convergent domain, and introduce a source $\mathbf J$. Completing the square gives the generating function.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U037; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A07; notes=253a:24; pdf=35; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:42:44.400-00:52:35.900; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000399..YIN253A-C02-T000413; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ010; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  Z[\mathbf J]
  &:=\int d^N\mathbf q\,
  e^{-\frac12\mathbf q^TA\mathbf q+\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf q}
  =(2\pi)^{N/2}(\det A)^{-1/2}
  e^{\frac12\mathbf J^TA^{-1}\mathbf J},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-finite-gaussian}\\
  \langle F(\mathbf q)\rangle
  &=\left.\frac1{Z[0]}F\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf J}\right)
  Z[\mathbf J]\right|_{\mathbf J=0},
  \qquad
  \langle q_nq_m\rangle=(A^{-1})_{nm}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-gaussian-source-derivatives}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U038; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A07; notes=253a:24-25; pdf=35-36; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:52:45.540-01:06:19.880; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000414..YIN253A-C02-T000428; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ010; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Four insertions generate $4!=24$ derivative assignments. Eight assignments give each covariance pairing, leaving three inequivalent terms. Wick's rule extends this pattern: pair every insertion once and sum over inequivalent pairings. Odd moments vanish.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U039; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A07; notes=253a:25; pdf=36; video=M0py5a4RWhE:00:52:45.540-01:06:19.880; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000414..YIN253A-C02-T000428; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ010; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  \langle q_nq_mq_rq_s\rangle
  ={}&(A^{-1})_{nm}(A^{-1})_{rs}
  +(A^{-1})_{nr}(A^{-1})_{ms}
  +(A^{-1})_{ns}(A^{-1})_{mr},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-four-point-gaussian}\\
  \left\langle\prod_{a=1}^{2r}q_{i_a}\right\rangle
  ={}&\sum_{P}\prod_{(a,b)\in P}(A^{-1})_{i_ai_b},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-wick-rule}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U040; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A07; notes=253a:26-27; pdf=37-38; video=M0py5a4RWhE:01:07:04.140-01:23:04.739; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000430..YIN253A-C02-T000455; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ011; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
For a Gaussian functional integral, the matrix becomes a differential operator. Its inverse is a Green kernel. Functional integration by parts finds the covariance directly and avoids choosing an overall normalization for every regulated mode. We will not attempt a more rigorous definition of the functional measure here.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U041; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A07; notes=253a:26-27; pdf=37-38; video=M0py5a4RWhE:01:07:04.140-01:23:04.739; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000430..YIN253A-C02-T000455; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ011; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  A\cdot g(\tau)&=(-\partial_\tau^2+\omega^2)g(\tau),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-functional-operator}\\
  A^{-1}\cdot g(\tau)&=\int d\tau'\,G(\tau,\tau')g(\tau'),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-functional-inverse}\\
  (-\partial_\tau^2+\omega^2)G(\tau,\tau')&=\delta(\tau-\tau'),
  \qquad
  \langle g(\tau_1)g(\tau_2)\rangle=G(\tau_1,\tau_2).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-functional-covariance}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U042; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A08; notes=253a:28-29; pdf=39-40; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:00:12.660-00:14:04.040; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000461..YIN253A-C02-T000484; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ012; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{Fourier space}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U043; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A08; notes=253a:28-29; pdf=39-40; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:00:12.660-01:18:28.140; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000461..YIN253A-C02-T000484; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ012; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Translation invariance makes the Fourier basis convenient. Reality relates the coefficients at opposite wave numbers, and $A$ acts by multiplication. The inverse kernel is immediate in this basis.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U044; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A08; notes=253a:28-29; pdf=39-40; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:00:12.660-00:14:04.040; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000461..YIN253A-C02-T000484; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ012; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  g(\tau)&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,
  \widetilde g(k)e^{ik\tau},
  &\widetilde g^*(k)&=\widetilde g(-k),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-fourier-convention}\\
  G(\tau,\tau')&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,
  \frac{e^{ik(\tau-\tau')}}{k^2+\omega^2},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-fourier-green-function}\\
  S^E&=\frac12\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,
  \widetilde g(-k)(k^2+\omega^2)\widetilde g(k),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-fourier-action}\\
  \left\langle\widetilde g(k_1)\widetilde g(k_2)\right\rangle
  &=\frac{2\pi\delta(k_1+k_2)}{k_1^2+\omega^2}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-fourier-covariance}
\end{align}
If this looks confusing, go back to the definition and see what the Fourier transform is doing. Any different prescription has to be shown to work.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U045; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A09; notes=253a:30-32; pdf=41-43; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:14:07.440-00:43:57.779; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000485..YIN253A-C02-T000548; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ013,YIN253A-C02-EQ014; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{Perturbation theory for the quartic oscillator}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U046; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A09; notes=253a:30-31; pdf=41-42; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:14:07.440-00:30:14.039; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000485..YIN253A-C02-T000523; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ013; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
If this model is unfamiliar, go back to an undergraduate quantum-mechanics treatment and see how its spectrum is obtained. Set the oscillator frequency to one and, for this part of the calculation, set $\hbar=1$. Add a quartic interaction normalized by $4!$. Expanding its Euclidean weight gives an asymptotic series whose coefficients are free Gaussian expectation values.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U047; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A09; notes=253a:30-31; pdf=41-42; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:14:40.139-00:30:14.039; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000487..YIN253A-C02-T000523; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ013; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  H&=\frac{p^2}{2}+\frac{q^2}{2}+\frac{g}{4!}q^4,
  &L^E&=\frac12(\partial_\tau q)^2+\frac{q^2}{2}+\frac{g}{4!}q^4,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-quartic-oscillator}\\
  \frac{Z_g}{Z_0}
  &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac1{n!}\left(-\frac{g}{4!}\right)^n
  \int \prod_{a=1}^{n}d\tau_a\,
  \left\langle \prod_{a=1}^{n}q^4(\tau_a)\right\rangle_0.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-quartic-expansion}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U048; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A09; notes=253a:31-32; pdf=42-43; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:30:22.799-00:43:57.779; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000524..YIN253A-C02-T000548; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ014; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
At first order, the four fields at one vertex admit three Wick pairings. The factor of three turns $-g/4!$ into $-g/8$ after the two propagators are included. At second order, the eight fields generate connected and disconnected vacuum graphs. The good news is that the perturbation-theory series does not converge. Finite-order terms remain useful at small coupling. As often happens here, that is a feature, not a bug.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U049; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A09; notes=253a:31-32; pdf=42-43; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:31:26.460-00:43:34.700; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000526..YIN253A-C02-T000546; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ014; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  -\frac{g}{4!}\int d\tau\,\langle q^4(\tau)\rangle_0
  &=-\frac{g}{8}\int d\tau\,\bigl(G_0(\tau,\tau)\bigr)^2,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-first-vacuum-graph}\\
  \left.\frac{Z_g}{Z_0}\right|_{g^2}
  &=\frac12\left(-\frac{g}{4!}\right)^2
  \int d\tau_1\,d\tau_2\,
  \left\langle q^4(\tau_1)q^4(\tau_2)\right\rangle_0.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-second-order-vacuum-graphs}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U050; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A10; notes=253a:33-37; pdf=44-48; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:45:10.920-01:18:28.140; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000549..YIN253A-C02-T000596; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ015,YIN253A-C02-EQ016; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{Connected vacuum graphs and the ground-state energy}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U051; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A10; notes=253a:33-34; pdf=44-45; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:45:10.920-00:59:44.359; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000549..YIN253A-C02-T000565; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ015; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Suppose an order-$g^n$ vacuum graph has $m_\ell$ connected components containing $\ell$ vertices. The component counts partition the $n$ vertices. Dividing by permutations of identical components gives the grouping factor that makes connected graphs exponentiate. You do not have to calculate every graph by hand; the exponentiation follows from expanding the exponential.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U052; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A10; notes=253a:33-34; pdf=44-45; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:47:01.500-00:59:44.359; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000552..YIN253A-C02-T000565; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ015; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  \sum_{\ell\geq1}m_\ell&=K,
  &\sum_{\ell\geq1}\ell m_\ell&=n,
  \nonumber\\
  N(\{m_\ell\})
  &=\frac{n!}{\prod_{\ell\geq1}(\ell!)^{m_\ell}m_\ell!},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-component-count}\\
  \frac{Z_g}{Z_0}
  &=\sum_{\{m_\ell\}}\prod_{\ell\geq1}\frac1{m_\ell!}
  \left(\frac{G^{(\ell)}}{\ell!}\right)^{m_\ell}
  =\exp\!\left(\sum_{\ell\geq1}\frac{G^{(\ell)}}{\ell!}\right).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-connected-exponentiation}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U053; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A10; notes=253a:34-36; pdf=45-47; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:00:55:10.559-01:16:44.179; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000561..YIN253A-C02-T000593; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ015,YIN253A-C02-EQ016; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
A connected vacuum graph has one unrestricted common Euclidean time. Its relative-time integrals converge because the free propagator decays exponentially, leaving one factor proportional to the interval length. In the spectral representation of $Z$, one term dominates at large $T$, assuming a unique ground state. Thus $\log Z$ is extensive and directly determines the ground-state energy.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U054; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A10; notes=253a:35-37; pdf=46-48; video=vk_RlYUKUyM:01:07:07.799-01:17:16.280; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000580..YIN253A-C02-T000594; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ016; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  Z&=\bra{q_f}e^{-HT}\ket{q_i},
  &\log Z&=-E_0T+O(1),
  &E_0&=-\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\log Z,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-vacuum-energy}\\
  \Delta E_0&=\frac{g}{8}\left(\frac1{2\omega}\right)^2+O(g^2),
  &\left.\Delta E_0\right|_{\omega=1}
  &=\frac{g}{4!}\langle\hat q^{\,4}\rangle_0
  =\frac{g}{32}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-first-vacuum-energy-shift}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U055; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A11,YIN253A-C02-A12; notes=253a:38-41; pdf=49-52; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:01:10.080-00:52:48.900; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000608..YIN253A-C02-T000703; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ017,YIN253A-C02-EQ018; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{The normalized two-point function and self-energy}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U056; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A11; notes=253a:38-39; pdf=49-50; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:01:10.080-01:18:54.780; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000608..YIN253A-C02-T000646; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ017; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Expand the numerator of the normalized two-point function. Every Wick graph factors into a component containing the external insertions and a collection of vacuum components. Summing the vacuum components produces $Z$, which cancels the normalization. The surviving graphs connect the two external points. Graphs make it easy to organize the possible contractions, though their combinatorial factors still have to be calculated.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U057; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A11; notes=253a:38-39; pdf=49-50; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:14:43.199-00:24:05.400; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000632..YIN253A-C02-T000646; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ017; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  \langle q(\tau)q(0)\rangle
  &=\begin{cases}
  \bra{0}\hat q(\tau)\hat q(0)\ket{0},&\tau\geq0,\\[2pt]
  \bra{0}\hat q(0)\hat q(\tau)\ket{0},&\tau<0,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-euclidean-ordering}\\
  G_0(\tau)&=\frac12e^{-|\tau|}
  =\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\widetilde G_0(k)e^{ik\tau},
  \qquad \widetilde G_0(k)=\frac1{k^2+1}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-free-euclidean-propagator}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U058; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A12; notes=253a:40-41; pdf=51-52; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:24:01.260-00:50:36.480; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000647..YIN253A-C02-T000695; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ018; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
For the one-vertex correction, the first external field has four choices at the vertex and the second has three. Four times three. It is very important to get this counting right. With the factor $-g/4!$, the coefficient is $-g/2$. Momentum space then organizes the connected graphs by their independent loop wave numbers. If these graph rules become confusing, always go back to the Wick expansion.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U059; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A12; notes=253a:40; pdf=51; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:25:16.620-00:33:47.539; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000649..YIN253A-C02-T000662; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ018; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{equation}
  G^{(1)}(\tau)
  =-\frac{g}{2}\int d\tau'\,
  G_0(\tau-\tau')G_0(\tau')G_0(0)
  =-\frac{g}{4}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{e^{ik\tau}}{(k^2+1)^2}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-tadpole-correction}
\end{equation}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U060; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A12; notes=253a:41; pdf=52; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:39:57.900-00:52:48.900; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000675..YIN253A-C02-T000698; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ018; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
What does 1PI stand for? A two-point graph is one-particle irreducible when cutting any single internal propagator leaves its two external sides connected. The self-energy $\Sigma(k)$ is the sum of these 1PI graphs with the external free propagators amputated. Repeated insertions form a geometric series.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U061; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A12; notes=253a:41; pdf=52; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:45:02.040-00:52:48.900; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000685..YIN253A-C02-T000698; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ018; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  G(\tau)
  &=\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}e^{ik\tau}\widetilde G_0(k)
  \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl(\widetilde G_0(k)\Sigma(k)\bigr)^n
  =\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{e^{ik\tau}}{k^2+1-\Sigma(k)},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-self-energy-resummation}\\
  \Sigma(k)&=-\frac{g}{4}
  +\frac{g^2}{8}\left(\frac14+\frac1{k^2+9}\right)+O(g^3).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-self-energy}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U062; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A13; notes=253a:42-43; pdf=53-54; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:56:20.780-01:13:22.940; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000704..YIN253A-C02-T000734; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ019; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{Poles and interacting energy gaps}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U063; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A13; notes=253a:42-43; pdf=53-54; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:56:20.780-01:13:22.940; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000704..YIN253A-C02-T000734; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ019; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The Fourier expression looks strange beside the spectral sum: one uses $e^{ik\tau}$ and the other uses decaying real exponentials. Analytic continuation in $k$ identifies them. For positive $\tau$, close the contour in the upper half-plane. A pole at $k=i\omega_n$ contributes $e^{-\omega_n\tau}$, so its position gives the energy gap.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U064; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A13; notes=253a:42-43; pdf=53-54; video=3VG2kDHso08:00:56:20.780-01:12:06.239; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000704..YIN253A-C02-T000730; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ019; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  \widetilde G(k)
  &=\frac1{k^2+1+\frac g4-\frac{g^2}{8}
  \left(\frac14+\frac1{k^2+9}\right)+O(g^3)},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-dressed-propagator}\\
  \int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\widetilde G(k)e^{ik\tau}
  &=i\sum_n e^{-\omega_n\tau}
  \underset{k\to i\omega_n}{\operatorname{Res}}\widetilde G(k),
  \qquad \omega_n=\bar E_n-\bar E_0,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-pole-spectral-map}\\
  \omega_1&=1+\frac g8-\frac{g^2}{32}+O(g^3)
  =\bar E_1-\bar E_0.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-first-interacting-gap}
\end{align}
The divergence of the full coupling series does not make this approximation useless; at finite order it remains a good asymptotic approximation.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U065; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A14; notes=253a:44-47; pdf=55-58; video=3VG2kDHso08:01:13:27.900-01:18:54.780,TtMNnZ8__UU:00:00:14.179-00:25:56.340; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000735..YIN253A-C02-T000801; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ020,YIN253A-C02-EQ021; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{A derivative interaction and its ultraviolet divergence}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U066; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A14; notes=253a:44-45; pdf=55-56; video=3VG2kDHso08:01:13:27.900-01:18:54.780; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000735..YIN253A-C02-T000744; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ020; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Are we allowed to make this nonlinear coordinate change in Lagrangian mechanics? Certainly. Consider a coordinate-dependent kinetic term. Its canonical momentum makes the Hamiltonian ordering issue explicit. A nonlinear coordinate $y$ gives a standard kinetic term through the stated perturbative order, while the path integral will be kept in the $\phi$ variable.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U067; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A14; notes=253a:43-45; pdf=54-56; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:00:14.179-00:51:10.020; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000749..YIN253A-C02-T000757; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ020; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\begin{align}
  L&=\frac12\dot q^{\,2}-\frac12q^2-\frac g8q^2\dot q^{\,2},
  &p&=\dot q\left(1-\frac g4q^2\right),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-derivative-model}\\
  H&=\frac12\left(1-\frac g4q^2\right)^{-1}p^2+\frac12q^2,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-derivative-hamiltonian}\\
  y&=\phi-\frac g{24}\phi^3+O(g^2),
  &V(y)&=\frac12y^2+\frac g{24}y^4+O(g^2),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-coordinate-redefinition}\\
  L^E&=\frac12(\partial_\tau\phi)^2+\frac12\phi^2
  -\frac g8\phi^2(\partial_\tau\phi)^2.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-derivative-euclidean-action}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U068; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A14; notes=253a:45-46; pdf=56-57; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:02:52.980-00:20:36.780; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000755..YIN253A-C02-T000787; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ020,YIN253A-C02-EQ021; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Up to a total derivative, the interaction may be written with a second derivative on one field. At first order, the contraction in which both derivatives act on the loop contains a coincident second derivative of the free propagator. In Fourier space its loop integrand approaches one at large wave number.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U069; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A14; notes=253a:45-46; pdf=56-57; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:02:52.980-00:20:36.780; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000755..YIN253A-C02-T000787; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ020,YIN253A-C02-EQ021; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  -\frac g8\phi^2(\partial_\tau\phi)^2
  &=-\frac g{24}\partial_\tau(\phi^3\partial_\tau\phi)
  +\frac g{24}\phi^3\partial_\tau^2\phi,
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-derivative-integration-by-parts}\\
  \frac g4\int d\tau'\,G_0(\tau-\tau')G_0(\tau')[-\partial^2G_0(0)]
  &=\frac g4\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{e^{ik\tau}}{(k^2+1)^2}
  \int\frac{dk'}{2\pi}\frac{k'^2}{k'^2+1}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-divergent-derivative-contraction}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U070; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A14; notes=253a:46-47; pdf=57-58; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:17:24.000-00:25:56.340; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000782..YIN253A-C02-T000801; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ021; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The calculation is completely correct. We have a problem: the integral diverges. It has exposed the ultraviolet content of the formal measure. Define that measure with a wave-number cutoff before evaluating contractions. The mixed derivative placement vanishes by symmetry because its loop integrand is odd.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U071; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A14; notes=253a:47; pdf=58; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:20:34.500-00:25:56.340; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000788..YIN253A-C02-T000801; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ021; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{equation}
  [D\phi]=\prod_kd\widetilde\phi_k
  \quad\longrightarrow\quad
  [D\phi]_\Lambda=\prod_{|k|<\Lambda}d\widetilde\phi_k,
  \qquad
  G(\tau)=\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{e^{ik\tau}}{k^2+1-\Sigma(k)}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-cutoff-measure}
\end{equation}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U072; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A15; notes=253a:48-51; pdf=59-62; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:26:19.320-00:51:10.020; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000802..YIN253A-C02-T000851; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ022,YIN253A-C02-EQ023; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
\subsection{Counterterms and the definition of the path integral}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U073; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A15; notes=253a:48-49; pdf=59-60; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:26:19.320-00:43:36.060; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000802..YIN253A-C02-T000837; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ022; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
With the cutoff in place, the order-$g$ self-energy is finite for fixed $\Lambda$. Its first integral grows linearly with the cutoff. Restore $\hbar$ to identify the quantum order of this loop effect: the Gaussian propagator carries one power of $\hbar$, while the interaction vertex carries $1/\hbar$ from the Euclidean weight.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U074; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A15; notes=253a:48-49; pdf=59-60; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:26:49.020-00:37:15.720; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000803..YIN253A-C02-T000826; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ022; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  \Sigma(k)
  &=\frac g4\int_{|k'|<\Lambda}\frac{dk'}{2\pi}\frac{k'^2}{k'^2+1}
  +\frac g4k^2\int\frac{dk'}{2\pi}\frac1{k'^2+1}+O(g^2)
  \nonumber\\
  &=\frac g4\left(\frac{k^2}{2}+\frac{\Lambda}{\pi}-\frac12
  +O(\Lambda^{-1})\right)+O(g^2),
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-cutoff-self-energy}\\
  Z&=\int[Dq]\exp\!\left[-\frac1\hbar\int d\tau\,L^E\right],
  &\widetilde G(k)&=\frac\hbar{k^2+1-\Sigma(k)}.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-restored-hbar}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U075; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A15; notes=253a:49; pdf=60; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:31:15.039-00:43:36.060; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000813..YIN253A-C02-T000837; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ022; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Who says we cannot add a local term of order $\hbar$? The classical Lagrangian fixes the $\hbar\to0$ limit. The quantum prescription may contain local terms of order $\hbar$, including terms associated with operator ordering and with the Jacobian of a nonlinear coordinate change. Add the quadratic counterterm $\Delta L^E=cg\hbar q^2/2$. It shifts the self-energy by $-cg\hbar$.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U076; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A15; notes=253a:49; pdf=60; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:35:14.579-00:43:36.060; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000822..YIN253A-C02-T000837; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ022; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  \Delta L^E&=c\,g\hbar\frac{q^2}{2},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-counterterm}\\
  \Sigma(k)&=\hbar g\left(\frac{k^2}{8}+\frac{\Lambda}{4\pi}
  -\frac18-c+O(\Lambda^{-1})\right)+O(g^2).
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-counterterm-self-energy}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U077; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A15; notes=253a:50-51; pdf=61-62; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:38:19.419-00:50:10.099; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000838..YIN253A-C02-T000851; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ023; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
Finiteness fixes the divergent part of $c$. The finite part of the counterterm is physics: it has to be fixed by additional input. Once that input is fixed, the pole of the propagator gives an unambiguous energy gap.

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U078; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A15; notes=253a:50-51; pdf=61-62; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:38:19.419-00:48:59.599; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000838..YIN253A-C02-T000847; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ023; class=NOTES_EXACT
\begin{align}
  c&=\frac{\Lambda}{4\pi}+c_{\mathrm{fin}},
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-counterterm-choice}\\
  \widetilde G(k)&=\frac\hbar{k^2+1-\Sigma(k)}
  \quad\text{has a pole at }k=i\omega_1,
  \nonumber\\
  \omega_1&=1+\hbar g\left(-\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi}
  +\frac c2+\frac18\right)+O(g^2)
  =\widehat E_1-\widehat E_0.
  \label{eq:253a-ch02-renormalized-gap}
\end{align}

% YIN-SOURCE: id=YIN253A-C02-U079; arguments=YIN253A-C02-A15; notes=253a:51; pdf=62; video=TtMNnZ8__UU:00:40:26.319-00:51:10.020; transcript=YIN253A-C02-T000840..YIN253A-C02-T000851; equations=YIN253A-C02-EQ023; class=SOURCE_COMPOSITE
The regularization scheme and its counterterms are part of the definition of the path integral. General quantum mechanics permits broad freedom in the Hamiltonian. In quantum field theory, these counterterms are more or less fixed, up to a small ambiguity, by Poincar\'e symmetry and causality.
