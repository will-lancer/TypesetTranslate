\subsection{Hydrodynamics and Hydrostatics}\label{sec:5.4}

In the absence of gravitation, the energy-momentum tensor of a perfect fluid
is that given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:2.10.7}:
\begin{equation}
  T^{\mu\nu}
  =
  p\eta^{\mu\nu}
  +
  (\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu,
  \tag{5.4.1}\label{eq:5.4.1}
\end{equation}
where \(u^\mu\) is the fluid four-velocity,
\[
  u^0=(1-\mathbf v^2)^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \mathbf u=\mathbf v u^0.
\]
The contravariant tensor that reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:5.4.1} in the absence
of gravitation is
\begin{equation}
  T^{\mu\nu}
  =
  pg^{\mu\nu}
  +
  (\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu,
  \tag{5.4.2}\label{eq:5.4.2}
\end{equation}
where \(u^\mu\) is the local value of \(\dd x^\mu/\dd\tau\) for a comoving
fluid element. Note that \(p\) and \(\rho\) are always defined as the
pressure and energy density measured by an observer in a locally inertial
frame that happens to be moving with the fluid at the instant of measurement,
and are therefore scalars. The conditions of energy-momentum conservation
give the hydrodynamic equations
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    0
    =
    \nabla_\nu T^{\mu\nu}
    ={}&
    g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu p
    +
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,
    \partial_\nu
    \left[
      \sqrt{-g}\,(\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu
    \right]
    \\
    &+
    \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\lambda}
    (\rho+p)u^\nu u^\lambda.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{5.4.3}\label{eq:5.4.3}
\end{equation}
The last term represents the gravitational force on the system. Note also
that since \(\eta_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu=-1\) in the absence of gravitation,
we must in the presence of gravitation have
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu=-1.
  \tag{5.4.4}\label{eq:5.4.4}
\end{equation}

Consider as an example the case of a fluid in hydrostatic equilibrium. Since
it is not moving, Eq.~\eqref{eq:5.4.4} gives
\[
  u^0=(-g_{00})^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  u^i=0.
\]
Furthermore, all temporal derivatives of \(g_{\mu\nu}\), \(p\), or \(\rho\)
vanish. In particular,
\[
  \Gamma^\nu{}_{00}
  =
  -\frac12 g^{\nu\lambda}\partial_\lambda g_{00},
\]
and
\[
  \partial_\nu
  \left[
    (\rho+p)u^\mu u^\nu
  \right]
  =0.
\]
Multiplying Eq.~\eqref{eq:5.4.3} by \(g_{\mu\lambda}\) gives then
\begin{equation}
  -\partial_\lambda p
  =
  (\rho+p)
  \partial_\lambda
  \ln\sqrt{-g_{00}}.
  \tag{5.4.5}\label{eq:5.4.5}
\end{equation}
This is trivial for \(\lambda=0\), whereas for \(\lambda\) spacelike it is
nothing but the ordinary nonrelativistic equation of hydrostatic
equilibrium, except that \(\rho+p\) appears instead of the mass density and
\(\ln\sqrt{-g_{00}}\) appears instead of the gravitational potential. This
equation is soluble if \(p\) is given as a function of \(\rho\). We then
find that
\begin{equation}
  \int
  \frac{\dd p(\rho)}{p(\rho)+\rho}
  =
  -\ln\sqrt{-g_{00}}
  +
  \text{constant}.
  \tag{5.4.6}\label{eq:5.4.6}
\end{equation}
For instance, if \(p(\rho)\) is given by a power law
\begin{equation}
  p(\rho)\propto\rho^N,
  \tag{5.4.7}\label{eq:5.4.7}
\end{equation}
then Eq.~\eqref{eq:5.4.6} gives for \(N\ne1\)
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\rho+p}{\rho}
  \propto
  (-g_{00})^{(1-N)/(2N)},
  \tag{5.4.8}\label{eq:5.4.8}
\end{equation}
and for \(N=1\)
\begin{equation}
  \rho
  \propto
  (-g_{00})^{-(p+\rho)/(2p)}.
  \tag{5.4.9}\label{eq:5.4.9}
\end{equation}
This, incidentally, shows that gravitation can never produce hydrostatic
equilibrium in a finite highly relativistic fluid with \(p=\rho/3\), for
then Eq.~\eqref{eq:5.4.9} gives
\begin{equation}
  \rho\propto(-g_{00})^{-2}.
  \tag{5.4.10}\label{eq:5.4.10}
\end{equation}
Since \(\rho\) must vanish outside the fluid, \(g_{00}\) would have to become
singular at its surface.
