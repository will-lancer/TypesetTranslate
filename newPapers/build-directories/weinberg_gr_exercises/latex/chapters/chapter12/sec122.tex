\subsection{General Definition of
  \texorpdfstring{\(T^{\mu\nu}\)}{T mu nu}}\label{sec:12.2}

We are going to define the energy-momentum tensor for a material system
described by an action \(I_M\), as the functional derivative of \(I_M\) with
respect to \(g_{\mu\nu}\). That is, we imagine \(g_{\mu\nu}(x)\) to be subject
to an infinitesimal variation
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow g_{\mu\nu}+\delta g_{\mu\nu},
  \tag{12.2.1}\label{eq:12.2.1}
\end{equation}
where \(\delta g_{\mu\nu}\) is arbitrary except that it is required to vanish
as \(\lvert x^\lambda\rvert\longrightarrow\infty\). The action \(I_M\) will
not be stationary with respect to this variation because, for the moment, we
are regarding \(g_{\mu\nu}(x)\) not as a dynamical variable like
\(x_n^\mu\) or \(A_\mu\), but as an external field. Rather, \(\delta I_M\)
will be some linear functional of the infinitesimal \(\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x)\)
and therefore takes the form
\begin{equation}
  \delta I_M
  =
  \frac12\int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}\,
  T^{\mu\nu}(x)\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x).
  \tag{12.2.2}\label{eq:12.2.2}
\end{equation}
The coefficient \(T^{\mu\nu}(x)\) is \emph{defined} to be the
energy-momentum tensor of this system. Equivalently, in the inverse-metric
form used throughout this edition,
\[
  T_{\mu\nu}
  =
  -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta I_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\]

A general proof that \(T^{\mu\nu}\) is a conserved symmetric tensor will be
given in the next section. However, let us first check that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.2.2} gives the correct energy-momentum tensor for the
collisionless plasma described by the action \eqref{eq:12.1.6}. We are
varying \(g_{\mu\nu}\) with \(A_\mu\) held fixed, so
\[
  \delta F^{\mu\nu}
  =
  F_{\rho\sigma}\delta(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma})
  =
  F_{\rho\sigma}g^{\mu\rho}\delta g^{\nu\sigma}
  +F_{\rho\sigma}g^{\nu\sigma}\delta g^{\mu\rho}.
\]
To calculate \(\delta g^{\nu\sigma}\), we note that
\[
  0
  =
  \delta(g_{\lambda\kappa}g^{\kappa\sigma})
  =
  g^{\kappa\sigma}\delta g_{\lambda\kappa}
  +g_{\lambda\kappa}\delta g^{\kappa\sigma},
\]
so
\[
  \delta g^{\nu\sigma}
  =
  -g^{\nu\lambda}g^{\sigma\kappa}\delta g_{\lambda\kappa},
\]
and therefore
\[
  \delta F^{\mu\nu}
  =
  -F^{\mu\kappa}g^{\nu\lambda}\delta g_{\lambda\kappa}
  +F^{\nu\lambda}g^{\mu\kappa}\delta g_{\lambda\kappa}.
\]
Also, we showed in Section~\ref{sec:4.7} that
\[
  \delta g=g\,g^{\lambda\kappa}\delta g_{\lambda\kappa}.
\]
A straightforward calculation gives then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta I_M={}&
\frac12\sum_n m_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd p\,
\left[
  -g_{\mu\nu}(x_n(p))
   \frac{\dd x_n^\mu(p)}{\dd p}
   \frac{\dd x_n^\nu(p)}{\dd p}
\right]^{-1/2}
\\[-2pt]
&\qquad\times
\frac{\dd x_n^\lambda(p)}{\dd p}
\frac{\dd x_n^\kappa(p)}{\dd p}\,
\delta g_{\lambda\kappa}(x_n(p))
\\
&+\frac12\int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}
\left\{
  F_\mu{}^\lambda(x)F^{\mu\kappa}(x)
  -\frac14g^{\lambda\kappa}(x)
   F_{\mu\nu}(x)F^{\mu\nu}(x)
\right\}\delta g_{\lambda\kappa}(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is of the form \eqref{eq:12.2.2}, with
\[
\begin{aligned}
T^{\lambda\kappa}(x)={}&
\frac1{\sqrt{-g(x)}}\sum_n m_n
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\tau_n\,
\frac{\dd x_n^\lambda}{\dd\tau_n}
\frac{\dd x_n^\kappa}{\dd\tau_n}
\delta^4(x-x_n)
\\
&+F_\mu{}^\lambda(x)F^{\mu\kappa}(x)
-\frac14g^{\lambda\kappa}(x)
F_{\mu\nu}(x)F^{\mu\nu}(x),
\end{aligned}
\]
in agreement with the previously obtained energy-momentum tensor, given by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:5.3.5} and \eqref{eq:5.3.7}.

The definition \eqref{eq:12.2.2} is closely analogous to a similar definition
of the electric current \(J^\mu\). We can break up the total matter action
into a purely electromagnetic term \(I_E\) and another term \(I'_M\) that
describes the charged particles and their electromagnetic interactions:
\begin{equation}
  I_M=I_E+I'_M,
  \tag{12.2.3}\label{eq:12.2.3}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  I_E
  =
  -\frac14\int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}\,
  F_{\mu\nu}(x)F^{\mu\nu}(x).
  \tag{12.2.4}\label{eq:12.2.4}
\end{equation}
Consider the effect on \(I'_M\) of an infinitesimal variation in the vector
potential
\begin{equation}
  A_\mu\longrightarrow A_\mu+\delta A_\mu.
  \tag{12.2.5}\label{eq:12.2.5}
\end{equation}
Since \(I'_M\) is not the whole action, the change in \(I'_M\) due to this
variation in \(A_\mu\) does not vanish, but it is necessarily a linear
functional of \(\delta A_\mu\):
\begin{equation}
  \delta I'_M
  =
  \int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}\,
  J^\mu(x)\delta A_\mu(x),
  \tag{12.2.6}\label{eq:12.2.6}
\end{equation}
and the coefficient \(J^\mu(x)\) is \emph{defined} to be the electromagnetic
current of the system.

For instance, for the collisionless plasma described by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.1.6}, the term \(I'_M\) is given as the sum of the first and
third terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.1.6}, and we immediately find that
\[
  \delta I'_M
  =
  \sum_n e_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
  \dd x_n^\mu\,\delta A_\mu(x_n).
\]
This is of the form \eqref{eq:12.2.6}, with
\[
  J^\mu(x)
  =
  \frac1{\sqrt{-g(x)}}\sum_n e_n
  \int\delta^4(x-x_n)\,\dd x_n^\mu,
\]
in agreement with Eq.~\eqref{eq:5.2.13}. The proof that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.2.6} always yields a \emph{conserved} current \(J^\mu(x)\)
is given in the next section.
