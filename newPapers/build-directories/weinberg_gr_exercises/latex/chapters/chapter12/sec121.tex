\subsection{The Matter Action: An Example}\label{sec:12.1}

We shall begin by displaying one example of a physical system whose equations
of motion can be derived from a principle of stationary action. The system is
a collisionless plasma, consisting of particles \(n\) of mass \(m_n\) and
charge \(e_n\), together with the electromagnetic field \(F_{\mu\nu}(x)\)
they produce. Writing
\[
  u_n^\mu\equiv\frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd\tau_n},
  \qquad
  \frac{D V^\mu}{D\tau_n}\equiv u_n^\nu\nabla_\nu V^\mu,
\]
the equations of motion in an arbitrary external gravitational field
\(g_{\mu\nu}\) are
\begin{equation}
  \frac{D u_n^\mu}{D\tau_n}
  \equiv
  \frac{\dd^2x_n^\mu}{\dd\tau_n^2}
  +\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\lambda}(x_n)
   \frac{\dd x_n^\nu}{\dd\tau_n}
   \frac{\dd x_n^\lambda}{\dd\tau_n}
  =
  \frac{e_n}{m_n}F^\mu{}_\nu(x_n)
  \frac{\dd x_n^\nu}{\dd\tau_n},
  \tag{12.1.1}\label{eq:12.1.1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \dd\tau_n
  =
  \left(-g_{\mu\nu}\,\dd x_n^\mu\dd x_n^\nu\right)^{1/2},
  \tag{12.1.2}\label{eq:12.1.2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\mu\!\left[\sqrt{-g(x)}\,F^{\mu\nu}(x)\right]
  =
  -\sum_n e_n
   \int
   \delta^4(x-x_n)
   \frac{\dd x_n^\nu}{\dd\tau_n}\,\dd\tau_n,
  \tag{12.1.3}\label{eq:12.1.3}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \partial_{[\lambda}F_{\mu\nu]}=0.
  \tag{12.1.4}\label{eq:12.1.4}
\end{equation}
[See Eqs.~\eqref{eq:5.2.9}, \eqref{eq:5.1.11}, \eqref{eq:5.2.6},
\eqref{eq:5.2.13}, and \eqref{eq:5.2.7}.] In order to satisfy
Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.1.4}, we introduce a vector potential \(A_\mu(x)\):
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu.
  \tag{12.1.5}\label{eq:12.1.5}
\end{equation}
The independent dynamical variables may then be taken as \(A_\mu(x)\) and
\(x_n^\nu(p)\), where \(p\) is some quantity that simultaneously
parameterizes all the space-time trajectories of the various particles.

We tentatively take the action for this system as
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  I_M={}&
  -\sum_n m_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd p\,
   \left[
     -g_{\mu\nu}(x_n(p))
      \frac{\dd x_n^\mu(p)}{\dd p}
      \frac{\dd x_n^\nu(p)}{\dd p}
   \right]^{1/2}
  \\
  &-\frac14\int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}\,
    F_{\mu\nu}(x)F^{\mu\nu}(x)
  \\
  &+\sum_n e_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd p\,
    \frac{\dd x_n^\mu(p)}{\dd p}A_\mu(x_n(p)).
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{12.1.6}\label{eq:12.1.6}
\end{equation}
(The subscript \(M\) is to remind us that this is the action only for matter
and radiation, with \(g_{\mu\nu}(x)\) taken as a prescribed external
gravitational field.) It is understood here that \(F_{\mu\nu}\) is given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.1.5}, and the indices on \(F^{\mu\nu}\) are raised with the
contravariant metric tensor as usual.

The principle of stationary action says that the action \(I_M\) will not be
changed by an infinitesimal variation in the dynamical variables
\[
  x_n^\mu(p)\longrightarrow x_n^\mu(p)+\delta x_n^\mu(p),
  \qquad
  A_\mu(x)\longrightarrow A_\mu(x)+\delta A_\mu(x),
\]
where
\[
  \delta x_n^\mu(p)\longrightarrow0
  \quad\text{for}\quad |p|\longrightarrow\infty,
  \qquad
  \delta A_\mu(x)\longrightarrow0
  \quad\text{for}\quad |x^\lambda|\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
if and only if \(x_n^\mu(p)\) and \(A_\mu(x)\) obey the dynamical equations
\eqref{eq:12.1.1}--\eqref{eq:12.1.3}. To check that this is correct, let us
compute the change in \(I_M\) produced by this variation, without yet assuming
that Eqs.~\eqref{eq:12.1.1}--\eqref{eq:12.1.3} are satisfied. We find that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta I_M={}&
\frac12\sum_n m_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd p\,
\left[
  -g_{\mu\nu}(x_n)
   \frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd p}
   \frac{\dd x_n^\nu}{\dd p}
\right]^{-1/2}
\\[-2pt]
&\quad\times
\left\{
  2g_{\mu\nu}(x_n)
   \frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd p}
   \frac{\dd\,\delta x_n^\nu}{\dd p}
  +\left.\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}\right|_{x_n}
   \frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd p}
   \frac{\dd x_n^\nu}{\dd p}\,
   \delta x_n^\lambda
\right\}
\\
&-\int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}\,F^{\mu\nu}(x)
  \partial_\mu\delta A_\nu(x)
\\
&+\sum_n e_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd p\,
\left\{
  \frac{\dd\,\delta x_n^\mu}{\dd p}A_\mu(x_n)
  +\frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd p}
   \partial_\lambda A_\mu(x_n)\,\delta x_n^\lambda
  +\frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd p}\delta A_\mu(x_n)
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
It is convenient at this point to change variables of integration from \(p\)
to the \(\tau_n\) defined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.1.2}. This gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta I_M={}&
\frac12\sum_n m_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\tau_n
\left\{
  2g_{\mu\lambda}(x_n)
   \frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd\tau_n}
   \frac{\dd\,\delta x_n^\lambda}{\dd\tau_n}
  +\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(x_n)
   \frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd\tau_n}
   \frac{\dd x_n^\nu}{\dd\tau_n}\,
   \delta x_n^\lambda
\right\}
\\
&-\int\dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}\,F^{\mu\nu}(x)
  \partial_\mu\delta A_\nu(x)
\\
&+\sum_n e_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\tau_n
\left\{
  \frac{\dd\,\delta x_n^\mu}{\dd\tau_n}A_\mu(x_n)
  +\frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd\tau_n}
   \partial_\lambda A_\mu(x_n)\,\delta x_n^\lambda
  +\frac{\dd x_n^\mu}{\dd\tau_n}\delta A_\mu(x_n)
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The condition that \(\delta x_n^\mu(\tau_n)\) and \(\delta A_\mu(x)\)
vanish on the boundaries of the region of integration allows us to integrate
by parts, and we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta I_M={}&
\sum_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\tau_n\,
g_{\mu\lambda}(x_n)
\left\{
  -m_n\frac{D u_n^\mu}{D\tau_n}
  +e_n F^\mu{}_\rho(x_n)u_n^\rho
\right\}\delta x_n^\lambda
\\
&+\int\dd^4x
\left\{
  \partial_\mu\!\left[\sqrt{-g(x)}F^{\mu\nu}(x)\right]
  +\sum_n e_n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\tau_n\,
    \delta^4(x-x_n)u_n^\nu
\right\}\delta A_\nu(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Evidently \(\delta I_M\) vanishes for general variations
\(\delta x_n^\lambda\) and \(\delta A_\nu\) if and only if
\(x_n^\lambda\) and \(A_\nu\) obey the dynamical equations
\eqref{eq:12.1.1} and \eqref{eq:12.1.3}, and we therefore conclude that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:12.1.6} does qualify as a suitable action for this system.
