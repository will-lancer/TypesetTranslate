\subsection{Round Trips by Parallel Transport}\label{sec:6.3}

Both for its own sake and as preparation for the next section, we now take up
the question whether a vector \(S_\mu\), when carried along a closed curve
\(C\) by the equation of parallel transport (see Sections~\ref{sec:4.9} and
\ref{sec:5.1}),
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd S_\mu}{\dd\tau}
  =
  \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
  \frac{\dd x^\nu}{\dd\tau}S_\lambda,
  \tag{6.3.1}\label{eq:6.3.1}
\end{equation}
will come back to its original value after one complete circuit of the curve.

We can answer this question by applying the method used in the familiar proof
of Stokes's theorem. Consider the curve \(C\) as the edge of some
two-dimensional surface \(A\), and divide \(A\) into small cells bounded by
little closed curves \(C_N\). The change in \(S_\mu\) when
parallel-transported around \(C\) can be written as the sum of the changes in
\(S_\mu\) when transported around each of these little curves,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta S_\mu
  =
  \sum_N\Delta_N S_\mu,
  \tag{6.3.2}\label{eq:6.3.2}
\end{equation}
because the change in \(S_\mu\) around any one interior cell is canceled by
the changes around adjacent cells, leaving only the contribution from the
outer cell edges that make up \(C\). Therefore we must ask only whether
\(S_\mu\) changes when parallel-transported around a small closed curve. If
the curve is small enough, we can expand
\(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(x)\) around some point
\(X=x(\tau_0)\) on the curve:
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(x)
  =
  \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
  +(x^\rho-X^\rho)
   \partial_\rho\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
  +\cdots.
  \tag{6.3.3}\label{eq:6.3.3}
\end{equation}
Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.1} gives to first order in \(x^\mu-X^\mu\)
\begin{equation}
  S_\mu(\tau)
  =
  S_\mu(\tau_0)
  +\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
   \bigl[x^\nu(\tau)-X^\nu\bigr]S_\lambda(\tau_0)
  +\cdots,
  \tag{6.3.4}\label{eq:6.3.4}
\end{equation}
and by using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:6.3.3} and \eqref{eq:6.3.4} in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.1} we obtain an equation valid to second order:
\begin{align*}
  S_\mu(\tau)
  \simeq{}&
  S_\mu(\tau_0)
  +\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
  \left[
    \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
    +(x^\rho(\tau')-X^\rho)
     \partial_\rho\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
    +\cdots
  \right]
  \\
  &\qquad\times
  \left[
    S_\lambda(\tau_0)
    +S_\sigma(\tau_0)\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\lambda\rho}(X)
     (x^\rho(\tau')-X^\rho)
    +\cdots
  \right]
  \frac{\dd x^\nu(\tau')}{\dd\tau'}\,\dd\tau'.
\end{align*}
Discarding terms of third or higher order in \(x-X\), this is
\begin{align*}
  S_\mu(\tau)
  \simeq{}&
  S_\mu(\tau_0)
  +\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(X)S_\lambda(\tau_0)
   \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
   \frac{\dd x^\nu}{\dd\tau'}\,\dd\tau'
  \\
  &+
  \left[
    \partial_\rho\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
    +\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
     \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\lambda\rho}(X)
  \right]S_\sigma(\tau_0)
  \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
  (x^\rho-X^\rho)\frac{\dd x^\nu}{\dd\tau'}\,\dd\tau'.
\end{align*}
If \(x^\mu(\tau)\) returns to its original value \(X^\mu\) at some
\(\tau=\tau_1\), then obviously
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_1}
  \frac{\dd x^\nu}{\dd\tau}\,\dd\tau=0,
\]
so the change in \(S_\mu\) when parallel-transported around the small closed
curve \(x^\mu(\tau)\) is of second order:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  \Delta S_\mu
  &\equiv S_\mu(\tau_1)-S_\mu(\tau_0)
  \\
  &=
  \left[
    \partial_\rho\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
    +\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(X)
     \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\lambda\rho}(X)
  \right]S_\sigma(\tau_0)
  \oint_C x^\rho\,\dd x^\nu.
  \end{split}
  \tag{6.3.5}\label{eq:6.3.5}
\end{equation}
Here
\[
  \oint_C x^\rho\,\dd x^\nu
  =
  \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_1}
  x^\rho\frac{\dd x^\nu}{\dd\tau}\,\dd\tau.
\]
This integral does not generally vanish; for instance, if our curve is a
small parallelogram with edges \(\delta a^\mu,\delta b^\mu\), it equals
\[
  \oint_C x^\rho\,\dd x^\nu
  =
  \delta a^\rho\delta b^\nu-\delta a^\nu\delta b^\rho.
\]
However, it is always antisymmetric in \(\rho\) and \(\nu\), as can be seen by
partial integration:
\begin{equation}
  \oint_C x^\rho\,\dd x^\nu
  =
  \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_1}
  \frac{\dd}{\dd\tau}(x^\rho x^\nu)\,\dd\tau
  -
  \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_1}
  x^\nu\frac{\dd x^\rho}{\dd\tau}\,\dd\tau
  =
  -\oint_C x^\nu\,\dd x^\rho.
  \tag{6.3.6}\label{eq:6.3.6}
\end{equation}
Thus the coefficient of this integral in Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.5} can be
replaced by its antisymmetric part, which is just half the curvature tensor
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.1.5}, so
\begin{equation}
  \Delta S_\mu
  =
  \frac12
  R^\sigma{}_{\mu\rho\nu}(X)S_\sigma(\tau_0)
  \oint_C x^\rho\,\dd x^\nu.
  \tag{6.3.7}\label{eq:6.3.7}
\end{equation}
\emph{Our conclusion is that an arbitrary vector \(S_\mu\) will not change
when parallel-transported around an arbitrary small closed curve at \(X\), if
and only if \(R^\sigma{}_{\mu\rho\nu}\) vanishes at \(X\).} We have already
remarked that the change in \(S_\mu\) when parallel-transported about a finite
closed curve \(C\) may be computed by breaking up the area \(A\) bounded by
\(C\) into small cells and then adding up the changes in \(S_\mu\) when
parallel-transported around the edges of these cells; hence, if
\(R^\sigma{}_{\mu\rho\nu}\) vanishes throughout \(A\), then an arbitrary
vector \(S_\mu\) will not change when parallel-transported around \(C\).

Now suppose that \(R^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu\rho}\) does vanish. Consider a closed
curve consisting of two segments \(A\) and \(B\) joining points \(x^\mu\) and
\(X^\mu\). The change in a vector \(S_\mu\) when parallel-transported from
\(x\) to \(X\) along \(A\) must be canceled by the change in \(S_\mu\) when
parallel-transported along \(B\) from \(X\) to \(x\), that is,
\[
  \Delta^A_{x\to X}S_\mu+\Delta^B_{X\to x}S_\mu=0.
\]
But the change in \(S_\mu\) when parallel-transported from \(x\) to \(X\)
along \(B\) is minus the change when parallel-transported from \(X\) to \(x\)
along \(B\):
\[
  \Delta^B_{x\to X}S_\mu=-\Delta^B_{X\to x}S_\mu,
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^A_{X\to x}S_\mu
  =
  \Delta^B_{X\to x}S_\mu.
  \tag{6.3.8}\label{eq:6.3.8}
\end{equation}
That is, we get the same value of \(S_\mu\) by parallel transportation from
\(X\) to \(x\), irrespective of which curve we follow. (For instance, if two
gyroscopes are placed in different intersecting orbits about the earth, and
have the same orientation when they pass close to each other at \(X^\mu\),
then any difference in their orientations when they next pass close to each
other at \(x^\mu\) will be a measure of some average of the curvature produced
by the earth's gravitational field.)

It follows that, given \(S_\mu\) at \(X\), we may determine a \emph{field}
\(S_\mu(x)\), defined throughout the spacetime region where
\(R^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu\rho}\) vanishes, by parallel transport from \(X\) to
\(x\); Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.8} ensures that the \(S_\mu(x)\) so defined will
depend only on \(x\), and not on the path from \(X\) to \(x\). For this field
the derivative along any curve \(x(\tau)\) is
\[
  \frac{\dd S_\mu}{\dd\tau}
  =
  \partial_\nu S_\mu\frac{\dd x^\nu}{\dd\tau},
\]
and since the direction of \(\dd x^\nu(\tau)/\dd\tau\) is arbitrary,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.1} becomes
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\nu S_\mu
  =
  \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}S_\lambda,
  \tag{6.3.9}\label{eq:6.3.9}
\end{equation}
or, in other words,
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\nu S_\mu=0.
  \tag{6.3.10}\label{eq:6.3.10}
\end{equation}
Hence, if the curvature tensor vanishes, we may always construct solutions of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.9}, with any given value of \(S_\mu(X)\), by parallel
transport of \(S_\mu\) from \(X\) to \(x\). Conversely, if there exists any
covariant vector field with vanishing covariant derivatives, then
Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.1} will certainly be satisfied; and since parallel
transport cannot change a field when carried about any closed curve, we
conclude from Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.7} that
\begin{equation}
  R^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu\rho}S_\sigma=0
  \tag{6.3.11}\label{eq:6.3.11}
\end{equation}
throughout the region where \(S_\mu\) satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.10}.

(These conclusions could have been obtained by using well-known results from
the theory of partial differential
equations\hyperref[ch6-ref-1]{\textsuperscript{1}} instead of the method of
parallel displacement. In this approach Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.11} appears as the
necessary and sufficient condition that Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.3.9} can be solved by
a power-series expansion in \(x^\mu-X^\mu\).)
