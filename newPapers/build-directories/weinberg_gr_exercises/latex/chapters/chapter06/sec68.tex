\subsection{The Bianchi Identities}\label{sec:6.8}

The curvature tensor obeys important differential identities, in addition to
the algebraic identities discussed in Section~\ref{sec:6.6}. These can be
most easily derived at a given point \(x\), by adopting a locally inertial
coordinate system in which \(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\) (but not its
derivatives) vanishes at \(x\). Then at \(x\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.6.2} gives
\begin{align*}
  \nabla_\eta R_{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}
  =\frac12\partial_\eta\bigl(
    &\partial_\mu\partial_\nu g_{\lambda\kappa}
    +\partial_\lambda\partial_\kappa g_{\mu\nu}
    -\partial_\mu\partial_\kappa g_{\lambda\nu}
    -\partial_\lambda\partial_\nu g_{\mu\kappa}
  \bigr),
\end{align*}
all other terms being at least of first order in \(\Gamma\). By permuting
\(\nu,\kappa,\eta\) cyclically we obtain the \emph{Bianchi identities}
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\eta R_{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}
  +\nabla_\nu R_{\lambda\mu\kappa\eta}
  +\nabla_\kappa R_{\lambda\mu\eta\nu}
  =0.
  \tag{6.8.1}\label{eq:6.8.1}
\end{equation}
These equations are manifestly generally covariant, so since they hold in
locally inertial systems they hold in general. (They can also, of course, be
checked by direct calculation.)

We shall be particularly concerned with the contracted form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:6.8.1}. Recalling that the covariant derivatives of
\(g^{\lambda\nu}\) vanish, we find on contraction of \(\lambda\) with
\(\nu\) that
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\eta R_{\mu\kappa}
  -\nabla_\kappa R_{\mu\eta}
  +\nabla_\nu R^\nu{}_{\mu\kappa\eta}
  =0.
  \tag{6.8.2}\label{eq:6.8.2}
\end{equation}
Contracting again gives
\[
  \nabla_\eta R
  -\nabla_\mu R^\mu{}_\eta
  -\nabla_\nu R^\nu{}_\eta
  =0,
\]
or
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu
  \left(
    R^\mu{}_\eta-\frac12\delta^\mu{}_\eta R
  \right)
  =0.
  \tag{6.8.3}\label{eq:6.8.3}
\end{equation}
An equivalent but more familiar form is
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu
  \left(
    R^{\mu\nu}-\frac12g^{\mu\nu}R
  \right)
  =0.
  \tag{6.8.4}\label{eq:6.8.4}
\end{equation}
