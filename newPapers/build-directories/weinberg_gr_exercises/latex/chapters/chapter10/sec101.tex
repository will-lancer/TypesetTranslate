\subsection{The Weak-Field Approximation}\label{sec:10.1}

We suppose the metric to be close to the Minkowski metric
\(\eta_{\mu\nu}\):
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},
  \tag{10.1.1}\label{eq:10.1.1}
\end{equation}
where \(\lvert h_{\mu\nu}\rvert\ll1\). To first order in \(h\), the Ricci
tensor in the curvature convention of this edition is
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mu\nu}
  \simeq
  \partial_\lambda\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
  -\partial_\nu\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\lambda\mu}
  +\order(h^2),
  \tag{10.1.2}\label{eq:10.1.2}
\end{equation}
and the affine connection is
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac12\eta^{\lambda\rho}
  \left(
    \partial_\mu h_{\rho\nu}
    +\partial_\nu h_{\rho\mu}
    -\partial_\rho h_{\mu\nu}
  \right)
  +\order(h^2).
  \tag{10.1.3}\label{eq:10.1.3}
\end{equation}

As long as we restrict ourselves to first order in \(h\), we must raise and
lower all indices using \(\eta^{\mu\nu}\), not \(g^{\mu\nu}\); that is,
\[
  \eta^{\lambda\rho}h_{\rho\nu}\equiv h^\lambda{}_\nu,
  \qquad
  \eta^{\lambda\rho}\partial_\rho\equiv\partial^\lambda,
  \qquad\text{etc.}
\]
With this understanding, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:10.1.2} and \eqref{eq:10.1.3}
yield the first-order Ricci tensor
\[
  R_{\mu\nu}\simeq R^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}
  \equiv
  -\frac12
  \left(
    \Box h_{\mu\nu}
    -\partial_\mu\partial^\lambda h_{\lambda\nu}
    -\partial_\nu\partial^\lambda h_{\lambda\mu}
    +\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h^\lambda{}_\lambda
  \right).
\]
The Einstein field equations therefore read
\begin{equation}
  \Box h_{\mu\nu}
  -\partial_\mu\partial^\lambda h_{\lambda\nu}
  -\partial_\nu\partial^\lambda h_{\lambda\mu}
  +\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h^\lambda{}_\lambda
  =-16\pi G S_{\mu\nu},
  \tag{10.1.4}\label{eq:10.1.4}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mu\nu}
  \equiv
  T_{\mu\nu}-\frac12\eta_{\mu\nu}T^\lambda{}_\lambda.
  \tag{10.1.5}\label{eq:10.1.5}
\end{equation}
Here \(T_{\mu\nu}\) is taken to lowest order in \(h_{\mu\nu}\), so it is
independent of \(h_{\mu\nu}\), and satisfies the ordinary conservation
conditions
\begin{equation}
  \partial^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0.
  \tag{10.1.6}\label{eq:10.1.6}
\end{equation}
(If gravitational forces play an important role in the structure of the
radiating system, then \(\tau^{\mu\nu}\) should be used in place of
\(T^{\mu\nu}\); see Section~\ref{sec:7.6}.) Note that it is this form of the
conservation law that is needed for the consistency of \eqref{eq:10.1.4},
because \eqref{eq:10.1.6} implies
\[
  \partial^\mu S_{\mu\nu}
  =\frac12\partial_\nu S^\lambda{}_\lambda,
\]
whereas the linearized Ricci tensor satisfies the contracted Bianchi identity
\[
  \partial_\mu R^{(1)\mu}{}_\nu
  =
  \frac12\partial_\nu R^{(1)\lambda}{}_\lambda.
\]

As already discussed in Section~\ref{sec:7.4}, we cannot expect a field
equation such as \eqref{eq:10.1.4} to yield unique solutions, because given
any solution, we can always generate other solutions by performing coordinate
transformations. The most general coordinate transformation that leaves the
field weak is of the form
\begin{equation}
  x^\mu\longrightarrow x'^\mu=x^\mu+\xi^\mu(x),
  \tag{10.1.7}\label{eq:10.1.7}
\end{equation}
where \(\partial_\nu\xi^\mu\) is at most of the same order of magnitude as
\(h_{\mu\nu}\). The metric in the new coordinate system is given by
\[
  g'^{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac{\partial x'^\mu}{\partial x^\lambda}
  \frac{\partial x'^\nu}{\partial x^\rho}g^{\lambda\rho},
\]
or, since \(g^{\mu\nu}\simeq\eta^{\mu\nu}-h^{\mu\nu}\),
\[
  h'^{\mu\nu}
  =
  h^{\mu\nu}-\partial^\mu\xi^\nu-\partial^\nu\xi^\mu.
\]
Thus, if \(h_{\mu\nu}\) is a solution of \eqref{eq:10.1.4}, then so will be
\begin{equation}
  h'_{\mu\nu}
  =
  h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_\mu\xi_\nu-\partial_\nu\xi_\mu,
  \tag{10.1.8}\label{eq:10.1.8}
\end{equation}
where the \(\xi_\mu\) are four small but otherwise arbitrary functions of
\(x^\mu\). That this is the case can be verified by direct inspection of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:10.1.4}; this property is called the \emph{gauge invariance} of
the field equation.

The gauge invariance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:10.1.4} is a nuisance when it comes to
actually solving the field equations. However, the difficulty can be removed
by choosing some particular gauge, that is, coordinate system. The most
convenient choice is to work in a \emph{harmonic coordinate system}, for which
\[
  g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}=0.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:10.1.3}, this gives to first order
\begin{equation}
  \partial^\mu h_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac12\partial_\nu h^\mu{}_\mu,
  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
  \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0,
  \tag{10.1.9}\label{eq:10.1.9}
\end{equation}
where
\(\bar h_{\mu\nu}\equiv h_{\mu\nu}
-\frac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h^\lambda{}_\lambda\).
That this choice is always possible follows from the general argument of
Section~\ref{sec:7.4}. It can also be seen from \eqref{eq:10.1.8} that if
\(h_{\mu\nu}\) does not satisfy \eqref{eq:10.1.9}, then we can find an
\(h'_{\mu\nu}\) that does, by performing the coordinate transformation
\eqref{eq:10.1.7} with
\[
  \Box\xi_\nu
  =
  \partial^\mu h_{\mu\nu}
  -\frac12\partial_\nu h^\mu{}_\mu.
\]
It will be assumed from now on that \(h_{\mu\nu}\) does satisfy
Eq.~\eqref{eq:10.1.9}. Using \eqref{eq:10.1.9} in \eqref{eq:10.1.4}, the
field equation now reads
\begin{equation}
  \Box h_{\mu\nu}=-16\pi G S_{\mu\nu}.
  \tag{10.1.10}\label{eq:10.1.10}
\end{equation}
One solution is the \emph{retarded potential}
\begin{equation}
  h_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{x},t)
  =
  4G\int \dd^3x'\,
  \frac{S_{\mu\nu}\bigl(\mathbf{x}',t-\lvert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'\rvert\bigr)}
       {\lvert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'\rvert}.
  \tag{10.1.11}\label{eq:10.1.11}
\end{equation}
We have already remarked that the conservation law
\eqref{eq:10.1.6} for \(T^{\mu\nu}\) is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
  \partial^\mu S_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac12\partial_\nu S^\mu{}_\mu,
  \tag{10.1.12}\label{eq:10.1.12}
\end{equation}
and in consequence the solution \eqref{eq:10.1.11} for a source
\(S_{\mu\nu}\) confined to a finite volume automatically satisfies the
harmonic coordinate conditions \eqref{eq:10.1.9}. (The proof is identical
to that used in electrodynamics in the calculation of the vector potential in
Lorenz gauge.) To \eqref{eq:10.1.11} we can add any solution of the
homogeneous equations
\begin{equation}
  \Box h_{\mu\nu}=0,
  \tag{10.1.13}\label{eq:10.1.13}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \partial^\mu h_{\mu\nu}
  =
  \frac12\partial_\nu h^\mu{}_\mu.
  \tag{10.1.14}\label{eq:10.1.14}
\end{equation}
We interpret \eqref{eq:10.1.11} as the gravitational radiation produced by
the source \(S_{\mu\nu}\), whereas any additional term satisfying
\eqref{eq:10.1.13} and \eqref{eq:10.1.14} represents the gravitational
radiation coming in from infinity. The occurrence in \eqref{eq:10.1.11} of
the time argument \(t-\lvert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'\rvert\) shows that
gravitational effects propagate with unit velocity, that is, with the speed
of light.
