``In Mathematicks he was greater.\\
Than Tycho Brahe or Erra Pater:\\
For he by Geometrick scale\\
Could take the size of Pots of Ale;\\
Resolve by Signes and Tangents straight,\\
If Bread or Butter wanted weight;\\
And wisely tell what hour o’th day\\
The Clock does strike, by Algebra.''

\medskip
\upshape
Samuel Butler, \textit{Sir Hudibras,\\
His Passing Worth}

We have already noticed that the Principle of Equivalence of Gravitation and
Inertia establishes a deep analogy between non-Euclidean geometry and the
theory of gravitation. This chapter is devoted to an outline of the language
common to both, that of tensor analysis.
