\begin{solution}
To first order,
\[
R_{0\ii0j}=\partial_i\partial_j\Phi.
\]
For slowly moving neighboring particles,
\[
\ddot\xi^i=-R^i{}_{0j0}\xi^j
=-\partial^i\partial_j\Phi\,\xi^j,
\]
which is the difference of their Newtonian accelerations.  The trace is
\(R_{00}=\nabla^2\Phi\); it vanishes in vacuum, so vacuum tides stretch in some
directions while compressing in others.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
The normal-coordinate metric expansion and
\(\delta\ln|g|=g^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}\) give
\[
\sqrt{|g|}=1-\frac16R_{\alpha\beta}x^\alpha x^\beta+\cdots.
\]
Rotational integration over a Euclidean ball uses
\(\int x^\alpha x^\beta\dd^nx
=\delta^{\alpha\beta}V_n r^2/(n+2)\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Vol}(B_r)
=V_n r^n\left[1-\frac{R(p)}{6(n+2)}r^2+\order(r^3)\right].
\]
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
For a hypersurface in flat space,
\[
{}^{(n)}R_{ijkl}=K_{ik}K_{jl}-K_{il}K_{jk}.
\]
On a round sphere \(K_{ij}=h_{ij}/a\), hence
\[
R_{ijkl}=\frac1{a^2}(h_{ik}h_{jl}-h_{il}h_{jk}),\qquad
R=\frac{2}{a^2}
\]
in two dimensions.  Thus its Gaussian curvature is \(1/a^2\).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Project one index of the spacetime Riemann tensor along \(n^\mu\) and the
other three into the hypersurface.  Differentiating the Weingarten equation
and antisymmetrizing gives
\[
h^{\alpha i}h^\beta{}_j h^\gamma{}_k n^\delta
R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
=D_jK^i{}_k-D_kK^i{}_j.
\]
Contracting \(i\) with \(k\) yields the stated equation.  With Einstein's
equation, its right side is \(8\pi G\) times the matter momentum density: the
ADM momentum constraint.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
For unit tangent \(u\) on the sphere, a transverse Jacobi scalar obeys
\[
\frac{\dd^2J}{\dd s^2}+\frac1{a^2}J=0.
\]
The field vanishing at the starting point is
\(J=C\sin(s/a)\); it next vanishes at \(s=\pi a\), the antipode.  There are
multiple great circles joining antipodes, and beyond the conjugate point a
nearby variation shortens the path, so the segment is no longer minimizing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Using the conformal connection difference gives
\[
\widetilde R=e^{-2\omega}\left[
R-2(n-1)\nabla^2\omega
-(n-1)(n-2)(\nabla\omega)^2\right].
\]
For \(n=2\), \(R=2K\), so
\[
\widetilde K=e^{-2\omega}(K-\nabla^2\omega),
\]
the standard conformal transformation of Gaussian curvature.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Schwarzschild is Ricci flat:
\[
R=0,\qquad R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}=0,
\qquad
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
=\frac{48M^2}{r^6}.
\]
The Kretschmann scalar is finite at \(r=2M\), confirming that the horizon
singularity is coordinate dependent, but diverges at \(r=0\), an invariant
curvature singularity.  Finite scalar invariants alone do not prove that a
spacetime is geodesically complete or nonsingular.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Define
\[
E_{\mu\nu}=C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta,\qquad
B_{\mu\nu}=\frac12\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\rho\sigma}
C^{\rho\sigma}{}_{\nu\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta.
\]
Weyl symmetries imply \(E_{\mu\nu}u^\nu=B_{\mu\nu}u^\nu=0\), symmetry, and
zero trace.  Each spatial symmetric trace-free tensor has five components.
The inverse \(3+1\) decomposition expresses all temporal components through
\(E\), mixed components through \(B\), and spatial components through \(E\);
together they supply all ten Weyl components.
\end{solution}
