\begin{exercise}[The transverse--traceless projector]{Tong, Cambridge Part III Sheet 4}
For a wave propagating along unit vector \(n^i\), construct the projector that
maps any symmetric spatial tensor to its transverse--traceless part.  Verify
idempotence, transversality, and zero trace.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Polarizations of a circular binary]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 13}
Use the quadrupole formula to derive \(h_+\) and \(h_\times\) from a circular
binary observed at inclination \(\iota\).  Explain the face-on circular and
edge-on linear polarization limits.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Angular pattern of quadrupole radiation]{Cambridge Part III, 2007 exam}
For an axisymmetric source with only \(Q_{zz}(t)\) independent, calculate the
far-zone waveform and energy flux as functions of polar angle.  Locate the
nodes and maxima.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Orbital decay from energy balance]{Cambridge Part III, 2012 exam}
Combine the Newtonian binding energy and quadrupole luminosity of a circular
binary to derive \(\dot r\), \(\dot P\), and the coalescence time from initial
separation \(r_0\).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Frequency evolution and time to merger]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 13}
Express the circular-binary chirp in terms of gravitational-wave frequency
\(f\) and chirp mass \(\mathcal M\).  Derive \(\dot f\), the time remaining to
coalescence, and the leading number of cycles between two frequencies.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Angular-momentum flux]{McGreevy, Problem Set 9}
Derive the leading quadrupole formula for gravitational-wave angular-momentum
loss.  Apply it to a circular binary and verify
\(\dot E=\Omega\,\dot J\).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Energy density of a stochastic background]{Cambridge Part III, Gravitational Waves examples}
For an isotropic stochastic background, relate the characteristic strain
\(h_c(f)\), one-sided strain spectrum \(S_h(f)\), and fractional energy
density \(\Omega_{\rm GW}(f)\).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Null memory from escaping radiation]{Cambridge Part III, 2024 exam}
Consider an anisotropic burst that carries energy to null infinity.  Show
schematically how its integrated energy flux produces a permanent
transverse--traceless metric change.  Use geodesic deviation to relate this
change to detector displacement memory.
\end{exercise}
