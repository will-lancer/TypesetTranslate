\begin{solution}
Differentiating the flat Killing equation and cyclically permuting indices
gives \(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\xi_\rho=0\).  Therefore
\[
\xi^\mu=a^\mu+\omega^\mu{}_\nu x^\nu,\qquad
\omega_{\mu\nu}=-\omega_{\nu\mu}.
\]
There are \(d\) translations and \(d(d-1)/2\) Lorentz generators, totaling
\(d(d+1)/2\).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Let \(\xi_{(i)}^\mu\) be the three rotational Killing fields.  Then
\[
K_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{i=1}^3\xi^{(i)}_\mu\xi^{(i)}_\nu
\]
obeys \(\nabla_{(\rho}K_{\mu\nu)}=0\).  Along a geodesic,
\[
K_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu
=\sum_i(\xi^{(i)}\cdot u)^2=L_x^2+L_y^2+L_z^2,
\]
the conserved total angular momentum squared.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Frobenius' theorem says that hypersurfaces orthogonal to \(\xi^\mu\) exist
exactly when
\(\xi\wedge\dd\xi=0\), or
\(\xi_{[\mu}\nabla_\nu\xi_{\rho]}=0\).  Choose the time coordinate along
Killing orbits and spatial coordinates on an orthogonal hypersurface,
propagated by the Killing flow.  Orthogonality then gives \(g_{0\ii}=0\), while
stationarity gives \(\partial_0g_{\mu\nu}=0\).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Static coordinates give
\[
\dd s^2=-(1-r^2/L^2)\dd t^2
+\frac{\dd r^2}{1-r^2/L^2}+r^2\dd\Omega^2.
\]
The Killing norm vanishes at \(r=L\).  For
\(f(r)=1-r^2/L^2\),
\[
\kappa=\frac12|f'(L)|=\frac1L.
\]
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Global anti-de Sitter may be written
\[
\dd s^2=L^2\sec^2\rho
(-\dd t^2+\dd\rho^2+\sin^2\rho\,\dd\Omega^2),
\quad0\leq\rho<\frac\pi2.
\]
Radial null rays satisfy \(\dd t=\pm\dd\rho\), so the center-to-boundary time
is \(\pi/2\).  The static proper distance is
\(L\int_0^{\pi/2}\sec\rho\,\dd\rho=\infty\).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
The generic metric has translations
\(\partial_x,\partial_y,\partial_z\).  If two scale factors coincide, a
rotation in their plane is added.  If all three coincide, all three rotations
are Killing and the spatial isometry group is the six-dimensional Euclidean
group.  Extra time symmetries require special scale-factor dependence.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Translations commute with the identification and descend.  Lorentz
transformations mixing the compact direction with time or noncompact
directions generally do not preserve the identification lattice, so they are
not global symmetries; rotations of unaffected noncompact directions may
survive.  Every sufficiently small patch lifts isometrically to Minkowski
space, but closed curves winding around the cylinder expose the global
topology.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
The differential of geodesic reflection at its fixed point is \(-1\).
Curvature is invariant under the isometry, but its covariant derivative has
five covariant slots and therefore changes sign; it must vanish at the fixed
point, and hence everywhere.  Maximally symmetric curvature
\[
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
=K(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}-g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho})
\]
is covariantly constant.  A product of two constant-curvature spaces with
different curvatures is locally symmetric but not maximally symmetric.
\end{solution}
