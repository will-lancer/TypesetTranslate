\begin{solution}
The mass equation gives
\[
m(r)=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho_c r^3+\order(r^5).
\]
Substitution in TOV yields
\[
p(r)=p_c-\frac{2\pi G}{3}
(\rho_c+p_c)(\rho_c+3p_c)r^2+\order(r^4).
\]
These regular series avoid the apparent \(0/0\) form at the origin and may be
evaluated at a small starting radius for numerical shooting.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Lane--Emden variables give
\[
R\propto K^{1/2}\rho_c^{(1-n)/(2n)},\qquad
M\propto K^{3/2}\rho_c^{(3-n)/(2n)}.
\]
Eliminating \(\rho_c\),
\[
R\propto K^{n/(3-n)}M^{(1-n)/(3-n)}.
\]
For \(n=1\), radius is independent of mass.  For \(n=3\), mass is independent
of central density, signaling a limiting mass.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
The electron number and competing energies scale as
\[
N_e=\frac{M}{\mu_em_p},\qquad
E_{\rm deg}\sim\frac{\hbar N_e^{4/3}}R,\qquad
E_g\sim-\frac{GM^2}R.
\]
Equating their magnitudes cancels \(R\) and gives
\[
M_{\rm Ch}\sim
\frac{(\hbar c/G)^{3/2}}{(\mu_em_p)^2},
\]
up to the Lane--Emden numerical coefficient.  Increasing density therefore
cannot support an arbitrarily larger mass.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
The surface redshift is
\[
1+z_s=(1-2GM/R)^{-1/2}.
\]
Buchdahl's inequality gives \(1-2GM/R\geq1/9\), so
\[
1+z_s\leq3,\qquad z_s\leq2.
\]
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
Radial modes extremize a quadratic functional whose lowest eigenvalue
\(\omega_0^2\) changes continuously along an equilibrium sequence.  At a mass
turning point there is a zero-frequency displacement tangent to the sequence;
crossing it changes the number of unstable modes by one.  More precisely, the
turning-point theorem compares extrema at fixed conserved baryon number (and
fixed entropy/composition).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
With opacity \(\kappa\), balance \(\kappa F_{\rm loc}=a\), where
\[
a=\frac{GM}{r^2\sqrt{1-2GM/r}}.
\]
Thus
\[
L_{\rm Edd}^{\rm loc}
=4\pi r^2F_{\rm loc}
=\frac{4\pi GM}{\kappa\sqrt{1-2GM/r}}.
\]
Energy and arrival rate each redshift, so
\[
L_{\rm Edd}^{\infty}
=(1-2GM/r)L_{\rm loc}
=\frac{4\pi GM}{\kappa}\sqrt{1-2GM/r}.
\]
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
The surface becomes trapped when its areal radius satisfies \(R(\tau)=2GM\).
Interior outgoing null expansions become negative once
\(2Gm(\chi)/R(\tau,\chi)>1\), so a trapped region develops before the final
\(R=0\) singularity.  Comoving matter reaches both events in finite proper
time, while the Schwarzschild \(t\) coordinate sends surface horizon crossing
to \(t\to\infty\).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
The sound time obeys \(t_s\gtrsim R\) because \(c_s\leq1\).  The dynamical
time is \(t_{\rm dyn}\sim(R^3/GM)^{1/2}\).  As \(GM/R\) approaches unity,
pressure gradients must respond on a time no shorter than light crossing,
while collapse proceeds on the same scale.  Causal equations of state
therefore lose hydrostatic support before \(R=2GM\), yielding model-dependent
bounds stronger than the horizon inequality.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
On a static slice,
\[
N_B=4\pi\int_0^R
\frac{n(r)r^2\,\dd r}{\sqrt{1-2Gm(r)/r}},
\qquad M_B=m_bN_B.
\]
The gravitational mass is \(M=m(R)\), determined by the asymptotic metric.
The difference
\[
E_{\rm bind}=M_B-M
\]
includes negative gravitational potential energy and changes in internal
energy; positive \(E_{\rm bind}\) is the energy released in assembling the
star.
\end{solution}
