\begin{exercise}[The innermost stable circular orbit]{Tong, Cambridge Part III Sheet 2}
For timelike Schwarzschild geodesics, find the circular-orbit energy and
angular momentum as functions of radius.  Determine where stability is lost
and compute the energy, angular momentum, and binding efficiency at the ISCO.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[The marginally bound orbit]{Cambridge Part III, General Relativity examples}
Find the unstable circular timelike orbit with unit conserved energy in
Schwarzschild spacetime.  Determine its radius and angular momentum and
contrast it with the photon sphere and ISCO.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Ingoing Eddington--Finkelstein coordinates]{Tong, Cambridge Part III, Black Holes examples}
Introduce the tortoise coordinate and ingoing null coordinate
\(v=t+r_\ast\).  Rewrite the Schwarzschild metric, show that it is regular at
the future horizon, and identify the radial ingoing and outgoing null curves.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Kruskal extension]{Cambridge Part III, 2010 exam}
Exponentiate the Schwarzschild null coordinates to construct Kruskal
coordinates.  Show that the horizon is regular, locate the singularity, and
identify the four regions of the maximally extended spacetime.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[From the horizon to the singularity]{McGreevy, Problem Set 7}
A radial geodesic falls from rest at infinity into a Schwarzschild black hole.
Compute the proper time elapsed between crossing \(r=2M\) and reaching
\(r=0\).  Explain why no choice of rocket thrust can make \(r\) remain
constant inside the horizon.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[The extremal Reissner--Nordstr\"om throat]{Tong, Cambridge Part III, Black Holes examples}
For \(|Q|=M\), calculate the proper radial distance from \(r=M+\epsilon\) to a
fixed exterior radius on a static slice.  Show that it diverges as
\(\epsilon\to0\), despite the finite areal radius of the horizon.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Einstein ring and lens mass]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 5}
In the thin-lens approximation, combine the Schwarzschild deflection angle
with small-angle geometry to derive the lens equation.  For perfect alignment
find the Einstein-ring angle and show how it determines the lens mass.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Photon capture cross section]{Cambridge Part III, 2023 exam}
Relate the impact parameter of a null Schwarzschild geodesic to its turning
point.  Find the critical impact parameter for capture and hence the
high-frequency absorption cross section of a Schwarzschild black hole.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Acceleration required to hover]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 4}
Compute the magnitude of the four-acceleration of a static observer at
Schwarzschild radius \(r\).  Compare its redshifted value at infinity with the
surface gravity in the horizon limit.
\end{exercise}
