\begin{solution}
The vacuum trace equation is
\[
3\Box f'(R)+f'(R)R-2f(R)=0.
\]
For \(f=R+\alpha R^2\) and linear \(R\),
\[
(6\alpha\Box-1)R=0,
\]
or
\[
(\Box-m_s^2)R=0,\qquad m_s^2=\frac1{6\alpha}.
\]
Stability about Minkowski requires \(\alpha>0\).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
The Friedmann equations give
\[
H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{k}{a^2},\qquad
\dot H=-4\pi G(\rho+p)+\frac{k}{a^2}.
\]
For \(k=0\), \(H=0\) implies \(\rho=0\), while \(\dot H>0\) requires
\(\rho+p<0\).  This violates the null energy condition.  Positive curvature
can supply the second term and permits a bounce without the same conclusion.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}

\[
d_{\rm event}(t)=a(t)\int_t^\infty\frac{\dd t'}{a(t')}=\frac1H.
\]
A signal emitted now can cover comoving distance
\[
\Delta\chi_{\max}=\int_{t_0}^\infty e^{-Ht}\dd t

\]
The comoving reach shrinks with time, while the horizon's proper radius stays
constant.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}
At fixed matter content, added entropy means more radiation energy at a given
scale factor.  The next expansion therefore reaches a larger maximum size and
lasts longer before recollapse.  Repeated cycles grow toward the future and
shrink toward the past, so they cannot be identical.  Exact periodicity
requires entropy dilution, export, or genuinely nonstandard microscopic
dynamics.
\end{solution}

\[

A constant ratio therefore satisfies
\[
r_\ast=-\frac{\xi}{w+\xi},
