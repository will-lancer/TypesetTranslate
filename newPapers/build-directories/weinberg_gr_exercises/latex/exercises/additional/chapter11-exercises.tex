\begin{exercise}[Regular central expansion of a relativistic star]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 12}
For central density \(\rho_c\) and pressure \(p_c\), use the TOV equations to
find the first nontrivial terms in \(m(r)\) and \(p(r)\) near \(r=0\).
Explain how these series provide regular initial data for numerical
integration.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Polytropic mass--radius scaling]{Cambridge Part III, Structure and Evolution of Stars}
For a Newtonian polytrope \(p=K\rho^{1+1/n}\), use Lane--Emden scaling to
eliminate the central density and derive the dependence of radius on mass and
\(K\).  Discuss the special cases \(n=1\) and \(n=3\).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[The Chandrasekhar mass from dimensional balance]{Cambridge Part III, Structure and Evolution of Stars}
Balance the ultra-relativistic electron degeneracy energy against Newtonian
gravitational binding for a white dwarf.  Show why the radius cancels and
derive the scaling of the limiting mass with \(G,\hbar\), and the mean
molecular weight per electron.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Maximum surface redshift from Buchdahl's bound]{Cambridge Part III, 2016 exam}
Assuming the hypotheses of Buchdahl's theorem, convert
\(2GM/R\leq8/9\) into an upper bound on the gravitational redshift of a photon
emitted from the stellar surface.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Turning points and radial stability]{Cambridge Part III, Structure and Evolution of Stars}
Consider a one-parameter sequence of equilibrium stars with fixed equation of
state and increasing central density.  Explain, using the radial pulsation
variational principle, why a change of stability occurs at an extremum of
mass, and state the usual qualification involving baryon number.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Relativistic Eddington luminosity]{Cambridge Part III, Structure and Evolution of Stars}
Balance radiation force against the proper acceleration of a static particle
outside a spherical star.  Derive the local Eddington luminosity at radius
\(r\) and the luminosity measured at infinity.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Homogeneous collapse]{Cambridge Part III, 2017 exam}
For homogeneous dust collapse, determine when the stellar surface crosses its
Schwarzschild radius.  Show that trapped surfaces form before the surface
reaches the singularity.  Contrast the proper time of the matter with the
exterior Schwarzschild time.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Causal sound crossing and compactness]{Rutgers Physics 617, General Relativity}
For a barotropic star with \(\dd p/\dd\rho\leq1\), compare its sound-crossing
and dynamical times.  Explain qualitatively why enforcing causality produces
a stronger compactness bound than \(R>2GM\), even without assuming constant
density.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Baryon mass and binding energy]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 12}
For a static spherical star with baryon density \(n(r)\), derive its conserved
baryon number and baryonic mass.  Show why the difference between baryonic and
gravitational mass is the relativistic binding energy.
\end{exercise}
