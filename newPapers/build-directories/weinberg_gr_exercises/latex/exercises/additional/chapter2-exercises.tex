\begin{exercise}[Two-body decay kinematics]{Cambridge Part III, General Relativity examples}
A particle of mass \(M\), initially at rest, decays into particles of masses
\(m_1\) and \(m_2\).  Use four-momentum conservation to find the daughter
energies and the magnitude of their three-momenta.  Express the latter using
the K\"all\'en function
\(\lambda(a,b,c)=a^2+b^2+c^2-2ab-2ac-2bc\).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Compton scattering from invariants]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 1}
A photon of initial frequency \(\omega\) scatters through angle \(\theta\) from
an electron initially at rest.  Without choosing components for the recoil
electron, square the momentum-conservation equation and derive the Compton
formula for the outgoing frequency \(\omega'\).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Invariant mass-shell measure]{McGreevy, Problem Set 1}
Starting from
\[
\dd^4p\,\delta(p^2+m^2)\Theta(p^0),
\]
show that \(\dd^3p/(2E_{\bm p})\) is Lorentz invariant.  Deduce the invariant
particle-number measure and explain why a relativistic one-particle
distribution function may be taken to be a scalar.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Classifying electromagnetic fields]{Tong, Cambridge Part III Sheet 1}
Use the two Lorentz invariants of \(F_{\mu\nu}\) to decide when an observer can
see (i) a purely electric field, (ii) a purely magnetic field, or (iii)
orthogonal fields of equal magnitude.  Show that the last case is the null
field of a plane electromagnetic wave.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[The ideal photon rocket]{Cambridge Part III, General Relativity examples}
An ideal rocket accelerates by emitting an infinitesimal collimated pulse of
light directly backward in its instantaneous rest frame.  If its rest mass
falls from \(M_i\) to \(M_f\), derive its final rapidity and speed.  Compare
the result with the nonrelativistic rocket equation.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Laboratory and center-of-momentum energies]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 1}
A particle of mass \(m_1\) and laboratory energy \(E_1\) strikes a stationary
particle of mass \(m_2\).  Find the center-of-momentum energy and the velocity
of the center-of-momentum frame.  Determine the fraction of \(E_1\) available
for producing new rest mass when \(E_1\gg m_1,m_2\).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[A covariant spin vector]{McGreevy, Problem Set 2}
Let \(S^\mu\) be the spin four-vector of a massive particle, with
\(S\cdot p=0\).  Starting from rest-frame spin
\(S^\mu_{\rm rest}=(0,\bm s)\), boost to a frame in which the particle has
velocity \(\bm v\).  Verify \(S^2=\bm s^2\) and identify the longitudinal
enhancement.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Maximal proper time]{Cambridge Part III, General Relativity examples}
Let \(A\) and \(B\) be timelike separated.  Prove that the inertial curve from
\(A\) to \(B\) has greater proper time than any other future-directed timelike
curve.  First prove the result for a broken line, then obtain the smooth case
by approximation.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Relativistic beaming and specific intensity]{MIT 8.962, Problem Set 2}
Show that \(I_\nu/\nu^3\) is invariant for freely propagating radiation.  For
Doppler factor \(\mathcal D\), derive the transformations of specific
intensity and bolometric intensity, and explain the forward beaming of an
isotropic emitter.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Velocity space as a hyperboloid]{Tong, Cambridge Part III Sheet 1}
The future unit four-velocities satisfy \(u_\mu u^\mu=-1\).  Parametrize this
hyperboloid by rapidity \(\chi\) and a direction \(\hat{\bm n}\), derive its
induced metric, and show that the geodesic distance between two four-velocities
is their relative rapidity.
\end{exercise}
