\begin{enumerate}
  \SupplementarySolution{1}{Critical Scaling, \(O(N)\) Goldstone Modes, and a Phase Diagram}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Let \(t\) denote the reduced temperature.  The correlation length scales
    as \(\xi\sim |t|^{-\nu}\).  If one correlation volume contributes an
    order-one singular free energy, the singular free-energy density obeys
    \[
      f_{\mathrm s}\sim \xi^{-d}\sim |t|^{d\nu}.
    \]
    On the other hand,
    \(C_{\mathrm s}\sim-\partial_t^2f_{\mathrm s}\sim|t|^{-\alpha}\),
    so \(f_{\mathrm s}\sim|t|^{2-\alpha}\), up to analytic terms.  Equating
    the two singular powers gives the hyperscaling relation
    \[
      \boxed{2-\alpha=d\nu.}
    \]
    \item[(b)]
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(i)]
      For \(g>0\) and \(\mu^2<0\), the uniform minima satisfy
      \[
        \boldsymbol\phi^2=v^2,
        \qquad v^2=-\frac{\mu^2}{4g}.
      \]
      The equations have \(O(N)\) symmetry, but choosing one point on this
      sphere as the thermodynamic vacuum leaves only its \(O(N-1)\)
      stabilizer unbroken.  This is spontaneous symmetry breaking:
      \[
        O(N)\longrightarrow O(N-1),
        \qquad \mathcal M_{\mathrm{vac}}\simeq S^{N-1}.
      \]
      The \(N-1\) fluctuations tangent to this sphere cost no potential
      energy.  Their long-wavelength quanta are the Goldstone bosons.

      \item[(ii)]
      Choose the last axis as the vacuum direction and write
      \(\boldsymbol\phi=(\boldsymbol\pi,v+\sigma)\), with
      \(\boldsymbol\pi\) containing \(N-1\) components.  The stationarity
      relation \(\mu^2+4gv^2=0\) gives
      \[
        F^{(2)}=\frac12\int d^dx\left[
          (\boldsymbol\nabla\boldsymbol\pi)^2
          +(\boldsymbol\nabla\sigma)^2
          +(-2\mu^2)\sigma^2\right].
      \]
      Thus \(m_\sigma^2=-2\mu^2=8gv^2\), whereas
      \(m_{\pi_a}^2=0\).  In momentum space,
      \[
        \langle\pi_a(\boldsymbol k)\pi_b(-\boldsymbol k)\rangle
          =\frac{\delta_{ab}}{\boldsymbol k^2}.
      \]
      Comparing this with \((\boldsymbol k^2+\xi^{-2})^{-1}\) shows
      \(\boxed{\xi_\pi=\infty}\).

      \item[(iii)]
      The transverse fluctuations contain the infrared integral
      \[
        \langle\boldsymbol\pi^2\rangle_{\mathrm{IR}}
          \propto\int_0 dk\,k^{d-3}.
      \]
      It diverges for \(d\leq2\), destroying a vacuum with a fixed
      continuous order-parameter direction.  Hence the lower critical
      dimension is
      \[
        \boxed{d_{\mathrm{lc}}=2.}
      \]
      For \(N=2\), a two-dimensional Berezinskii--Kosterlitz--Thouless phase
      can have algebraic order, but it still has no nonzero spontaneous
      magnetization and does not change this lower-critical-dimension
      statement.
    \end{enumerate}

    \item[(c)]
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(i)]
      An \(O(2)\) invariant polynomial can depend on \(\phi_2,\phi_3\) only
      through \(\rho^2=\phi_2^2+\phi_3^2\).  Therefore the quadratic and
      quartic terms must have the form
      \[
        \frac12\mu_1^2\phi_1^2+\frac12\mu_2^2\rho^2
          +a\phi_1^4+b\phi_1^2\rho^2+c\rho^4.
      \]
      In the notation of the question this requires
      \[
        \boxed{\mu_2^2=\mu_3^2,\qquad
          g_{12}=g_{13},\qquad g_{22}=g_{33}=g_{23},}
      \]
      with \(\mu_1^2\), \(g_{11}\), and the common cross-coupling free.
      The displayed powers already make the independent
      \(\phi_1\mapsto-\phi_1\) symmetry manifest.

      The source's phrase ``positive-definite matrix'' is stronger than is
      compatible with these relations: the vector \((0,1,-1)\) is a null
      vector of the resulting \(g_{ij}\).  Positivity of the quartic on the
      physical variables \(\phi_i^2\geq0\), rather than strict matrix
      positive-definiteness, is sufficient.  We retain the source's
      all-equal specialization in the next part.

      \item[(ii)]
      Put \(r_1=\mu_1^2\), \(r_2=\mu_2^2\), and
      \(\rho^2=\phi_2^2+\phi_3^2\).  The uniform potential becomes
      \[
        V=\frac12r_1\phi_1^2+\frac12r_2\rho^2
          +g(\phi_1^2+\rho^2)^2.
      \]
      Its mean-field minima and symmetry patterns are
      \[
      \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
        \text{region}&\text{minimum}&\text{unbroken subgroup}
          &\text{Goldstones}\\ \hline
        r_1>0,\ r_2>0
          &\phi_1=\rho=0&\mathbb Z_2\times O(2)&0\\
        r_1<0,\ r_1<r_2
          &\phi_1^2=-r_1/(4g),\ \rho=0&O(2)&0\\
        r_2<0,\ r_2<r_1
          &\phi_1=0,\ \rho^2=-r_2/(4g)
          &\mathbb Z_2\times O(1)&1
      \end{array}
      \]
      The disordered-to-\(\phi_1\) boundary is \(r_1=0\), \(r_2>0\), and
      the disordered-to-\(O(2)\)-ordered boundary is
      \(r_2=0\), \(r_1>0\).  For \(r_1,r_2<0\), the two ordered phases meet
      on \(r_1=r_2\).

      On the negative diagonal, the displayed special couplings enlarge the
      symmetry accidentally to \(O(3)\), and
      \[
        \phi_1^2+\rho^2=-\frac{r_1}{4g}.
      \]
      Hence \(O(3)\to O(2)\) there and two directions are gapless.  Viewed
      only inside the stated \(\mathbb Z_2\times O(2)\), a generic point on
      that sphere breaks the \(\mathbb Z_2\) and reduces \(O(2)\) to
      \(O(1)\); the second zero mode comes from the accidental enhancement.
      The negative diagonal is the mean-field coexistence boundary between
      the two ordered phases, and the origin is their multicritical meeting
      point with the disordered phase.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{2}{Two-Flavor QCD and the Pion Effective Theory}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)]
    With \(q=(u,d)^{\mathsf T}\), the massless two-flavor Lagrangian is
    \[
      \mathcal L=-\frac14G^a_{\mu\nu}G^{a\mu\nu}
        +\overline q\,i\gamma^\mu D_\mu q.
    \]
    The left- and right-handed fields may be rotated independently.  Up to
    finite central quotients, the classical global group is
    \[
      U(2)_L\times U(2)_R
        \simeq SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_V\times U(1)_A.
    \]
    The singlet axial \(U(1)_A\) has an Adler--Bell--Jackiw anomaly.  Thus the
    continuous quantum global group is
    \[
      \boxed{SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_V,}
    \]
    again modulo finite identifications.  A discrete axial remnant survives
    the anomaly, but there is no continuous singlet axial generator.

    \item[(ii)]
    Under \(q_L\mapsto Lq_L\), \(q_R\mapsto Rq_R\), the condensate is
    invariant only for \(L=R\).  Baryon number remains unbroken, while
    \[
      SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\longrightarrow SU(2)_V.
    \]
    Consequently
    \[
      \mathcal M_{\mathrm{vac}}
        =\frac{SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R}{SU(2)_V}
        \simeq SU(2)\simeq S^3,
    \]
    with three Goldstone coordinates.  A field \(U(x)\in SU(2)\) can
    represent this manifold and transforms as
    \(U\mapsto LUR^\dagger\).  Lorentz invariance, the nonlinear chiral
    symmetry, and parity forbid a one-derivative scalar.  At two derivatives
    the invariant metric on this homogeneous space is unique up to its
    normalization, giving the displayed sigma-model Lagrangian.  Since
    \([\mathcal L]=4\), \([\partial_\mu]=1\), and \(U\) is dimensionless,
    \[
      \boxed{[f_\pi]=1.}
    \]

    \item[(iii)]
    Set \(A=2i\pi/f_\pi\).  The Maurer--Cartan expansion is
    \[
      U^\dagger\partial_\mu U
       =\partial_\mu A+\frac12[A,\partial_\mu A]
        +\frac16[A,[A,\partial_\mu A]]+\cdots.
    \]
    Cyclicity of the trace removes the cubic term and gives
    \[
      \operatorname{tr}(\partial^\mu U^\dagger\partial_\mu U)
       =\frac4{f_\pi^2}\operatorname{tr}(\partial_\mu\pi)^2
        +\frac4{3f_\pi^4}
          \operatorname{tr}[\pi,\partial_\mu\pi]^2+O(\pi^6).
    \]
    The trace identity
    \[
      \operatorname{tr}[\pi,X]^2
        =-2\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\pi^2X^2-(\pi X)^2\right)
    \]
    then yields
    \[
      \boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pion}}
        =\operatorname{tr}(\partial_\mu\pi)^2
        -\frac{2}{3f_\pi^2}
          \operatorname{tr}\!\left[
            \pi^2(\partial_\mu\pi)^2-(\pi\partial_\mu\pi)^2
          \right]+O(\pi^6).}
    \]

    \item[(iv)]
    Electromagnetism gauges the vector flavor transformation generated by
    \(Q\):
    \[
      U\mapsto VUV^\dagger,
      \qquad V(x)=e^{ie\alpha(x)Q}.
    \]
    The commutator covariant derivative is precisely the one that makes
    \(D_\mu U\mapsto V(D_\mu U)V^\dagger\), and therefore preserves gauge
    invariance of the trace kinetic term.

    Write the Hermitian pion matrix as
    \[
      \pi=\begin{pmatrix}
        \pi^0/2&\pi^+/\sqrt2\\[2pt]
        \pi^-/\sqrt2&-\pi^0/2
      \end{pmatrix}.
    \]
    Since \(q_u-q_d=1\),
    \[
      [Q,\pi]=\begin{pmatrix}
        0&\pi^+/\sqrt2\\[2pt]
        -\pi^-/\sqrt2&0
      \end{pmatrix},
      \qquad
      D_\mu\pi=\partial_\mu\pi-ieA_\mu[Q,\pi]+O(\pi^2).
    \]
    Thus
    \[
      \boxed{q(\pi^+)=+e,\qquad q(\pi^-)=-e,\qquad q(\pi^0)=0.}
    \]
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{3}{An Abelian Coset with Charged Matter}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    The Maurer--Cartan form is
    \[
      g^{-1}\partial_\mu g
        =(i\partial_\mu\theta_L,i\partial_\mu\theta_R).
    \]
    Projection parallel and perpendicular to the diagonal gives
    \[
      \boxed{\begin{aligned}
        E_\mu&=i(a_\mu,a_\mu),&
        a_\mu&=\frac12\partial_\mu(\theta_L+\theta_R),\\
        F_\mu&=\frac i2
          (\partial_\mu\theta_A,-\partial_\mu\theta_A),&
        \theta_A&=\theta_L-\theta_R .
      \end{aligned}}
    \]
    \item[(b)]
    Left multiplication by a global group element gives
    \[
      \theta_L\mapsto\theta_L+\alpha_L,\qquad
      \theta_R\mapsto\theta_R+\alpha_R.
    \]
    Right multiplication by
    \(h^{-1}=(e^{-i\beta(x)},e^{-i\beta(x)})\) instead gives
    \[
      \theta_L\mapsto\theta_L-\beta,\qquad
      \theta_R\mapsto\theta_R-\beta.
    \]
    Therefore \(\theta_L+\theta_R\) is representative dependent, whereas
    \(\theta_A=\theta_L-\theta_R\) is the physical Goldstone coordinate.
    It shifts by the broken global parameter
    \(\alpha_L-\alpha_R\).
    \item[(c)]
    Under the representative change,
    \[
      \widetilde\Phi\mapsto
        e^{i(q_L+q_R)\beta}\widetilde\Phi,\qquad
      a_\mu\mapsto a_\mu-\partial_\mu\beta .
    \]
    Hence, with \(q_V=q_L+q_R\),
    \[
      D_\mu\widetilde\Phi
        =(\partial_\mu+iq_Va_\mu)\widetilde\Phi
    \]
    transforms by the same phase as \(\widetilde\Phi\).  Substitution of
    the dressed field yields the useful representative-independent form
    \[
      \boxed{D_\mu\widetilde\Phi
        =e^{-i(q_L\theta_L+q_R\theta_R)}
        \left[\partial_\mu
        -\frac i2(q_L-q_R)\partial_\mu\theta_A\right]\Phi .}
    \]
    \item[(d)]
    Normalize the Abelian trace by
    \(\operatorname{Tr}[(ix_L,ix_R)(iy_L,iy_R)]
      =-(x_Ly_L+x_Ry_R)\).  Then
    \[
      \boxed{\operatorname{Tr}(F_\mu F^\mu)
        =-\frac12\partial_\mu\theta_A\partial^\mu\theta_A,}
    \]
    and
    \[
      \boxed{(D_\mu\widetilde\Phi)^\dagger
        D^\mu\widetilde\Phi
        =\left|\left(\partial_\mu
          -\frac i2(q_L-q_R)\partial_\mu\theta_A\right)\Phi\right|^2.}
    \]
    The first expression depends only on the representative-independent
    difference \(\theta_A\).  In the second, a global \(G\) transformation
    shifts \(\theta_A\) by a constant and multiplies \(\Phi\) by its charge
    phase, so the norm is unchanged.  Under a local representative change,
    the phase of \(\widetilde\Phi\) is canceled by the transformation of
    \(a_\mu\), proving invariance directly.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{4}{Goldstone Power Counting, Curvature, and Pseudo-Goldstones}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Direct counting gives
    \[
      \nu=4L-2I+\sum_iV_id_i.
    \]
    For a connected graph,
    \(L=I-\sum_iV_i+1\), so \(I=L+\sum_iV_i-1\).
    Substitution yields
    \[
      \boxed{\nu=2+2L+\sum_iV_i(d_i-2).}
    \]
    A one-loop graph with only \(d_i=2\) vertices has
    \(\nu=2+2=4\).  It competes with a tree graph containing one
    four-derivative vertex.
    \item[(b)]
    Substitution of the normal-coordinate expansion into the kinetic term
    gives
    \[
      \mathcal L_4
        =\frac16R_{acbd}\pi_c\pi_d
          \partial_\mu\pi_a\partial^\mu\pi_b.
    \]
    Thus the four-point vertex is entirely determined by \(R_{acbd}\).
    For a sphere of radius \(F\),
    \[
      R_{acbd}=\frac1{F^2}
        (\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}-\delta_{ad}\delta_{cb}).
    \]
    On shell, the resulting crossing-symmetric amplitude is
    \[
      \boxed{\mathcal M_{ab,cd}
        =\frac1{F^2}\left(
         \delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}s+
         \delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}t+
         \delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}u\right).}
    \]
    \item[(c)]
    Choose the aligned vacuum \(n_3=1\) and parameterize
    \[
      n_3=\sqrt{1-\boldsymbol\pi^2/F^2}
        =1-\frac{\boldsymbol\pi^2}{2F^2}+\cdots.
    \]
    The perturbation becomes
    \[
      \Delta\mathcal L=hF-\frac{h}{2F}\boldsymbol\pi^2+\cdots.
    \]
    Comparing the quadratic term with
    \(-m^2\boldsymbol\pi^2/2\) gives
    \[
      \boxed{m_{\pi_1}^2=m_{\pi_2}^2=h/F.}
    \]
    The squared mass is linear in the explicit-breaking source, the general
    pseudo-Goldstone behavior.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{5}{Current Poles, PCAC, and Pion Thresholds}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Lorentz covariance lets the part of the Fourier transform relevant to
    a scalar intermediate state be written
    \[
      G^\mu_{ab}(q)=q^\mu G_{ab}(q^2).
    \]
    Current conservation would imply \(q^2G_{ab}=0\) away from contact
    terms.  The equal-time contact term is the nonzero expectation value
    \(\langle[J^0_a,\Phi_b]\rangle\), so \(G_{ab}\) cannot vanish
    identically.  It must instead have
    \[
      G_{ab}(q^2)\sim\frac{C_{ab}}{q^2-i0}.
    \]
    The spectral density therefore contains a contribution proportional to
    \(\delta(q^2)\).  This is a massless one-particle state coupled both to
    the broken current and to \(\Phi_b\).
    \item[(b)]
    Translation invariance and the on-shell condition give, up to the
    overall phase convention for one-particle states,
    \[
      \boxed{
      \bra{\Omega}\partial_\mu A^\mu_a(0)\ket{\pi_b(p)}
        =f_\pi m_\pi^2\delta_{ab}.}
    \]
    Thus the divergence is small in precisely the pseudo-Goldstone
    parameter \(m_\pi^2\).  In the chiral limit \(m_\pi\to0\), the axial
    current is conserved while its one-pion matrix element remains
    nonzero.
    \item[(c)]
    At threshold \(s=4m_\pi^2\), \(t=u=0\), so
    \[
      A(s,t,u)=\frac{3m_\pi^2}{f_\pi^2},\qquad
      A(t,s,u)=A(u,t,s)=-\frac{m_\pi^2}{f_\pi^2}.
    \]
    Hence
    \[
      T^0_{\rm thr}=\frac{7m_\pi^2}{f_\pi^2},\qquad
      T^1_{\rm thr}=0,\qquad
      T^2_{\rm thr}=-\frac{2m_\pi^2}{f_\pi^2}.
    \]
    The \(S\)-wave scattering lengths are therefore
    \[
      \boxed{a_0^0=\frac{7m_\pi^2}{32\pi f_\pi^2},\qquad
        a_0^2=-\frac{m_\pi^2}{16\pi f_\pi^2}.}
    \]
    Bose symmetry excludes an \(I=1\) \(S\)-wave at threshold.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{6}{Goldberger--Treiman, Baryon Counting, and Soft-Pion Commutators}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Let \(q=p'-p\).  Anticommuting \(\gamma_5\) through the Dirac matrices
    and using the two on-shell equations gives
    \[
      \overline u(p')\slashed q\gamma_5u(p)
        =2m_N\overline u(p')\gamma_5u(p),
    \]
    up to the conventional factors of \(i\) associated with the chapter's
    Dirac conventions.  The derivative vertex is therefore on-shell
    equivalent to
    \[
      -\frac{g_Am_N}{f_\pi}
        \overline N\gamma_5\tau_a\pi_aN.
    \]
    Identifying the coefficient with the pseudoscalar pion--nucleon
    coupling gives
    \[
      \boxed{g_{\pi NN}f_\pi=g_Am_N.}
    \]
    In the chapter's convention \(F_\pi=2f_\pi\), this is
    \(G_{\pi N}=2m_Ng_A/F_\pi\).
    \item[(b)]
    Before using topology,
    \[
      \nu=4L-2I_\pi-I_N+\sum_iV_id_i.
    \]
    The connected-graph identities are
    \[
      L=I_\pi+I_N-\sum_iV_i+1,\qquad
      2I_N+E_N=\sum_iV_in_i.
    \]
    Eliminating \(I_\pi\) and \(I_N\) gives
    \[
      \boxed{\nu
        =2+2L-E_N+\sum_iV_i
         \left(d_i+\frac{n_i}{2}-2\right).}
    \]
    Pure-pion vertices have \(n_i=0,d_i\geq2\), and leading
    pion--nucleon vertices have \(n_i=2,d_i\geq1\), so every bracket is
    nonnegative.
    \item[(c)]
    Contract the axial-current identity with \(q_\mu\), isolate its pion
    pole, and apply LSZ to the outgoing pion.  With
    \(\bra{\Omega}A^\mu_a\ket{\pi_b(q)}=if_\pi q^\mu\delta_{ab}\),
    the regular part gives
    \[
      \boxed{
      \lim_{q\to0}
      \bra{\beta;\pi^a(q)}\mathcal O\ket{\alpha}
        =-\frac{i}{f_\pi}
         \bra{\beta}[Q_5^a,\mathcal O]\ket{\alpha}
         +\text{external-leg poles}.}
    \]
    Reversing the phase convention for the pion field reverses both sides,
    so physical consequences are unchanged.  Pole terms must be retained
    when the soft current can attach to an on-shell external particle.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{7}{Running Couplings, Low-Energy QCD, and Chiral Symmetry}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(i)]
      Since
      \[
        \frac{d}{d\log\mu}\frac1\alpha=\frac{b}{2\pi},
      \]
      integration between a reference scale \(\mu_0\) and \(\mu\) gives
      \[
        \boxed{
        \frac1{\alpha_3(\mu)}
          =\frac1{\alpha_3(\mu_0)}
           +\frac{b}{2\pi}\log\frac{\mu}{\mu_0}},
        \qquad
        \alpha_3(\mu)
          =\frac{\alpha_3(\mu_0)}{
            1+\dfrac{b\alpha_3(\mu_0)}{2\pi}
              \log(\mu/\mu_0)}.
      \]
      For QCD, \(b>0\): the coupling decreases logarithmically toward the
      ultraviolet and grows toward the infrared.  Extrapolating the one-loop
      formula to its infrared pole defines
      \[
        \boxed{\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
          =\mu\exp\!\left[-\frac{2\pi}{b\alpha_3(\mu)}\right].}
      \]
      Perturbation theory fails already when \(\alpha_3\) becomes order one,
      so this is an order-of-magnitude definition of the strong scale rather
      than a claim that the one-loop pole is physical.

      \item[(ii)]
      When \(b>0\), the theory is asymptotically free: it becomes weakly
      coupled at high energy and strongly coupled in the infrared.  When
      \(b<0\), the direction is reversed: the theory is infrared free, while
      its one-loop coupling grows toward a possible ultraviolet Landau pole.
      For \(b=0\), this leading-order equation gives no running.

      \item[(iii)]
      Write \(A_i=1/\alpha_i\), \(A_G=1/\alpha_G\), and
      \(L=(2\pi)^{-1}\log(M_Z/M_{\mathrm{GUT}})\).  One-loop running from the
      common high-scale value gives
      \[
        A_i(M_Z)=A_G+b_iL.
      \]
      Eliminating \(A_G\) and \(L\),
      \[
        A_3-A_2=(b_3-b_2)L,
        \qquad A_1-A_2=(b_1-b_2)L,
      \]
      and hence
      \[
        \boxed{
        \frac1{\alpha_3(M_Z)}
        =\frac1{\alpha_2(M_Z)}
        +\frac{b_3-b_2}{b_1-b_2}
          \left[\frac1{\alpha_1(M_Z)}
                -\frac1{\alpha_2(M_Z)}\right].}
      \]
    \end{enumerate}

    \item[(b)]
    Let \(q=(u,d)^{\mathsf T}\),
    \(M=\operatorname{diag}(m_u,m_d)\), and
    \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(2/3,-1/3)\).  The renormalizable low-energy
    gauge Lagrangian is
    \[
      \boxed{
      \mathcal L=-\frac14G^a_{\mu\nu}G^{a\mu\nu}
        -\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
        +\overline q\,(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu-M)q,}
    \]
    where
    \[
      D_\mu=\partial_\mu-ig_3G_\mu^aT^a-ieA_\mu Q.
    \]
    Every displayed dimension-four-or-less gauge-invariant operator is
    unchanged by the common phase
    \[
      q\longmapsto e^{i\beta/3}q.
    \]
    Its Noether charge assigns baryon number \(1/3\) to each quark.  The
    gauge symmetry did not require this global \(U(1)_B\); rather, Lorentz
    invariance, gauge invariance, and renormalizability leave no
    baryon-number-violating operator.  The first such Standard-Model
    operators occur at higher dimension.  Thus baryon number is accidental
    at the level of this Lagrangian.

    \item[(c)]
    If the small quark masses and electromagnetic effects are neglected,
    the QCD terms have
    \[
      SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_V
    \]
    as their continuous global symmetry, up to finite quotients.  The
    classical \(U(1)_A\) is removed by the quantum anomaly.  Strong QCD can
    form a condensate
    \(\langle\overline q_Rq_L\rangle\propto\mathbf1\), which breaks
    \(SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\) spontaneously to the vector subgroup
    \(SU(2)_V\).  The small values of \(m_u,m_d\), their difference, and
    electromagnetism explicitly perturb this pattern.

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(i)]
      A linear realization uses a scalar \(\sigma\) and an isotriplet
      \(\boldsymbol\pi\), combined into
      \(\Sigma=\sigma\mathbf1+i\pi^a\tau^a\), with
      \(\Sigma\mapsto L\Sigma R^\dagger\).  For example,
      \[
        \mathcal L_\Sigma
          =-\frac12(\partial_\mu\sigma)^2
           -\frac12(\partial_\mu\boldsymbol\pi)^2
           -\frac\lambda4
             (\sigma^2+\boldsymbol\pi^2-v^2)^2+h\sigma.
      \]
      At \(h=0\), choose \(\langle\sigma\rangle=v\).  Then
      \[
        SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\simeq O(4)
          \longrightarrow SU(2)_V\simeq O(3).
      \]
      The three fields \(\pi^1,\pi^2,\pi^3\), identified with
      \(\pi^\pm,\pi^0\), are the Goldstones.  The small explicit breakings
      represented by \(h\), the nondegenerate mass matrix, and
      electromagnetism make them pseudo-Goldstone bosons.

      \item[(ii)]
      After the radial mode is integrated out, put
      \(U=\exp(i\tau^a\pi^a/f_\pi)\), with
      \(U\mapsto LUR^\dagger\).  In the volume's mostly-plus convention, a
      leading chiral Lagrangian is
      \[
        \boxed{
        \mathcal L_{\mathrm{eff}}
          =-\frac{f_\pi^2}{4}
             \operatorname{tr}(\partial_\mu U^\dagger\partial^\mu U)
           +\frac{Bf_\pi^2}{2}
             \operatorname{tr}(MU^\dagger+UM^\dagger)+\cdots.}
      \]
      Covariant derivatives add electromagnetism.  The omitted terms have
      more derivatives or further powers of the mass spurion and are
      suppressed for \(E\ll\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\).

      \item[(iii)]
      Yes, in the parametric sense.  Exact isospin would place the proton and
      neutron in one degenerate \(SU(2)_V\) doublet.  Their splitting must
      therefore be proportional to the small symmetry-breaking parameters,
      schematically
      \[
        m_n-m_p=c_m(m_d-m_u)+c_{\mathrm{em}}e^2+\cdots.
      \]
      This explains why the splitting is small compared with a typical
      hadronic mass.  Symmetry alone does not determine the low-energy
      constants or the numerical splitting, and the strong and
      electromagnetic contributions need not have the same sign.
    \end{enumerate}

    \item[(d)]
    The strange quark is light enough compared with the chiral scale to be
    included, with reduced accuracy, by extending
    \(SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\) to
    \(SU(3)_L\times SU(3)_R\).  The kaons and eta then join the pions as an
    approximate pseudo-Goldstone octet.  The expansion converges less well
    because \(m_s\) is not nearly as small as \(m_u,m_d\).

    Charm is too heavy to be treated as another light chiral flavor:
    \(m_c\gg\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\), and there is no light charmed
    pseudo-Goldstone multiplet.  It should instead be integrated out for the
    pion theory, or described with a heavy-quark effective theory.  Thus the
    light-flavor chiral analysis extends usefully to \(s\), but not to \(c\)
    in the same way.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{8}{Quark-Mass Ratios, Loop Counterterms, and the WZW Term}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Write the common charged electromagnetic shift as \(\Delta\).
    The leading formulas are
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      m_{\pi^+}^2&=B_0(m_u+m_d)+\Delta,&
      m_{\pi^0}^2&=B_0(m_u+m_d),\\
      m_{K^+}^2&=B_0(m_u+m_s)+\Delta,&
      m_{K^0}^2&=B_0(m_d+m_s).
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    Eliminating \(B_0,\Delta,m_s\) yields
    \[
      \boxed{
      \frac{m_u}{m_d}
        =\frac{
         2m_{\pi^0}^2-m_{K^0}^2-m_{\pi^+}^2+m_{K^+}^2}
        {m_{K^0}^2+m_{\pi^+}^2-m_{K^+}^2}.}
    \]
    Corrections to Dashen's leading electromagnetic relation and higher
    chiral orders modify the numerical value.
    \item[(b)]
    The pure-Goldstone counting formula is
    \[
      \nu=2+2L+\sum_iV_i(d_i-2).
    \]
    A one-loop graph with only \(d_i=2\) vertices has \(\nu=4\).  A tree
    graph with one \(d_i=4\) vertex also has
    \(\nu=2+(4-2)=4\).  They must therefore be included together.

    The loop contains ultraviolet poles and logarithms proportional to
    local \(Q^4\) operators.  Renormalized coefficients \(L_i^r(\mu)\) in
    \(\mathcal L_4\) absorb the poles and run so that their
    \(\ln\mu\) dependence cancels the loop logarithms.  Only the sum is a
    scale-independent amplitude.
    \item[(c)]
    At lowest order,
    \(U^{-1}dU=(2i/F)dB+\cdots\), so the five-form starts with
    \[
      \operatorname{Tr}(dB\,dB\,dB\,dB\,dB).
    \]
    Converting the five-ball integral to its four-dimensional boundary
    gives a term of the form
    \[
      \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
        \operatorname{Tr}
        (B\,\partial_\mu B\,\partial_\nu B\,
         \partial_\rho B\,\partial_\sigma B).
    \]
    Thus the leading interaction contains five meson fields, four
    four-dimensional derivatives, and the parity-odd epsilon tensor.
    Two different extensions of the same boundary field differ by a map
    from \(S^5\) to \(SU(3)\).  Since \(\pi_5(SU(3))=\mathbb Z\), the action
    changes by \(2\pi\) times the coefficient; single-valuedness of
    \(e^{iI}\) requires that coefficient to be an integer.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{9}{Matrix-Scalar Vacua and Goldstone Bosons}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    The potential is
    \[
      V(\phi)=-m^2\operatorname{Tr}(\phi^\dagger\phi)
        +\frac\lambda4
          \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(\phi^\dagger\phi)^2\right].
    \]
    Assume \(m^2>0\) and \(\lambda>0\).  Diagonalize the positive
    semidefinite matrix \(X=\phi^\dagger\phi\), and call its eigenvalues
    \(x_i\geq0\).  Then
    \[
      V=\sum_{i=1}^N\left(-m^2x_i+\frac\lambda4x_i^2\right).
    \]
    Every term is minimized at \(x_i=2m^2/\lambda\).  Thus, with
    \(v=\sqrt{2m^2/\lambda}\),
    \[
      \phi^\dagger\phi=v^2\mathbf1.
    \]
    The polar decomposition therefore gives the complete set
    \[
      \boxed{\mathcal M_{\mathrm{vac}}
        =\{\phi_0=vW:W\in U(N)\}\simeq U(N).}
    \]

    \item[(b)]
    A unitary matrix is normal, so choose a basis in which
    \[
      \phi_0=v\operatorname{diag}
        (e^{i\theta_1},\ldots,e^{i\theta_N}).
    \]
    For a generic minimum, all eigenvalues are distinct.  A matrix
    \(U\in SU(N)\) obeys \(U^\dagger\phi_0U=\phi_0\) precisely when it is a
    diagonal phase matrix in this basis, subject to unit determinant.
    Consequently the generic stabilizer is the maximal torus
    \[
      \boxed{H=U(1)^{N-1}.}
    \]
    More generally, if the distinct eigenvalues have multiplicities
    \(n_1,\ldots,n_k\), the stabilizer is
    \(S[U(n_1)\times\cdots\times U(n_k)]\).

    \item[(c)]
    Counting the generators broken inside the stated \(SU(N)/\mathbb Z_N\)
    symmetry gives
    \[
      \boxed{N_{\mathrm{GB}}
        =\dim SU(N)-\dim U(1)^{N-1}
        =(N^2-1)-(N-1)=N(N-1).}
    \]
    For the general multiplicity pattern of part (b), this becomes
    \(N^2-\sum_a n_a^2\).  A particularly simple special vacuum is
    \[
      \phi_0=ve^{i\theta}\mathbf1.
    \]
    It commutes with every \(SU(N)\) transformation, so the stated symmetry
    is unbroken and contributes no Goldstone bosons.

    There is a useful qualification for the Lagrangian exactly as printed.
    It accidentally has the larger biunitary symmetry
    \(U(N)_L\times U(N)_R\), acting by
    \(\phi\mapsto L\phi R^\dagger\).  This larger group is broken to a
    diagonal \(U(N)\) at every minimum and would give \(N^2\) Goldstone
    modes.  The \(N(N-1)\) count, and the possibility of fewer modes at a
    special \(\phi_0\), answer the question for the explicitly identified
    \(SU(N)/\mathbb Z_N\) symmetry; generic additional interactions that
    retain only that symmetry lift the accidental extra flat directions.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{10}{Positivity, \(U(1)_A\), and the Neutral-Pion Anomaly}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    The gauge covariant derivative is flavor blind, so \([\slashed D,M]=0\).
    Since \(\slashed D^\dagger=-\slashed D\),
    \[
      (\slashed D+M)^\dagger(\slashed D+M)
        =-\slashed D^{\,2}+M^2\geq m_{\min}^2.
    \]
    Every singular value of \(\slashed D+M\) is therefore at least
    \(m_{\min}\), and
    \[
      \boxed{\|(\slashed D+M)^{-1}\|\leq\frac1{m_{\min}}.}
    \]
    Correlators built from finitely many propagators are uniformly bounded
    as a small vector-symmetry-breaking perturbation is removed, provided
    no mass eigenvalue approaches zero.  There is then no singular factor
    capable of canceling that perturbation and leaving a symmetry-breaking
    order parameter.  The argument deliberately fails in a chiral
    zero-mass limit.
    \item[(b)]
    Classically,
    \[
      J^\mu_5=\sum_{f=1}^{N_f}\overline q_f\gamma^\mu\gamma_5q_f
    \]
    is conserved for massless quarks.  Quantum mechanically, with the
    conventional generator normalization,
    \[
      \boxed{\partial_\mu J^\mu_5
        =\frac{N_fg^2}{16\pi^2}
         F^a_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{a\mu\nu}.}
    \]
    Thus the singlet axial charge is not conserved.  Goldstone's theorem
    applies to the eight conserved non-singlet axial generators, but not to
    this anomalous ninth generator.  No extra light singlet pseudoscalar is
    required.
    \item[(c)]
    The vertex produces a gauge-invariant amplitude proportional to
    \[
      g_{\pi\gamma\gamma}
        \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
        \epsilon_{1\mu}\epsilon_{2\nu}k_{1\rho}k_{2\sigma}.
    \]
    Summing the two photon polarizations and including the \(1/2!\) for
    identical particles gives
    \[
      \Gamma=\frac{g_{\pi\gamma\gamma}^2m_\pi^3}{64\pi}.
    \]
    With the anomalous coupling specified in the exercise,
    \[
      \boxed{\Gamma(\pi^0\to\gamma\gamma)
        =\frac{\alpha^2m_\pi^3}{64\pi^3f_\pi^2}.}
    \]
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{11}{The Two-Dimensional Goldstone Obstruction}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] After Wick rotation the action is
    \[
      S_E[X]=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^2\sigma_E\,(\partial X)^2
      =\frac12\int d^2\sigma_E\,
      X\left(-\frac{\nabla^2}{2\pi}\right)X .
    \]
    The inverse of the quadratic kernel therefore obeys
    \[
      -\frac{\nabla^2}{2\pi}G_2(z,z')=\delta^2(z-z'),
      \qquad
      \nabla^2G_2(z,z')=-2\pi\delta^2(z-z').
    \]
    Translation and rotation invariance make \(G_2\) a function of
    \(r=|z-z'|\).  Away from the origin the radial equation gives
    \(G_2=A\ln r+B\).  Integrating the Green-function equation over a small
    disk and using Gauss's law gives \(2\pi A=-2\pi\), so
    \[
      \boxed{G_2(z,z')=-\ln\frac{|z-z'|}{L}}
    \]
    for an arbitrary infrared reference length \(L\).  Equivalently, with
    an ultraviolet cutoff \(a\),
    \[
      \big\langle [X(z)-X(z')]^2\big\rangle
      =2\ln\frac{|z-z'|}{a}.
    \]

    \item[(b)] The logarithm does not approach zero at large separation.  A local
    insertion therefore produces a response that does not die away, and the
    fluctuation of the field difference grows without bound.  In the
    infinite-volume limit the zero mode cannot be localized about one
    chosen value \(x\), so the proposed vacua \(\ket{x}\) do not
    define distinct ordered phases.

    In \(D>2\) Euclidean dimensions the massless Green function behaves as
    \(r^{2-D}\), and hence a local disturbance decays.  This infrared test
    does not rule out an ordered vacuum there.  In \(D=1\), corresponding to
    quantum mechanics, the massless Green function grows linearly with
    separation, so the delocalization is even stronger.  Two dimensions are
    the logarithmic borderline.

    If the shift \(X\mapsto X+a\) were spontaneously broken, Goldstone's
    theorem would require precisely such a derivatively coupled massless
    field.  Its infrared fluctuations erase the assumed order parameter,
    contradicting the premise of distinct symmetry-breaking vacua.  Thus the
    free massless scalar theory can exist as a two-dimensional conformal
    field theory, but \(X\) cannot be the Goldstone boson of a spontaneously
    broken continuous shift symmetry in two dimensions.
  \end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
