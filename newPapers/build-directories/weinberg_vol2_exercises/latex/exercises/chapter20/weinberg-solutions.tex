\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Weinberg Exercises}
\ExerciseEditorialNote

\begin{enumerate}
  \WeinbergSolution{1}
  Write the Yukawa coupling as \(y\bar\psi\psi\phi\), and choose the
  expansion point to be the origin.  Canonical dimensions give
  \[
    [\bar\psi(x)\psi(0)]=3,\qquad
    [\phi]=1,\qquad [\partial_\mu]=1.
  \]
  Consequently a coefficient multiplying an operator of dimension \(d\)
  has dimension \(3-d\).  The complete basis capable of carrying a
  \emph{power} singularity is therefore
  \[
    \boxed{\mathbf{1},\qquad \phi,\qquad \phi^2,\qquad
      \partial_\mu\phi.}
  \]
  The vector coefficient of \(\partial_\mu\phi\) is built from \(x^\mu\).
  In a mass-independent scheme its leading short-distance forms have the
  schematic behavior
  \[
    C_{\mathbf{1}}=O(m_\psi/x^2),\qquad
    C_\phi=O(y/x^2),\qquad
    C_{\partial\phi}^{\mu}=O(yx^\mu/x^2).
  \]
  A coefficient of \(\phi^2\) is allowed by all generic symmetries.  In a
  massive theory it begins with a mass multiplying a logarithm (rather
  than a nonanalytic \(1/\sqrt{x^2}\)); it is absent in the strictly
  massless theory at the lowest orders.  At the boundary dimension
  \(d=3\), logarithmically singular coefficients can multiply
  \[
    \phi^3,\qquad \Box\phi,\qquad \bar\psi\psi.
  \]
  Operators proportional to equations of motion may be eliminated, and
  extra discrete symmetries can remove some entries.  This is why the
  power-counting list is best understood as the generic list before such
  reductions.

  A practical one-loop determination is a matching calculation.  Insert
  the bilocal product in amputated Green functions with zero, one, and two
  soft external scalar legs and, for the last operator, expand the
  one-scalar amplitude to first order in its soft momentum \(p\):
  \[
    \mathcal G(x;p_i)
      =\sum_O C_O(x,\mu)
        \langle p_i|O_R(0)|0\rangle_{\rm amp}.
  \]
  The zero-leg graph fixes \(C_{\mathbf{1}}\), the constant and linear terms
  of the one-leg graph fix \(C_\phi\) and
  \(C_{\partial\phi}^{\mu}\), and the two-leg graph fixes
  \(C_{\phi^2}\).  One includes the Yukawa and \(\phi^4\) one-loop graphs,
  wave-function and parameter counterterms, and the composite-operator
  mixing matrix.  Lower-dimensional contributions already determined are
  subtracted before solving for the next coefficient.  Matching at
  generic off-shell soft momenta avoids infrared singularities; the
  remaining \(x\)-dependence is the desired short-distance coefficient
  and is independent of the external states.

  \WeinbergSolution{2}
  Introduce chiral bilinears
  \[
    X_\alpha=\bar\psi_R\lambda_\alpha\psi_L,\qquad
    X_\alpha^\dagger=\bar\psi_L\lambda_\alpha\psi_R.
  \]
  Up to the harmless phase convention used to make the pseudoscalar
  density Hermitian,
  \[
    S_\alpha=X_\alpha+X_\alpha^\dagger,\qquad
    P_\alpha=i(X_\alpha-X_\alpha^\dagger).
  \]
  Hence the difference of scalar and pseudoscalar two-point functions is
  an order parameter for chiral symmetry: all chirally invariant
  perturbative terms, including the coefficient of \(\mathbf{1}\), cancel.
  The same cancellation removes gauge-field operators and
  non-derivative quark bilinears.  In the massless theory the first
  vacuum operator that can remain is a dimension-six four-quark
  operator.  Thus, at Euclidean momentum \(Q^2\),
  \[
    \Pi_{\alpha\beta}^{S}(Q^2)
      -\Pi_{\alpha\beta}^{P}(Q^2)
      =O(Q^{-4})
  \]
  up to logarithms.  The dispersion expansion
  \[
    \Pi^S-\Pi^P
      =\frac1{Q^2}\int_0^\infty ds\,(\rho^S-\rho^P)
       -\frac1{Q^4}\int_0^\infty ds\,s(\rho^S-\rho^P)
       +\cdots
  \]
  then gives the superconvergent sum rule
  \[
    \boxed{\int_0^\infty ds\,
      \left[\rho_{\alpha\beta}^{S}(s)
            -\rho_{\alpha\beta}^{P}(s)\right]=0.}
  \]
  The next moment is not forced to vanish; it is controlled by
  dimension-six chiral order parameters.

  Vector flavor symmetry of the vacuum implies
  \[
    \rho_{\alpha\beta}^{S,P}(s)
      =\delta_{\alpha\beta}\rho_{S,P}(s).
  \]
  Therefore the nontrivial statement is equivalently
  \[
    \int_0^\infty ds\,\bigl[\rho_S(s)-\rho_P(s)\bigr]=0.
  \]
  For any matrix \(c_{\alpha\beta}\), the corresponding rule is
  \[
    \int_0^\infty ds\,c_{\alpha\beta}
    \bigl[\rho_{\alpha\beta}^{S}(s)
          -\rho_{\alpha\beta}^{P}(s)\bigr]=0.
  \]
  If \(c_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{\alpha\beta}=0\), the combination vanishes
  pointwise by the unbroken \(SU(N)_V\), so those additional relations are
  group-theoretic identities rather than new dynamical sum rules.

  \WeinbergSolution{3}
  Let the struck parton have momentum \(P=xp\), mass
  \(m_i=xm_N\), and charge \(eQ_i\).  Crossing the Compton amplitude
  \(\gamma+\text{parton}\to\gamma+\text{parton}\) gives the one-photon
  exchange amplitude for
  \(e(k)+i(P)\to e(k')+i(P+q)\), with \(q=k-k'\).
  Using Eq.~\hyperref[eq:8.7.7]{(8.7.7)} for invariant phase space and
  Eq.~\hyperref[eq:8.7.38]{(8.7.38)} for the spin sum gives the elastic
  Dirac-target result
  \[
    \frac{d^2\sigma_i}{d\Omega\,d\nu}
      =\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_{\rm MOTT}
       Q_i^2
       \left(1+\frac{q^2}{2m_i^2}
         \tan^2\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
       \delta\!\left(\nu-\nu_i\right).
  \]
  The polarization-dependent part follows from the sum quoted below
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.6.8}; the terms independent of
  \(e\cdot e'\) give the first term, while the transverse spin-\(\tfrac12\)
  response gives the term proportional to
  \(\tan^2(\theta/2)\).

  The on-shell condition for the recoiling parton fixes
  \[
    0=(P+q)^2+m_i^2
      =q^2+2x\,p\cdot q,
    \qquad
    \nu_i=\frac{q^2}{2m_Nx}.
  \]
  Substituting \(m_i=xm_N\), weighting by the probability
  \(\mathcal F_i(x)\,dx\), and summing over partons yields
  \[
  \boxed{
    \frac{d^3\sigma}{d\Omega\,d\nu}
      =\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_{\rm MOTT}
       \sum_iQ_i^2\int_0^1dx\,\mathcal F_i(x)
       \left(1+\frac{q^2}{2m_N^2x^2}
         \tan^2\frac{\theta}{2}\right)
       \delta\!\left(\nu-\frac{q^2}{2m_Nx}\right).}
  \]
  This is Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.6.8}.  The dimensions also check: the delta
  function supplies the inverse energy required by
  \(d^3\sigma/(d\Omega\,d\nu)\).

  \WeinbergSolution{4}
  We classify local operators modulo total derivatives, the massless
  equations of motion, and the Bianchi identity.  A rank-\(s\) symmetric
  traceless tensor has twist four when its canonical dimension is
  \(s+4\).  A convenient spanning set consists of the following classes
  (flavor, color, and derivative-distribution labels are implicit):
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathcal O_{qF}^{\mu_1\ldots\mu_s}
      &={\rm ST}\,
        \bar\psi_f\gamma^\alpha
        F^A_{\alpha}{}^{\mu_1}t^A
        D^{\mu_2}\cdots D^{\mu_s}\psi_{f'},\\
    \mathcal O_{4q}^{\mu_1\ldots\mu_s}
      &={\rm ST}\,
        \bigl(\bar\psi_f\Gamma^{\mu_1}T\,
          D^{\mu_3}\cdots D^{\mu_r}\psi_{f'}\bigr)
        \bigl(\bar\psi_g\Gamma'^{\mu_2}T\,
          D^{\mu_{r+1}}\cdots D^{\mu_s}\psi_{g'}\bigr),\\
    \mathcal O_{3F}^{\mu_1\ldots\mu_s}
      &={\rm ST}\,
        {\rm Tr}\!\left(
        F_{\alpha}{}^{\mu_1}
        D^{\mu_2}\cdots D^{\mu_{s-1}}
        F_{\beta}{}^{\mu_s}F^{\alpha\beta}\right).
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Here \({\rm ST}\) symmetrizes the displayed free indices and removes
  traces; \(T=\mathbf{1}\) or \(t^A\) gives the independent color
  contractions, and the allowed \(\Gamma,\Gamma'\) and flavor tensors
  are restricted by the parity and charge-conjugation channel.  The
  derivative count in each line makes the dimension \(s+4\).

  One may initially also write two-field representatives
  \[
    {\rm ST}\,\bar\psi\gamma^{\mu_1}
      D^{\mu_2}\cdots D^{\mu_s}D^2\psi,\qquad
    {\rm ST}\,{\rm Tr}\!\left(
      F_{\alpha}{}^{\mu_1}
      D^{\mu_2}\cdots D^{\mu_s}D^2F^{\alpha\mu_s}\right),
  \]
  with the indices arranged to avoid duplication.  They are not new:
  \[
    (i\slashed D)^2=-D^2-\frac g2\sigma^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu},
    \qquad D_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=g\,\bar\psi\gamma^\nu t^A\psi\,t^A,
  \]
  together with commutators of covariant derivatives, reduces them to
  the \(qF\), \(4q\), and \(3F\) classes above.  Dual field strengths give
  the corresponding parity-odd (polarized) tensors.  Thus these three
  classes, with all independent color, flavor, and Lorentz contractions,
  are the gauge-invariant twist-four basis.

  \WeinbergSolution{5}
  The one-loop beta function of the four-dimensional scalar theory is
  \[
    \mu\frac{dg}{d\mu}=\beta_0g^2+\cdots,\qquad
    \beta_0=\frac{3}{16\pi^2}>0.
  \]
  A chain of logarithmic subgraphs produces integrals of the form
  \[
    \int_0^\infty dt\,e^{-pt}
      (\beta_0g\,t)^n
      =\frac{n!}{p^{n+1}}(\beta_0g)^n .
  \]
  Therefore the Borel transform has renormalon singularities on the real
  axis.  With the kernel \(\exp(-z/g)\) used in
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.7.3}, their locations have the form
  \[
    z=\pm\frac{p}{\beta_0}
      =\pm\frac{16\pi^2p}{3},
    \qquad p>0,
  \]
  where the allowed positive numbers \(p\) are fixed by the powers in the
  relevant short- and long-distance expansion.  Equivalently, an OPE
  correction of dimension \(d\) locates a singularity at the
  corresponding real value proportional to \(d/\beta_0\); a change in
  the normalization of the Borel variable rescales all these positions.

  Because \(\beta_0>0\), the axis assignment is the reverse of an
  asymptotically free theory: ultraviolet renormalons lie on the
  \emph{positive} real \(z\)-axis and obstruct the positive-\(g\) Borel
  integral, while infrared renormalons lie on the negative axis and give
  alternating signs.  In addition, the scalar instanton discussed in
  Section 20.7 gives the separate negative-axis singularity
  \(z=-16\pi^2\).  Thus the expected singularities are real-axis
  singularities (renormalon towers of both signs), not generic isolated
  complex poles, and the positive-axis ultraviolet tower is the
  non-Borel-summable one for \(g>0\).
\end{enumerate}
