\begin{enumerate}
  \SupplementarySolution{1}{Charges, Goldstone's Theorem, and the Abelian Higgs Mechanism}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] The canonical Noether construction gives
      \[
        [\phi_m(t,\mathbf x),Q_a]=(T_a)_{mn}\phi_n(t,\mathbf x),
      \]
      with an overall sign fixed by the convention
      \(\delta\phi=\ii\alpha[\,\phi,Q\,]\).  It follows directly by inserting
      \(J^0_a=\pi_m\,\ii(T_a)_{mn}\phi_n+\cdots\) and using the equal-time
      canonical brackets.
    \item[(b)] Generator \(a\) is broken if some local operator has
      \[
        \boxed{\bra{0}[Q_a,\mathcal O(0)]\ket{0}\ne0.}
      \]
      For the elementary scalar one may take
      \(\mathcal O=\phi_m\), so the order parameter is
      \((T_a)_{mn}\langle\phi_n\rangle\).  It vanishes for every operator in
      a symmetric vacuum.
    \item[(c)] Although \([H,Q_a]=0\), a broken charge does not act within
      a single vacuum sector: it changes the boundary condition at spatial
      infinity.  Consequently ordinary finite-energy particles need not
      fill degenerate group multiplets.  Formally,
      \[
        H Q_a\ket{0}=Q_aH\ket{0}=E_0Q_a\ket{0} .
      \]
      Thus the nonzero broken-charge state has zero excitation energy; in
      infinite volume it is represented by the zero-momentum limit of a
      collective excitation rather than a normalizable second vacuum
      vector.
    \item[(d)] Consider
      \(C^\mu(x)=\bra{0}[J^\mu_a(x),\mathcal O(0)]\ket{0}\).
      Its equal-time integral is the nonzero order parameter.  Inserting
      energy-momentum eigenstates and using current conservation shows that
      a contributing state satisfies
      \[
        \bra{0}J^\mu_a(0)\ket{\pi(p)}=if_a p^\mu,
        \qquad p^2=0.
      \]
      Equivalently, the Fourier transform contains
      \(f_a p^\mu/(p^2+\ii0)\).  A mass gap would make the spatial integral
      decay and contradict the nonzero equal-time charge commutator.
      Therefore every independent broken global generator produces a
      massless spin-zero mode, subject to the usual qualifications about
      Lorentz invariance and independent charge densities.
    \item[(e)] Up to a vacuum constant and the total derivative
      \(F\widetilde F\), the Lagrangian is
      \[
        \mathcal L=-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
          +|(\partial_\mu-\ii qA_\mu)\Phi|^2
          -m^2|\Phi|^2-\lambda|\Phi|^4 .
      \]
      Its physical parameters are \(q,m^2,\lambda\).  Stability requires
      \(\lambda>0\); spontaneous breaking occurs for \(m^2<0\), with
      \(v^2=-m^2/\lambda\) when
      \(\Phi=(v+h+\ii\chi)/\sqrt2\).  The potential becomes
      \[
        V=\frac{\lambda}{4}(h^2+2vh+\chi^2)^2 .
      \]
      In unitary gauge \(\chi=0\),
      \[
        m_h^2=2\lambda v^2,\qquad m_A^2=q^2v^2.
      \]
      The nonderivative interactions are
      \[
        \mathcal L_{\rm int}=
        \frac{m_A^2}{v}hA^2+\frac{m_A^2}{2v^2}h^2A^2
        -\frac{m_h^2}{2v}h^3-\frac{m_h^2}{8v^2}h^4 .
      \]
      Before breaking, the complex scalar and massless vector have
      \(2+2\) physical modes.  Afterwards they appear as one Higgs mode
      plus three polarizations of a massive vector.  The Goldstone field
      supplies the longitudinal polarization and is absent as a separate
      gauge-invariant particle.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{2}{Unitarity Gauge for General Higgsing}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    After applying \(g_\phi^{-1}\), the maximum occurs at the identity.
    Vary it along \(e^{\ii\epsilon T_a}\):
    \[
      0=\left.\frac \dd{\dd\epsilon}
      \operatorname{Re}\!\left[
        \widetilde\phi^\dagger e^{\ii\epsilon\tau_a}v\right]
      \right|_{\epsilon=0}
      =-\operatorname{Im}
        (\widetilde\phi^\dagger\tau_av).
    \]
    This proves the condition.  If \(\phi=v\), then unitarity of \(D(g)\)
    and Cauchy--Schwarz give
    \[
      \operatorname{Re}[v^\dagger D(g)v]\le v^\dagger v.
    \]
    The identity reaches the bound, so \(g_\phi=e\) is an allowed choice
    and \(\widetilde\phi=v\).  The same statement applies to the normalized
    vacuum \(v/\sqrt2\).
    \item[(b)]
    Because \(D(h)v=v\),
    \[
      f_\phi(g_\phi h)
        =\operatorname{Re}[\phi^\dagger D(g_\phi)D(h)v]
        =f_\phi(g_\phi).
    \]
    Thus unitary gauge fixes only \(G/H\); a local \(H\) transformation is
    residual.  Under it,
    \[
      \widetilde A_\mu\mapsto
        h^{-1}\widetilde A_\mu h+\ii h^{-1}\partial_\mu h .
    \]
    Since \(\mathfrak h^\perp\) is invariant under the adjoint action of
    \(H\), the \(\mathfrak h\) projection transforms as an \(H\) connection,
    including the inhomogeneous derivative term.  The
    \(\mathfrak h^\perp\) projection transforms homogeneously in the
    representation obtained by restricting the adjoint representation of
    \(G\) to \(H\).
    \item[(c)]
    Put \(\Theta=\theta^a\tau_a\).  The expansion is
    \[
      f_{\widetilde\phi}(e^{\ii\Theta})
      =\operatorname{Re}(\widetilde\phi^\dagger v)
       -\operatorname{Im}(\widetilde\phi^\dagger\Theta v)
       -\frac12\operatorname{Re}
          (\widetilde\phi^\dagger\Theta^2v)+O(\theta^3).
    \]
    The linear term vanishes by part~(a).  At
    \(\widetilde\phi=v\), the quadratic term is
    \[
      -\frac12v^\dagger\Theta^2v
        =-\frac12\|\Theta v\|^2.
    \]
    It is strictly negative in every broken direction.  Eigenvalues of the
    Hessian vary continuously, so this remains true for
    \(\widetilde\phi\) in a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(v\).
    A flat direction satisfies \(\Theta v=0\), which is precisely the
    infinitesimal stabilizer condition \(\Theta\in\mathfrak h\).  Hence the
    local maximum is unique after quotienting by \(H\), even though distant
    Gribov copies can obstruct a global slice.
    \item[(d)]
    In compact form the scalar part of the unitary-gauge Lagrangian is
    \[
      \mathcal L_{\rm scalar}
       =-\frac12\left[
         \partial_\mu h-\ii\tau_a\widetilde A_\mu^a(v+h)\right]^\dagger
        \left[
         \partial^\mu h-\ii\tau_b\widetilde A^{b\mu}(v+h)\right]
        -V\!\left(\frac{v+h}{\sqrt2}\right).
    \]
    The differentiated condition
    \(\operatorname{Im}[(v+h)^\dagger\tau_av]=0\) removes the mixing
    between \(\partial_\mu h\) and \(\widetilde A_\mu^a\) that is linear in
    \(v\).  The terms quadratic in the vector fields at \(h=0\) are
    \[
      -\frac14\widetilde A_\mu^a\widetilde A^{b\mu}
        v^\dagger\{\tau_a,\tau_b\}v.
    \]
    Substituting
    \(\widetilde A_\mu^a=g_a\widehat A_\mu^a\) and comparing with
    \(-\tfrac12(\mu^2)_{ab}\widehat A_\mu^a\widehat A^{b\mu}\) gives
    \[
      \boxed{(\mu^2)_{ab}
        =\frac{g_ag_b}{2}v^\dagger\{\tau_a,\tau_b\}v.}
    \]
    Hermiticity makes the matrix real and symmetry is manifest.  For any
    real coefficients \(c_a\),
    \[
      c_a(\mu^2)_{ab}c_b
        =\left\|\sum_a g_ac_a\tau_av\right\|^2\ge0.
    \]
    It vanishes exactly when
    \(\sum_ag_ac_a\tau_a\in\mathfrak h\).  The massless vectors are
    therefore precisely those of the unbroken subgroup.
    \item[(e)]
    Write
    \(\phi=\rho e^{\ii\vartheta}/\sqrt2\) and
    \(D(e^{\ii\alpha})=e^{\ii p\alpha}\).  Maximization requires
    \(p\alpha=\vartheta\pmod{2\pi}\), so one may take
    \[
      g_\phi=e^{\ii\vartheta/p},\qquad
      \widetilde\phi=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt2},\qquad
      \widetilde A_\mu=A_\mu-\frac1p\partial_\mu\vartheta.
    \]
    The \(p\) choices of the root differ by the residual group
    \(H=\mathbb Z_p\).  The mass formula gives
    \[
      \boxed{m_A^2=g^2p^2v^2.}
    \]
    This is the usual Abelian Higgs unitary gauge: the phase is removed,
    the radial mode remains, and the phase gradient becomes the
    longitudinal vector polarization.
    \item[(f)]
    Take
    \(\tau_i=\sigma_i/2\) for \(SU(2)\) and
    \(\tau_Y=-\mathbf1/2\).  The stationarity condition removes the three
    orbit directions, leaving
    \[
      \widetilde\phi=\frac1{\sqrt2}
        \begin{pmatrix}v+h\\0\end{pmatrix}
    \]
    with \(h\) real.  Direct evaluation of the anticommutators gives
    \[
      \boxed{\mu^2=\frac{v^2}{4}
      \begin{pmatrix}
        g_2^2&0&0&0\\
        0&g_2^2&0&0\\
        0&0&g_2^2&-g_1g_2\\
        0&0&-g_1g_2&g_1^2
      \end{pmatrix}.}
    \]
    The charged combinations
    \(W^\pm=(A^1\mp \ii A^2)/\sqrt2\) have
    \(m_W^2=g_2^2v^2/4\).  The neutral eigenvectors are
    \[
      Z_\mu=\frac{g_2A_\mu^3-g_1B_\mu}
        {\sqrt{g_1^2+g_2^2}},\qquad
      A_\mu^{\rm em}=\frac{g_1A_\mu^3+g_2B_\mu}
        {\sqrt{g_1^2+g_2^2}},
    \]
    with \(m_Z^2=(g_1^2+g_2^2)v^2/4\) and \(m_\gamma=0\).  The null
    generator is \(Q=T_3+Y\), which annihilates the stated vacuum.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{3}{Goldstone's Theorem and Fundamental \(SU(3)\) Higgsing}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    First consider the classical theory and write the scalar fields as
    real components.  Invariance of the potential under an infinitesimal
    transformation gives
    \[
      \frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi_i}
        (T^a)_{ij}\phi_j=0.
    \]
    Let \(v_i\) be a stationary vacuum and differentiate this identity
    with respect to \(\phi_k\).  At \(\phi=v\),
    \[
      (M^2)_{ki}(T^a)_{ij}v_j=0,\qquad
      (M^2)_{ki}
        =\left.\frac{\partial^2V}
          {\partial\phi_k\partial\phi_i}\right|_{\phi=v}.
    \]
    If generator \(a\) is broken, \(T^av\ne0\), so the Hessian has a
    zero eigenvector.  Each linearly independent broken orbit direction
    is therefore a massless classical normal mode.

    The quantum statement is encoded in the current--field correlator
    \[
      G^\mu_{ai}(p)=\ii\int \dd^4x\,e^{-\ii p\cdot x}
        \bra{0}T\{J^\mu_a(x)\phi_i(0)\}\ket{0} .
    \]
    The Ward identity following from \(\partial_\mu J^\mu_a=0\) is
    \[
      p_\mu G^\mu_{ai}(p)
        =\bra{0}[Q^a,\phi_i(0)]\ket{0}
        =-\ii(T^a)_{ij}v_j
    \]
    up to the common Fourier-sign convention.  Lorentz invariance makes
    the spin-zero contribution proportional to \(p^\mu\).  A nonzero
    right-hand side can then persist as \(p\to0\) only if
    \[
      G^\mu_{ai}(p)\supset
        \frac{p^\mu F_{ai}}{p^2-\ii0},
        \qquad F_{ai}\ne0.
    \]
    Equivalently, insertion of a complete set gives a state
    \(\ket{\pi^a(p)}\) with
    \(\bra{0}J^\mu_a(0)\ket{\pi^b(p)}
      =\ii p^\mu F_a{}^b\) and \(p^2=0\).  This is the quantum Goldstone
    boson.  The theorem assumes a global symmetry; after the symmetry is
    gauged this scalar need not remain in the physical spectrum.

    \item[(b)]
    Take Hermitian generators \(T^a=\lambda^a/2\), write
    \(A_\mu=A_\mu^aT^a\), and use
    \(D_\mu=\partial_\mu-igA_\mu\).  For
    \(\Phi'=U\Phi\), covariance \(D'_\mu\Phi'=UD_\mu\Phi\) requires
    \[
      \boxed{A'_\mu
        =UA_\mu U^{-1}+\frac{\ii}{g}U\partial_\mu U^{-1}.}
    \]
    Since
    \([D_\mu,D_\nu]=-igF_{\mu\nu}\), it follows immediately that
    \[
      F'_{\mu\nu}=UF_{\mu\nu}U^{-1}.
    \]
    Thus, apart from the topological \(\theta\)-term, the canonically
    normalized renormalisable Lagrangian is
    \[
      \mathcal L=-\frac12\operatorname{tr}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
        -(D_\mu\Phi)^\dagger D^\mu\Phi
        -V(\Phi^\dagger\Phi),
      \qquad
      V=\lambda\left(\Phi^\dagger\Phi-\frac{v^2}{2}\right)^2 ,
    \]
    where an additive constant is immaterial and
    \(\lambda>0\).  This is the same as
    \(m_0^2\Phi^\dagger\Phi+\lambda(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)^2\) in the broken
    range \(m_0^2<0\).

    An \(SU(3)\) transformation can rotate any vacuum to
    \[
      \Phi_{\mathrm v}=\frac{v}{\sqrt2}
        \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
    \]
    The generators \(T^{1,2,3}\), acting in the upper \(2\times2\)
    block, leave this vector fixed.  Hence
    \[
      \boxed{SU(3)\longrightarrow SU(2),}
    \]
    with five broken generators \(T^{4,5,6,7,8}\).  Before gauging, the
    five vectors \(T^a\Phi_{\mathrm v}\) are precisely the five
    zero-eigenvectors of the potential Hessian found in part~(a), so the
    classical Goldstone count is verified.

    To see the Higgs mechanism explicitly, write locally
    \[
      \Phi(x)=U(x)\frac1{\sqrt2}
        \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\v+h(x)\end{pmatrix},
      \qquad U(x)\in SU(3)/SU(2).
    \]
    A gauge transformation by \(U^{-1}\) removes the five fields in
    \(U(x)\).  The vacuum kinetic term then gives
    \[
      (M_A^2)_{ab}
        =g^2\Phi_{\mathrm v}^\dagger
          \{T^a,T^b\}\Phi_{\mathrm v}.
    \]
    In the Gell-Mann basis,
    \[
      m_{1,2,3}^2=0,\qquad
      m_{4,5,6,7}^2=\frac{g^2v^2}{4},\qquad
      m_8^2=\frac{g^2v^2}{3}.
    \]
    Each of the five Goldstone fields supplies the longitudinal
    polarization of one massive vector.  The only remaining scalar is
    the radial mode, with
    \[
      \boxed{m_h^2=2\lambda v^2.}
    \]
    The degree-of-freedom check is
    \(8\cdot2+6=3\cdot2+5\cdot3+1=22\).
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{4}{Neutrino Masses and a Hypercharge-One Scalar Triplet}
  With \(Q=T_3+Y\), one generation of chiral fermions is
  \[
    \begin{array}{c|c}
      \text{field}&SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\\ \hline
      Q_L=(u_L,d_L)&(\mathbf3,\mathbf2,+\tfrac16)\\
      u_R&(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,+\tfrac23)\\
      d_R&(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,-\tfrac13)\\
      L_L=(\nu_L,e_L)&(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,-\tfrac12)\\
      e_R&(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,-1)\\
      \nu_R&(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,0)
    \end{array}
  \]
  A Dirac bilinear joins a left-handed doublet to a right-handed singlet,
  so it is neither an \(SU(2)_L\) singlet nor, in general, neutral under
  hypercharge.  The exception among possible bare terms is a Majorana
  mass for the gauge singlet \(\nu_R\).

  A Higgs field \(H\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,+\tfrac12)\), together with
  \(\widetilde H=\ii\sigma_2H^*\), permits
  \[
    \mathcal L_Y=
      -\overline Q_LY_dHd_R-\overline Q_LY_u\widetilde Hu_R
      -\overline L_LY_eHe_R-\overline L_LY_\nu\widetilde H\nu_R
      +\mathrm{H.c.}
  \]
  Each term is a singlet of all three gauge factors.  With
  \(\langle H\rangle=(0,v/\sqrt2)^{\mathsf T}\), they give
  \(M_f=Y_fv/\sqrt2\).

  Since \(\nu_R\) is a gauge singlet, add
  \[
    \mathcal L_M=-\frac12\nu_R^{\mathsf T}CM_R\nu_R+\mathrm{H.c.}
  \]
  For one flavor the neutral-fermion mass matrix is
  \[
    \mathcal M_\nu=
      \begin{pmatrix}0&m_D\\m_D&M_R\end{pmatrix},
      \qquad m_D=\frac{Y_\nu v}{\sqrt2}.
  \]
  If \(M_R\gg m_D\), its eigenvalues are
  \[
    m_{\rm light}\simeq-\frac{m_D^2}{M_R},\qquad
    m_{\rm heavy}\simeq M_R.
  \]
  With flavors,
  \(M_{\rm light}=-M_D M_R^{-1}M_D^{\mathsf T}\).
  Integrating out \(\nu_R\), or omitting it from the low-energy theory,
  leaves the dimension-five Weinberg operator
  \[
    \mathcal L_5=
      \frac{c_{ij}}{\Lambda}
      (L_i^{\mathsf T}C\,\ii\sigma_2H)
      (H^{\mathsf T}\ii\sigma_2L_j)+\mathrm{H.c.},
      \qquad
      (m_\nu)_{ij}=\frac{c_{ij}v^2}{2\Lambda}
  \]
  in the displayed normalization.

  The triplet may be represented by a traceless matrix transforming as
  \(\phi\mapsto U\phi U^\dagger\).  Under \(T_3\), its upper-right,
  diagonal, and lower-left components have weights \(+1,0,-1\),
  respectively.  Since \(Y=+1\), their electric charges are
  \[
    \begin{array}{c|c|c}
      \text{component}&T_3&Q\\ \hline
      \phi^1-\ii\phi^2&+1&+2\\
      \phi^3&0&+1\\
      \phi^1+\ii\phi^2&-1&0
    \end{array}.
  \]
  Thus the neutral field is the lower-left component
  \[
    \phi_{21}=\frac12(\phi^1+\ii\phi^2).
  \]

  The lepton-triplet Yukawa interaction is
  \[
    \boxed{\mathcal L_\phi
      =-\frac12y_{ij}L_i^{\mathsf T}C\,\ii\sigma_2\phi L_j
        +\mathrm{H.c.},}
  \]
  where \(y_{ij}=y_{ji}\).  The two lepton doublets have total
  hypercharge \(-1\), cancelled by \(Y_\phi=+1\), while
  \(\ii\sigma_2\) supplies the invariant \(SU(2)\) tensor.  The
  neutrino-neutrino term is
  \[
    \mathcal L_\phi\supset
      -\frac12y_{ij}\phi_{21}\,
        \nu_{Li}^{\mathsf T}C\nu_{Lj}+\mathrm{H.c.}
  \]
  Consequently a neutral condensate
  \(u=\langle\phi_{21}\rangle\) gives the left-handed Majorana mass
  \[
    \boxed{(m_\nu)_{ij}=y_{ij}u.}
  \]

  \SupplementarySolution{5}{The Abelian Higgs Model and a Two-Scalar Vacuum}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)]
    The parent action is written with a mostly-minus metric.  Its physical
    masses are signature independent; in this volume's mostly-plus
    convention the scalar kinetic term carries the corresponding minus
    sign.  Write, in unitary gauge,
    \[
      \phi=v+\frac{h}{\sqrt2}.
    \]
    Then the quadratic terms are
    \[
      \mathcal L^{(2)}
        =-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
         +\frac12m_A^2A_\mu A^\mu
         +\frac12\partial_\mu h\,\partial^\mu h
         -\frac12m_h^2h^2
    \]
    in the source signature, with
    \[
      \boxed{m_A^2=2e^2v^2,\qquad m_h^2=2\lambda v^2.}
    \]
    The phase of \(\phi\) is eaten.  The spectrum is therefore one
    massive spin-one particle with three polarizations and one massive
    spin-zero particle.

    \item[(ii)]
    Varying the action with respect to \(A_\nu\) gives
    \[
      \partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=J^\nu,\qquad
      \boxed{J^\nu=-ie\left[
        \phi^\dagger D^\nu\phi-(D^\nu\phi)^\dagger\phi\right].}
    \]
    In a region with fixed modulus write \(\phi=ve^{i\theta}\).  Then
    \[
      \mathbf J=2ev^2(\boldsymbol\nabla\theta-e\mathbf A),
      \qquad
      \boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf J=-2e^2v^2\mathbf B.
    \]
    For static fields, Maxwell's equation
    \(\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mathbf J\), together with
    \(\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\), implies
    \[
      -\nabla^2\mathbf B
        =\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf J
        =-2e^2v^2\mathbf B.
    \]
    Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf B=\frac1{\xi^2}\mathbf B,\qquad
        \xi=\frac1{\sqrt2\,|e|v}=\frac1{m_A}.}
    \]
    A field applied at a planar surface decays as
    \(B(x)\propto e^{-x/\xi}\).  This is the Meissner effect, and
    \(\xi\) here is the magnetic penetration depth.

    \item[(iii)]
    Assemble the scalars into
    \(\boldsymbol\phi=(\phi_1,\phi_2)^{\mathsf T}\).  Besides the local
    common-phase \(U(1)\), the action has a global flavor
    \(SU(2)_F\) symmetry
    \(\boldsymbol\phi\mapsto U_F\boldsymbol\phi\).  The central phase of
    \(U(2)\) is already the constant part of the gauged \(U(1)\), so the
    faithful physical global symmetry is \(SU(2)_F\), up to a discrete
    quotient.

    Choose
    \[
      \langle\boldsymbol\phi\rangle=
        \begin{pmatrix}v\\0\end{pmatrix}.
    \]
    A global rotation generated by
    \(T_3=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,-1/2)\) multiplies the vacuum by
    \(e^{\ii\beta/2}\), which can be cancelled by a gauge rotation.
    Hence the vacuum preserves the diagonal combination generated by
    \[
      \boxed{T_3-\frac12Q_{\rm gauge}.}
    \]
    Three generators of \(SU(2)_F\times U(1)_{\rm gauge}\) move the
    vacuum.  One is a gauge direction and supplies the longitudinal mode
    of \(A_\mu\); the other two give physical Goldstone bosons.

    Expanding about this vacuum gives
    \[
      \boxed{m_A^2=2e^2v^2,\qquad
        m_h^2=2\lambda v^2,}
    \]
    together with two massless real scalars, the two components of
    \(\phi_2\).  Thus the spectrum contains one massive vector, one
    massive radial scalar, and two massless scalars.

    Before quotienting by gauge transformations the minima form
    \[
      |\phi_1|^2+|\phi_2|^2=v^2,\qquad \mathcal V\simeq S^3.
    \]
    The gauged common phase acts along the Hopf fibers, so the
    gauge-inequivalent vacuum manifold is
    \[
      \boxed{\mathcal M_{\rm vac}
        =S^3/U(1)\simeq\mathbb{CP}^1\simeq S^2.}
    \]
    Its two tangent directions are the two physical Goldstone fields
    found above.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{6}{Precision Electroweak Widths and Loop Diagnostics}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Squaring the neutral-current amplitude, summing final spins and colors,
    and using the two-body massless phase space gives
    \[
      \Gamma(Z\to f\bar f)
        =N_c\frac{g^2m_Z}{48\pi\cos^2\theta_W}
         [(g_V^f)^2+(g_A^f)^2].
    \]
    The relations
    \(g^2/\cos^2\theta_W=4m_Z^2/v^2\) and
    \(v^{-2}=\sqrt2G_F\) yield
    \[
      \boxed{\Gamma
        =N_c\frac{G_Fm_Z^3}{6\pi\sqrt2}
         [(g_V^f)^2+(g_A^f)^2].}
    \]
    For a charged lepton,
    \(g_A=-1/2\) while \(g_V=-1/2+2s_W^2\) is close to zero.
    The interaction is therefore dominantly axial and strongly violates
    parity.
    \item[(b)]
    Put \(m_1=m+\delta m/2\) and \(m_2=m-\delta m/2\).  At
    \(\delta m=0\), the logarithmic term has the limiting value
    \(2m^2\), which cancels \(m_1^2+m_2^2=2m^2\).  Expanding the logarithm
    and the rational prefactor gives
    \[
      \boxed{\Delta m^2
        =\frac43(\delta m)^2
         +O\!\left(\frac{(\delta m)^4}{m^2}\right).}
    \]
    Thus an exactly degenerate weak doublet respects custodial symmetry
    and does not contribute to \(T\), whereas the leading violation is
    positive and quadratic in the mass splitting, multiplied by \(N_c\)
    in the full self-energy correction.
    \item[(c)]
    A magnetic dipole operator flips chirality, so the amplitude supplies
    one lepton mass.  Its conventional normalization contains another,
    while the heavy weak propagator supplies \(1/m_W^2\).  Consequently
    \(a_\ell^{\rm EW}\propto G_Fm_\ell^2\).

    The bracket is flavor-independent at this accuracy, so
    \[
      \frac{a_\mu^{\rm EW}}{a_e^{\rm EW}}
        =\left(\frac{m_\mu}{m_e}\right)^2
        \simeq(206.77)^2
        \simeq\boxed{4.28\times10^4}.
    \]
    This mass enhancement is why weak and hadronic effects are far more
    visible in the muon anomaly than in the electron anomaly.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{7}{A Standard-Model Family inside \(SU(5)\)}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    The decompositions are
    \[
      \boxed{\overline{\mathbf5}
        =(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf{1},+\tfrac13)
         \oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf2,-\tfrac12)}
    \]
    and
    \[
      \boxed{\mathbf{10}
        =(\mathbf3,\mathbf2,+\tfrac16)
         \oplus(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf{1},-\tfrac23)
         \oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1},+1).}
    \]
    The first contains \(d^c\) and \(L=(\nu,e)_L\).  The antisymmetric
    \(\mathbf{10}\) contains \(Q=(u,d)_L\), \(u^c\), and \(e^c\).
    The dimensions \(5+10=15\) equal the number of left-handed Weyl fields
    in one family without a right-handed neutrino.
    \item[(b)]
    In the fundamental,
    \[
      \operatorname{Tr}Y^2
        =3\left(\frac13\right)^2
         +2\left(\frac12\right)^2
        =\frac56.
    \]
    A conventionally normalized \(SU(5)\) generator has trace square
    \(1/2\), so
    \[
      T_Y=\sqrt{\frac35}\,Y.
    \]
    Equality of the covariant derivative,
    \(g_1T_Y=g'Y\), then requires
    \[
      \boxed{g_1=\sqrt{\frac53}\,g',
        \qquad \alpha_1=\frac53\alpha_Y.}
    \]
    The factor is a generator normalization, not an additional physical
    interaction.
    \item[(c)]
    Set \(A_i=\alpha_i^{-1}(\mu)\) and
    \(L=\ln(M_U/\mu)\).  Subtraction eliminates the unified coupling:
    \[
      A_i-A_j=\frac{b_i-b_j}{2\pi}L.
    \]
    Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{L=\frac{2\pi(A_i-A_j)}{b_i-b_j},\qquad
        \alpha_U^{-1}
         =\frac{b_iA_j-b_jA_i}{b_i-b_j}.}
    \]
    The third coupling must satisfy
    \(A_k=\alpha_U^{-1}+b_kL/(2\pi)\).  Failure of this equality measures
    the need for thresholds, additional matter, higher-loop corrections,
    or the failure of the proposed unification model.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{8}{Unified Multiplets, Proton Decay, and Thresholds}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Only coefficient differences enter
    \[
      \alpha_i^{-1}(\mu)-\alpha_j^{-1}(\mu)
        =\frac{b_i-b_j}{2\pi}\ln\frac{M_U}{\mu}.
    \]
    If every \(b_i\) receives the same shift \(\Delta b\), these
    differences and the extracted \(M_U\) are unchanged.  Each individual
    equation does change:
    \[
      \alpha_U^{-1}\longrightarrow
      \alpha_U^{-1}
        -\frac{\Delta b}{2\pi}\ln\frac{M_U}{\mu}
    \]
    for fixed low-energy couplings in this sign convention.
    A degenerate complete representation of the unified group has the same
    total index in every subgroup after proper normalization, and hence
    supplies precisely such a common shift.  Split or incomplete
    multiplets need not.
    \item[(b)]
    Tree-level \(X\) exchange gives
    \[
      \mathcal L_{\rm eff}
        \sim\frac{g_U^2}{M_X^2}(qq)(q\ell).
    \]
    The coefficient has dimension \(-2\).  A proton-decay amplitude is
    therefore of order \(g_U^2m_p^2/M_X^2\), apart from dimensionless
    group factors and a hadronic matrix element.  Multiplying its square
    by the one-body decay scale \(m_p\) gives
    \[
      \boxed{\Gamma_p\sim
        \frac{\alpha_U^2m_p^5}{M_X^4},\qquad
        \tau_p\sim\frac{M_X^4}{\alpha_U^2m_p^5}.}
    \]
    The fourth power makes nucleon lifetime predictions exceptionally
    sensitive to the heavy gauge-boson mass.
    \item[(c)]
    With thresholds,
    \[
      A_i-A_j
        =\frac{b_i-b_j}{2\pi}L+(\delta_i-\delta_j).
    \]
    Thus
    \[
      L=\frac{2\pi[(A_i-A_j)-(\delta_i-\delta_j)]}{b_i-b_j}.
    \]
    Nonuniversal differences \(\delta_i-\delta_j\) move the inferred
    meeting scale and can change whether the third coupling meets the
    first two.  Once \(L\) is fixed, any one equation gives
    \(\alpha_U^{-1}=A_i-b_iL/(2\pi)-\delta_i\); a common part of all
    \(\delta_i\) therefore shifts only the unified coupling.

    For a degenerate complete multiplet,
    \(b_1^{(r)}=b_2^{(r)}=b_3^{(r)}\) after summing its components, and all
    masses give the same logarithm.  Its threshold is common and cannot
    alter coupling differences, so it cannot repair a mismatch.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{9}{\(R_\xi\)-Gauge Feynman Rules for the Abelian Higgs Model}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Define
    \[
      \mu=\sqrt2\,q\phi_0,\qquad
      m_\zeta^2=\lambda\phi_0^2=2m^2.

    \[
      \begin{split}
      \mathcal L={}&-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
       -\frac12(\partial\zeta)^2-\frac12(\partial\chi)^2
       -\frac12\mu^2A_\mu A^\mu-\frac12m_\zeta^2\zeta^2
       \mu A^\mu\partial_\mu\chi\\
      &+qA^\mu(\zeta\partial_\mu\chi-\chi\partial_\mu\zeta)
       -q\mu\,\zeta A_\mu A^\mu
       -\frac{q^2}{2}A_\mu A^\mu(\zeta^2+\chi^2)\\
      &-\frac{\lambda\phi_0}{2\sqrt2}\,

       -\frac{\lambda}{16}(\zeta^2+\chi^2)^2 .
      \end{split}

    The term
    \(\mu A^\mu\partial_\mu\chi\) is the announced quadratic mixing.

    \item[(b)]

      \mathcal L_{\rm gf}
       =-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial\cdot A)^2
        +\mu\chi\,\partial\cdot A
        -\frac12\xi\mu^2\chi^2.
    \]
    Integrating the middle term by parts gives
    \(-\mu A^\mu\partial_\mu\chi\), exactly cancelling the mixing.

    There is a qualification to the printed Faddeev--Popov instruction.
    Under the full infinitesimal gauge transformation
    \[
      \delta A_\mu=\partial_\mu\alpha,\qquad
      \delta\zeta=-q\alpha\chi,\qquad
      \delta\chi=q\alpha(\sqrt2\phi_0+\zeta),

    the gauge functional \(G=\partial\cdot A-\xi\mu\chi\) varies as
    \[
      \delta G=
        \left(\partial^2-\xi\mu^2-\xi q\mu\zeta\right)\alpha.

    \(\zeta\) term.  The determinant is then field independent, so it can
    indeed be omitted when finding the free propagators.  Under the exact
    nonlinear transformation, however, the determinant is field

      \mathcal L_{\rm gh}\ \sim\
        \bar c(\partial^2-\xi\mu^2-\xi q\mu\zeta)c

    and hence a ghost--antighost--\(\zeta\) vertex.  Thus the source's
    field-independence claim is correct for its linearized free-field

    transformation.

    \item[(c)]

      \boxed{

        \frac{-\ii}{p^2+\mu^2-\ii0}
        \left[
          \eta_{\mu\nu}
          -(1-\xi)\frac{p_\mu p_\nu}
            {p^2+\xi\mu^2-\ii0}

      \boxed{

    propagator is proportional to \(\eta_{\mu\nu}\), while

  \SupplementarySolution{10}{Diagrammatic Understanding of the BCS Instability}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Write the momentum of a low-energy fermion as
    \(\mathbf k=\mathbf k_F+\widehat{\mathbf n}\,\ell\), where only the normal displacement
    \(\ell\) and frequency scale toward zero.  A generic four-fermion
    process cannot keep all four legs in the shrinking shell.  The
    exceptional channels are forward scattering and, for the pairing
    instability, Cooper scattering
    \[
      (\mathbf k,-\mathbf k)\longrightarrow(\mathbf k',-\mathbf k').
    \]
    Its total momentum remains zero for every point on the Fermi surface,
    so each pair bubble has an unconstrained angular integral and a
    logarithmic radial integral.  A chain with \(n\) bubbles therefore
    contributes
    \[
      V^{n+1}\bigl[\nu_F\log(E_D/E)\bigr]^n.
    \]
    Diagrams in which the loops overlap impose additional momentum
    constraints and lose at least one independent Cooper logarithm.
    Thus the leading infrared terms are the cactus diagrams: a sequence
    of Cooper bubbles attached at articulation points, precisely the
    topology drawn in the question.

    \item[(b)]
    A bubble insertion in a single-fermion line produces an analytic
    self-energy near the Fermi surface,
    \[
      \Sigma(\ii\omega,\ell)
        =\Sigma(0,0)+(1-Z^{-1})\ii\omega
          +\delta v_F\,\ell+\cdots .
    \]
    The constant term shifts the chemical potential and hence the
    location of the Fermi surface; the other displayed terms merely
    replace the residue and Fermi velocity by their renormalized values.
    No Cooper logarithm is generated in this one-particle correction.
    Once \(\nu_F\) and \(v_F\) are understood as physical low-energy
    parameters, using the corrected line changes only nonsingular
    prefactors.  It is therefore consistent to use the free quasiparticle
    propagator in the leading-log cactus sum.

    \item[(c)]
    Let
    \[
      G(\ii\omega,\mathbf k)=\frac1{\ii\omega-\xi_{\mathbf k}},
      \qquad \xi_{-\mathbf k}=\xi_{\mathbf k},
    \]
    and define the one-loop zero-momentum pair kernel
    \[
      K(\ii\Omega)=
        \int_{\mathbf k,\omega}
        G(\ii\omega,\mathbf k)G(\ii\Omega-\ii\omega,-\mathbf k),
      \qquad
      \int_{\mathbf k,\omega}\equiv
        \int\frac{\dd^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{\dd\omega}{2\pi}.
    \]
    The spin-singlet tensors at the external legs factor out.  With the
    interaction convention
    \(H_{\rm int}=V\Phi^\dagger\Phi\), successive rungs alternate in sign:
    \[
      \chi=K-KVK+KVKVK-\cdots
        =\boxed{\frac{K}{1+VK}}.
    \]
    A different sign convention for the interaction moves both minus
    signs simultaneously; the invariant pole condition for an attractive
    \(V\) is \(1-|V|K=0\).

    \item[(d)]
    The frequency integral, done by closing the contour around the
    occupied pole, gives at zero temperature
    \[
      K(\ii\Omega)=
        \int_{\mathbf k}
        \frac{1-2n_F(\xi_{\mathbf k})}{2\xi_{\mathbf k}-\ii\Omega}.
    \]
    For a round Fermi surface the density of states per spin is
    \[
      \nu_F=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(S^{d-1})k_F^{d-1}}
        {(2\pi)^dv_F},
    \]
    so the symmetric Debye shell yields
    \[
      \begin{split}
      K(\ii\Omega)
       &=\nu_F\int_0^{E_D}\dd\xi
          \left(\frac1{2\xi-\ii\Omega}
                +\frac1{2\xi+\ii\Omega}\right)\\
       &=\nu_F\log
          \frac{\sqrt{\Omega^2+4E_D^2}}{|\Omega|}
        \simeq \nu_F\log\frac{2E_D}{|\Omega|}
        \quad (|\Omega|\ll E_D).
      \end{split}
    \]
    This logarithm is the infrared enhancement of every cactus bubble.

    \item[(e)]
    For \(V=-|V|\), the denominator of the susceptibility vanishes when
    \[
      1=|V|\nu_F
        \log\frac{\sqrt{\Omega_*^2+4E_D^2}}{|\Omega_*|}.
    \]
    Hence
    \[
      \boxed{|\Omega_*|
       =\frac{2E_D}
        {\sqrt{\exp[2/(|V|\nu_F)]-1}}
       \simeq2E_D e^{-1/(|V|\nu_F)}.}
    \]
    After analytic continuation this is an instability pole of the
    assumed normal Fermi-liquid state, rather than a stable
    positive-energy particle pole.  An arbitrarily weak attraction makes
    the pair channel grow and drives condensation.

    The leading Cooper-channel renormalization group equation is
    \[
      \frac{\dd V}{\dd\ell}=-\nu_FV^2,\qquad
      V(\ell)=\frac{V}
        {1+\nu_FV\ell}.
    \]
    For \(V<0\) it diverges at
    \(\ell_*=1/(|V|\nu_F)\), giving
    \[
      E_{\rm BCS}\sim E_D e^{-1/(|V|\nu_F)}.
    \]
    Thus the susceptibility pole and the RG strong-coupling scale agree,
    up to the cutoff-dependent factor \(2\).

    \item[(f)]
    Solving the supplied Schrödinger equation for \(a_{\mathbf k}\) gives
    \[
      a_{\mathbf k}=
        \frac{Vw_{\mathbf k}C}{\omega-2\epsilon_{\mathbf k}}.
    \]
    Multiplication by \(w_{\mathbf k}^*\) and summation produces
    \(C=VC\sum_{\mathbf k}|w_{\mathbf k}|^2/
      (\omega-2\epsilon_{\mathbf k})\).  A nonzero state therefore obeys
    exactly the displayed condition (1).

    Measure energy from the Fermi surface and write a bound-state energy
    as \(\omega=-E_B\), \(E_B>0\).  With constant \(\nu_F\) in the Debye
    shell and \(V=-|V|\), condition (1) becomes
    \[
      1=\frac{|V|\nu_F}{2}
        \log\left(1+\frac{2E_D}{E_B}\right).
    \]
    Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{E_B=
        \frac{2E_D}{\exp[2/(|V|\nu_F)]-1}
        \simeq2E_D e^{-2/(|V|\nu_F)},\qquad
        \omega=-E_B.}
    \]
    The wavefunction is
    \(a_{\mathbf k}\propto
      w_{\mathbf k}/(E_B+2\epsilon_{\mathbf k})\), and it is a genuine
    two-particle bound state below the continuum built on \(\ket{F}\).

    The Cooper calculation uses only empty particle states
    \(0<\epsilon<E_D\), whereas the many-body pair susceptibility obtains
    equal logarithmic contributions from particles and holes on the two
    sides of the Fermi surface.  That doubles the logarithm and changes
    the exponent from \(2/(|V|\nu_F)\) to \(1/(|V|\nu_F)\).  Both results
    demonstrate Cooper's central conclusion: an arbitrarily weak
    attraction is nonperturbatively important.  The parent assignment's
    reference to (14) is the source typo already noted in the exercise;
    the comparison is with its displayed condition (1).
  \end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
