\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Weinberg Exercises}
\ExerciseEditorialNote

\begin{enumerate}
  \WeinbergSolution{1}
  To leading order in \(m_s\), the state called \(\eta\) in the problem
  is the octet Goldstone boson \(\eta_8\).  Singlet--octet mixing changes
  its two-photon amplitude only at relative order \(m_s\).  Use the
  axial generator
  \[
    \lambda_8=\frac1{\sqrt3}\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)
  \]
  in the Fujikawa formula.  With
  \(Q=e\,\operatorname{diag}(2/3,-1/3,-1/3)\),
  \[
    \operatorname{tr}(Q^2\lambda_8)
      =\frac{N_ce^2}{3\sqrt3}.
  \]
  This is \(1/\sqrt3\) times the corresponding trace for the neutral
  pion generator.  At this order the octet decay constants are equal,
  so comparison with Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.2.37} gives
  \[
    \mathcal L_{\eta\gamma\gamma}
      =-\frac{N_ce^2}{48\sqrt3\,\pi^2F_\pi}\,
        \eta_8\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
        F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma},
    \qquad
    g_\eta=\frac{N_ce^2}{48\sqrt3\,\pi^2F_\pi}.
  \]
  The leading-order mass relation is
  \[
    m_\eta^2=\frac43 B_0m_s=\frac43m_K^2
    \qquad (m_u=m_d=0).
  \]
  Therefore Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.1.2} yields
  \[
    \boxed{\Gamma(\eta\to\gamma\gamma)
      =\frac{N_c^2\alpha^2m_\eta^3}
             {432\pi^3F_\pi^2}
      +O(m_s^{5/2}).}
  \]
  Since \(m_\eta^3=O(m_s^{3/2})\), this is the leading nonzero rate.

  \WeinbergSolution{2}
  Normalize the cubic anomaly coefficient by
  \(A(\mathbf3)=1\), so that
  \[
    \operatorname{tr}_{R}
      \bigl(T^a\{T^b,T^c\}\bigr)
      =A(R)\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}
      \bigl(t^a\{t^b,t^c\}\bigr).
  \]
  The \(N\) left-handed fundamentals therefore contribute \(N\).

  It remains to find the coefficient of the symmetric tensor
  \(\mathbf6\).  Cubic anomalies are additive under direct sums and obey
  \[
    A(R\otimes S)=d(S)A(R)+d(R)A(S).
  \]
  Since
  \(\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3=\mathbf6\oplus\overline{\mathbf3}\),
  \[
    6=A(\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3)
      =A(\mathbf6)+A(\overline{\mathbf3})
      =A(\mathbf6)-1,
  \]
  and hence \(A(\mathbf6)=7\).  A right-handed \(\mathbf6\) is counted
  as a left-handed \(\overline{\mathbf6}\), whose coefficient is
  \(-7\).  Thus the total anomaly is
  \[
    \boxed{D^{abc}
      =(N-7M)\,
       \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}
       \bigl(t^a\{t^b,t^c\}\bigr).}
  \]
  In particular, the chiral \(SU(3)\) symmetry is anomaly-free precisely
  when \(N=7M\).

  \WeinbergSolution{3}
  In the notation of Section 22.5 the color group is \(SU(3)\), so set
  \(N=3\).  For \(n=4\), let
  \(\ell_a,\ldots,\ell_g\) denote the net helicity multiplicities of the
  representations (a)--(g) listed there.  Equations
  \eqref{eq:22.5.7} and \eqref{eq:22.5.8} become
  \[
    35\ell_a-\ell_b+7\ell_c+32\ell_d
      +40\ell_f+24\ell_g=3,
  \]
  \[
    21\ell_a+\ell_b+13\ell_c+24\ell_d
      +8\ell_e+10\ell_f+6\ell_g=1.
  \]
  One explicit integral solution is
  \[
    \boxed{(\ell_a,\ell_b,\ell_c,\ell_d,\ell_e,\ell_f,\ell_g)
      =(-1,1,1,1,-2,0,0).}
  \]
  Indeed the two left-hand sides are respectively
  \(-35-1+7+32=3\) and
  \(-21+1+13+24-16=1\).  Negative values simply mean an excess of the
  opposite helicity, as in the definition of \(\ell\).

  For \(n=2\), the cubic \(SU(2)^3\) equation
  \eqref{eq:22.5.5} vanishes identically.  Measure quadratic indices in
  units of the doublet index.  The representation
  \((\mathbf2,\mathbf{1},k=1)\) contributes \(1\) to
  \eqref{eq:22.5.6}, whereas
  \((\mathbf2,\mathbf3,k=1)\) contributes
  \(d_{\mathbf3}=3\).  Both can be formed from three elementary
  doublets: the first from three left-handed constituents, and the
  second from one left-handed and two right-handed constituents.
  Consequently a solution different from the one in the text is
  \[
    \boxed{\ell(\mathbf2,\mathbf{1},1)=-2,\qquad
      \ell(\mathbf2,\mathbf3,1)=1,}
  \]
  with every other \(\ell\) zero.  Its mixed anomaly is
  \(-2+3=1\), exactly the right-hand side of
  \eqref{eq:22.5.6}.

  \WeinbergSolution{4}
  Keep the antifields \(K_n\) external and define
  \[
    Z[J,K]=\int[d\chi]\,
      \exp\left\{\ii S[\chi,K]+\ii\int \dd^4x\,J_n\chi^n\right\}.
  \]
  The useful BV identity is
  \[
    \Delta e^{\ii S}
      =\left(\ii\Delta S-\frac12(S,S)\right)e^{\ii S}.
  \]
  Thus the quantum master equation
  \((S,S)-2\ii\Delta S=0\) says exactly that the action and the
  Jacobian of the field-space transformation cancel in the functional
  integral.  Apply \(\Delta\) to the full integrand and integrate the
  \(\chi\) derivative by parts.  The quantum-master terms cancel,
  leaving the Ward identity
  \[
    \int \dd^4x\,J_n(x)
      \left\langle
        \frac{\delta_R S}{\delta K_n(x)}
      \right\rangle_{J,K}=0.
  \]
  No assumption that \(\Delta S\) vanishes has been made.

  Let \(W=-\ii\ln Z\), set
  \(\chi^n=\delta_R W/\delta J_n\), and Legendre transform only in
  \(J\):
  \[
    \Gamma[\chi,K]=W[J,K]-\int \dd^4x\,J_n\chi^n.
  \]
  At fixed \(\chi\),
  \[
    \frac{\delta_R\Gamma}{\delta K_n}
      =\frac{\delta_R W}{\delta K_n}
      =\left\langle\frac{\delta_R S}{\delta K_n}\right\rangle,
    \qquad
    J_n=-\frac{\delta_L\Gamma}{\delta\chi^n}.
  \]
  Substitution in the Ward identity gives
  \[
    \int \dd^4x\,
      \frac{\delta_R\Gamma}{\delta K_n(x)}
      \frac{\delta_L\Gamma}{\delta\chi^n(x)}=0.
  \]
  In antibracket notation this is
  \[
    \boxed{(\Gamma,\Gamma)=0,}
  \]
  the Zinn--Justin equation \eqref{eq:17.1.10}.  The role of
  \(-2\ii\Delta S\) in the quantum master equation is precisely to include
  the non-invariance of the measure that would otherwise spoil this
  derivation.
\end{enumerate}
