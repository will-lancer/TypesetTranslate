\begin{enumerate}
  \SupplementarySolution{1}{Fujikawa's Jacobian, Heat Kernel, and Index}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Let
    \(M_{mn}=\int d^4x\,\phi_m^\dagger U\phi_n\).  The transformed
    coefficients obey \(a'_m=M_{mn}a_n\), and the barred coefficients
    transform with the corresponding transpose matrix.  A Grassmann
    differential transforms inversely, so
    \[
      \prod_m da'_m=(\det M)^{-1}\prod_m da_m,\qquad
      \prod_m d\bar b'_m=(\det M)^{-1}\prod_m d\bar b_m.
    \]
    Hence
    \[
      [d\psi'][d\bar\psi']=(\det M)^{-2}[d\psi][d\bar\psi].
    \]
    For \(U=1+i\alpha\gamma_5T\),
    \[
      \ln J=-2\operatorname{Tr}\ln M
        =-2i\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha\gamma_5T)+O(\alpha^2).
    \]
    In a finite basis this is an ordinary trace.  In field theory it
    contains the undefined coincidence limit
    \(\delta^4(x-x)\).  The formal spin trace vanishes while the mode sum
    diverges, so their product cannot be assigned a value before a
    symmetry-preserving regulator is specified.
    \item[(b)]
    At short distance the diagonal heat kernel supplies an overall factor
    of order \(M^4\).  Expanding the exponential gives
    \[
      e^{-\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2}
        =e^{-D^2/M^2}
         \left[
          1-\frac{E}{M^2}
          +\frac{E^2}{2M^4}+\cdots
         \right],
      \qquad
      E=\frac14[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]F_{\mu\nu},
    \]
    up to derivative terms that are suppressed or combine covariantly.
    The \(M^4\) term contains
    \(\operatorname{tr}_D\gamma_5=0\).  The possible \(M^2\) term
    contains only two gamma matrices and vanishes because
    \(\operatorname{tr}_D(\gamma_5\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu)=0\).

    The \(E^2\) term contains four gamma matrices and is finite:
    \[
      \operatorname{tr}_D\!
        \left(\gamma_5
         [\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]
         [\gamma^\rho,\gamma^\sigma]\right)
        \propto\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
    \]
    Therefore
    \[
      \lim_{M\to\infty}\mathcal A_M(x)
        \propto
        \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
        \operatorname{tr}
         \bigl(TF_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}\bigr).
    \]
    Dimensional analysis also shows why this is the unique finite local
    term: two field strengths have dimension four and exactly compensate
    the four-dimensional heat-kernel measure.
    \item[(c)]
    After Fourier transforming the coincidence limit and scaling
    \(k=M\ell\), gamma-matrix counting again selects the term quadratic in
    the curvature.  Its regulator dependence is proportional to
    \[
      \int d^4\ell\,f''(\ell^2)
        =\pi^2\int_0^\infty ds\,s f''(s).
    \]
    Integrating twice by parts,
    \[
      \int_0^\infty ds\,s f''(s)
        =[sf'(s)]_0^\infty-\int_0^\infty f'(s)\,ds
        =f(0)-f(\infty)=1.
    \]
    Thus every admissible smooth regulator gives the same coefficient.
    Changing the profile of \(f\) changes only terms that vanish with
    \(M^{-1}\) or local scheme-dependent terms that do not alter the
    anomaly class.
    \item[(d)]
    If \(\lambda_n\neq0\), anticommutation gives
    \[
      i\slashed D(\gamma_5\phi_n)
        =-\lambda_n(\gamma_5\phi_n).
    \]
    The two states have the same value of
    \(f(\lambda_n^2/M^2)\), while their matrix elements of \(\gamma_5\)
    enter with opposite signs.  Hence every nonzero pair cancels in
    \[
      \operatorname{Tr}\!
        \left[\gamma_5f(\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2)\right].
    \]
    Zero modes can instead be chosen to satisfy
    \(\gamma_5\phi=\pm\phi\).  Because \(f(0)=1\), only these modes remain,
    and integration over spacetime gives
    \[
      \boxed{\operatorname{Tr}\!
        \left[\gamma_5f(\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2)\right]
        =n_+-n_-=\operatorname{ind}(i\slashed D).}
    \]
    The heat-kernel evaluation of the same trace equates this integer to
    the integral of the Chern--Pontryagin density.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{2}{Axial and Standard-Model Anomalies}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    An anomaly is the failure of a symmetry of the classical action to
    survive quantization.  Equivalently, no regulator and set of local
    counterterms preserves all of the corresponding Ward identities.  If
    the symmetry is global, the quantum theory remains consistent, but
    the current is not conserved.  The anomalous Ward identity then has
    observable consequences, such as the decay
    \(\pi^0\to\gamma\gamma\).  A gauge symmetry instead removes
    unphysical polarizations and is needed for the BRST construction of
    the physical Hilbert space.  A gauge anomaly therefore destroys gauge
    invariance, unitarity, and consistency; it must cancel among the
    chiral fields.

    \item[(b)]
    The equations of motion and their adjoints give
    \[
      \gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi=0,\qquad
      (D_\mu\overline\psi)\gamma^\mu=0.
    \]
    Consequently the vector current
    \(J^\mu=-i\overline\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\) obeys
    \[
      \partial_\mu J^\mu
       =-i(D_\mu\overline\psi)\gamma^\mu\psi
        -i\overline\psi\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi=0.
    \]
    Since \(\{\gamma_5,\gamma^\mu\}=0\), the same calculation gives
    \(\partial_\mu J_5^\mu=0\) classically for
    \(J_5^\mu=-i\overline\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\psi\).

    Quantum mechanically, choose a regulator that preserves the vector
    gauge symmetry.  Under the local axial change of variables
    \(\psi\mapsto e^{i\beta(x)\gamma_5}\psi\), the action produces the
    classical divergence, while the fermion measure has the regulated
    Jacobian
    \[
      \delta\log\mathcal D\psi\,\mathcal D\overline\psi
       =-2i\int d^4x\,\beta(x)
        \lim_{M\to\infty}
        \operatorname{tr}_{D}
        \left[\gamma_5e^{-\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2}\right]_{x,x}.
    \]
    In the heat-kernel expansion only the term quadratic in
    \(F_{\mu\nu}\) has four gamma matrices and survives the
    \(\gamma_5\) trace.  The Gaussian momentum integral and the supplied
    identity give
    \[
      \lim_{M\to\infty}
        \operatorname{tr}_{D}
        \left[\gamma_5e^{-\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2}\right]_{x,x}
       =\frac{ie^2}{32\pi^2}
        \epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
        F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta},
    \]
    with the displayed sign fixed by the volume's orientation and current
    convention.  The anomalous Ward identity is therefore
    \[
      \boxed{\partial_\mu J_5^\mu
       =-\frac{e^2}{16\pi^2}
        \epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
        F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta}.}
    \]
    The same regulator preserves
    \(\partial_\mu J^\mu=0\): the anomaly has been assigned to the axial,
    not the gauge, Ward identity.

    \item[(c)]
    Work entirely with the left-handed spectrum in the question.  With
    \(A(\mathbf3)=1\) and
    \(A(\overline{\mathbf3})=-1\), the pure color anomaly is
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(3)^3}=2-1-1=0.
    \]
    There is no local \(SU(2)^3\) anomaly because every \(SU(2)\)
    representation is real or pseudoreal.  There are
    \(3+1=4\) left-handed weak doublets, so the mod-two global
    \(SU(2)\) anomaly also vanishes.  Anomalies with only one insertion
    from a simple group vanish because its generators are traceless.

    Omitting common positive Dynkin-index factors, the remaining mixed
    gauge anomalies are
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      SU(3)^2U(1)_Y:\quad&
       2\left(\frac16\right)-\frac23+\frac13=0,\\
      SU(2)^2U(1)_Y:\quad&
       3\left(\frac16\right)-\frac12=0.
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    Including color and weak multiplicities,
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \mathcal A_{Y^3}
       &=6\left(\frac16\right)^3
        +3\left(-\frac23\right)^3
        +3\left(\frac13\right)^3
        +2\left(-\frac12\right)^3+1=0,\\
      \mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2Y}
       &=6\left(\frac16\right)
        +3\left(-\frac23\right)
        +3\left(\frac13\right)
        +2\left(-\frac12\right)+1=0.
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    The neutral \(\nu^c\) contributes to none of these sums.

    Finally, write the physical electric charges in terms of the
    doublet hypercharges:
    \[
      Q_u=Y_Q+\frac12,\qquad
      Q_d=Y_Q-\frac12,\qquad
      Q_e=Y_L-\frac12.
    \]
    The \(SU(2)^2U(1)_Y\) condition is
    \(3Y_Q+Y_L=0\).  Hence
    \[
      Q_p=2Q_u+Q_d=3Y_Q+\frac12
       =-Y_L+\frac12=-Q_e.
    \]
    Thus the equality of the proton and electron charge magnitudes is a
    direct consequence of anomaly cancellation.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{3}{Standard-Model Anomalies, CKM Mixing, and CP Violation}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)]
    The perturbative color anomaly cancels between two color triplets in
    \(Q\) and the two antitriplets:
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(3)^3}=2-1-1=0.
    \]
    The weak representation is pseudoreal, so there is no perturbative
    \(SU(2)^3\) anomaly.  The number of weak doublets per family is
    \(3+1=4\), which is even, so the Witten mod-two anomaly is absent as
    well.  Anomalies with a single simple-group generator vanish by
    tracelessness.  The remaining coefficients are
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \mathcal A_{SU(3)^2Y}
       &\propto
        2\left(\frac16\right)-\frac23+\frac13=0,\\
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2Y}
       &\propto
        3\left(\frac16\right)-\frac12=0,\\
      \mathcal A_{Y^3}
       &=6\left(\frac16\right)^3
        +3\left(-\frac23\right)^3
        +3\left(\frac13\right)^3
        +2\left(-\frac12\right)^3+1=0,\\
      \mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2Y}
       &=6\left(\frac16\right)
        +3\left(-\frac23\right)
        +3\left(\frac13\right)
        +2\left(-\frac12\right)+1=0.
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    The sterile neutrino has \(Y=0\) and changes none of the results.

    \item[(ii)]
    Gauge invariance is not merely an optional global symmetry.  It
    enforces the Ward or Slavnov--Taylor identities that decouple
    negative-norm polarizations.  With an uncancelled gauge anomaly the
    BRST charge ceases to be nilpotent, longitudinal gauge modes do not
    decouple, and amplitudes become gauge dependent and nonunitary.
    No choice of local counterterm can repair a nontrivial anomaly.

    \item[(iii)]
    Baryon number is perturbatively conserved at ordinary vertices, but
    its current has electroweak mixed anomalies.  In an all-left-handed
    sum,
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2B}
        =N_g\,3\left(\frac13\right)T(\mathbf2)
        =\frac{N_g}{2},
    \]
    and the \(U(1)_Y^2U(1)_B\) coefficient is also nonzero.  The
    topologically quantized selection rule comes from the \(SU(2)_L\)
    term.  For a transition of topological charge \(\nu\),
    \[
      \boxed{\Delta B=\Delta L=N_g\nu,\qquad
       \Delta(B-L)=0.}
    \]
    Thus for the observed \(N_g=3\), a unit transition has
    \(\Delta B=\Delta L=3\).  At zero temperature the corresponding
    instanton amplitude is exponentially tiny, so baryon number is an
    excellent accidental symmetry in laboratory perturbation theory.
    At temperatures near or above the electroweak scale, thermal
    sphaleron transitions are not exponentially suppressed in the same
    way and can efficiently reprocess \(B+L\), while preserving \(B-L\).

    \item[(iv)]
    Let unitary matrices diagonalize the Yukawa couplings according to
    \[
      U_{uL}^\dagger Y_uU_{uR}=D_u,\qquad
      U_{dL}^\dagger Y_dU_{dR}=D_d,
    \]
    where \(D_u,D_d\) are real, positive, and diagonal.  The neutral and
    Higgs couplings are diagonal in this mass basis, whereas the charged
    current becomes
    \[
      \mathcal L_W
       =\frac{g}{\sqrt2}\,
        \overline u_L^{\,\mathrm{mass}}\gamma^\mu
        V_{\mathrm{CKM}}d_L^{\,\mathrm{mass}}W_\mu^+
        +\mathrm{H.c.},
      \qquad
      \boxed{V_{\mathrm{CKM}}=U_{uL}^\dagger U_{dL}.}
    \]
    A \(3\times3\) unitary matrix has three angles and six phases.
    Independent rephasings of the six quark fields remove five phases:
    the common rephasing is baryon number and leaves \(V_{\mathrm{CKM}}\)
    unchanged.  Thus the CKM matrix contains
    \[
      \boxed{3\ \text{mixing angles}+1\ \text{irreducible phase}.}
    \]

    \item[(v)]
    The weak source of CP violation is the irreducible CKM phase.  A
    basis-independent measure is the Jarlskog invariant
    \[
      J=\operatorname{Im}
       \left(V_{ij}V_{kl}V_{il}^*V_{kj}^*\right)
    \]
    for any choice of distinct \(i,k\) and \(j,l\), together with
    nondegenerate up- and down-type masses.  Equivalently one may use the
    imaginary part of a determinant or trace constructed from
    \([Y_uY_u^\dagger,Y_dY_d^\dagger]\).

    The second source is the strong-CP angle.  Axial quark rephasings move
    phase between the QCD theta term and the Yukawa determinants, but the
    invariant combination is
    \[
      \boxed{\overline\theta
       =\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}+\arg\det(Y_uY_d)}
    \]
    up to the sign convention for the theta term.  The Jarlskog invariant
    and \(\overline\theta\) are independent.  Field redefinitions can
    change their parametrization but cannot relate the two physical
    invariants.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{4}{Anomalies Determine Standard-Model Hypercharge}
  A gauge anomaly is an obstruction to gauging the symmetry and makes a
  dynamical gauge theory inconsistent.  An ABJ anomaly is the quantum
  nonconservation of a classically conserved global current; the theory is
  consistent, but the symmetry is not exact.  A 't~Hooft anomaly is the
  obstruction encountered when an exact global symmetry is coupled to
  background gauge fields.  It is invariant under renormalization-group
  flow and must be reproduced by the infrared degrees of freedom, symmetry
  breaking, or a topological sector.

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)]
    Convert each right-handed field to a left-handed conjugate when
    evaluating anomalies.  The \(SU(3)^3\) coefficient is
    \(2-1-1=0\) independently of the hypercharges.  The local
    \(SU(2)^3\) coefficient vanishes because the doublet is pseudoreal,
    and the global mod-two anomaly vanishes because there are four
    doublets: three in \(Q_L\) and one in \(L_L\).  Diagrams with only one
    generator of either simple group vanish by tracelessness.  Thus the
    nontrivial local gauge-anomaly conditions are
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      SU(3)^2U(1):\quad&2q-u-d=0,\\
      SU(2)^2U(1):\quad&3q+l=0,\\
      U(1)^3:\quad&
        6q^3+2l^3-3u^3-3d^3-x^3=0.
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    These are all the conditions requested before imposing the mixed
    gravitational anomaly.

    \item[(ii)]
    The first equation gives \(u+d=2q\).  Hence
    \[
      u-d=(u+d)-2d=2(q-d).
    \]
    Since all charges are integers, one may define
    \[
      y=\frac{u-d}{2}\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
      u=q+y,\qquad d=q-y.
    \]

    \item[(iii)]
    The second mixed-anomaly equation gives \(l=-3q\).  Substitute this
    and \(u=q+y,d=q-y\) into the cubic equation:
    \[
    \begin{split}
      0={}&6q^3+2(-3q)^3
        -3\big[(q+y)^3+(q-y)^3\big]-x^3\\
       ={}&-54q^3-18qy^2-x^3.
    \end{split}
    \]
    Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{54q^3+18y^2q+x^3=0.}
    \]

    \item[(iv)]
    For \(q\ne0\), set
    \[
      \widetilde y=\frac{y}{q},\qquad
      \widetilde x=\frac{x}{q}.
    \]
    These numbers are rational, and division by \(q^3\) gives
    \[
      \boxed{54+18\widetilde y^{\,2}
       +\widetilde x^{\,3}=0.}
    \]

    \item[(v)]
    Define rational \(v,w\) by the change of variables in the question.
    Solving for them gives
    \[
      v=-\frac{3+\widetilde y}{\widetilde x},
      \qquad
      w=-\frac{3-\widetilde y}{\widetilde x}.
    \]
    The cubic equation is precisely
    \[
      v^3+w^3
       =-\frac{(3+\widetilde y)^3
                    +(3-\widetilde y)^3}
                   {\widetilde x^{\,3}}
       =1.
    \]
    Clearing denominators would give an integer solution of
    \(a^3+b^3=c^3\).  By the stated result, the solution must be trivial:
    \(v=0,w=1\) or \(w=0,v=1\).  These alternatives give
    \[
      \widetilde x=-6,\qquad
      \widetilde y=\mp3.
    \]
    It follows that
    \[
      l=-3q,\qquad x=-6q,\qquad
      \{u,d\}=\{4q,-2q\}.
    \]
    The two orderings merely interchange the labels \(u_R,d_R\).
    Choosing \(q=1\), one conventional ordering is
    \[
      \boxed{l=-3,\qquad u=4,\qquad d=-2,\qquad x=-6.}
    \]
    These are six times the usual Standard-Model hypercharges.

    \item[(vi)]
    The mixed gauge--gravitational coefficient, with the same chirality
    signs, is
    \[
      \mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2U(1)}
       =6q+2l-3u-3d-x.
    \]
    Using \(l=-3q\), \(u+d=2q\), and \(x=-6q\), one obtains
    \[
      6q-6q-6q+6q=0.
    \]
    Thus it cancels automatically on the nonzero-\(q\) solution.
  \end{enumerate}

  If \(q=0\), the equations instead imply
  \[
    l=x=0,\qquad d=-u,
  \]
  with \(u\) arbitrary.  This is a vectorlike branch and is not an
  overall rescaling of the Standard-Model solution.  The assumption
  \(q\ne0\) is therefore essential to the uniqueness statement.

  \SupplementarySolution{5}{Standard-Model Anomaly Cancellation}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] Including color and weak multiplicities,
      \[
        \sum_{\rm LH}Y^3
        =6\left(\frac16\right)^3
        +3\left(-\frac23\right)^3
        +3\left(\frac13\right)^3
        +2\left(-\frac12\right)^3+1=0.
      \]
    \item[(b)] A triangle with one generator \(T^a\) of a simple
      \(SU(N)\) factor and two commuting \(U(1)\) generators is
      proportional to \(\operatorname{tr}T^a=0\).  The same trace
      factorizes for \(SU(N)G_1G_2\) when the other generators act on
      different tensor factors, so it vanishes as well.
    \item[(c)] The QCD spectrum is vectorlike: for every left-handed color
      triplet contribution there is a conjugate right-handed contribution.
      Equivalently, in a left-handed basis the triplets and antitriplets
      have opposite cubic indices, so the \(SU(3)^3\) sum is zero.
    \item[(d)] The doublet of \(SU(2)\) is pseudoreal, and
      \(\{\tau^a,\tau^b\}\propto\delta^{ab}\mathbf{1}\).  Contracting with a
      third traceless generator gives
      \(\operatorname{tr}(\{\tau^a,\tau^b\}\tau^c)=0\).
      Thus there is no local \(SU(2)^3\) anomaly.  This statement is
      separate from the global Witten anomaly.
    \item[(e)] Taking \(T(\mathbf3)=1/2\),
      \[
        \mathcal A_{33Y}
          =\frac12(2Y_Q-Y_u-Y_d).
      \]
      The Standard-Model values give
      \(2(1/6)-2/3-(-1/3)=0\).
    \item[(f)] Similarly,
      \[
        \mathcal A_{22Y}
          =\frac12(3Y_Q+Y_L)
          =\frac12\left(\frac12-\frac12\right)=0.
      \]
    \item[(g)] The mixed gravitational coefficient is the linear trace:
      \[
        6\left(\frac16\right)
        +3\left(-\frac23\right)+3\left(\frac13\right)
        +2\left(-\frac12\right)+1=0.
      \]
    \item[(h)] Let the physical right-handed charges be
      \(Y_u,Y_d,Y_e,Y_\nu=0\), and put \(Y_Q=q\).  The mixed conditions give
      \[
        Y_L=-3q,\qquad Y_u+Y_d=2q,\qquad Y_e=2Y_L=-6q.
      \]
      The cubic condition then implies
      \(Y_uY_d=-8q^2\), so
      \(\{Y_u,Y_d\}=\{4q,-2q\}\).  Up to exchanging the names \(u,d\)
      and choosing the normalization \(q=1/6\), these are precisely the
      Standard-Model hypercharges.  The nontrivial solution is chiral.
    \item[(i)] Per generation the \(SU(2)^2B\) coefficient is
      \(3(1/3)T(\mathbf2)=1/2\), and the \(SU(2)^2L\) coefficient is also
      \(T(\mathbf2)=1/2\).  The anomalous changes therefore obey
      \(\Delta B=\Delta L\), so \(B+L\) is violated and \(B-L\) is
      preserved.  The same cancellation holds for all gauge and
      gravitational anomaly coefficients of \(B-L\) when the
      hypercharge-neutral right-handed neutrino is included; \(B\) and
      \(L\) separately remain anomalous.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{6}{Characteristic Forms and BRST Descent}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    The Bianchi identity is \(DF=0\).  Since the trace of a covariant
    derivative equals the ordinary exterior derivative,
    \[
      d\,\operatorname{Tr}F^n
        =\operatorname{Tr}D(F^n)
        =n\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((DF)F^{n-1}\bigr)=0.
    \]
    Under an infinitesimal gauge transformation,
    \(\delta_\alpha F=[F,\alpha]\), and hence
    \[
      \delta_\alpha\operatorname{Tr}F^n
        =n\operatorname{Tr}\bigl([F,\alpha]F^{n-1}\bigr)=0
    \]
    by cyclicity.  Thus Bianchi plus cyclicity prove closedness and gauge
    invariance.  Closed invariant forms have topological integrals and,
    through descent, furnish local anomaly polynomials in two dimensions
    higher than the anomalous theory.
    \item[(b)]
    Acting successively on \(\Gamma\),
    \[
      [\delta_\alpha,\delta_\beta]\Gamma
        =\delta_\alpha\mathcal G(\beta,A)
         -\delta_\beta\mathcal G(\alpha,A).
    \]
    The gauge algebra requires this to equal
    \(\delta_{[\alpha,\beta]}\Gamma
      =\mathcal G([\alpha,\beta],A)\).  Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{\delta_\alpha\mathcal G(\beta,A)
        -\delta_\beta\mathcal G(\alpha,A)
        =\mathcal G([\alpha,\beta],A).}
    \]
    For an Abelian group the commutator vanishes, leaving
    \[
      \delta_\alpha\mathcal G(\beta,A)
        =\delta_\beta\mathcal G(\alpha,A).
    \]
    This is an integrability condition on any proposed Abelian anomaly.
    \item[(c)]
    Replacing the gauge parameter by the ghost packages the gauge algebra
    into \(s^2=0\).  The two successive variations in the Wess--Zumino
    condition are exactly the terms in \(s\mathcal G\), so consistency is
    \[
      s\mathcal G=0.
    \]
    If \(\mathcal G=sB\), redefining the effective action by
    \(\Gamma'=\Gamma-B\) gives \(s\Gamma'=0\).  Such a term is therefore
    removable.  Candidate anomalies are classified by
    \[
      \boxed{H_{\rm loc}^{\,1}(s)
        =\frac{\{\text{local }\mathcal G:\operatorname{gh}\mathcal G=1,
            \ s\mathcal G=0\}}
         {\{\mathcal G=sB:\operatorname{gh}B=0,\ B\text{ local}\}}.}
    \]
    If total derivatives are retained locally, this is written
    \(H_{\rm loc}^{1}(s\mid d)\).
    \item[(d)]
    Direct differentiation, using graded cyclicity, gives
    \[
      d\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\,dA+\frac23A^3\right)
        =\operatorname{Tr}\left(dA\,dA+2A^2dA\right)
        =\operatorname{Tr}F^2.
    \]
    Under \(sA=-D\omega\), the variation of
    \(\operatorname{Tr}F^2\) vanishes.  Hence \(d(sQ_3)=0\), and locally
    \(sQ_3\) is exact.  A standard representative is
    \[
      \boxed{Q_2^{\,1}=\operatorname{Tr}(\omega\,dA),\qquad
        sQ_3+dQ_2^{\,1}=0.}
    \]
    Adding \(d\operatorname{Tr}(\omega A)\) changes the representative
    but not its integrated anomaly class.  This is the first nontrivial
    step of the descent from the four-form \(\operatorname{Tr}F^2\).
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{7}{Bulk and Axion-String Anomaly Inflow}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Since \(F\) is gauge invariant,
    \[
      \delta_\alpha S_{\rm CS}
        =k\int_{X_5}d\alpha\wedge F\wedge F
        =k\int_{X_5}d(\alpha F\wedge F)
        =k\int_{M_4}\alpha F\wedge F.
    \]
    Thus a boundary theory with
    \[
      \delta_\alpha\Gamma_{\partial}
        =-k\int_{M_4}\alpha F\wedge F
    \]
    is rendered consistent by the bulk term:
    \(\delta_\alpha(S_{\rm CS}+\Gamma_\partial)=0\).
    Gauge charge flows between the boundary and bulk.  Separate
    conservation fails in each subsystem, but the combined system has an
    exactly conserved gauge current.
    \item[(b)]
    For an Abelian field,
    \(F\wedge F=d(A\wedge F)\).  Away from the string,
    \[
      S_\theta
        =c\int d(\theta A\wedge F)
         -c\int d\theta\wedge A\wedge F.
    \]
    The second term defines a bulk Chern--Simons current proportional to
    \(d\theta\wedge F\).  Although \(d^2\theta=0\) for a smooth
    single-valued field, a \(2\pi\) winding gives distributionally
    \[
      d^2\theta=2\pi\,\delta_2(\Sigma),
    \]
    where \(\Sigma\) is the string world sheet.  Consequently
    \[
      d{*J_{\rm inflow}}
        =-2\pi c\,\delta_2(\Sigma)\wedge F
    \]
    up to the orientation convention.  The right-hand side is localized
    on \(\Sigma\) and has precisely the form of the two-dimensional gauge
    anomaly of chiral zero modes.  Choosing \(c\) from the underlying
    fermion anomaly makes their sum vanish.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{8}{The Axial Anomaly in Dimensional Regularization}
  Define \(\gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3\) and split every
  \(d\)-dimensional object into four-dimensional and evanescent parts,
  \(\ell=\bar\ell+\widehat\ell\).  Take \(\gamma_5\) to anticommute with
  \(\bar\gamma^\mu\) and commute with \(\widehat\gamma^\mu\).  This
  't~Hooft--Veltman prescription preserves shifts of the regulated
  integral and hence both vector Ward identities.

  With incoming photon momenta \(k_1,k_2\) and
  \(q=k_1+k_2\), include both orientations of the fermion loop:
  \[
    T^{\lambda\mu\nu}
      =-e^2\int\frac{d^d\ell}{(2\pi)^d}
      \frac{\operatorname{tr}[
       \gamma^\lambda\gamma_5\slashed\ell\gamma^\mu
       (\slashed\ell+\slashed k_1)\gamma^\nu
       (\slashed\ell-\slashed k_2)]}
      {\ell^2(\ell+k_1)^2(\ell-k_2)^2}
      +(\mu,k_1\leftrightarrow\nu,k_2).
  \]
  Contracting with \(k_{1\mu}\) or \(k_{2\nu}\), writing the momentum as a
  difference of adjacent inverse propagators, and shifting \(\ell\)
  proves \(k_{1\mu}T^{\lambda\mu\nu}
  =k_{2\nu}T^{\lambda\mu\nu}=0\).

  For the axial contraction, the same cancellation would occur in exactly
  four dimensions.  The failure to anticommute \(\gamma_5\) through
  \(\widehat{\slashed\ell}\) leaves
  \[
    q_\lambda T^{\lambda\mu\nu}
      =-8e^2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k_{1\rho}k_{2\sigma}
       \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy
       \int\frac{d^dL}{(2\pi)^d}
       \frac{\widehat L^{\,2}}{(L^2-\Delta+i0)^3}.
  \]
  Here
  \(\Delta=-xy\,q^2-x(1-x-y)k_1^2
  -y(1-x-y)k_2^2\); its precise value drops out of the finite anomalous
  term.  Rotational symmetry in \(d\) dimensions gives
  \[
    \int d^dL\,\widehat L^{\,2}f(L^2)
      =\frac{d-4}{d}\int d^dL\,L^2f(L^2).
  \]
  The second integral has a \(1/(d-4)\) ultraviolet pole.  Using
  \[
    \int\frac{d^dL_E}{(2\pi)^d}
      \frac{L_E^2}{(L_E^2+\Delta)^3}
      =\frac{d}{2(4\pi)^{d/2}}
        \Gamma\!\left(2-\frac d2\right)\Delta^{d/2-2}
  \]
  and the Feynman-parameter area \(1/2\), the evanescent zero times the
  ultraviolet pole has the finite limit
  \[
    \boxed{q_\lambda T^{\lambda\mu\nu}
      =-\frac{e^2}{2\pi^2}
       \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k_{1\rho}k_{2\sigma},}
  \]
  with the overall sign tied to the stated \(\epsilon\) and Fourier
  conventions.

  Finally, between two photon states the matrix element is
  \[
    \bra{\gamma_1\gamma_2}
      F^{\alpha\beta}\widetilde F_{\alpha\beta}\ket{0}
    =8\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k_{1\rho}k_{2\sigma}
      \varepsilon_{1\mu}\varepsilon_{2\nu},
  \]
  including the two ways of assigning the external photons.
  Multiplication by \(-e^2/(16\pi^2)\) reproduces the contracted
  triangle.  Thus the regulated calculation is exactly the matrix
  element of the operator equation in the exercise.

  \SupplementarySolution{9}{Electroweak \(B\), \(L\), and \(B+L\) Selection Rules}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Only left-handed \(SU(2)_L\) doublets contribute.  Per generation the
    quark doublet has three colors, baryon number \(1/3\), and index
    \(T(\mathbf2)=1/2\), so
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2B}^{(1)}
        =3\left(\frac13\right)\frac12=\frac12.
    \]
    Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{\mathcal A_{SU(2)^2B}=\frac{N_g}{2}.}
    \]
    Right-handed quarks are \(SU(2)_L\) singlets and have zero quadratic
    index, so they do not affect this coefficient.
    \item[(b)]
    Each generation has one lepton doublet of lepton number \(1\), giving
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2L}^{(1)}=1\cdot\frac12=\frac12.
    \]
    Thus the baryon and lepton coefficients are equal:
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2(B-L)}=0,\qquad
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2(B+L)}=N_g.
    \]
    A unit electroweak topological transition produces one unit of baryon
    number and one unit of lepton number per generation,
    \[
      \boxed{\Delta B=\Delta L=N_g,\qquad
        \Delta(B-L)=0,\qquad
        \Delta(B+L)=2N_g.}
    \]
    \item[(c)]
    With the topological-charge convention in the problem, the local
    equations are
    \[
      \partial_\mu J_B^\mu
        =N_g\frac{g^2}{32\pi^2}
          W^a_{\mu\nu}\widetilde W^{a\mu\nu},
      \qquad
      \partial_\mu J_L^\mu
        =N_g\frac{g^2}{32\pi^2}
          W^a_{\mu\nu}\widetilde W^{a\mu\nu}.
    \]
    Hence
    \[
      \partial_\mu J_{B+L}^\mu
        =2N_g\frac{g^2}{32\pi^2}
          W^a_{\mu\nu}\widetilde W^{a\mu\nu}.
    \]
    Integrating over spacetime gives
    \[
      \boxed{\Delta B=\Delta L=N_g\nu,\qquad
        \Delta(B+L)=2N_g\nu,\qquad
        \Delta(B-L)=0.}
    \]
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{10}{Anomaly Matching in an \(SU(2)\) Gauge Theory}
  A gauge anomaly makes a dynamical gauge symmetry inconsistent.  An ABJ
  anomaly is the quantum nonconservation of a classical global current.
  A 't~Hooft anomaly is an obstruction to gauging an exact global symmetry
  as a background symmetry; it must match between ultraviolet and
  infrared descriptions.

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)]
    Within the chiral subgroup stipulated in the question, the axial
    \(U(1)\) is broken by the \(SU(2)\) instanton anomaly, whereas the
    vector combination survives.  The continuous group is therefore
    \[
      SU(N_f)_L\times SU(N_f)_R\times U(1)_V
    \]
    up to common center quotients, and the axial transformation leaves a
    discrete \(\mathbb Z_{2N_f}\) remnant.  The physical right-handed
    fields transform as
    \[
    \begin{array}{c|ccc}
       &SU(N_f)_L&SU(N_f)_R&U(1)_V\\ \hline
      \psi_L&\mathbf{N_f}&\mathbf1&+1\\
      \psi_R&\mathbf1&\mathbf{N_f}&+1
    \end{array}
    \]
    For anomaly sums, \(\psi_R\) is replaced by the left-handed conjugate
    \(\psi_R^c\), which is in
    \(\overline{\mathbf{N_f}}\) of \(SU(N_f)_R\) and has \(V=-1\).

    \item[(ii)]
    There are two gauge components for every flavor fundamental.  In the
    normalization of the question,
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \mathcal A_{SU(N_f)_L^3}&=2,\\
      \mathcal A_{SU(N_f)_L^2V}&=2,\\
      \mathcal A_{V^3}
       &=2N_f(1)^3+2N_f(-1)^3=0,\\
      \mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2V}
       &=2N_f-2N_f=0.
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    The first line is a perturbative cubic anomaly for \(N_f\ge3\);
    the corresponding local cubic invariant vanishes for \(N_f=2\).

    A gauge singlet made from \(SU(2)\) fundamentals contains an even
    number of doublets.  In the theory \(S_0\), it therefore contains an
    even number of fermions and is bosonic.  A confining phase cannot
    furnish massless gauge-singlet fermions to match the nonzero flavor
    anomaly.  Barring a compensating topological sector, the chiral
    symmetry must consequently break, with its Goldstone effective action
    matching the anomaly.

    \item[(iii)]
    The perturbative \(SU(2)_{\rm gauge}^3\) anomaly vanishes because both
    the fundamental and adjoint representations are pseudoreal or real
    and have no symmetric cubic invariant.  The Witten anomaly counts
    doublets modulo two.  The \(N_f\) Dirac fermions give \(2N_f\)
    left-handed doublets, an even number, while the adjoint has integer
    isospin and does not change the mod-two obstruction.  Hence neither
    gauge anomaly is present.

    \item[(iv)]
    Convert the physical \(\psi_R\) to \(\psi_R^c\).  Both \(\psi_L\) and
    \(\psi_R^c\) then have \(X=-1\).  The mixed gauge coefficient is
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)_{\rm gauge}^2X}
       =-N_fI(\mathbf2)-N_fI(\overline{\mathbf2})
         +qI(\operatorname{adj})
       =-2N_f+4q.
    \]
    It vanishes for
    \[
      \boxed{q=\frac{N_f}{2}.}
    \]

    \item[(v)]
    It is useful to list the physical-field charges:
    \[
    \begin{array}{c|cccc}
       &SU(N_f)_L&SU(N_f)_R&U(1)_V&U(1)_X\\ \hline
      \psi_L&\mathbf{N_f}&\mathbf1&+1&-1\\
      \psi_R&\mathbf1&\mathbf{N_f}&+1&+1\\
      \lambda_L&\mathbf1&\mathbf1&0&N_f/2
    \end{array}
    \]
    In a left-handed anomaly sum, \(\psi_R^c\) has \(V=-1,X=-1\).
    The adjoint has three gauge components.  Thus
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \mathcal A_{SU(N_f)_L^3}&=2,\\
      \mathcal A_{SU(N_f)_L^2X}&=2(-1)=-2,\\
      \mathcal A_{X^3}
       &=-2N_f-2N_f
         +3\left(\frac{N_f}{2}\right)^3
        =\frac{N_f(3N_f^2-32)}8,\\
      \mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2X}
       &=-2N_f-2N_f+3\frac{N_f}{2}
        =-\frac{5N_f}{2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    For \(N_f=3\), the last two coefficients are
    \(-15/8\) and \(-15/2\), respectively.

    \item[(vi)]
    At \(N_f=3\), \(q=3/2\).  Adding the charges and tensoring the
    flavor representations of the constituents gives
    \[
    \begin{array}{c|cccc}
       &SU(3)_L&SU(3)_R&U(1)_V&U(1)_X\\ \hline
      \chi&\mathbf3&\overline{\mathbf3}&0&-\tfrac12\\
      \eta&\overline{\mathbf3}&\mathbf1&+2&-\tfrac12\\
      \widetilde\eta&\mathbf1&\mathbf3&-2&-\tfrac12
    \end{array}
    \]
    For example,
    \(X(\chi)=-1+3/2-1=-1/2\).  The epsilon tensor turns the
    antisymmetric product of two \(SU(3)\) fundamentals into an
    antifundamental, which gives the representation of \(\eta\);
    the conjugate construction gives the representation of
    \(\widetilde\eta\).

    \item[(vii)]
    The bifundamental \(\chi\) supplies three copies of an
    \(SU(3)_L\) fundamental, while \(\eta\) supplies one
    antifundamental.  Therefore
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(3)_L^3}^{\mathrm{IR}}
       =3-1=2.
    \]
    Both the fundamental and antifundamental have quadratic index one, so
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(3)_L^2X}^{\mathrm{IR}}
       =3\left(-\frac12\right)
        +\left(-\frac12\right)=-2.
    \]
    There are \(9\) components of \(\chi\), \(3\) of \(\eta\), and
    \(3\) of \(\widetilde\eta\), all with \(X=-1/2\).  Hence
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \mathcal A_{X^3}^{\mathrm{IR}}
       &=(9+3+3)\left(-\frac12\right)^3
        =-\frac{15}{8},\\
      \mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2X}^{\mathrm{IR}}
       &=(9+3+3)\left(-\frac12\right)
        =-\frac{15}{2}.
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    Every requested infrared coefficient equals the \(N_f=3\)
    ultraviolet coefficient.  The proposed massless composites therefore
    pass these 't~Hooft anomaly-matching tests in the stipulated chiral
    subgroup.
  \end{enumerate}

  The calculation above follows the subgroup used by the examination.
  Because the gauge fundamental is pseudoreal, the actual continuous
  flavor group is enhanced to \(SU(2N_f)\); a complete phase analysis
  should formulate matching for that enhanced symmetry as well.

  \SupplementarySolution{11}{Spectator Matching and a Wess--Zumino Compensator}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Let the confined elementary fermions have anomaly \(A_{\rm UV}\).
    Add spectators of anomaly \(-A_{\rm UV}\), making the weakly gauged
    ultraviolet theory consistent.  Confinement does not affect the
    spectators.  If \(G\) remains unbroken, there are no Goldstone bosons
    to carry an anomalous gauge variation.

    The infrared theory must still be a consistent gauge theory.  Its
    anomaly is
    \[
      A_{\rm IR}^{\rm composite}-A_{\rm UV}.
    \]
    Gauge consistency forces this to vanish, and therefore
    \[
      \boxed{A_{\rm IR}^{\rm composite}=A_{\rm UV}.}
    \]
    This conclusion uses no weak-coupling expansion for the confining
    dynamics; it follows from the consistency of the arbitrarily weak
    spectator gauging.
    \item[(b)]
    In the ultraviolet, the three color copies of the left-handed flavor
    doublet each have baryon number \(1/3\).  Hence
    \[
      \mathcal A_{\rm UV}
        =3\left(\frac13\right)T(\mathbf2)
        =\frac12.
    \]
    A single left-handed nucleon doublet has baryon number \(1\), so
    \[
      \mathcal A_{\rm IR}=1\cdot T(\mathbf2)=\frac12.
    \]
    It matches the perturbative \(SU(2)_L^2U(1)_B\) anomaly.

    There are three ultraviolet \(SU(2)_L\) doublets and one proposed
    infrared doublet.  Both numbers are odd, so their parity also agrees.
    This is the datum relevant to the nonperturbative mod-two \(SU(2)\)
    anomaly, although the perturbative cubic \(SU(2)^3\) anomaly vanishes
    identically.
    \item[(c)]
    Add
    \[
      S_{\rm WZ}=\frac{C}{f}\int a\,F\wedge F.
    \]
    Because \(\delta_\alpha a=-f\alpha\),
    \[
      \delta_\alpha S_{\rm WZ}
        =-C\int\alpha F\wedge F,
    \]
    which cancels the variation of \(\Gamma\).

    The one-form
    \[
      da+fA
    \]
    is gauge invariant when \(A\to A+d\alpha\).  The canonical kinetic
    term is therefore
    \[
      S_{\rm kin}=-\frac12\int(da+fA)\wedge{*}(da+fA).
    \]
    In the gauge \(a=0\), it contains
    \(-f^2A_\mu A^\mu/2\).  For a canonically normalized gauge kinetic
    term,
    \[
      \boxed{m_A=|f|.}
    \]
    The anomaly has been cancelled by adding a transforming degree of
    freedom, not by a counterterm built from \(A\) alone.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{12}{Heat-Kernel Derivation of the Two-Dimensional Chiral Anomaly}
  The fermion equations and their adjoints give the classical identity
  \[
    \partial_\mu j_5^\mu
      =2im\overline\psi\gamma_5\psi
  \]
  with the current convention in the question.  Quantum mechanically the
  composite divergence contains a coincident-point trace.  A
  gauge-covariant regulator defines its anomalous part by
  \[
    \mathcal A(x)
      =2\lim_{M\to\infty}
       \operatorname{tr}\!
       \left[\gamma_5e^{-\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2}\right]_{x,x},
  \]
  where the overall \(i\) is supplied or removed by the Euclidean
  continuation convention.

  The squared Dirac operator has the form
  \[
    \slashed D^{\,2}
      =D^2+\frac{iq}{4}
        [\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]F_{\mu\nu}.
  \]
  The diagonal free heat kernel is
  \(M^2/(4\pi)+O(M^0)\).  Its leading term has
  \(\operatorname{tr}\gamma_5=0\).  The first surviving contribution is
  linear in the spin coupling:
  \[
    \frac{M^2}{4\pi}
      \left(-\frac{iq}{4M^2}\right)
      \operatorname{tr}\!
       \left(\gamma_5[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]\right)F_{\mu\nu}.
  \]
  Using the two-dimensional trace identity with
  \(\epsilon^{01}=+1\) gives
  \[
    \boxed{\mathcal A(x)
      =\frac{q}{2\pi}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}}
  \]
  after the same continuation is applied to the current.  Terms with more
  inverse powers of \(M\) vanish, while a zero-field term cannot survive
  the \(\gamma_5\) trace.  Combining the explicit mass breaking with the
  regulated Jacobian yields
  \[
    \boxed{\partial_\mu j_5^\mu
      =2im\overline\psi\gamma_5\psi
       +\frac{q}{2\pi}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}.}
  \]

  The regulator was built from the gauge-covariant Dirac operator, so the
  vector Ward identity remains exact.  The incompatible classical axial
  Ward identity is therefore the one that acquires the local anomaly.

\end{enumerate}
