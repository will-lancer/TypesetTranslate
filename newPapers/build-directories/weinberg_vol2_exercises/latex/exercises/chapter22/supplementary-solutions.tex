\begin{enumerate}
  \SupplementarySolution{1}{Fujikawa's Jacobian, Heat Kernel, and Index}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Let
    \(M_{mn}=\int d^4x\,\phi_m^\dagger U\phi_n\).  The transformed
    coefficients obey \(a'_m=M_{mn}a_n\), and the barred coefficients
    transform with the corresponding transpose matrix.  A Grassmann
    differential transforms inversely, so
    \[
      \prod_m da'_m=(\det M)^{-1}\prod_m da_m,\qquad
      \prod_m d\bar b'_m=(\det M)^{-1}\prod_m d\bar b_m.
    \]
    Hence
    \[
      [d\psi'][d\bar\psi']=(\det M)^{-2}[d\psi][d\bar\psi].
    \]
    For \(U=1+i\alpha\gamma_5T\),
    \[
      \ln J=-2\operatorname{Tr}\ln M
        =-2i\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha\gamma_5T)+O(\alpha^2).
    \]
    In a finite basis this is an ordinary trace.  In field theory it
    contains the undefined coincidence limit
    \(\delta^4(x-x)\).  The formal spin trace vanishes while the mode sum
    diverges, so their product cannot be assigned a value before a
    symmetry-preserving regulator is specified.
    \item[(b)]
    At short distance the diagonal heat kernel supplies an overall factor
    of order \(M^4\).  Expanding the exponential gives
    \[
      e^{-\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2}
        =e^{-D^2/M^2}
         \left[
          1-\frac{E}{M^2}
          +\frac{E^2}{2M^4}+\cdots
         \right],
      \qquad
      E=\frac14[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]F_{\mu\nu},
    \]
    up to derivative terms that are suppressed or combine covariantly.
    The \(M^4\) term contains
    \(\operatorname{tr}_D\gamma_5=0\).  The possible \(M^2\) term
    contains only two gamma matrices and vanishes because
    \(\operatorname{tr}_D(\gamma_5\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu)=0\).

    The \(E^2\) term contains four gamma matrices and is finite:
    \[
      \operatorname{tr}_D\!
        \left(\gamma_5
         [\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]
         [\gamma^\rho,\gamma^\sigma]\right)
        \propto\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
    \]
    Therefore
    \[
      \lim_{M\to\infty}\mathcal A_M(x)
        \propto
        \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
        \operatorname{tr}
         \bigl(TF_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}\bigr).
    \]
    Dimensional analysis also shows why this is the unique finite local
    term: two field strengths have dimension four and exactly compensate
    the four-dimensional heat-kernel measure.
    \item[(c)]
    After Fourier transforming the coincidence limit and scaling
    \(k=M\ell\), gamma-matrix counting again selects the term quadratic in
    the curvature.  Its regulator dependence is proportional to
    \[
      \int d^4\ell\,f''(\ell^2)
        =\pi^2\int_0^\infty ds\,s f''(s).
    \]
    Integrating twice by parts,
    \[
      \int_0^\infty ds\,s f''(s)
        =[sf'(s)]_0^\infty-\int_0^\infty f'(s)\,ds
        =f(0)-f(\infty)=1.
    \]
    Thus every admissible smooth regulator gives the same coefficient.
    Changing the profile of \(f\) changes only terms that vanish with
    \(M^{-1}\) or local scheme-dependent terms that do not alter the
    anomaly class.
    \item[(d)]
    If \(\lambda_n\neq0\), anticommutation gives
    \[
      i\slashed D(\gamma_5\phi_n)
        =-\lambda_n(\gamma_5\phi_n).
    \]
    The two states have the same value of
    \(f(\lambda_n^2/M^2)\), while their matrix elements of \(\gamma_5\)
    enter with opposite signs.  Hence every nonzero pair cancels in
    \[
      \operatorname{Tr}\!
        \left[\gamma_5f(\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2)\right].
    \]
    Zero modes can instead be chosen to satisfy
    \(\gamma_5\phi=\pm\phi\).  Because \(f(0)=1\), only these modes remain,
    and integration over spacetime gives
    \[
      \boxed{\operatorname{Tr}\!
        \left[\gamma_5f(\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2)\right]
        =n_+-n_-=\operatorname{ind}(i\slashed D).}
    \]
    The heat-kernel evaluation of the same trace equates this integer to
    the integral of the Chern--Pontryagin density.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{2}{Anomalous Ward Identities and Removable Terms}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    In a background field,
    \[
      \partial_\mu\langle J_5^\mu(x)\rangle_A
        =C F_{\alpha\beta}(x)\widetilde F^{\alpha\beta}(x).
    \]
    Each derivative with respect to \(A\) inserts a vector current.  At
    \(A=0\),
    \[
      \frac{\delta^2(F_{\alpha\beta}\widetilde F^{\alpha\beta})(x)}
        {\delta A_\nu(y)\delta A_\rho(z)}
        =
        4\epsilon^{\nu\rho\lambda\sigma}
         \partial_\lambda^x\delta^4(x-y)
         \partial_\sigma^x\delta^4(x-z),
    \]
    with the overall sign fixed by the definition of
    \(\widetilde F\).  Thus, suppressing conventional factors of \(i\)
    associated with current insertions,
    \[
      \partial_\mu^x
      \langle T J_5^\mu(x)J^\nu(y)J^\rho(z)\rangle
        =
        4C\epsilon^{\nu\rho\lambda\sigma}
         \partial_\lambda^x\delta^4(x-y)
         \partial_\sigma^x\delta^4(x-z).
    \]
    Fourier transformation gives
    \[
      q_\mu T^{\mu\nu\rho}(k_1,k_2)
        =4C\epsilon^{\nu\rho\lambda\sigma}
         k_{1\lambda}k_{2\sigma},
      \qquad q=k_1+k_2,
    \]
    again up to the common Fourier-sign convention.
    \item[(b)]
    If
    \(\mathcal G(\alpha,A)=\delta_\alpha B[A]\), define
    \(\Gamma'=\Gamma-B\).  Then
    \[
      \delta_\alpha\Gamma'
        =\mathcal G-\delta_\alpha B=0.
    \]
    Locality of \(B\) is essential: a nonlocal subtraction would change
    the physical long-distance theory rather than select a
    renormalization prescription.

    In BRST language replace \(\alpha\) by the ghost \(\omega\).
    Consistency requires \(s\mathcal G=0\).  An anomaly of the form
    \(\mathcal G=sB\) is exact and removable, while a closed functional
    that is not exact defines a nonzero class in local BRST cohomology at
    ghost number one.  Finiteness and locality say that the obstruction is
    a definite local quantum effect; non-exactness says that no allowed
    counterterm can erase it.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{3}{Spectral Flow from Two to Four Dimensions}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    The number of momentum levels per interval \(dp\) is
    \(L\,dp/(2\pi)\).  In time \(dt\), every charged level moves by
    \(dp=qE\,dt\), so
    \[
      dN_{\rm cross}=\frac{qEL}{2\pi}\,dt.
    \]
    A crossing creates a right-moving particle and a left-moving hole, so
    the axial charge \(Q_5=N_R-N_L\) changes by two units.  Therefore
    \[
      \frac{dQ_5}{dt}
        =2\frac{qEL}{2\pi}
        =\frac{qEL}{\pi}.
    \]
    Dividing by \(L\) gives
    \[
      \boxed{\partial_\mu J_5^\mu=\frac{q}{\pi}E}
    \]
    for the convention in which \(J_5^\mu\) counts chirality but does not
    include an additional factor of \(q\).  Reversing the orientation of
    the circle or the sign convention for \(E\) reverses both sides.
    \item[(b)]
    Each lowest-Landau-level channel behaves as a massless
    \(1+1\)-dimensional fermion along the magnetic field.  The number of
    channels per transverse area is
    \[
      \frac{|qB|}{2\pi}.
    \]
    Multiplying by the two-dimensional production rate per unit length,
    and using the spin orientation of the lowest level to restore the sign
    of \(qB\), gives
    \[
      \frac1V\frac{dQ_5}{dt}
        =\frac{qE}{\pi}\frac{qB}{2\pi}
        =\boxed{\frac{q^2}{2\pi^2}\,
          \mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B}.
    \]
    This is the usual four-dimensional axial anomaly.  In covariant
    notation it is proportional to
    \(q^2F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}\); the sign relating that scalar
    to \(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B\) depends on the definitions of
    \(F_{0i}\) and \(\widetilde F\).
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{4}{Chiral Spectra, Representation Indices, and Anomaly Matching}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    The local gauge anomaly and the mixed gauge--gravitational anomaly
    vanish when
    \[
      \sum_iq_i^3=0,\qquad \sum_iq_i=0.
    \]
    Here
    \[
      1+5-7-8+9=0,
    \]
    and
    \[
      1^3+5^3-7^3-8^3+9^3
        =1+125-343-512+729=0.
    \]
    A Lorentz-invariant mass pairs two left-handed Weyl fields of opposite
    charges, or permits a Majorana mass for a neutral field.  The set has
    neither a zero charge nor any pair \(q,-q\).  Thus it is anomaly-free
    but genuinely chiral: none of its fields can be removed by a
    gauge-invariant mass term.
    \item[(b)]
    On \(R\otimes S\),
    \(T^a=T_R^a\otimes\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}\otimes T_S^a\).
    In the completely symmetric trace, all mixed terms contain
    \(\operatorname{tr}T_R^a\) or \(\operatorname{tr}T_S^a\), which
    vanishes for a simple algebra.  The unmixed terms give
    \[
      A(R\otimes S)=d(S)A(R)+d(R)A(S).
    \]
    Complex conjugation reverses all three generators, so
    \(A(\overline R)=-A(R)\).

    For \(SU(3)\),
    \[
      A(\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3)=3+3=6.
    \]
    But
    \(\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3=\mathbf6\oplus\overline{\mathbf3}\),
    whence
    \[
      6=A(\mathbf6)-1,\qquad
      \boxed{A(\mathbf6)=7.}
    \]
    \item[(c)]
    The background-field variation of the generating functional is a
    local functional representing the anomaly.  Integrating out a
    momentum shell can add local counterterms, which change the anomaly
    only by a cohomologically trivial variation.  A nontrivial class,
    often quantized by charges or topology, therefore cannot vary
    continuously with \(\mu\).

    The infrared functional must reproduce the same background variation.
    Gapless particles may do so through their own anomalous loops.  If the
    symmetry is spontaneously broken, Goldstone fields reproduce it
    through a Wess--Zumino-type action.  A gapped phase may instead carry
    a topological sector whose response has the required variation.
    A unique, trivially gapped, symmetry-preserving infrared theory is
    impossible when the ultraviolet anomaly is nonzero.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{5}{Standard-Model Anomaly Cancellation}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] Including color and weak multiplicities,
      \[
        \sum_{\rm LH}Y^3
        =6\left(\frac16\right)^3
        +3\left(-\frac23\right)^3
        +3\left(\frac13\right)^3
        +2\left(-\frac12\right)^3+1=0.
      \]
    \item[(b)] A triangle with one generator \(T^a\) of a simple
      \(SU(N)\) factor and two commuting \(U(1)\) generators is
      proportional to \(\operatorname{tr}T^a=0\).  The same trace
      factorizes for \(SU(N)G_1G_2\) when the other generators act on
      different tensor factors, so it vanishes as well.
    \item[(c)] The QCD spectrum is vectorlike: for every left-handed color
      triplet contribution there is a conjugate right-handed contribution.
      Equivalently, in a left-handed basis the triplets and antitriplets
      have opposite cubic indices, so the \(SU(3)^3\) sum is zero.
    \item[(d)] The doublet of \(SU(2)\) is pseudoreal, and
      \(\{\tau^a,\tau^b\}\propto\delta^{ab}\mathbf{1}\).  Contracting with a
      third traceless generator gives
      \(\operatorname{tr}(\{\tau^a,\tau^b\}\tau^c)=0\).
      Thus there is no local \(SU(2)^3\) anomaly.  This statement is
      separate from the global Witten anomaly.
    \item[(e)] Taking \(T(\mathbf3)=1/2\),
      \[
        \mathcal A_{33Y}
          =\frac12(2Y_Q-Y_u-Y_d).
      \]
      The Standard-Model values give
      \(2(1/6)-2/3-(-1/3)=0\).
    \item[(f)] Similarly,
      \[
        \mathcal A_{22Y}
          =\frac12(3Y_Q+Y_L)
          =\frac12\left(\frac12-\frac12\right)=0.
      \]
    \item[(g)] The mixed gravitational coefficient is the linear trace:
      \[
        6\left(\frac16\right)
        +3\left(-\frac23\right)+3\left(\frac13\right)
        +2\left(-\frac12\right)+1=0.
      \]
    \item[(h)] Let the physical right-handed charges be
      \(Y_u,Y_d,Y_e,Y_\nu=0\), and put \(Y_Q=q\).  The mixed conditions give
      \[
        Y_L=-3q,\qquad Y_u+Y_d=2q,\qquad Y_e=2Y_L=-6q.
      \]
      The cubic condition then implies
      \(Y_uY_d=-8q^2\), so
      \(\{Y_u,Y_d\}=\{4q,-2q\}\).  Up to exchanging the names \(u,d\)
      and choosing the normalization \(q=1/6\), these are precisely the
      Standard-Model hypercharges.  The nontrivial solution is chiral.
    \item[(i)] Per generation the \(SU(2)^2B\) coefficient is
      \(3(1/3)T(\mathbf2)=1/2\), and the \(SU(2)^2L\) coefficient is also
      \(T(\mathbf2)=1/2\).  The anomalous changes therefore obey
      \(\Delta B=\Delta L\), so \(B+L\) is violated and \(B-L\) is
      preserved.  The same cancellation holds for all gauge and
      gravitational anomaly coefficients of \(B-L\) when the
      hypercharge-neutral right-handed neutrino is included; \(B\) and
      \(L\) separately remain anomalous.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{6}{Characteristic Forms and BRST Descent}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    The Bianchi identity is \(DF=0\).  Since the trace of a covariant
    derivative equals the ordinary exterior derivative,
    \[
      d\,\operatorname{Tr}F^n
        =\operatorname{Tr}D(F^n)
        =n\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((DF)F^{n-1}\bigr)=0.
    \]
    Under an infinitesimal gauge transformation,
    \(\delta_\alpha F=[F,\alpha]\), and hence
    \[
      \delta_\alpha\operatorname{Tr}F^n
        =n\operatorname{Tr}\bigl([F,\alpha]F^{n-1}\bigr)=0
    \]
    by cyclicity.  Thus Bianchi plus cyclicity prove closedness and gauge
    invariance.  Closed invariant forms have topological integrals and,
    through descent, furnish local anomaly polynomials in two dimensions
    higher than the anomalous theory.
    \item[(b)]
    Acting successively on \(\Gamma\),
    \[
      [\delta_\alpha,\delta_\beta]\Gamma
        =\delta_\alpha\mathcal G(\beta,A)
         -\delta_\beta\mathcal G(\alpha,A).
    \]
    The gauge algebra requires this to equal
    \(\delta_{[\alpha,\beta]}\Gamma
      =\mathcal G([\alpha,\beta],A)\).  Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{\delta_\alpha\mathcal G(\beta,A)
        -\delta_\beta\mathcal G(\alpha,A)
        =\mathcal G([\alpha,\beta],A).}
    \]
    For an Abelian group the commutator vanishes, leaving
    \[
      \delta_\alpha\mathcal G(\beta,A)
        =\delta_\beta\mathcal G(\alpha,A).
    \]
    This is an integrability condition on any proposed Abelian anomaly.
    \item[(c)]
    Replacing the gauge parameter by the ghost packages the gauge algebra
    into \(s^2=0\).  The two successive variations in the Wess--Zumino
    condition are exactly the terms in \(s\mathcal G\), so consistency is
    \[
      s\mathcal G=0.
    \]
    If \(\mathcal G=sB\), redefining the effective action by
    \(\Gamma'=\Gamma-B\) gives \(s\Gamma'=0\).  Such a term is therefore
    removable.  Candidate anomalies are classified by
    \[
      \boxed{H_{\rm loc}^{\,1}(s)
        =\frac{\{\text{local }\mathcal G:\operatorname{gh}\mathcal G=1,
            \ s\mathcal G=0\}}
         {\{\mathcal G=sB:\operatorname{gh}B=0,\ B\text{ local}\}}.}
    \]
    If total derivatives are retained locally, this is written
    \(H_{\rm loc}^{1}(s\mid d)\).
    \item[(d)]
    Direct differentiation, using graded cyclicity, gives
    \[
      d\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\,dA+\frac23A^3\right)
        =\operatorname{Tr}\left(dA\,dA+2A^2dA\right)
        =\operatorname{Tr}F^2.
    \]
    Under \(sA=-D\omega\), the variation of
    \(\operatorname{Tr}F^2\) vanishes.  Hence \(d(sQ_3)=0\), and locally
    \(sQ_3\) is exact.  A standard representative is
    \[
      \boxed{Q_2^{\,1}=\operatorname{Tr}(\omega\,dA),\qquad
        sQ_3+dQ_2^{\,1}=0.}
    \]
    Adding \(d\operatorname{Tr}(\omega A)\) changes the representative
    but not its integrated anomaly class.  This is the first nontrivial
    step of the descent from the four-form \(\operatorname{Tr}F^2\).
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{7}{Bulk and Axion-String Anomaly Inflow}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Since \(F\) is gauge invariant,
    \[
      \delta_\alpha S_{\rm CS}
        =k\int_{X_5}d\alpha\wedge F\wedge F
        =k\int_{X_5}d(\alpha F\wedge F)
        =k\int_{M_4}\alpha F\wedge F.
    \]
    Thus a boundary theory with
    \[
      \delta_\alpha\Gamma_{\partial}
        =-k\int_{M_4}\alpha F\wedge F
    \]
    is rendered consistent by the bulk term:
    \(\delta_\alpha(S_{\rm CS}+\Gamma_\partial)=0\).
    Gauge charge flows between the boundary and bulk.  Separate
    conservation fails in each subsystem, but the combined system has an
    exactly conserved gauge current.
    \item[(b)]
    For an Abelian field,
    \(F\wedge F=d(A\wedge F)\).  Away from the string,
    \[
      S_\theta
        =c\int d(\theta A\wedge F)
         -c\int d\theta\wedge A\wedge F.
    \]
    The second term defines a bulk Chern--Simons current proportional to
    \(d\theta\wedge F\).  Although \(d^2\theta=0\) for a smooth
    single-valued field, a \(2\pi\) winding gives distributionally
    \[
      d^2\theta=2\pi\,\delta_2(\Sigma),
    \]
    where \(\Sigma\) is the string world sheet.  Consequently
    \[
      d{*J_{\rm inflow}}
        =-2\pi c\,\delta_2(\Sigma)\wedge F
    \]
    up to the orientation convention.  The right-hand side is localized
    on \(\Sigma\) and has precisely the form of the two-dimensional gauge
    anomaly of chiral zero modes.  Choosing \(c\) from the underlying
    fermion anomaly makes their sum vanish.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{8}{The Axial Anomaly in Dimensional Regularization}
  Define \(\gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3\) and split every
  \(d\)-dimensional object into four-dimensional and evanescent parts,
  \(\ell=\bar\ell+\widehat\ell\).  Take \(\gamma_5\) to anticommute with
  \(\bar\gamma^\mu\) and commute with \(\widehat\gamma^\mu\).  This
  't~Hooft--Veltman prescription preserves shifts of the regulated
  integral and hence both vector Ward identities.

  With incoming photon momenta \(k_1,k_2\) and
  \(q=k_1+k_2\), include both orientations of the fermion loop:
  \[
    T^{\lambda\mu\nu}
      =-e^2\int\frac{d^d\ell}{(2\pi)^d}
      \frac{\operatorname{tr}[
       \gamma^\lambda\gamma_5\slashed\ell\gamma^\mu
       (\slashed\ell+\slashed k_1)\gamma^\nu
       (\slashed\ell-\slashed k_2)]}
      {\ell^2(\ell+k_1)^2(\ell-k_2)^2}
      +(\mu,k_1\leftrightarrow\nu,k_2).
  \]
  Contracting with \(k_{1\mu}\) or \(k_{2\nu}\), writing the momentum as a
  difference of adjacent inverse propagators, and shifting \(\ell\)
  proves \(k_{1\mu}T^{\lambda\mu\nu}
  =k_{2\nu}T^{\lambda\mu\nu}=0\).

  For the axial contraction, the same cancellation would occur in exactly
  four dimensions.  The failure to anticommute \(\gamma_5\) through
  \(\widehat{\slashed\ell}\) leaves
  \[
    q_\lambda T^{\lambda\mu\nu}
      =-8e^2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k_{1\rho}k_{2\sigma}
       \int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy
       \int\frac{d^dL}{(2\pi)^d}
       \frac{\widehat L^{\,2}}{(L^2-\Delta+i0)^3}.
  \]
  Here
  \(\Delta=-xy\,q^2-x(1-x-y)k_1^2
  -y(1-x-y)k_2^2\); its precise value drops out of the finite anomalous
  term.  Rotational symmetry in \(d\) dimensions gives
  \[
    \int d^dL\,\widehat L^{\,2}f(L^2)
      =\frac{d-4}{d}\int d^dL\,L^2f(L^2).
  \]
  The second integral has a \(1/(d-4)\) ultraviolet pole.  Using
  \[
    \int\frac{d^dL_E}{(2\pi)^d}
      \frac{L_E^2}{(L_E^2+\Delta)^3}
      =\frac{d}{2(4\pi)^{d/2}}
        \Gamma\!\left(2-\frac d2\right)\Delta^{d/2-2}
  \]
  and the Feynman-parameter area \(1/2\), the evanescent zero times the
  ultraviolet pole has the finite limit
  \[
    \boxed{q_\lambda T^{\lambda\mu\nu}
      =-\frac{e^2}{2\pi^2}
       \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k_{1\rho}k_{2\sigma},}
  \]
  with the overall sign tied to the stated \(\epsilon\) and Fourier
  conventions.

  Finally, between two photon states the matrix element is
  \[
    \langle\gamma_1\gamma_2|
      F^{\alpha\beta}\widetilde F_{\alpha\beta}|0\rangle
    =8\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k_{1\rho}k_{2\sigma}
      \varepsilon_{1\mu}\varepsilon_{2\nu},
  \]
  including the two ways of assigning the external photons.
  Multiplication by \(-e^2/(16\pi^2)\) reproduces the contracted
  triangle.  Thus the regulated calculation is exactly the matrix
  element of the operator equation in the exercise.

  \SupplementarySolution{9}{Electroweak \(B\), \(L\), and \(B+L\) Selection Rules}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Only left-handed \(SU(2)_L\) doublets contribute.  Per generation the
    quark doublet has three colors, baryon number \(1/3\), and index
    \(T(\mathbf2)=1/2\), so
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2B}^{(1)}
        =3\left(\frac13\right)\frac12=\frac12.
    \]
    Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{\mathcal A_{SU(2)^2B}=\frac{N_g}{2}.}
    \]
    Right-handed quarks are \(SU(2)_L\) singlets and have zero quadratic
    index, so they do not affect this coefficient.
    \item[(b)]
    Each generation has one lepton doublet of lepton number \(1\), giving
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2L}^{(1)}=1\cdot\frac12=\frac12.
    \]
    Thus the baryon and lepton coefficients are equal:
    \[
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2(B-L)}=0,\qquad
      \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2(B+L)}=N_g.
    \]
    A unit electroweak topological transition produces one unit of baryon
    number and one unit of lepton number per generation,
    \[
      \boxed{\Delta B=\Delta L=N_g,\qquad
        \Delta(B-L)=0,\qquad
        \Delta(B+L)=2N_g.}
    \]
    \item[(c)]
    With the topological-charge convention in the problem, the local
    equations are
    \[
      \partial_\mu J_B^\mu
        =N_g\frac{g^2}{32\pi^2}
          W^a_{\mu\nu}\widetilde W^{a\mu\nu},
      \qquad
      \partial_\mu J_L^\mu
        =N_g\frac{g^2}{32\pi^2}
          W^a_{\mu\nu}\widetilde W^{a\mu\nu}.
    \]
    Hence
    \[
      \partial_\mu J_{B+L}^\mu
        =2N_g\frac{g^2}{32\pi^2}
          W^a_{\mu\nu}\widetilde W^{a\mu\nu}.
    \]
    Integrating over spacetime gives
    \[
      \boxed{\Delta B=\Delta L=N_g\nu,\qquad
        \Delta(B+L)=2N_g\nu,\qquad
        \Delta(B-L)=0.}
    \]
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{10}{Anomaly Poles, Symmetric Masses, and Real Representations}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Let \(q\) enter the current whose divergence is anomalous.  If the
    correlator were analytic at \(q=0\), its Taylor expansion would contain
    no term whose contraction with \(q_\lambda\) reproduces a fixed
    nonzero anomalous polynomial at the required order while maintaining
    the other Ward identities.  The longitudinal part must instead have
    the form
    \[
      T_{\rm L}^{\lambda\mu\nu}
        =C\frac{q^\lambda}{q^2}
         \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
         k_{1\rho}k_{2\sigma},
    \]
    so that contraction with \(q_\lambda\) removes the pole and yields the
    anomaly.

    The \(1/q^2\) singularity signals massless propagation.  In a symmetric
    confining phase it must be supplied by massless composite fermions.
    In a broken phase a Goldstone pole and its anomalous effective
    interaction can supply it.  A completely massive, symmetry-preserving
    spectrum with no topological response cannot.
    \item[(b)]
    A symmetry-preserving Dirac mass pairs \(R\) with \(\overline R\); a
    Majorana mass is possible only in a real or pseudoreal representation.
    Since
    \[
      A(\overline R)=-A(R),\qquad
      A(R)=0\quad\text{for real or pseudoreal }R,
    \]
    every massive block has zero cubic \(G^3\) anomaly.

    At external momenta \(p\ll m\), massive loops are analytic in \(p/m\)
    and can be represented by local invariant operators.  They cannot
    produce the nonanalytic massless pole required by a nonzero anomaly.
    Thus only degrees of freedom that remain massless, or a Goldstone or
    topological sector, can carry the infrared matching contribution.
    \item[(c)]
    Complex conjugating the symmetric trace and using the intertwiner,
    \[
      D_R^{abc*}
        =\operatorname{tr}
         \left[T_R^{a*}\{T_R^{b*},T_R^{c*}\}\right]
        =-D_R^{abc}.
    \]
    For Hermitian generators \(D_R^{abc}\) is real: cyclicity makes the
    trace of \(T^aT^bT^c+T^aT^cT^b\) equal to its complex conjugate.
    Therefore
    \[
      \boxed{D_R^{abc}=0.}
    \]
    All finite-dimensional \(SU(2)\) representations are real or
    pseudoreal, so no perturbative cubic anomaly exists.  The argument is
    local and perturbative; it does not test the sign of the fermion
    determinant under a large gauge transformation, which is where a
    global \(SU(2)\) anomaly can reside.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{11}{Spectator Matching and a Wess--Zumino Compensator}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Let the confined elementary fermions have anomaly \(A_{\rm UV}\).
    Add spectators of anomaly \(-A_{\rm UV}\), making the weakly gauged
    ultraviolet theory consistent.  Confinement does not affect the
    spectators.  If \(G\) remains unbroken, there are no Goldstone bosons
    to carry an anomalous gauge variation.

    The infrared theory must still be a consistent gauge theory.  Its
    anomaly is
    \[
      A_{\rm IR}^{\rm composite}-A_{\rm UV}.
    \]
    Gauge consistency forces this to vanish, and therefore
    \[
      \boxed{A_{\rm IR}^{\rm composite}=A_{\rm UV}.}
    \]
    This conclusion uses no weak-coupling expansion for the confining
    dynamics; it follows from the consistency of the arbitrarily weak
    spectator gauging.
    \item[(b)]
    In the ultraviolet, the three color copies of the left-handed flavor
    doublet each have baryon number \(1/3\).  Hence
    \[
      \mathcal A_{\rm UV}
        =3\left(\frac13\right)T(\mathbf2)
        =\frac12.
    \]
    A single left-handed nucleon doublet has baryon number \(1\), so
    \[
      \mathcal A_{\rm IR}=1\cdot T(\mathbf2)=\frac12.
    \]
    It matches the perturbative \(SU(2)_L^2U(1)_B\) anomaly.

    There are three ultraviolet \(SU(2)_L\) doublets and one proposed
    infrared doublet.  Both numbers are odd, so their parity also agrees.
    This is the datum relevant to the nonperturbative mod-two \(SU(2)\)
    anomaly, although the perturbative cubic \(SU(2)^3\) anomaly vanishes
    identically.
    \item[(c)]
    Add
    \[
      S_{\rm WZ}=\frac{C}{f}\int a\,F\wedge F.
    \]
    Because \(\delta_\alpha a=-f\alpha\),
    \[
      \delta_\alpha S_{\rm WZ}
        =-C\int\alpha F\wedge F,
    \]
    which cancels the variation of \(\Gamma\).

    The one-form
    \[
      da+fA
    \]
    is gauge invariant when \(A\to A+d\alpha\).  The canonical kinetic
    term is therefore
    \[
      S_{\rm kin}=-\frac12\int(da+fA)\wedge{*}(da+fA).
    \]
    In the gauge \(a=0\), it contains
    \(-f^2A_\mu A^\mu/2\).  For a canonically normalized gauge kinetic
    term,
    \[
      \boxed{m_A=|f|.}
    \]
    The anomaly has been cancelled by adding a transforming degree of
    freedom, not by a counterterm built from \(A\) alone.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementarySolution{12}{Heat-Kernel Derivation of the Two-Dimensional Chiral Anomaly}
  The fermion equations and their adjoints give the classical identity
  \[
    \partial_\mu j_5^\mu
      =2im\overline\psi\gamma_5\psi
  \]
  with the current convention in the question.  Quantum mechanically the
  composite divergence contains a coincident-point trace.  A
  gauge-covariant regulator defines its anomalous part by
  \[
    \mathcal A(x)
      =2\lim_{M\to\infty}
       \operatorname{tr}\!
       \left[\gamma_5e^{-\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2}\right]_{x,x},
  \]
  where the overall \(i\) is supplied or removed by the Euclidean
  continuation convention.

  The squared Dirac operator has the form
  \[
    \slashed D^{\,2}
      =D^2+\frac{iq}{4}
        [\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]F_{\mu\nu}.
  \]
  The diagonal free heat kernel is
  \(M^2/(4\pi)+O(M^0)\).  Its leading term has
  \(\operatorname{tr}\gamma_5=0\).  The first surviving contribution is
  linear in the spin coupling:
  \[
    \frac{M^2}{4\pi}
      \left(-\frac{iq}{4M^2}\right)
      \operatorname{tr}\!
       \left(\gamma_5[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]\right)F_{\mu\nu}.
  \]
  Using the two-dimensional trace identity with
  \(\epsilon^{01}=+1\) gives
  \[
    \boxed{\mathcal A(x)
      =\frac{q}{2\pi}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}}
  \]
  after the same continuation is applied to the current.  Terms with more
  inverse powers of \(M\) vanish, while a zero-field term cannot survive
  the \(\gamma_5\) trace.  Combining the explicit mass breaking with the
  regulated Jacobian yields
  \[
    \boxed{\partial_\mu j_5^\mu
      =2im\overline\psi\gamma_5\psi
       +\frac{q}{2\pi}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}.}
  \]

  The regulator was built from the gauge-covariant Dirac operator, so the
  vector Ward identity remains exact.  The incompatible classical axial
  Ward identity is therefore the one that acquires the local anomaly.

\end{enumerate}
