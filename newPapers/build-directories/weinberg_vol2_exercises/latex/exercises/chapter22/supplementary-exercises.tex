  \SupplementaryExercise{1}{Fujikawa's Jacobian, Heat Kernel, and Index}
  {Inspired by Adel Bilal, Lectures on Anomalies (2008),
  Section 3.}
  {bilal-lectures-anomalies-2008-v2ch22-p01}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Expand independent Grassmann fields in a finite orthonormal basis,
    \(\psi=\sum_na_n\phi_n\) and
    \(\bar\psi=\sum_n\bar b_n\phi_n^\dagger\).  Under
    \(\psi'=U\psi\), \(\bar\psi'=\bar\psi U\), derive the transformation
    of \(\prod_n \dd a_n\,\dd\bar b_n\).  Specialize to
    \(U=1+\ii\alpha\gamma_5T\) and explain why the formal trace requires a
    regulator in the infinite-dimensional limit.
    \item[(b)]
    In Euclidean space regulate the chiral Jacobian with
    \[
      \mathcal A_M(x)
        =-2\,\operatorname{tr}\!
         \left[\gamma_5T e^{-\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2}\right]_{x,x}.
    \]
    Use
    \(\slashed D^{\,2}=D^2+\tfrac14[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]F_{\mu\nu}\)
    and gamma-matrix counting to show that the first finite term as
    \(M\to\infty\) is proportional to
    \(\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
      \operatorname{tr}(T F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma})\).
    Explain why the terms of orders \(M^4\) and \(M^2\) vanish.
    \item[(c)]
    Replace the exponential in part~(b) by a smooth function
    \(f(-\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2)\) satisfying
    \(f(0)=1\), \(f(\infty)=0\), and
    \(sf'(s)\to0\) at both endpoints.  Show that the coefficient of the
    four-dimensional anomaly depends only on these endpoint values, not
    on the shape of \(f\).
    \item[(d)]
    Let \(\ii\slashed D\phi_n=\lambda_n\phi_n\) in Euclidean space and
    \(\{\gamma_5,\slashed D\}=0\).  Prove that all nonzero eigenvalues
    occur in opposite-sign pairs whose contributions to
    \(\operatorname{Tr}\gamma_5f(\slashed D^{\,2}/M^2)\) cancel.  Show
    that the integrated regulated trace equals \(n_+-n_-\), the index of
    the Dirac operator.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementaryExercise{2}{Axial and Standard-Model Anomalies}
  {Adapted from Cambridge Part III, The Standard Model, 2021 Paper 305,
  Question 2.}
  {cambridge-part-iii-standard-model-2021-p305-q2-v2ch22-p02}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)]
    Define an anomaly in quantum field theory.  Distinguish carefully
    between an anomaly in a global symmetry and an anomaly in a local
    gauge symmetry, and explain the physical consequence in each case.
    \item[(b)]
    Consider a massless Dirac fermion of charge \(e\) coupled to a
    \(U(1)\) gauge field.  In the volume's mostly-plus convention take
    \[
      \mathcal L=-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
        -\overline\psi\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu-ieA_\mu)\psi,
      \qquad
      \gamma_5=\ii\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3 .
    \]
    Show that the vector gauge current is conserved.  Show also that the
    global axial transformation
    \(\psi\mapsto e^{\ii\beta\gamma_5}\psi\) has the classically conserved
    current
    \[
      J_5^\mu=-\ii\overline\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\psi .
    \]
    Using a regulator that preserves the vector gauge symmetry, derive the
    quantum anomaly
    \[
      \boxed{\partial_\mu J_5^\mu
        =-\frac{e^2}{16\pi^2}
          \epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
          F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta}.}
    \]
    You may use
    \[
      \operatorname{tr}_{D}\!\left[
        \gamma_5[\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu]
        [\gamma_\rho,\gamma_\sigma]\right]
        =16\ii\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
    \]
    The source uses mostly-minus gamma matrices and writes its axial
    bilinear without the displayed factor \(-\ii\); the current and kinetic
    term above are the explicit translation to the volume convention.
    \item[(c)]
    Verify that one Standard-Model family is free of all perturbative gauge
    anomalies.  Include the mixed gauge--gravitational check, and explain
    why the cancellation relates the proton and electron charges by
    \(Q_p=-Q_e\).

    For unambiguous anomaly bookkeeping, use only left-handed fields:
    \[
    \begin{gathered}
      Q:(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{1/6},\qquad
      u^c:(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-2/3},\qquad
      d^c:(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{1/3},\\
      L:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/2},\qquad
      e^c:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_1,\qquad
      \nu^c:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0 .
    \end{gathered}
    \]
    \emph{Source convention note.}  The examination's recall line prints
    conjugate color representations together with the hypercharge signs of
    physical right-handed fields.  Those two conventions cannot be used
    simultaneously in a left-handed anomaly sum; the consistent
    all-left-handed list above makes the required conjugation explicit.
  \end{enumerate}

  \SupplementaryExercise{3}{Standard-Model Anomalies, CKM Mixing, and CP Violation}
  {Adapted from Cambridge Part III, The Standard Model, 2022 Paper 305,
  Question 4.}
  {cambridge-part-iii-standard-model-2022-p305-q4-v2ch22-p03}
  Consider one Standard-Model family, including a right-handed neutrino.
  For anomaly calculations, write every fermion as a left-handed field:
  \[
  \begin{gathered}
    Q:(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{1/6},\qquad
    u^c:(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-2/3},\qquad
    d^c:(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{1/3},\\
    L:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/2},\qquad
    e^c:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_1,\qquad
    \nu^c:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0,
  \end{gathered}
  \]
  under \(SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\).
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)]
    Show that all perturbative gauge anomalies and the mixed
    gauge--gravitational anomaly cancel for this spectrum.  Include the
    \(SU(3)^3\), \(SU(3)^2U(1)_Y\), \(SU(2)^2U(1)_Y\),
    \(U(1)_Y^3\), and gravitational--\(U(1)_Y\) checks, and explain why
    there is no perturbative \(SU(2)^3\) anomaly.  Also check the
    nonperturbative mod-two \(SU(2)\) anomaly.
    \item[(ii)]
    What would go wrong with the quantum gauge theory if any gauge anomaly
    failed to cancel?
    \item[(iii)]
    Is baryon number anomalous in the Standard Model?  Identify the gauge
    fields responsible, state the resulting selection rule for a
    topological transition of unit charge, and discuss how significant the
    effect is in ordinary perturbation theory and at high temperature.
    \item[(iv)]
    For three generations, write the quark Yukawa couplings and the charged
    weak-current coupling,
    \[
      -\mathcal L_Y
       =\overline Q_LY_dHd_R+\overline Q_LY_u\widetilde H u_R
        +\mathrm{H.c.},
      \qquad
      \mathcal L_W=\frac{g}{\sqrt2}\,
        \overline u_L\gamma^\mu d_LW^+_\mu+\mathrm{H.c.}
    \]
    Diagonalize the two Yukawa matrices, define the
    Cabibbo--Kobayashi--Maskawa matrix in the mass eigenbasis, and determine
    the number of its independent physical parameters.
    \item[(v)]
    Exhibit two independent sources of CP violation in the Standard Model.
    State the associated basis-invariant quantities, and explain whether
    the two sources can be related by field redefinitions.
  \end{enumerate}

  \emph{Source convention note.}  The examination's recall line combines
  conjugate color representations with the hypercharge signs of physical
  right-handed fields.  Those conventions cannot be mixed in a chiral
  anomaly sum; the consistent all-left-handed spectrum displayed above is
  the intended one.

  \SupplementaryExercise{4}{Anomalies Determine Standard-Model Hypercharge}
  {Adapted from Cambridge Part III, The Standard Model, 2024 Paper 305,
  Question 3.}
  {cambridge-part-iii-standard-model-2024-p305-q3-v2ch22-p04}
  Explain first the meaning and physical implications of each of the
  following: a gauge anomaly, a chiral or Adler--Bell--Jackiw anomaly, and
  a 't~Hooft anomaly.

  Consider one Standard-Model generation, without a right-handed neutrino,
  whose left- and right-handed Weyl fermions transform under
  \(U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)\) as
  \[
    Q_L:(\mathbf2,\mathbf3)_q,\qquad
    L_L:(\mathbf2,\mathbf1)_l,\qquad
    u_R:(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)_u,\qquad
    d_R:(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)_d,\qquad
    e_R:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_x.
  \]
  The integer subscripts are arbitrary hypercharges:
  \(q,l,u,d,x\in\mathbb Z\).  Remember that a right-handed fermion enters
  an anomaly sum with the opposite chirality sign.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)]
    Write all conditions for cancellation of local gauge anomalies for one
    generation, excluding the mixed gauge--gravitational anomaly.  Include
    the pure simple-group anomalies and all mixed anomalies, explaining
    which vanish identically.
    \item[(ii)]
    Show that the anomaly equations require
    \(u-d=2y\) for some \(y\in\mathbb Z\).
    \item[(iii)]
    Reduce the anomaly equations to the Diophantine equation
    \[
      54q^3+18y^2q+x^3=0.
    \]
    \item[(iv)]
    Assume \(q\ne0\).  Divide out the overall normalization and show that
    there must be rational numbers \(\widetilde x,\widetilde y\in\mathbb Q\)
    satisfying
    \[
      54+18\widetilde y^{\,2}+\widetilde x^{\,3}=0.
    \]
    \item[(v)]
    Retaining the assumption \(q\ne0\), show that the anomaly equations
    have a unique solution up to an overall rescaling and interchange of
    \(u_R\) and \(d_R\).  With \(q=1\), determine \(l,u,d,x\).

    You may assume without proof that there is no nontrivial integer
    solution of \(a^3+b^3=c^3\).  The change of variables
    \[
      \widetilde x=-\frac{6}{v+w},
      \qquad
      \widetilde y=\frac{3(v-w)}{v+w}
    \]
    may be useful.
    \item[(vi)]
    Show that this solution automatically satisfies the mixed
    gauge--gravitational anomaly.
  \end{enumerate}

  Finally, identify the additional vectorlike branch that would have been
  present had the assumption \(q\ne0\) not been imposed.  This qualification
  is logically necessary for the uniqueness claim in part~(v).

  \SupplementaryExercise{10}{Anomaly Matching in an \(SU(2)\) Gauge Theory}
  {Adapted from Cambridge Part III, The Standard Model, 2025 Paper 305,
  Question 3.}
  {cambridge-part-iii-standard-model-2025-p305-q3-v2ch22-p10}
  Briefly describe the implications when a symmetry has a gauge anomaly, a
  chiral or Adler--Bell--Jackiw anomaly, or a 't~Hooft anomaly.

  Consider \(SU(2)\) Yang--Mills theory coupled to \(N_f\) massless Dirac
  fermions in the fundamental representation.  In two-component notation,
  \[
    S_0=\int \dd^4x\left[-\frac12\operatorname{Tr}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
      +\ii\sum_{i=1}^{N_f}\left(
        \overline\psi_{L i}\overline\sigma^\mu D_\mu\psi_L^i
       +\overline\psi_{R i}\sigma^\mu D_\mu\psi_R^i\right)\right].
  \]
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)]
    The source stipulates the classical chiral subgroup
    \(SU(N_f)_L\times SU(N_f)_R\times U(1)_L\times U(1)_R\).
    What subgroup survives in the quantum theory?  Give a table showing
    how the fermions transform under it, and identify the discrete axial
    remnant.
    \item[(ii)]
    For \(N_f\ge3\), compute the
    \(SU(N_f)_L^3\), \(SU(N_f)_L^2U(1)\), \(U(1)^3\), and mixed
    \(U(1)\)--gravity 't~Hooft anomalies, where \(U(1)\) is the continuous
    Abelian symmetry that survives quantum mechanically.  Explain why,
    under the ordinary assumption of no compensating topological order,
    confinement implies chiral-symmetry breaking in this theory.
    \item[(iii)]
    Add a massless left-handed Weyl fermion \(\lambda_L\) in the adjoint
    representation of the gauge group.  Does the extended theory have a
    perturbative gauge anomaly or the mod-two global \(SU(2)\) anomaly?
    Justify both answers.
    \item[(iv)]
    The extended theory has a candidate Abelian symmetry
    \[
      U(1)_X:\qquad
      \psi_L\mapsto e^{-\ii\alpha}\psi_L,\qquad
      \psi_R\mapsto e^{\ii\alpha}\psi_R,\qquad
      \lambda_L\mapsto e^{\ii q\alpha}\lambda_L.
    \]
    Determine \(q\) by requiring the mixed
    \(SU(2)_{\rm gauge}^2U(1)_X\) anomaly to vanish.  Use the source
    normalization
    \[
      I(\mathbf2)=I(\overline{\mathbf2})=1,
      \qquad I(\operatorname{adj}_{SU(N)})=2N.
    \]
    \item[(v)]
    Compute the ultraviolet \(SU(N_f)_L^3\),
    \(SU(N_f)_L^2U(1)_X\), \(U(1)_X^3\), and mixed
    \(U(1)_X\)--gravity 't~Hooft anomalies of the extended theory.
    \item[(vi)]
    For \(N_f=3\), suppose that the theory confines without breaking its
    chiral symmetry and that its massless left-handed gauge singlets are
    \[
      \chi_i{}^j=\overline\psi_{R i}\lambda_L\psi_L^j,
      \qquad
      \eta_i=\epsilon_{ijk}\psi_L^j\lambda_L\psi_L^k,
      \qquad
      \widetilde\eta^{\,\ii}
       =\epsilon^{ijk}\overline\psi_{R j}\lambda_L
          \overline\psi_{R k}.
    \]
    Determine how \(\chi\), \(\eta\), and \(\widetilde\eta\) transform
    under the full continuous global subgroup, including \(U(1)_X\).
    \item[(vii)]
    Treating these composites as free Weyl fermions, calculate their
    \(SU(3)_L^3\), \(SU(3)_L^2U(1)_X\), \(U(1)_X^3\), and mixed
    \(U(1)_X\)--gravity anomalies and show that they match the ultraviolet
    anomalies.
  \end{enumerate}

  Normalize the flavor coefficients by
  \(A(\mathbf{N_f})=1\),
  \(A(\overline{\mathbf{N_f}})=-1\), and
  \(I(\mathbf{N_f})=I(\overline{\mathbf{N_f}})=1\).
  Because the \(SU(2)\) fundamental is pseudoreal, the actual continuous
  flavor symmetry of the massless theory is enhanced to \(SU(2N_f)\).
  The examination nevertheless formulates the problem in the displayed
  chiral subgroup; perform the requested matching in that subgroup.
