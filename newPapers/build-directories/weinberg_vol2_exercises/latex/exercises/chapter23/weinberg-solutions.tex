\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Weinberg Exercises}
\ExerciseEditorialNote

\begin{enumerate}
  \WeinbergSolution{1}
  Let a configuration of characteristic size \(R\) be obtained by
  dilating a fixed configuration.  In four Euclidean dimensions, a
  positive term containing \(k\) derivatives scales as
  \[
    I_k(R)=R^{4-k}I_k(1).
  \]
  The usual two-derivative Goldstone action therefore scales as \(R^2\)
  and drives a nontrivial configuration to zero size.  A four-derivative
  term is scale invariant, so it does not alter the derivative of the
  action with respect to \(R\).  To oppose collapse one needs a positive
  term that grows as \(R\to0\), hence a term with more than four
  derivatives.  The minimal possibility is a positive six-derivative
  invariant:
  \[
    I(R)=R^2I_2+R^{-2}I_6+\cdots .
  \]
  It has a stationary scale
  \(R^4=I_6/I_2\).  Thus the required addition is a stabilizing
  higher-derivative interaction of order strictly greater than four
  (six derivatives at minimum), with a sign that makes its Euclidean
  contribution positive.

  \WeinbergSolution{2}
  The vacuum manifold is
  \[
    \frac{SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R}{SU(2)_V}\simeq SU(2)\simeq S^3.
  \]
  Finite energy compactifies six-dimensional space to \(S^6\), so the
  sectors are labeled by
  \[
    \pi_6(S^3)=\mathbb Z_{12}.
  \]
  Consequently the skyrmion charge is conserved only modulo \(12\).
  Charges add in \(\mathbb Z_{12}\): a skyrmion and an antiskyrmion
  cancel, and twelve unit skyrmions are topologically equivalent to the
  vacuum and may annihilate.  This is a discrete \(\mathbb Z_{12}\)
  conservation law rather than the integer-valued baryon-number law of
  the ordinary three-dimensional skyrmion.

  \WeinbergSolution{3}
  Represent \(S^n\) as the cube \(I^n\) with its boundary collapsed to a
  base point, and let \(a,b,c,d\) be based maps \(I^n\to\mathcal M\).
  For \(n>1\), define two products: \(*_1\) concatenates maps in the
  first coordinate and \(*_2\) concatenates them in the second.  The
  constant based map \(e\) is the identity for both products, up to
  based homotopy.

  Subdividing the first two coordinates into four rectangles gives the
  interchange law
  \[
    (a*_1b)*_2(c*_1d)
      =(a*_2c)*_1(b*_2d).
  \]
  The two products first coincide:
  \[
    a*_1b
      =(a*_2e)*_1(e*_2b)
      =(a*_1e)*_2(e*_1b)
      =a*_2b.
  \]
  Calling this common product simply \(*\), a second use of the
  interchange law gives
  \[
    a*b
      =(e*_1a)*_2(b*_1e)
      =(e*_2b)*_1(a*_2e)
      =b*a.
  \]
  Hence the group operation on \(\pi_n(\mathcal M)\) is commutative for
  every \(n>1\), so every higher homotopy group is Abelian.

  \WeinbergSolution{4}
  A unit instanton in \(SU(N)\) has \(4N\) bosonic collective
  coordinates: four translations, one scale, and \(4N-5\) gauge
  orientations.  With the gauge action normalized as
  \(S_E=(2g^2)^{-1}\int\operatorname{tr}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\), the
  classical action is
  \[
    S_{\mathrm{inst}}=\frac{8\pi^2}{g^2}.
  \]
  Normalizing each zero mode supplies one inverse power of \(g\).
  Therefore, apart from the scale integral, renormalization factors,
  and a perturbative series regular at weak coupling, a unit-instanton
  contribution in \(SU(N)\) behaves as
  \[
    g^{-4N}\exp\left(-\frac{8\pi^2}{g^2}\right).
  \]
  Setting \(N=4\) gives
  \[
    \boxed{g^{-16}\exp\left(-\frac{8\pi^2}{g^2}\right).}
  \]

  \WeinbergSolution{5}
  Introduce phases \(\alpha_u,\alpha_d,\alpha_s\) for the three diagonal
  chiral condensates.  At leading order in the light-quark masses, their
  potential and the anomalous constraint are
  \[
    V=-v\sum_{i=u,d,s}m_i\cos\alpha_i,
    \qquad
    \alpha_u+\alpha_d+\alpha_s=\frac{a}{M},
  \]
  after the vacuum value of the strong \(CP\) angle has been absorbed
  into the definition of \(a\).  For a small axion displacement,
  \[
    V-V(0)=\frac v2\sum_i m_i\alpha_i^2+O(\alpha^4).
  \]
  Minimize the quadratic form with the constraint.  A Lagrange
  multiplier gives
  \[
    m_i\alpha_i=\text{constant},\qquad
    \alpha_i=
      \frac{m_i^{-1}}{m_u^{-1}+m_d^{-1}+m_s^{-1}}\,
      \frac aM .
  \]
  Substitution yields
  \[
    V-V(0)=
      \frac12\frac{v}{m_u^{-1}+m_d^{-1}+m_s^{-1}}\,
      \frac{a^2}{M^2}+O(a^4).
  \]
  Thus
  \[
    \boxed{
    m_a^2=\frac{v}{M^2}
      \frac{m_um_dm_s}
      {m_um_d+m_um_s+m_dm_s}.}
  \]
  The formal decoupling limit \(m_s\to\infty\) recovers
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:23.6.26}, although that limit lies outside the
  three-light-flavor expansion used in the derivation.  Taking any one
  quark mass to zero makes the axion mass vanish, as required because
  the vacuum angle is then removable by a chiral rotation.

  \WeinbergSolution{6}
  The potential is
  \[
    V(\phi)=-\frac12m^2\phi^2+\frac14\lambda\phi^4+g\phi.
  \]
  At \(g=0\) its degenerate minima are
  \(\phi=\pm v_0\), where \(v_0=m/\sqrt\lambda\).  To leading order in
  \(\lvert g\rvert\), their energy-density splitting is
  \[
    \epsilon=2\lvert g\rvert v_0
      =\frac{2\lvert g\rvert m}{\sqrt\lambda}.
  \]
  The wall tension may be evaluated in the degenerate potential
  \(V_0=\lambda(\phi^2-v_0^2)^2/4\):
  \[
    S_1=\int_{-v_0}^{v_0}\dd\phi\,\sqrt{2V_0(\phi)}
      =\sqrt{\frac\lambda2}
       \int_{-v_0}^{v_0}\dd\phi\,(v_0^2-\phi^2)
      =\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\frac{m^3}{\lambda}.
  \]
  In four Euclidean dimensions the thin-wall result is
  \(B=27\pi^2S_1^4/(2\epsilon^3)\).  Hence
  \[
    B=
    \frac{4\pi^2}{3}\,
      \frac{m^9}{\lambda^{5/2}\lvert g\rvert^3},
    \qquad
    \boxed{\exp(-B)=
      \exp\left[
       -\frac{4\pi^2m^9}
              {3\lambda^{5/2}\lvert g\rvert^3}
      \right].}
  \]
  The result is dimensionless because \(g\) has mass dimension three,
  and the thin-wall approximation is valid when
  \(\lvert g\rvert\ll m^3/\sqrt\lambda\).
\end{enumerate}
