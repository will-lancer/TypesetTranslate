\chapterbackmatter{Solutions to Weinberg Exercises}
\ExerciseEditorialNote

\begin{enumerate}
  \WeinbergSolution{1}
  Write the background field as \(\bar A_\mu\) and expand the complex
  fundamental scalar about zero.  In background Feynman gauge its one-loop
  contribution is
  \[
    \Gamma_{\rm scalar}^{(1)}
      =\operatorname{Tr}\ln(-\bar D^2),
  \]
  where both the bubble and the seagull term are contained in the
  determinant.  Gauge invariance makes the logarithmically divergent
  quadratic term transverse, and the heat-kernel coefficient gives
  \[
    \Gamma_{\rm scalar,div}^{(1)}
      =-\frac{1}{(4\pi)^2}\frac{1}{\epsilon}
        \frac{1}{12}\int d^4x\,
        \operatorname{tr}_{\boldsymbol N}
        (\bar F_{\mu\nu}\bar F^{\mu\nu}).
  \]
  With
  \(\operatorname{tr}_{\boldsymbol N}(T^aT^b)
    =T(\boldsymbol N)\delta^{ab}\) and
  \(T(\boldsymbol N)=1/2\), this changes the usual one-loop coefficient by
  \(-T(\boldsymbol N)/3=-1/6\).  The gauge and ghost determinants give
  \(11C_A/3=11N/3\).  Therefore, for one complex scalar in the defining
  representation and no other matter,
  \[
    \boxed{\beta(g)
      =-\frac{g^3}{16\pi^2}
        \left(\frac{11}{3}N-\frac{1}{6}\right)
        +O(g^5).}
  \]
  The scalar contribution itself is positive,
  \(+\frac{g^3}{16\pi^2}\frac16\), so it weakens asymptotic freedom.  A
  single real scalar, when a real representation permits one, contributes
  half as much.

  \WeinbergSolution{2}
  Put
  \(\delta(\mu)=g_*-g_\mu\).  The assumed zero of the beta-function gives
  \[
    \frac{d\delta}{d\ln\mu}=-a\delta+O(\delta^2),
    \qquad
    \delta(\mu)=A\mu^{-a}+O(\mu^{-2a}),
  \]
  where \(A\) depends on the trajectory.  Expanding the anomalous dimension
  about the fixed point,
  \[
    \gamma^{\mathcal O}(g)
      =\gamma_*^{\mathcal O}
       +c(g_*-g)+O((g_*-g)^2),
  \]
  and integrating the defining equation for \(N_E^{\mathcal O}\) gives
  \[
    N_E^{\mathcal O-1}
      =C E^{-\gamma_*^{\mathcal O}}
       \exp\left[\frac{cA}{a}E^{-a}
       +O(E^{-2a})\right].
  \]
  Hence
  \[
    \boxed{N_E^{\mathcal O-1}
      =C E^{-\gamma_*^{\mathcal O}}
       \left[1+\frac{cA}{a}E^{-a}
       +O(E^{-2a})\right].}
  \]
  Thus the leading correction is a relative power \(E^{-a}\), rather than
  another logarithm.  Its coefficient is not universal.  If
  \(c=\gamma^{\mathcal O\,\prime}(g_*)\) vanishes, the first nonzero higher
  derivative supplies the corresponding higher power of \(E^{-a}\).

  \WeinbergSolution{3}
  Take an analytic change of variable
  \[
    \widetilde g=g+A g^2+C g^3+O(g^4).
  \]
  Since
  \(\widetilde\beta(\widetilde g)
    =(d\widetilde g/dg)\beta(g)\), expansion first in \(g\), followed by
  inversion of the coupling redefinition, yields
  \[
    \widetilde\beta(\widetilde g)
      =b\widetilde g^2+b'\widetilde g^3+
       \left[b''-Ab'+(C-A^2)b\right]\widetilde g^4
       +O(\widetilde g^5).
  \]
  Thus \(b\) and \(b'\) are unchanged, but
  \[
    \widetilde b''=b''-Ab'+(C-A^2)b.
  \]
  For the generic case \(b\neq0\), set \(A=0\) and choose
  \[
    C=\frac{\widetilde b''_{\rm desired}-b''}{b}.
  \]
  This makes the coefficient of \(\widetilde g^4\) any prescribed number.
  The result is the elementary manifestation of scheme dependence beyond
  the first two coefficients of a one-coupling beta-function.

  \WeinbergSolution{4}
  For a Dirac fermion of charge \(Q_f e\), color multiplicity \(N_{c,f}\),
  and \(E\gg m_f\), the one-loop vacuum polarization gives
  \[
    \alpha^{-1}(E)=\alpha^{-1}(0)
      -\frac{2}{3\pi}\sum_f N_{c,f}Q_f^2
       \left[\ln\left(\frac{E}{m_f}\right)-\frac56\right].
  \]
  At \(E=100\,\mathrm{GeV}\), the active charged leptons are
  \(e,\mu,\tau\), and the active quarks are \(u,d,s,c,b\); the top quark is
  above the scale.  Using representative masses in GeV
  \[
    (m_e,m_\mu,m_\tau)=(0.000511,0.1057,1.777),
  \]
  \[
    (m_u,m_d,m_s,m_c,m_b)=(0.0048,0.0095,0.15,1.5,4.7)
  \]
  gives
  \[
    \sum_f N_{c,f}Q_f^2
      \left[\ln(100/m_f)-\frac56\right]\simeq42.64
  \]
  and therefore
  \[
    \boxed{\alpha^{-1}(100\,\mathrm{GeV})\simeq128.0,
      \qquad \alpha(100\,\mathrm{GeV})\simeq\frac1{128}.}
  \]
  Treating the light quarks as free particles is only an estimate because
  their vacuum polarization is affected by low-energy QCD.  Replacing that
  part by the experimentally determined hadronic vacuum polarization moves
  the result slightly, to roughly \(1/128.8\); either calculation displays
  the important shift from \(\alpha(0)^{-1}=137.036\).

  \WeinbergSolution{5}
  Section 11.4 uses Feynman gauge, so the off-shell propagator and its
  exponent are gauge dependent.  For \(p^2\gg m_e^2\), the coefficient of
  the ultraviolet logarithm in \(Z_2\) gives the electron-field anomalous
  dimension
  \[
    \gamma^{(\psi)}(e)=-\frac{e^2}{16\pi^2}+O(e^4).
  \]
  Together with
  \(\beta(e)=e^3/(12\pi^2)+O(e^5)\), this integrates to the leading-log
  wave-function factor
  \[
    \exp\left[2\int_{e_R}^{e_p}
      \frac{\gamma^{(\psi)}(e)}{\beta(e)}\,de\right]
      =\left(\frac{e_p}{e_R}\right)^{-3/2},
  \]
  where \(p=\sqrt{p^2}\) is large and spacelike.  Hence, apart from a
  finite normalization constant and mass-suppressed terms,
  \[
    \boxed{S'(p)\sim
      \frac{-i\slashed p}{p^2}
      \left(\frac{e_p}{e_R}\right)^{-3/2}.}
  \]
  Using the one-loop running charge,
  \[
    S'(p)\sim\frac{-i\slashed p}{p^2}
      \left[
        1-\frac{e_R^2}{6\pi^2}
        \left(\ln\frac{p}{m_e}-\frac56\right)
      \right]^{3/4}.
  \]
  Expanding this gives
  \(1-e_R^2\ln(p/m_e)/(8\pi^2)+\cdots\), in agreement with the direct
  one-loop self-energy.  The expression is meaningful below the QED
  Landau scale, where the running coupling remains perturbative.

  \WeinbergSolution{6}
  Write the interaction as
  \(g(\phi_n\phi_n)^2/4\).  The one-loop beta-function and the linearized
  flow of the coefficient \(g_2\) of
  \(\phi_n\phi_n/2\) in \(d=4-\epsilon\) are
  \[
    \beta_g=-\epsilon g+\frac{N+8}{8\pi^2}g^2+O(g^3),
  \]
  \[
    \mu\frac{dg_2}{d\mu}
      =\left[-2+\frac{N+2}{8\pi^2}g+O(g^2)\right]g_2.
  \]
  The nontrivial fixed point is therefore
  \[
    g_*=\frac{8\pi^2}{N+8}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2),
  \]
  and the unique thermal eigenvalue is
  \[
    \lambda_2=-2+\frac{N+2}{N+8}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2).
  \]
  Since the correlation-length exponent is
  \(\nu=-1/\lambda_2\),
  \[
    \boxed{\nu
      =\frac12+\frac{N+2}{4(N+8)}\epsilon
       +O(\epsilon^2).}
  \]
  For \(N=1\), this reduces to
  \(\nu=\frac12+\epsilon/12+O(\epsilon^2)\), the result obtained in
  Section 18.5.
\end{enumerate}
