\subsection{Ghosts}

We now consider the effect of the factor $\operatorname{Det}\mathcal F$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:15.5.22} on the Feynman rules for a non-Abelian gauge theory. In
order to be able to treat this effect as a modification of the Feynman rules,
recall that as shown in Section 9.5, the determinant of any matrix
$\mathcal F_{a x,b y}$ may be expressed as a path integral
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Det}\mathcal F\propto
  \int\left[\prod_{a,x}d\omega_a^*(x)\right]
  \left[\prod_{a,x}d\omega_a(x)\right]\exp(iI_{GH}),
  \tag{15.6.1}\label{eq:15.6.1}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  I_{GH}\equiv\int d^4x\,d^4y\,
  \omega_a^*(x)\omega_b(y)\mathcal F_{a x,b y}.
  \tag{15.6.2}\label{eq:15.6.2}
\end{equation}
Here $\omega_a^*$ and $\omega_a$ are a set of independent
anticommuting classical variables, and the constant of proportionality is
field-independent. (We have to choose the $\omega_a$ and
$\omega_a^*$ field variables to be fermionic in order to reproduce the
factor $\operatorname{Det}\mathcal F$; had we chosen these field variables to
be bosonic, the path integral \eqref{eq:15.6.1} would have been proportional to
$(\operatorname{Det}\mathcal F)^{-1}$.) The fields $\omega_a^*$ and
$\omega_a$ are not necessarily related by complex conjugation; indeed,
in Section 15.7 we shall see that for some purposes we need to assume that
$\omega_a^*$ and $\omega_a$ are independent real variables. The
whole effect of the factor $\operatorname{Det}\mathcal F$ is the same as that
of including $I_{GH}(\omega,\omega^*)$ in the full effective action, and
integrating over `fields' $\omega$ and $\omega^*$. That is, for arbitrary
gauge-fixing functionals $f_a(x)$,
\begin{align}
  \bigl\langle T\{\mathcal O_A\cdots\}\bigr\rangle_V
  \propto{}&\int\left[\prod_{n,x}d\psi_n(x)\right]
  \left[\prod_{a,\mu,x}dA_{a\mu}(x)\right]
  \notag\\
  &\mathbin{\cdot}\left[\prod_{a,x}
  d\omega_a(x)d\omega_a^*(x)\right]
  \exp\bigl(iI_{\mathrm{MOD}}[\psi,A,\omega,\omega^*]\bigr)
  \mathcal O_A\cdots,
  \tag{15.6.3}\label{eq:15.6.3}
\end{align}
where $I_{\mathrm{MOD}}$ is a modified action
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{MOD}}=\int d^4x
  \left[\mathcal L-\frac{1}{2\xi}f_a f_a\right]+I_{GH}.
  \tag{15.6.4}\label{eq:15.6.4}
\end{equation}

The fields $\omega_a$ and $\omega_a^*$ are Lorentz scalars (at least
in covariant gauges) but satisfy Fermi statistics. The connection between spin
and statistics is not really violated here, because there are no particles
described by these fields that can appear in initial or final states. For that
reason, $\omega_a$ and $\omega_a^*$ are called the fields of `ghost'
and `antighost' particles. Inspection of Eq.~\eqref{eq:15.6.2} shows that the
action respects the conservation of a quantity known as `ghost number,' equal
to $+1$ for $\omega_a$, $-1$ for $\omega_a^*$, and zero for all other
fields.

The Feynman rules for the ghosts are simplest in the case in which the
`matrix' $\mathcal F$ may be expressed as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal F=\mathcal F_0+\mathcal F_1,
  \tag{15.6.5}\label{eq:15.6.5}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_0$ is field-independent and of zeroth order in coupling
constants, while $\mathcal F_1$ is field-dependent and proportional to one or
more coupling constant factors. In this case, the ghost propagator is just
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{ab}(x,y)
  =(\mathcal F_0^{-1})_{a x,b y}
  \tag{15.6.6}\label{eq:15.6.6}
\end{equation}
and the ghost vertices are to be read off from the interaction term
\begin{equation}
  I'_{GH}=\int d^4x\,d^4y\,
  \omega_a^*(x)\omega_b(y)(\mathcal F_1)_{a x,b y}.
  \tag{15.6.7}\label{eq:15.6.7}
\end{equation}

For instance, in the generalized $\xi$-gauge discussed in the previous
section, we have
\begin{equation}
  f_a=\partial_\mu A_a^\mu
  \tag{15.6.8}\label{eq:15.6.8}
\end{equation}
and for infinitesimal gauge parameters $\lambda_a$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:15.1.9}
gives:
\begin{equation*}
  A_{\lambda a}^\mu
  =A_a^\mu+\partial^\mu\lambda_a
  +C_{acb}\lambda_b A_c^\mu,
\end{equation*}
so that
\begin{align}
  \mathcal F_{a x,b y}
  &\equiv\left.\frac{\delta\,\partial_\mu A_{\lambda a}^\mu(x)}
  {\delta\lambda_b(y)}\right|_{\lambda=0}
  \notag\\
  &=\Box\delta^4(x-y)\delta_{ab}
  +C_{acb}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^\mu}
  \left[A_c^\mu(x)\delta^4(x-y)\right].
  \tag{15.6.9}\label{eq:15.6.9}
\end{align}
This is of the form \eqref{eq:15.6.5}, with
\begin{equation}
  (\mathcal F_0)_{a x,b y}
  =\Box\delta^4(x-y)\delta_{ab},
  \tag{15.6.10}\label{eq:15.6.10}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  (\mathcal F_1)_{a x,b y}
  =-C_{abc}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^\mu}
  \left[A_c^\mu(x)\delta^4(x-y)\right].
  \tag{15.6.11}\label{eq:15.6.11}
\end{equation}
From Eqs.~\eqref{eq:15.6.6} and \eqref{eq:15.6.10}, we see that the ghost
propagator is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{ab}(x,y)=\delta_{ab}(2\pi)^{-4}
  \int d^4p\,(p^2-i\epsilon)^{-1}e^{ip\cdot(x-y)},
  \tag{15.6.12}\label{eq:15.6.12}
\end{equation}
so in this gauge the ghosts behave like spinless fermions of zero mass,
transforming according to the adjoint representation of the gauge group.
Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:15.6.7} and \eqref{eq:15.6.11} and integrating by parts,
we find that the ghost interaction term in the action is now
\begin{equation}
  I'_{GH}=\int d^4x\,C_{abc}
  \frac{\partial\omega_a^*}{\partial x^\mu}
  A_c^\mu\omega_b.
  \tag{15.6.13}\label{eq:15.6.13}
\end{equation}
This interaction corresponds to vertices to which are attached one outgoing
ghost line, one incoming ghost line, and one vector boson line. If these lines
carry (incoming) momenta $p$, $q$, $k$ respectively and gauge group indices
$a$, $b$, $c$ respectively, and the gauge field carries a vector
index $\mu$, then the contribution of such a vertex to the integrand is given
by the momentum-space Feynman rules as
\begin{equation}
  i(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p+q+k)\mathbin{\cdot}ip_\mu C_{abc}.
  \tag{15.6.14}\label{eq:15.6.14}
\end{equation}
The ghosts propagate around loops, with single vector boson lines attached at
each vertex along the loops, and with an extra minus sign supplied for each
loop as is usual for fermionic field variables.

The extra minus sign for ghost loops suggests that each ghost field
$\omega_a$, together with the associated antighost field
$\omega_a^*$, represents something like a negative degree of freedom.
These negative degrees of freedom are necessary because in using covariant
gauge field propagators we are really over-counting; the physical degrees of
freedom are the components of $A_a^\mu(x)$, less the parameters
$\Lambda_a(x)$ needed to describe a gauge transformation.

In summary, the modified action \eqref{eq:15.6.4} may be written in generalized
$\xi$-gauge as
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{MOD}}=\int d^4x\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{MOD}}
  \tag{15.6.15}\label{eq:15.6.15}
\end{equation}
with a modified Lagrangian density:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{MOD}}={}&\mathcal L_M
  -\frac14F_a^{\mu\nu}F_{a\mu\nu}
  -\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_\mu A_a^\mu)
  (\partial_\nu A_a^\nu)
  \notag\\
  &-\partial_\mu\omega_a^*\partial^\mu\omega_a
  +C_{abc}(\partial_\mu\omega_a^*)
  A_c^\mu\omega_b.
  \tag{15.6.16}\label{eq:15.6.16}
\end{align}
It is important that this Lagrangian is \emph{renormalizable} (if the matter
Lagrangian $\mathcal L_M$ is), in the elementary sense that its terms involve
products of fields and their derivatives of total dimensionality (in powers
of mass) four or less. (The kinematic term
$-\partial_\mu\omega_a^*\partial^\mu\omega_a$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:15.6.16} fixes the dimensionality of the fields $\omega$ and
$\omega^*$ to be mass to the power unity, just like ordinary scalar and gauge
fields.) However, there is more to renormalizability than power counting; it
is necessary also that there be a counterterm to absorb every divergence. In
the next section we shall consider a remarkable symmetry that will be used in
Section 17.2 to show that non-Abelian gauge theories are indeed renormalizable
in this sense, and that can even take the place of the Faddeev--Popov--De Witt
approach that we have been following.
