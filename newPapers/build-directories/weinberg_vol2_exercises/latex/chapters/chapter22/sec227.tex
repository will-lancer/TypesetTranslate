\subsection{Anomalies and Goldstone Bosons}
\label{sec:22.7}

In the same 1971 paper in which they introduced the consistency
conditions, Wess and Zumino~\hyperref[ch22-ref-18]{[18]} also noted that
the possibility of anomalies has important consequences for the
interactions of Goldstone bosons. To understand their point, it is helpful
to apply the `anomaly matching' argument developed by
't Hooft~\hyperref[ch22-ref-16]{[16]} in 1979, which has already been used
in \hyperref[sec:22.5]{Section 22.5}.

Consider a broken global symmetry group $G$ that is realized linearly in
some underlying theory of trapped massless fermions, as for instance the
global chiral $SU(3)\times SU(3)$ symmetry in quantum chromodynamics with
three massless quarks. In this underlying theory introduce fictitious
gauge fields, so that aside from possible anomalies the global symmetry
$G$ becomes local. In general this local symmetry will be broken by
anomalies, since in the real world the symmetry is purely global, and
there is no reason why a global symmetry should admit an extension to an
anomaly-free local symmetry. However these anomalies can be cancelled by
adding suitable massless spectator fermions. As long as the gauge
couplings introduced in this way are sufficiently small, and the
spectator fermions have only these very weak gauge interactions, the
dynamics of the theory will not be substantially changed by these
modifications.

Next consider the effective field theory that describes physics at low
energy, where the trapped fermions are unobservable. The only degrees of
freedom in this theory will be the massless particles: the fictitious
gauge bosons and spectator fermions, and a set of Goldstone bosons with
fields $\xi_a$, one for each independent broken symmetry. Since the
underlying theory had been made gauge-invariant and anomaly-free, the
same must be true of the effective field theory. But the spectator
fermions produce an anomaly which had previously cancelled the anomaly
due to the trapped massless fermions in the underlying theory, so in
order for them to cancel the anomaly due to the Goldstone bosons,
\emph{the gauged effective field theory of the Goldstone bosons must have
an anomaly for the fictitious local symmetries which is equal to that
produced by the trapped fermions in the underlying theory}. That is, in
place of Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.6.2}, the effective action $\Gamma[\xi,A]$ of
the fictitious gauge fields and Goldstone bosons is subject to the
condition
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_\beta(x)\Gamma[\xi,A]=G_\beta[x;A],
 \tag{22.7.1}\label{eq:22.7.1}
\end{equation}
where $G_\beta[x;A]$ is the anomaly function of the underlying theory, in
which there are no Goldstone bosons, and $\mathcal T_\beta$ is a
generator of the gauge group $G$, now acting on both gauge and Goldstone
boson fields. (The index $\beta$ on $\mathcal T_\beta$ runs over values
$i$ labelling a complete set of independent generators $Y_i$ of the
unbroken symmetry subgroup $H$, together with values $a$ labelling a set
of independent broken symmetry generators $X_a$; there is one Goldstone
boson field $\xi_a$ for each $X_a$.)

Of course, Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.1} may also be used to study the
interactions of Goldstone bosons with real weakly coupled gauge fields.
For instance, where the underlying theory includes electroweak gauge
bosons coupled to the quarks, we would identify some of what we have
called `fictitious' gauge fields with the electroweak gauge fields. In
such cases some of the `spectator' fermions must also be real, in order
to cancel the anomalies produced by loops of trapped fermions in the
gauge symmetries of the real weakly coupled gauge fields, as for instance
the leptons cancel the electroweak anomalies produced by the quarks.

Now let us turn to the implications of Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.1}. To
calculate the gauge symmetry generator $\mathcal T_\beta(x)$ in this
context, note that under a general group transformation
$g=\exp(-i\int\zeta_\beta(x)\mathcal T_\beta(x)d^4x)$, the Goldstone
boson fields $\xi_a(x)$ transform into fields $\xi'_a(x)$ given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:19.6.18}, and the gauge fields $A^\mu_\beta(x)$ transform
into gauge-transformed fields $A^{\prime\mu}_\beta(x)$, so that
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_\beta(x)=\mathcal T_\beta^A(x)+\mathcal T_\beta^\xi(x).
 \tag{22.7.2}\label{eq:22.7.2}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal T_\beta^A(x)$ acts on the gauge fields and is given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.6.1}:
\begin{equation}
 -i\mathcal T_\beta^A(x)
 =-\frac{\partial}{\partial x^\mu}
 \frac{\delta}{\delta A_{\beta\mu}(x)}
 -C_{\beta\gamma\alpha}A_{\gamma\mu}(x)
 \frac{\delta}{\delta A_{\alpha\mu}(x)},
 \tag{22.7.3}\label{eq:22.7.3}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ are the totally antisymmetric structure
constants of the gauge group $G$, while $\mathcal T_\beta^\xi(x)$ acts
on the Goldstone fields and is given by the infinitesimal limit of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:19.6.17}, which (for the exponential parameterization
$\gamma(\xi)=\exp(i\xi_aX_a)$) reads:\footnote{The minus sign in the
first term on the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.4} and the
factor $-i$ on the left-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.3} appear
because it is
$\exp[-i\int\Lambda_\beta(x)\mathcal T_\beta(x)]$ that induces a gauge
transformation \eqref{eq:15.1.17} with gauge parameter $\Lambda_\beta$,
as can be seen by requiring the $\mathcal T_\beta(x)$ to satisfy the
commutation relations \eqref{eq:22.6.5}.}
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 T_\beta\exp\bigl(i\xi_a(x)X_a\bigr)
 ={}&-\mathcal T_\beta^\xi(x)\exp\bigl(i\xi_a(x)X_a\bigr)
 \\
 &+\exp\bigl(i\xi_a(x)X_a\bigr)\theta_{\beta i}(x)Y_i.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.4}\label{eq:22.7.4}
\end{equation}
Here $T_\beta$ are matrices representing the generators of $G$ in any
representation; they are divided into sets $X_a$ and $Y_i$, which
represent the broken and unbroken symmetry generators, respectively.
Also the $\theta_{\beta i}(x)$ are $\xi$-dependent functions whose form
will not concern us here.

About the anomaly functions $G_\beta[x;A]$, we will assume only the
consistency conditions \eqref{eq:22.6.6}
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_\alpha(x)G_\beta[y;A]
 -\mathcal T_\beta(x)G_\alpha[y;A]
 =iC_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\delta^4(x-y)G_\gamma[y;A]
 \tag{22.7.5}\label{eq:22.7.5}
\end{equation}
and the absence of anomalies in the unbroken symmetries
\begin{equation}
 G_i[x;A]=0.
 \tag{22.7.6}\label{eq:22.7.6}
\end{equation}
As mentioned in the previous section, as long as the trace
$\operatorname{Tr}[T_i\{T_j,T_k\}]$ vanishes for the generators of
unbroken symmetry subgroup (as it does for the non-chiral generators of
$SU(3)\times SU(3)$), it is always possible to add a local functional to
the action so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.6} is satisfied.

Under the assumptions \eqref{eq:22.7.5} and \eqref{eq:22.7.6}, it is
always possible to find a solution of the anomalous Slavnov--Taylor
identities \eqref{eq:22.7.1}:
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma[\xi,A]
 =-i\int_0^1dt\int \xi_b(y)G_b[y;A_{-t\xi}]\,d^4y,
 \tag{22.7.7}\label{eq:22.7.7}
\end{equation}
where $[A_{-t\xi}(x)]_\mu$ is the result of acting on
$A_\mu\equiv T_\beta A_{\mu\beta}$ with a gauge transformation
\eqref{eq:15.1.17} having $\Lambda_a=-t\xi_a$ and $\Lambda_i=0$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 [A_{-t\xi}(x)]_\mu
 ={}&\exp\bigl(-itX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr)A_\mu(x)
 \exp\bigl(itX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr)
 \\
 &-i\bigl[\partial_\mu\exp\bigl(-itX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 \exp\bigl(itX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.8}\label{eq:22.7.8}
\end{equation}
In contrast with the case in which unbroken symmetries have anomalies,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:22.7.7} and \eqref{eq:22.7.8} define a \emph{local}
(though complicated) functional of gauge and Goldstone boson fields.
Any other solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.1} will differ from this one by
an anomaly-free functional.

Here is an outline of the proof~\hyperref[ch22-ref-24]{[24]} that the
action \eqref{eq:22.7.7} satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.1}. Instead of
working with the local generator $\mathcal T_\beta(x)$, it is convenient
to introduce an arbitrary function $\eta_\beta(x)$, and define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T[\eta]
 =\int d^4x\,\eta_\beta(x)\mathcal T_\beta(x).
 \tag{22.7.9}\label{eq:22.7.9}
\end{equation}
To evaluate $\mathcal T[\eta]\xi_b(x)$, we introduce the matrix
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \eta_{-t\xi}(x)
 \equiv{}&\exp\bigl(-iX_a\xi_a(x)t\bigr)
 \bigl[\eta(x)+\mathcal T[\eta]\bigr]
 \exp\bigl(iX_a\xi_a(x)t\bigr)
 \\
 \equiv{}&[\eta_{-t\xi}(x)]_\beta T_\beta,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.10}\label{eq:22.7.10}
\end{equation}
where $\eta(x)\equiv\eta_\beta(x)T_\beta$. Then
\[
 \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\eta_{-t\xi}(x)
 =-i\bigl[X_a\xi_a(x),\eta_{-t\xi}(x)\bigr]
 +i\bigl(\mathcal T[\eta]\xi_a(x)\bigr)X_a,
\]
so that
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T[\eta]\xi_b(x)
 =-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[\eta_{-t\xi}(x)]_b
 +iC_{a\gamma b}\xi_a(x)[\eta_{-t\xi}(x)]_\gamma.
 \tag{22.7.11}\label{eq:22.7.11}
\end{equation}
To evaluate $\mathcal T[\eta]G_b[y;A]$, we apply $\mathcal T[\eta]$ to
the gauge field, and after a straightforward calculation find that
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T[\eta][A_{-t\xi}(x)]_\mu
 =\left.
 \bigl(\mathcal T^A[\eta_{-t\xi}]A_\mu(x)\bigr)
 \right|_{A\to A_{-t\xi}},
 \tag{22.7.12}\label{eq:22.7.12}
\end{equation}
so, using the consistency condition \eqref{eq:22.7.5},
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T[\eta]G_b[y;A_{-t\xi}]
 ={}&\int d^4x\,[\eta_{-t\xi}(x)]_\gamma
 \left.
 \bigl(\mathcal T_\gamma^A(x)G_b[y;A]\bigr)
 \right|_{A\to A_{-t\xi}}
 \\
 ={}&\int d^4x\,[\eta_{-t\xi}(x)]_a
 \left.
 \bigl(\mathcal T_b^A(y)G_a[x;A]\bigr)
 \right|_{A\to A_{-t\xi}}
 \\
 &+iC_{\gamma ba}[\eta_{-t\xi}(y)]_\gamma
 G_a[y;A_{-t\xi}].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.13}\label{eq:22.7.13}
\end{equation}
The structure constant terms in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:22.7.11} and
\eqref{eq:22.7.13} cancel, yielding
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T[\eta]\Gamma[\xi,A]
 =\int_0^1dt\int d^4y\Biggl\{
 &-\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
 [\eta_{-t\xi}(y)]_b\right]G_b[y;A_{-t\xi}]
 \\
 &-i[\eta_{-t\xi}(y)]_a
 \left.
 \bigl(\mathcal T[\xi]G_a[y;A]\bigr)
 \right|_{A\to A_{-t\xi}}
 \Biggr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.14}\label{eq:22.7.14}
\end{equation}
Another straightforward calculation shows that
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[A_{-t\xi}(x)]_\mu
 =i\left.
 \bigl(\mathcal T^A(\xi)A_\mu(x)\bigr)
 \right|_{A\to A_{-t\xi}},
 \tag{22.7.15}\label{eq:22.7.15}
\end{equation}
so the terms in the integrand in Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.14} add up to a
$t$-derivative
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T[\eta]\Gamma[\xi,A]
 &=-\int_0^t dt\int d^4y\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
 \left\{[\eta_{-t\xi}(y)]_bG_b[y;A_{-t\xi}]\right\}
 \\
 &=-\left[
 [\eta_{-t\xi}(y)]_bG_b[y;A_{-t\xi}]
 \right]_{t=0}^{t=1}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.16}\label{eq:22.7.16}
\end{equation}
At $t=1$ we have
\[
 \eta_{-\xi}(x)
 =\exp\bigl(-iX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr)
 \bigl[\eta(x)+\mathcal T[\eta]\bigr]
 \exp\bigl(iX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr).
\]
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.4} we see that this is a linear combination of
the generators of the unbroken symmetry subgroup $H$, so the coefficient
$[\eta_{-\xi}(y)]_b$ of any broken symmetry generator $X_b$ vanishes.
Also, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:22.7.10} and \eqref{eq:22.7.8} show immediately
that at $t=0$, $\eta_{-t\xi}(y)=\eta(y)$ and
$[A_{-t\xi}(y)]_\mu=A_\mu(y)$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.16} yields
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T[\eta]\Gamma[\xi,A]
 =\int d^4y\,[\eta_0(y)]_bG_b[y;A_0]
 =\int d^4y\,\eta_b(y)G_b[y;A],
 \tag{22.7.17}\label{eq:22.7.17}
\end{equation}
which with Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.6} is equivalent to the desired result
\eqref{eq:22.7.1}, as was to be proved.\par\medskip

The solution \eqref{eq:22.7.7} of Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.1} is not unique,
but it is the unique solution that vanishes for $\xi=0$. To see this,
note that
\begin{equation}
 \exp\left[-i\int\eta_\beta(x)\mathcal T_\beta(x)d^4x\right]
 \Gamma[\xi,A]
 =\Gamma[\xi',A'],
 \tag{22.7.18}\label{eq:22.7.18}
\end{equation}
where the primes indicate a gauge transformation with transformation
parameter $\eta_\beta$. It is convenient to represent the exponential
here as
\[
 \exp(z)=1+\int_0^1dt\,\exp(zt)z,
\]
so Eqs.~\eqref{eq:22.7.1} and \eqref{eq:22.7.18} yield
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma[\xi,A]
 &-i\int_0^1dt\,
 \exp\left[-it\int\eta_\beta(x)\mathcal T_\beta^A(x)d^4x\right]
 \int\eta_b(y)G_b[y;A]d^4y
 \\
 &=\Gamma[\xi',A'].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.19}\label{eq:22.7.19}
\end{equation}
In particular, if we take $\eta_a=-\xi_a$ and $\eta_i=0$, then
$\xi'_a=0$, in which case by assumption Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.19} has a
vanishing right-hand side, and therefore yields the formula:
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma[\xi,A]
 =-i\int_0^1dt\,
 \exp\left[it\int\xi_a(x)\mathcal T_a^A(x)d^4x\right]
 \int\xi_b(y)G_b[y;A]d^4y.
 \tag{22.7.20}\label{eq:22.7.20}
\end{equation}
The functional operator
$\exp[it\int\xi_a(x)\mathcal T_a^A(x)d^4x]$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.20} simply produces a gauge transformation
\eqref{eq:15.1.17} with gauge parameter
$\Lambda_\beta(x)=-t\xi_a(x)$, so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.20} may be
written as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.7}.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.7} may be applied to study the electroweak interactions
of the octet of pseudoscalar Goldstone bosons, but it has important
implications for the interactions of the Goldstone bosons themselves, in
the absence of real gauge fields. In the case where $A=0$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.8} becomes a `pure gauge' field
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 [A_{-t\xi}(x)]_\mu
 &=-i\bigl[\partial_\mu\exp\bigl(-itX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 \exp\bigl(itX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr)
 \\
 &=-i\bigl[\partial_\mu V\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 V^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr),
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.21}\label{eq:22.7.21}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 V\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)
 \equiv\exp\bigl(-itX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr).
 \tag{22.7.22}\label{eq:22.7.22}
\end{equation}
With $A_\alpha^\mu(x)=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.1} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal T_\beta(x)\Gamma[\xi,0]=0,
 \tag{22.7.23}\label{eq:22.7.23}
\end{equation}
so the result of using Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.21} in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.7} is a $G$-invariant local functional of the
Goldstone boson field $\xi_a(x)$, though, as we shall see, it is not in
general the integral over spacetime of a $G$-invariant function of
$\xi_a(x)$ and its derivatives.

The simplest example is the case of a completely broken symmetry group.
Here the condition \eqref{eq:22.7.6} is empty, and we can use the symmetric
form \eqref{eq:22.3.38} for the anomaly:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 G_a[x;A]
 =\frac{i}{24\pi^2}\epsilon^{\kappa\nu\lambda\rho}
 \operatorname{Tr}\Biggl\{T_a\Bigl[
 &\partial_\kappa A_\nu(x)\partial_\lambda A_\rho(x)
 -\frac{1}{2}i\partial_\kappa A_\nu(x)A_\lambda(x)A_\rho(x)
 \\
 &+\frac{1}{2}iA_\kappa(x)\partial_\nu A_\lambda(x)A_\rho(x)
 -\frac{1}{2}iA_\kappa(x)A_\nu(x)\partial_\lambda A_\rho(x)
 \Bigr]\Biggr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.24}\label{eq:22.7.24}
\end{equation}
where now $T_a$ is the specific representation of the group generator
furnished by the left-handed fermions (including antifermions, where the
distinction is relevant) of the theory. In this case when we use
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.21} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.24}, we find that the only
terms in the trace in \eqref{eq:22.7.24} that survive when contracted with
$\epsilon^{\kappa\nu\lambda\rho}$ are then all proportional to
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{
 T_a(\partial_\kappa V)V^{-1}(\partial_\nu V)V^{-1}
 (\partial_\lambda V)V^{-1}(\partial_\rho V)V^{-1}
 \Bigr\},
\]
with coefficients $-1$, $+1/2$, $-1/2$ and $+1/2$, respectively, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.7} becomes here
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma[\xi,0]
 ={}&-\frac{1}{48\pi^2}\epsilon^{\kappa\nu\lambda\rho}
 \int d^4y\,\xi_a(y)\int_0^1dt\,
 \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{
 T_a\bigl[\partial_\kappa V\bigl(t\xi(y)\bigr)\bigr]
 V^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(y)\bigr)
 \\
 &\quad{}\cdot
 \bigl[\partial_\nu V\bigl(t\xi(y)\bigr)\bigr]
 V^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(y)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_\lambda V\bigl(t\xi(y)\bigr)\bigr]
 V^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(y)\bigr)
 \\
 &\quad{}\cdot
 \bigl[\partial_\rho V\bigl(t\xi(y)\bigr)\bigr]
 V^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(y)\bigr)
 \Bigr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.25}\label{eq:22.7.25}
\end{equation}
As promised, this is not the integral of an invariant function of fields
and field derivatives. For instance, for small Goldstone boson fields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.25} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma[\xi,0]
 ={}&-\frac{1}{240\pi^2}\epsilon^{\kappa\nu\lambda\rho}
 \operatorname{Tr}\{T_aT_bT_cT_dT_e\}
 \int d^4y\,\xi_a\partial_\kappa\xi_b\partial_\nu\xi_c
 \partial_\lambda\xi_d\partial_\rho\xi_e
 +O(\xi^6).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.26}\label{eq:22.7.26}
\end{equation}
Any function of the covariant derivatives of Goldstone boson fields would
have a term of lowest order in the fields which would be simply a product
of partial derivatives of the Goldstone fields, and therefore could not
have a lowest-order term of the form \eqref{eq:22.7.26}.

For an example of greater practical importance, consider the
$SU(3)\times SU(3)$ chiral symmetry of quantum chromodynamics with massless
$u$, $d$, and $s$ quarks, spontaneously broken to the diagonal $SU(3)$
subgroup of Gell-Mann and Ne'eman. In order to use the results of this
section, we \emph{must} label internal momenta in the fermion loop integral
so that the vector currents of the diagonal $SU(3)$ subgroup are
anomaly-free, in which case the anomaly takes the Bardeen form
\eqref{eq:22.3.34}:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 G_a[V,A]
 =\frac{in}{16\pi^2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
 \operatorname{Tr}\Biggl\{t_a\Bigl[
 &V_{\mu\nu}V_{\rho\sigma}
 +\frac{1}{3}A_{\mu\nu}A_{\rho\sigma}
 -\frac{32}{3}A_\mu A_\nu A_\rho A_\sigma
 \\
 &+\frac{8}{3}i\bigl(
 A_\mu A_\nu V_{\rho\sigma}
 +A_\mu V_{\rho\sigma}A_\nu
 +V_{\rho\sigma}A_\mu A_\nu
 \bigr)\Bigr]\Biggr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.27}\label{eq:22.7.27}
\end{equation}
where $V_\mu$, $A_\mu$, $V_{\mu\nu}$, and $A_{\mu\nu}$ are defined by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:22.3.35}--\eqref{eq:22.3.37}; $t_a$ is here half the
Gell-Mann matrix $\lambda_a$, given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:19.7.2}; and $n$ is
the number of quark types (`colors') of each flavor. (We are now using a
lower case $t$ for the matrices representing the group generators,
because in this trace we sum only over the left-handed quarks, not the
left-handed antiquarks.) For a pure gauge field like \eqref{eq:22.7.21}
the field strengths $V_{\mu\nu}$ and $A_{\mu\nu}$ vanish, so that using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.27} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.7} yields the anomalous
effective Goldstone boson action
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma[\xi,0]
 =-\frac{2n}{3\pi^2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
 \int_0^1dt\int d^4x\,
 \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{
 \xi_at_a[A_{-t\xi}]_\mu[A_{-t\xi}]_\nu
 [A_{-t\xi}]_\rho[A_{-t\xi}]_\sigma
 \Bigr\},
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.28}\label{eq:22.7.28}
\end{equation}
where `$A$' now denotes the axial-vector as opposed to the vector gauge
field. To find $[A_{-t\xi}]_\mu$ we use Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.21}, which
here reads
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 [V_{-t\xi}(x)]_\mu+\gamma_5[A_{-t\xi}(x)]_\mu
 ={}&-i\bigl[\partial_\mu
 \exp\bigl(-it\gamma_5t_a\xi_a(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 \exp\bigl(it\gamma_5t_a\xi_a(x)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.29}\label{eq:22.7.29}
\end{equation}
Multiplying with $(1+\gamma_5)/2$ and $(1-\gamma_5)/2$ and taking the
difference yields the axial-vector term
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 [A_{-t\xi}(x)]_\mu
 ={}&-\frac{1}{2}i
 \bigl[\partial_\mu\exp(-it\xi_at_a)\bigr]\exp(it\xi_at_a)
 \\
 &+\frac{1}{2}i
 \bigl[\partial_\mu\exp(it\xi_at_a)\bigr]\exp(-it\xi_at_a)
 \\
 ={}&\frac{1}{2}i\exp(it\xi_at_a)
 U^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_\mu U\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 \exp(-it\xi_at_a),
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.30}\label{eq:22.7.30}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 U(t\xi)\equiv\exp(2it_a\xi_at).
 \tag{22.7.31}\label{eq:22.7.31}
\end{equation}
Using this in Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.28} yields the effective anomalous action
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma[\xi,0]
 ={}&-\frac{n}{24\pi^2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
 \int_0^1dt\int d^4x\,
 \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{
 \xi_at_aU^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_\mu U\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 \\
 &\quad{}\cdot U^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_\nu U\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 U^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_\rho U\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 U^{-1}\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)
 \\
 &\quad{}\cdot
 \bigl[\partial_\sigma U\bigl(t\xi(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 \Bigr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.32}\label{eq:22.7.32}
\end{equation}

As noted by Witten~\hyperref[ch22-ref-25]{[25]}, this may be expressed in
a convenient five-dimensional form.We take $t$ as our fifth coordinate,
and define $\xi_a(x,t)\equiv t\xi_a(x)$. Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.32}
becomes
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma[\xi,0]
 ={}&-\frac{in}{240\pi^2}\epsilon^{ijklm}\int d^5z\,
 \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{
 U^{-1}\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_iU\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)\bigr]
 U^{-1}\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_jU\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)\bigr]
 \\
 &\quad{}\cdot
 U^{-1}\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_kU\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)\bigr]
 U^{-1}\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)
 \bigl[\partial_lU\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)\bigr]
 U^{-1}\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)
 \\
 &\quad{}\cdot
 \bigl[\partial_mU\bigl(\xi(z)\bigr)\bigr]
 \Bigr\},
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.33}\label{eq:22.7.33}
\end{equation}
where $i$, $j$, etc. run over the values 1, 2, 3, 0, 5, with $z^i=x^i$
for $i=1,2,3,0$ and $z^5=t$, and the integral is over the region
$0\leq z^5\leq1$. (An extra factor $1/5$ appears in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.33} to take account of the fact that any one of the
five indices $i$, $j$, $k$, $l$, or $m$ may take the value 5.) Since
$\xi_a(z)$ takes a fixed value zerofor $z^5=0$and all values of the other
components $z^\mu$of $z^i$, we can identify these values of $z^i$ as a
single point, and consider the region of integration in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.33} as a five-dimensional ball, with the
four-dimensional boundary $z^5=1$ taken as ordinary spacetime.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.33} is thus a special case of the
`Wess Zumino--Witten' action given by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:19.8.1} and
\eqref{eq:19.8.3}, which was also proportional to an integer $n$; the
only difference is that $n$ is now identified as the number of colors.
We saw in \hyperref[sec:19.8]{Section 19.8} that the integral
\eqref{eq:19.8.3} depends only on the values of $\xi_a(z)$ on the
spacetime boundary of the five-ball, so in deriving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.33} we haveshown that Eq.~\eqref{eq:19.8.2} applies
for any continuation of $\xi_a(x)$ into the interior of the five-ball,
not just for $\xi_a(x,t)=t\xi_a(x)$.

More generally, consider an arbitrary gauge group $G$ that is
spontaneously broken to a subgroup $H$ that by itself is anomaly-free
--- that is, for which the $D$-symbol \eqref{eq:22.3.12} vanishes for any
three generators of $H$. Chu, Ho, and Zumino~\hyperref[ch22-ref-26]{[26]}
have shown that we can add a local function $B[A]$ to the action, in such
a way that the currents of the subgroup $H$ are anomaly free, even when
the gauge fields of the broken symmetries are taken into account. This
functional is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 B[A]
 ={}&\frac{1}{48\pi^2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\int d^4x\,
 \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{
 [A_h^\mu,A^\nu](F^{\rho\sigma}+F_h^{\rho\sigma})
 +A^\mu A_h^\nu A_h^\rho A_h^\sigma
 \\
 &\qquad{}
 -A_h^\mu A^\nu A^\rho A^\sigma
 +\frac{1}{2}A_h^\mu A^\nu A_h^\rho A^\sigma
 \Bigr\},
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.34}\label{eq:22.7.34}
\end{equation}
where again $A^\mu\equiv T_\alpha A_\alpha^\mu$ and
$F^{\mu\nu}\equiv T_\alpha F_\alpha^{\mu\nu}$, while
$A_h^\mu\equiv T_iA_i^\mu$ and $F_h^{\mu\nu}\equiv T_iF_i^{\mu\nu}$ are
the terms in $A^\mu$ and $F^{\mu\nu}$ in the algebra of the unbroken
symmetry subgroup $H$. The total anomaly is then
\begin{equation}
 G'_\beta[x;A]
 =G_\beta[x;A]+\mathcal T_\beta(x)B[A]
 \tag{22.7.35}\label{eq:22.7.35}
\end{equation}
(where $G_\beta[x;A]$ is the symmetrized anomaly
\eqref{eq:22.3.38}) and satisfies the desired condition
\begin{equation}
 G'_i[x;A]=0.
 \tag{22.7.36}\label{eq:22.7.36}
\end{equation}
The anomalous part of the effective action for Goldstone bosons and gauge
fields is given by using Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.35} in place of $G_\beta$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.7}. In this way, Chu \emph{et al.}~%
\hyperref[ch22-ref-26]{[26]} found an anomalous effective action
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma'[\xi,A]
 =\Gamma[\xi,A]-B[A_{-\xi}]+B[A],
 \tag{22.7.37}\label{eq:22.7.37}
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma[\xi,A]$ is the previously derived effective action
\eqref{eq:22.7.7}, and $A_{-\xi}(x)$ is obtained by setting $t=1$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.8}. In particular, where the gauge field vanishes
$A_{-\xi}$ is the pure gauge field
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [A_{-\xi}(x)]_\mu
 &=-i\bigl[\partial_\mu V\bigl(\xi(x)\bigr)\bigr]
 V^{-1}\bigl(\xi(x)\bigr),
 \\
 V\bigl(\xi(x)\bigr)
 &=\exp\bigl(-iX_a\xi_a(x)\bigr),
 \end{aligned}
\]
so here the Goldstone boson action is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma'[\xi,0]
 ={}&\Gamma[\xi,0]
 -\frac{1}{48\pi^2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\int d^4x\,
 \operatorname{Tr}\Bigl\{
 A_{-\xi}^\mu A_{-\xi h}^\nu A_{-\xi h}^\rho A_{-\xi h}^\sigma
 \\
 &\qquad{}
 -A_{-\xi h}^\mu A_{-\xi}^\nu A_{-\xi}^\rho A_{-\xi}^\sigma
 +\frac{1}{2}
 A_{-\xi h}^\mu A_{-\xi}^\nu A_{-\xi h}^\rho A_{-\xi}^\sigma
 \Bigr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22.7.38}\label{eq:22.7.38}
\end{equation}
This result is not unique; in particular, in parity-conserving theories
like quantum chromodynamics we can add additional local terms to the
effective action to cancel any parity-non-conserving terms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:22.7.38}.
