\noindent\textit{Proof that $TM_x$ is an $m$-dimensional vector space.}
Since
\[
g^{-1} : g(U) \to U
\]
is a smooth mapping, we can choose an open set $W$ containing $x$ and a smooth map $F : W \to \mathbb{R}^m$ that coincides with $g^{-1}$ on $W \cap g(U)$. Letting $U_0 = g^{-1}(W \cap g(U))$, we have the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=large]
U_0 \arrow[r, "g"] \arrow[d, "{F \circ g}"'] & W \cap g(U) \arrow[d, "F"] \\
\mathbb{R}^m \arrow[r, "g"] & W
\end{tikzcd}
\]
therefore
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=large]
\mathbb{R}^m \arrow[r, "\dd g_u"] \arrow[d, "{d(F \circ g)_u}"'] & \mathbb{R}^k \arrow[d, "\dd F_x"] \\
\mathbb{R}^m \arrow[r, "I"] & \mathbb{R}^m
\end{tikzcd}
\]
This diagram clearly implies that $\dd g_u$ has rank $m$, and hence that its image $TM_x$ has dimension $m$.

Now consider two smooth manifolds, $M \subset \mathbb{R}^k$ and $N \subset \mathbb{R}^l$, and a

smooth map
\[
f: M \to N
\]
with $f(x) = y$. The derivative
\[
\dd f_x : TM_x \to TN_y
\]
is defined as follows. Since $f$ is smooth there exist an open set $W$ containing $x$ and a smooth map
\[
F: W \to \mathbb{R}^l
\]
that coincides with $f$ on $W \cap M$. Define $\dd f_x(v)$ to be equal to $\dd F_x(v)$ for all $v \in TM_x$.

To justify this definition we must prove that $\dd F_x(v)$ belongs to $TN_y$ and that it does not depend on the particular choice of $F$.

Choose parametrizations
\[
g : U \to M \subset \mathbb{R}^k \quad \text{and} \quad h : V \to N \subset \mathbb{R}^l
\]
for neighborhoods $g(U)$ of $x$ and $h(V)$ of $y$. Replacing $U$ by a smaller set if necessary, we may assume that $g(U) \subset W$ and that $f$ maps $g(U)$ into $h(V)$. It follows that
\[
h^{-1} \circ f \circ g : U \to V
\]
is a well-defined smooth mapping.

Consider the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=large]
W \arrow[r, "F"] & \mathbb{R}^l \\
U \arrow[u, "g"] \arrow[r, "{h^{-1} \circ f \circ g}"] & V \arrow[u, "h"]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
of smooth mappings between open sets. Taking derivatives, we obtain a commutative diagram of linear mappings
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=large]
\mathbb{R}^m \arrow[r, "\dd(h^{-1} \circ f \circ g)_u"] \arrow[d, "\dd g_u"'] & \mathbb{R}^n \arrow[d, "\dd h_v"] \\
TM_x \arrow[r, "\dd f_x"'] & TN_y
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $u = g^{-1}(x)$, $v = h^{-1}(y)$.

It follows immediately that $\dd F_x$ carries $TM_x = \operatorname{Image}(\dd g_u)$ into $TN_y = \operatorname{Image}(\dd h_v)$. Furthermore the resulting map $\dd f_x$ does not depend on the particular choice of $F$, for we can obtain the same linear

transformation by going around the bottom of the diagram. That is:
\[
\dd f_x = \dd h_v \circ \dd(h^{-1} \circ f \circ g)_u \circ (\dd g_u)^{-1}.
\]
This completes the proof that
\[
\dd f_x : TM_x \to TN_y
\]
is a well-defined linear mapping.
