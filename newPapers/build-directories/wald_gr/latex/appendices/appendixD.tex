\section{Conformal Transformations}

Let \(M\) be an \(n\)-dimensional manifold with metric \(g_{ab}\) of any signature.
If \(\Omega\) is a smooth, strictly positive function, then the metric
\(\widetilde g_{ab}=\Omega^2g_{ab}\) is said to arise from \(g_{ab}\) via a
\emph{conformal transformation}. It should be emphasized that a conformal
transformation is not, in general, associated with a diffeomorphism of \(M\). [As
discussed at the end of Appendix~C, a diffeomorphism \(\psi:M\to M\) for which
\((\psi^*g)_{ab}=\Omega^2g_{ab}\) is called a \emph{conformal isometry}.] Conformal
transformations occur in many contexts in general relativity, in particular, in the
definition of asymptotic flatness (Chapter~11). The derivative operator and curvature
of \(\widetilde g_{ab}\) are related in a relatively simple way to those of \(g_{ab}\).
In this appendix we derive these relations and also discuss the behavior under
conformal transformations of solutions to some equations.

First, we note that in the case where \(g_{ab}\) is a Lorentz metric, a vector \(v^a\)
is timelike, null, or spacelike with respect to the metric \(g_{ab}\) if and only if
it satisfies the same property with respect to \(\widetilde g_{ab}\). Thus,
\((M,g_{ab})\) and \((M,\widetilde g_{ab})\) have identical causal structure.
Conversely, if the light cones of two Lorentz metrics \(g_{ab}\) and
\(\widetilde g_{ab}\) coincide at a point \(p\in M\), then at \(p\),
\(\widetilde g_{ab}\) must be a multiple of \(g_{ab}\),
\(\widetilde g_{ab}=\Omega^2g_{ab}\). [\emph{Proof}---Let
\(t^a,x_1^a,\ldots,x_{n-1}^a\) be an orthonormal basis of \(g_{ab}\). Then
\(t^a\pm x_i^a\) is null with respect to \(g_{ab}\) and hence with respect to
\(\widetilde g_{ab}\), which implies that, with respect to \(\widetilde g_{ab}\),
\(t^a\) and \(x_i^a\) are orthogonal and their norms have the same magnitude. The
fact that \(t^a+2^{-1/2}(x_i^a+x_j^a)\) is null for \(i\ne j\) then shows that
\(x_i^a\) is orthogonal to \(x_j^a\). Thus, apart from a constant multiple,
\(t^a,x_1^a,\ldots,x_{n-1}^a\) is an orthonormal basis of
\(\widetilde g_{ab}\).] Consequently, if the spacetimes \((M,g_{ab})\) and
\((M,\widetilde g_{ab})\) have identical causal structure, then
\(\widetilde g_{ab}\) must be related to \(g_{ab}\) by a conformal transformation.

Since in the situation under consideration two metrics, \(g_{ab}\) and
\(\widetilde g_{ab}\), are present, confusion can arise as to which metric is being
used to raise and lower indices. We shall deal with this problem by explicitly
writing the metric in all formulas in which indices are raised or lowered. We shall
denote the inverse metric to \(g_{ab}\) as \(g^{ab}\) and the inverse metric to
\(\widetilde g_{ab}\) as \(\widetilde g^{ab}\). Clearly, we have
\(\widetilde g^{ab}=\Omega^{-2}g^{ab}\), since then
\(\widetilde g^{ab}\widetilde g_{bc}=g^{ab}g_{bc}=\delta^a{}_c\). Note that
\(\widetilde g^{ab}\) is \emph{not} equal to \(\widetilde g_{ab}\) with indices
raised by \(g^{ab}\).

Let \(\nabla_a\) denote the derivative operator associated with \(g_{ab}\), and let
\(\widetilde\nabla_a\) denote the derivative operator associated with
\(\widetilde g_{ab}\). The relation between \(\widetilde\nabla_a\) and \(\nabla_a\)
is given by Eqs.~(3.1.7) and~(3.1.28). Reversing the roles of
\(\widetilde\nabla_a\) and \(\nabla_a\) in these equations (so that
\(C^c{}_{ab}\) now is defined by
\(\widetilde\nabla_a\omega_b=\nabla_a\omega_b-C^c{}_{ab}\omega_c\)), we find
\begin{equation}
  C^c{}_{ab}
  =\frac{1}{2}\widetilde g^{cd}
  \left\{\nabla_a\widetilde g_{bd}
  +\nabla_b\widetilde g_{ad}
  -\nabla_d\widetilde g_{ab}\right\}.
  \tag{D.1}\label{eq:D.1}
\end{equation}
However, since \(\nabla_ag_{bc}=0\), we have
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_a\widetilde g_{bc}
  =\nabla_a(\Omega^2g_{bc})
  =2\Omega g_{bc}\nabla_a\Omega.
  \tag{D.2}\label{eq:D.2}
\end{equation}
Hence, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  C^c{}_{ab}
  &=\Omega^{-1}g^{cd}
  \left\{g_{bd}\nabla_a\Omega+g_{ad}\nabla_b\Omega-g_{ab}\nabla_d\Omega\right\}\\
  &=2\delta^c{}_{(a}\nabla_{b)}\ln\Omega
  -g_{ab}g^{cd}\nabla_d\ln\Omega,
\end{aligned}
  \tag{D.3}\label{eq:D.3}
\end{equation}
which expresses \(C^c{}_{ab}\) in terms of \(\Omega\) and \(g_{ab}\).

We can use Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.3} to compare the geodesics with respect to
\(\nabla_a\) with those with respect to \(\widetilde\nabla_a\). The tangent, \(v^a\),
to an affinely parameterized geodesic \(\gamma\) with respect to \(\nabla_a\)
satisfies
\begin{equation}
  v^a\nabla_av^b=0.
  \tag{D.4}\label{eq:D.4}
\end{equation}
Hence, we have
\begin{equation}
  v^a\widetilde\nabla_av^b
  =v^a\nabla_av^b+v^aC^b{}_{ac}v^c
  =2v^bv^c\nabla_c\ln\Omega
  -(g_{ac}v^av^c)g^{bd}\nabla_d\ln\Omega.
  \tag{D.5}\label{eq:D.5}
\end{equation}
Thus, in general \(\gamma\) fails to be a geodesic with respect to
\(\widetilde\nabla_a\). However, in the case of a null geodesic,
\(g_{ac}v^av^c=0\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.5} is just the (non-affinely parameterized)
geodesic equation (3.3.2) with \(\alpha=2v^c\nabla_c\ln\Omega\). Thus, null
geodesics are conformally invariant, i.e., the null geodesics with respect to
\(\nabla_a\) coincide with those with respect to \(\widetilde\nabla_a\), with the
affine parameter \(\widetilde\lambda\) for \(\widetilde\nabla_a\)-geodesics related
to the affine parameter \(\lambda\) for \(\nabla_a\)-geodesics by
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd\widetilde\lambda}{\dd\lambda}=c\Omega^2,
  \tag{D.6}\label{eq:D.6}
\end{equation}
where \(c\) is a constant.

The relation between the curvature, \(\widetilde R_{abc}{}^d\), associated with
\(\widetilde\nabla_a\) and the curvature, \(R_{abc}{}^d\), associated with
\(\nabla_a\) is given by Eq.~(7.5.8). Hence, using our formula \eqref{eq:D.3} for
\(C^c{}_{ab}\), we find
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde R_{abc}{}^d
  &=R_{abc}{}^d-2\nabla_{[a}C^d{}_{b]c}
  +2C^e{}_{c[a}C^d{}_{b]e}\\
  &=R_{abc}{}^d+2\delta^d{}_{[a}\nabla_{b]}\nabla_c\ln\Omega
  -2g^{\dd e}g_{c[a}\nabla_{b]}\nabla_e\ln\Omega\\
  &\quad+2(\nabla_{[a}\ln\Omega)\delta^d{}_{b]}\nabla_c\ln\Omega
  -2(\nabla_{[a}\ln\Omega)g_{b]c}g^{\dd f}\nabla_f\ln\Omega\\
  &\quad-2g_{c[a}\delta^d{}_{b]}g^{ef}
  (\nabla_e\ln\Omega)\nabla_f\ln\Omega.
\end{aligned}
  \tag{D.7}\label{eq:D.7}
\end{equation}
Contracting over \(b\) and \(d\), we obtain
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde R_{ac}
  &=R_{ac}-(n-2)\nabla_a\nabla_c\ln\Omega
  -g_{ac}g^{\dd e}\nabla_d\nabla_e\ln\Omega\\
  &\quad+(n-2)(\nabla_a\ln\Omega)\nabla_c\ln\Omega
  -(n-2)g_{ac}g^{\dd e}(\nabla_d\ln\Omega)\nabla_e\ln\Omega.
\end{aligned}
  \tag{D.8}\label{eq:D.8}
\end{equation}
Contracting Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.8} with \(\widetilde g^{ac}=\Omega^{-2}g^{ac}\), we
obtain
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde R
  =\Omega^{-2}\left\{
  R-2(n-1)g^{ac}\nabla_a\nabla_c\ln\Omega
  -(n-2)(n-1)g^{ac}(\nabla_a\ln\Omega)\nabla_c\ln\Omega\right\},
  \tag{D.9}\label{eq:D.9}
\end{equation}
where \(\widetilde R\equiv\widetilde g^{ab}\widetilde R_{ab}\) and
\(R\equiv g^{ab}R_{ab}\). Finally, from the definition of the Weyl tensor,
Eq.~(3.2.28), we find that \(C_{abc}{}^d\) is unchanged by a conformal
transformation of the metric,
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde C_{abc}{}^d=C_{abc}{}^d.
  \tag{D.10}\label{eq:D.10}
\end{equation}
(Note, however, that because it would be natural to use different metrics to raise
and lower indices on \(\widetilde C_{abc}{}^d\) and \(C_{abc}{}^d\), the equality of
\(\widetilde C_{abc}{}^d\) and \(C_{abc}{}^d\) depends crucially on the index
positions. For example, we have
\(\widetilde C_{abcd}\equiv\widetilde g_{\dd e}\widetilde C_{abc}{}^e
=\Omega^2g_{\dd e}C_{abc}{}^e=\Omega^2C_{abcd}\).)
Equations~\eqref{eq:D.8}--\eqref{eq:D.10} are the desired formulas expressing how
curvature is changed by conformal transformations.

Next, we analyze the conformal invariance of certain equations involving the metric.
An equation for a field \(\Psi\) is said to be \emph{conformally invariant} if there
exists a number \(s\in\mathbb R\) (called the \emph{conformal weight} of the field)
such that \(\Psi\) is a solution with metric \(g_{ab}\) if and only if
\(\widetilde\Psi=\Omega^s\Psi\) is a solution with metric
\(\widetilde g_{ab}=\Omega^2g_{ab}\). Many equations for physical fields are
conformally invariant, and the study of the behavior of equations under conformal
transformations also is useful for many mathematical purposes.

As a first example, we show that the equation
\begin{equation}
  g^{ab}\nabla_a\nabla_b\phi=0
  \tag{D.11}\label{eq:D.11}
\end{equation}
for a scalar field is \emph{not} conformally invariant if \(\dim M\ne2\). (In the
case of a Riemannian metric, Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.11} is a natural generalization of
Laplace's equation to curved space. For a Lorentz metric, Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.11} is the
massless Klein--Gordon equation considered in Chapters~4 and~10.) We have
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde g^{ab}\widetilde\nabla_a\widetilde\nabla_b(\widetilde\phi)
  &=\Omega^{-2}g^{ab}\nabla_a[\nabla_b(\Omega^s\phi)]\\
  &=\Omega^{-2}g^{ab}\left[
  \nabla_a\nabla_b(\Omega^s\phi)-C^c{}_{ab}\nabla_c(\Omega^s\phi)\right]\\
  &=\Omega^{s-2}g^{ab}\nabla_a\nabla_b\phi
  +(2s+n-2)\Omega^{s-3}g^{ab}\nabla_a\Omega\nabla_b\phi\\
  &\quad+s\Omega^{s-3}\phi g^{ab}\nabla_a\nabla_b\Omega\\
  &\quad+s(n+s-3)\Omega^{s-4}\phi g^{ab}\nabla_a\Omega\nabla_b\Omega.
\end{aligned}
  \tag{D.12}\label{eq:D.12}
\end{equation}
Thus, if \(n=2\), we may choose \(s=0\) and Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.12} then implies
\(\widetilde g^{ab}\widetilde\nabla_a\widetilde\nabla_b\widetilde\phi=0\) if and
only if \(g^{ab}\nabla_a\nabla_b\phi=0\). However, if \(n\ne2\), no choice of \(s\)
will make
\(\widetilde g^{ab}\widetilde\nabla_a\widetilde\nabla_b\widetilde\phi\) vanish
whenever \(g^{ab}\nabla_a\nabla_b\phi\) vanishes. Thus, Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.11} is not
conformally invariant except in two dimensions.

However, for \(n>1\), it is possible to modify Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.11} in a simple
manner so that it becomes conformally invariant. First, if we choose
\(s=1-n/2\) then the \(\nabla_a\Omega\nabla_b\phi\) term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.12}
will be eliminated. Using this choice of \(s\) and the behavior of the scalar
curvature, \(R\), under conformal transformations given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.9}, we
find that for \(n\ne1\) the addition of the term \(\alpha R\phi\) to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.11} will cancel the \(\phi g^{ab}\nabla_a\nabla_b\Omega\) term and
the \(\phi g^{ab}\nabla_a\Omega\nabla_b\Omega\) term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.12}
provided we choose \(\alpha=-(n-2)/4(n-1)\). Thus, the equation
\begin{equation}
  g^{ab}\nabla_a\nabla_b\phi-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}R\phi=0
  \tag{D.13}\label{eq:D.13}
\end{equation}
is conformally invariant, with weight \(s=1-n/2\), since we have
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \left(\widetilde g^{ab}\widetilde\nabla_a\widetilde\nabla_b
  -\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\widetilde R\right)[\Omega^{1-n/2}\phi]
  =\Omega^{-1-n/2}
  \left[g^{ab}\nabla_a\nabla_b-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}R\right]\phi.
\end{aligned}
  \tag{D.14}\label{eq:D.14}
\end{equation}
Thus, Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.13} provides a conformally invariant generalization to curved
geometry of the Laplace and Klein--Gordon equations in flat spaces.

Next, we demonstrate that Maxwell's equations,
\begin{equation}
  g^{ac}\nabla_cF_{ab}=0,
  \tag{D.15}\label{eq:D.15}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{[a}F_{bc]}=0,
  \tag{D.16}\label{eq:D.16}
\end{equation}
are conformally invariant in four dimensions. We have
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde g^{ac}\widetilde\nabla_c(\Omega^sF_{ab})
  &=\Omega^{-2}g^{ac}\left\{
  \nabla_c(\Omega^sF_{ab})-C^d{}_{ca}\Omega^sF_{\dd b}
  -C^d{}_{cb}\Omega^sF_{ad}\right\}\\
  &=\Omega^{s-2}g^{ac}\nabla_cF_{ab}
  +(n-4+s)\Omega^{s-3}g^{ac}F_{ab}\nabla_c\Omega.
\end{aligned}
  \tag{D.17}\label{eq:D.17}
\end{equation}
On the other hand, we have
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\nabla_{[a}(\Omega^sF_{bc]})
  =\Omega^s\nabla_{[a}F_{bc]}
  +s\Omega^{s-1}(\nabla_{[a}\Omega)F_{bc]}.
  \tag{D.18}\label{eq:D.18}
\end{equation}
Thus, inspection of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D.17} and \eqref{eq:D.18} shows that for
\(n\ne4\) Maxwell's equations fail to be conformally invariant, but in the
physically relevant case of four dimensions, conformal invariance holds with
conformal weight \(s=0\)\footnote{Note that for a tensor field, the assignment of
conformal weight depends on index positions; i.e., the conformal weight of \(F^{ab}\)
would be \(s=-4\). An invariant notion of conformal weight is
\(s'=s-N_l+N_u\), where \(N_l\) is the number of \lq\lq lower indices\rq\ and
\(N_u\) is the number of \lq\lq upper indices\rq\ of the tensor. Thus, the invariant
conformal weight of the Maxwell field is \(-2\).}.

Finally, we note the conformal invariance properties of the conservation equation,
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_aT^{ab}=0
  \tag{D.19}\label{eq:D.19}
\end{equation}
for a symmetric tensor field \(T^{ab}=T^{ba}\). We have
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde\nabla_a(\Omega^sT^{ab})
  &=\nabla_a(\Omega^sT^{ab})
  +C^a{}_{ac}\Omega^sT^{cb}+C^b{}_{ac}\Omega^sT^{ac}\\
  &=\Omega^s\nabla_aT^{ab}
  +(s+n+2)\Omega^{s-1}T^{ab}\nabla_a\Omega
  -\Omega^{s-1}g^{ba}T\nabla_a\Omega,
\end{aligned}
  \tag{D.20}\label{eq:D.20}
\end{equation}
where \(T=g_{cd}T^{cd}\). Thus, we see that Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.19} is not conformally
invariant. However, if we impose in addition to Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.19} and
\(T^{ab}=T^{ba}\) the requirement that \(T=0\), then Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.19} becomes
conformally invariant with \(s=-n-2\). Conversely, suppose that the stress-energy
tensor of a conformally invariant field is itself conformally invariant in the sense
that \(T^{ab}\longrightarrow\widetilde T^{ab}=\Omega^wT^{ab}\) under conformal
transformations of the metric and field variables. (This will be the case if \(T^{ab}\)
is obtained by functional differentiation of a conformally invariant action with
respect to the metric [see Appendix~E]. We use the notation \(w\) rather than \(s\)
here because the conformal weight of \(T^{ab}\) need not be equal to the conformal
weight of the field.) Then the conservation equation must be satisfied in both the
original and conformally transformed spaces. Hence, Eq.~\eqref{eq:D.20} shows that
we must have \(T=0\) (as well as \(w=-n-2\)). Thus, if the stress tensor of a
conformally invariant field is conformally invariant, its trace must vanish
identically.
