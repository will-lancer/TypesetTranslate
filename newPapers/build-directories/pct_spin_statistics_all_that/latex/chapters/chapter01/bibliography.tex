\chapterbackmatter{Bibliography}\label{sec:1.4-bibliography}

The present chapter is about what is sometimes, and with justice, called
Wignerism: the theory of symmetry in quantum mechanics. The original sources
are:

\begin{thebibliography}{10}
\bibitem{ref:1.1}
E.~P.~Wigner, \emph{Gruppentheorie und ihre Anwendung auf die Quantenmechanik
der Atomspektren}, Friedr.~Vieweg Braunschweig, 1931; English translation,
\emph{Group Theory and Its Application to the Quantum Mechanics of Atomic
Spectra}, Academic, New York, 1959. The general method for analyzing
symmetry in quantum mechanics is given here and worked out for the case of the
rotation group.

\bibitem{ref:1.2}
E.~P.~Wigner, ``\"Uber die Operation der Zeitumkehr in der Quantenmechanik,''
\emph{G\"ott. Nach.}, 546--559 (1931). Here an anti-unitary symmetry appeared
for the first time.

\bibitem{ref:1.3}
E.~P.~Wigner, ``Unitary Representations of the Inhomogeneous Lorentz Group,''
\emph{Ann. Math.}, \textbf{40}, 149 (1939).

\bibitem{ref:1.4}
G.~C.~Wick, E.~P.~Wigner, and A.~S.~Wightman, ``Intrinsic Parity of Elementary
Particles,'' \emph{Phys. Rev.}, \textbf{88}, 101 (1952). The notion of
superselection rule first appears here.
\end{thebibliography}
