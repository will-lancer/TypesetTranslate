\subsection{The Global Nature of Local Commutativity}
\label{sec:ch4-global-locality}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=146 print=134 kind=prose id=p134-local-commutativity
Local commutativity asserts the vanishing of commutators or
anti-commutators, $[\varphi(x),\psi(y)]_{\pm}$, for all space-like $x-y$.
An assumption which is apparently weaker is that this condition holds in some
smaller region, say $(x-y)^2>a>0$. Theorem 4-1 asserts that such apparently
weaker assumptions are in fact not weaker, since local commutativity can be
deduced from them.

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=146 print=134 kind=prose id=p134-theorem-scope
We prove the theorem for a single hermitian scalar field and the commutator.
The proof for an arbitrary set of fields with anti-commutators or commutators
is just a matter of adjoining a few suffices and changing a few signs, a task
we leave to the reader.

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=146 print=134 kind=theorem id=4-1
\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Theorem 4-1}\label{thm:ch4-global-locality}\par\smallskip
% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=146 print=134 kind=theorem-statement id=4-1-statement
Let $\varphi$ be a hermitian scalar field satisfying axioms I and II, but,
instead of satisfying axiom III, having the property

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=146 print=134 kind=display id=4-1
\begin{equation}
  [\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]_{-}=0
  \tag{4-1}\label{eq:ch4-local-commutativity}
\end{equation}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=146 print=134 kind=theorem-statement id=4-1-continuation
for $x$ and $y$ varying in some space-like separated open sets. Suppose the
vacuum is cyclic with respect to $\varphi$. Then $\varphi$ is local, that is,
(4-1) holds for all space-like separated $x$ and $y$.

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=remarks id=ch4-remarks
\par\medskip\noindent\textit{Remarks:}

\begin{enumerate}
% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=remark id=ch4-remark-1
\item Because of the transformation law of the field we have

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=display id=ch4-remark-1-transform
\begin{equation*}
  U(a,\Lambda)[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]_{\pm}U(a,\Lambda)^{-1}
  =[\varphi(\Lambda x+a),\varphi(\Lambda y+a)]_{\pm}
\end{equation*}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=remark id=ch4-remark-1-continuation
so the vanishing of (4-1) in the neighborhood of a point $\{x,y\}$ implies
its vanishing in a neighborhood of $\{\Lambda x+a,\Lambda y+a\}$. Thus, the
assumption of the theorem immediately implies the vanishing of the commutator
for all $\{x,y\}$ such that $(x-y)^2$ is in some open set of the positive real
axis.

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=remark id=ch4-remark-2
\item The asserted vanishing of the commutator means that this operator
vanishes on the domain of definition $D$ of $\varphi$.
\end{enumerate}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=proof id=ch4-global-locality-proof
\par\medskip\noindent\textit{Proof:}\par\smallskip

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=prose id=p135-f-distributions
Consider the two distributions $F_1$ and $F_2$, where $F_1$ is defined by

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=display id=4-2
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 F_1(&x_1-x_2,x_2-x_3,\ldots,x_{j-1}-x_j,x_j-x,x-y,y-y_1,\\
      &y_1-y_2,\ldots,y_{k-1}-y_k) \\
 &=\bra{\Omega}\varphi(x_1)\cdots\varphi(x_j)\varphi(x)\varphi(y)
   \varphi(y_1)\cdots\varphi(y_k)\ket{\Omega}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-2}\label{eq:ch4-f1-definition}
\end{equation}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=prose id=p135-f2-boundary-values
and $F_2$ is obtained from it by interchange of $x$ and $y$. They are
respectively boundary values of holomorphic functions $F_1$ and $F_2$.
$F_1$ is holomorphic in the extended tube $\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$ in the
variables

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=display id=4-3
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &x_1-x_2-i\eta_1,\ldots,x_{j-1}-x_j-i\eta_{j-1},
   x_j-x-i\eta,x-y-i\eta',\\
 &\qquad y-y_1-i\eta'',y_1-y_2-i\rho_1,\ldots,
   y_{k-1}-y_k-i\rho_{k-1} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-3}\label{eq:ch4-f1-extended-tube-variables}
\end{equation}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=prose id=p135-boundary-limit
To get $F_1$ as a boundary value one has to let
$\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_{j-1},\eta,\eta',\eta'',\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{k-1}
\longrightarrow0$ in $V_+$. The same holds for $F_2$ with $x$ and $y$
interchanged in the arguments. However, if we want to regard $F_2$ as a
function of the arguments (4-3), then those arguments have to vary over the
permuted extended tube $\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$, where $\mathcal{P}$
is the transformation which carries (4-3) into

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=display id=4-4
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &x_1-x_2-i\eta_1,\ldots,x_{j-1}-x_j-i\eta_{j-1},
   (x_j-x-i\eta)+(x-y-i\eta'),\\
 &\qquad -(x-y-i\eta'),
   (x-y-i\eta')+(y-y_1-i\eta''),\\
 &\qquad y_1-y_2-i\rho_1,\ldots,y_{k-1}-y_k-i\rho_{k-1} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-4}\label{eq:ch4-permuted-tube-transformation}
\end{equation}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=prose id=p135-second-view-f2
It is this second way of looking at $F_2$ which will be important for the
following.

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=147 print=135 kind=prose id=p135-jost-overlap
Now by the discussion at the end of Section 2-4, we know that
$\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$ and $\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$ possess real
points in common and, in fact, open sets of real points for which $x-y$ lies in
a given neighborhood of space-like vectors. In such a neighborhood
$F_1=F_1'$ and $F_2=F_2'$. Thus, the hypothesis of the present theorem implies
$F_1=F_2$ in a real open set, $\mathcal{U}$, and therefore in a complex open
set.

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=prose id=p136-hasty-argument
If we were to proceed hastily at this point, we would argue as follows. The
equality of $F_1$ and $F_2$ holds throughout
$\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}\cup\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$ by analytic
continuation. Thus we can pass to boundary values and get $F_1=F_2'$ for all
space-like $x-y$. This argument is invalid for two reasons. First, although we
know $F_1$ is single-valued throughout $\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$ and $F_2$
throughout $\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$, we have not shown that they are
single-valued throughout
$\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}\cup\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$. Second, if in the
relation

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=display id=4-5
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 F_1(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{j+k+1})
 ={}&F_2(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{j-2},\zeta_{j-1}+\zeta_j,-\zeta_j,\\
 &\qquad\zeta_j+\zeta_{j+1},\ldots,\zeta_{j+k+1})
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-5}\label{eq:ch4-boundary-argument}
\end{equation}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=prose id=p136-hasty-argument-continuation
we approach the boundary in $\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$ so that the imaginary parts
of $\zeta_1\ldots\zeta_{j+k+1}$ lie within the appropriate cone making
$F_1\longrightarrow F_1'$, then on the right-hand side we shall not be
approaching the boundary in $\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$, so that we cannot be sure
that $F_2\longrightarrow F_2'$. We shall avoid these objections by a more
careful argument. A different method from the one given here is to be found in
Ref. 1; it does not use the edge of the wedge theorem.

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=prose id=p136-scaling-path
Continuing with the proof, by the first remark we may assume that $\mathcal{U}$
depends on $x-y$ only through $(x-y)^2$. We can continue the relation
$F_1=F_2$ along the path given by points of the form

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=display id=4-6
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &\rho(x_1-x_2),\ldots,\rho(x_{j-1}-x_j),\rho(x_j-x),\rho(x-y),
   \rho(y-y_1),\\
 &\qquad\rho(y_1-y_2),\ldots,\rho(y_{k-1}-y_k),\quad \rho>0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-6}\label{eq:ch4-scaling-path}
\end{equation}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=prose id=p136-scaling-path-continuation
where the point with $\rho=1$ lies in the neighborhood $\mathcal{U}$ introduced
above; it can easily be seen that this curve lies in the common set of Jost
points of the extended tubes $\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$ and
$\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$ if the point with $\rho=1$ does. Clearly, we
can take the vector $x-y$ to a point with any positive value of
$\xi^2=\rho^2(x-y)^2$ whatever, the open set $\mathcal{U}$ being transformed
to another open set $\rho\mathcal{U}$. Therefore if $(x-y)^2>0$,

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=display id=ch4-locality-open-set
\begin{equation*}
F_1=F_2
\end{equation*}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=prose id=p136-locality-open-set-continuation
in some open set $\rho\mathcal{U}$. (Clearly, this argument avoids the first
pitfall mentioned in the preceding paragraph, the possible several-valuedness
of $F_1$ and $F_2$ in $\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}\cup
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}'_{j+k+1}$.)

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=prose id=p136-final-step
The last step in the proof shows that the equality $F_1=F_2$, if
$(x-y)^2>0$, and all the variables lie in $\rho\mathcal{U}$, implies its
validity for all $x_1,\ldots,x_j$, $y_1,\ldots,y_k$ and all space-like $x-y$.
For this purpose we introduce two new tempered distributions, $f_1$ and
$f_2$. Let $(x'-y')$ such that $(x'-y')^2>0$ be given and let
$\rho\mathcal{U}$ be the corresponding open set. The equation

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=148 print=136 kind=display id=4-7
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 f_1(&x_1-x_2,\ldots,x_{j-1}-x_j,x_j,-y_1,
      y_1-y_2,\ldots,y_{k-1}-y_k) \\
 &=\int h(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,
   \bra{\Omega}\varphi(x_1)\cdots\varphi(x_j)\varphi(x)\varphi(y)
   \varphi(y_1)\cdots\varphi(y_k)\ket{\Omega}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-7}\label{eq:ch4-tempered-distribution}
\end{equation}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=prose continuation-of=p136-final-step id=p137-tempered-distribution-continuation
defines $f_1$, and $f_2$ is defined in the same way except that
$\varphi(x)$ and $\varphi(y)$ are interchanged. Here the test function $h$ has
a compact support $K$ containing the given point $x'-y'$, the support $K$ being
small enough so that, for every vector $x-y$ in $K$ over which we are
integrating in (4-7), the set $\rho\mathcal{U}$ always contains a fixed open
set $V$ in the space of the remaining variables
$x_1-x_2,\ldots,x_{j-1}-x_j,x_j,-y_1,y_1-y_2,\ldots,y_{k-1}-y_k$.

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=prose id=p137-tempered-distribution-holomorphy
The distributions $f_1$ and $f_2$ are, respectively, boundary values of
holomorphic functions $f_1$ and $f_2$ holomorphic when their arguments vary
over the tube $\mathcal{T}_{j+k+1}$ in the variables

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=display id=4-7-continuation-variables
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
 &x_1-x_2-\ii\eta_1,\ldots,x_{j-1}-x_j-\ii\eta_{j-1},x_j-x-\ii\eta,
   x-y-\ii\eta',\\
 &\qquad y-y_1-\ii\eta'',y_1-y_2-\ii\rho_1,\ldots,
   y_{k-1}-y_k-\ii\rho_{k-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=prose id=p137-tempered-distribution-spectrum
This follows from Theorem 2-6, because the Fourier transforms of $f_1$ and
$f_2$ vanish unless their arguments lie in the physical spectrum. Now we apply
Theorem 2-17 to the difference $f_1-f_2$. It is holomorphic in
$\mathcal{T}_{j+k+1}$ and vanishes for an open set of real points; therefore it
vanishes identically and so does the boundary value $f_1-f_2$. Since the test
function $h$ is arbitrary provided its support $K$ lies in a sufficiently small
neighborhood of $x'-y'$, we conclude $F_1=F_2$ in some neighborhood of $x'-y'$.
Since $x'-y'$ was an arbitrary space-like vector we conclude\footnote{%
% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=footnote id=fn-p137-section-2-1
See Section~2-1.} $F_1=F_2$ if $(x-y)^2>0$. This means

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=display id=4-8
\begin{equation}
  \braket{\Phi}{[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]_-\Psi}=0
  \quad\text{for }(x-y)^2>0,\quad \ket{\Psi},\ket{\Phi}\in D_0,
  \tag{4-8}\label{eq:ch4-locality-domain}
\end{equation}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=prose id=p137-locality-domain
the domain of vectors obtained by applying the smeared fields to the vacuum.
Since $D_0$ is dense (the cyclicity axiom), and the scalar product is
continuous, we get (4-8) for all $\ket{\Phi}\in D$, $\ket{\Psi}\in D_0$.
Using the hermiticity relation

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=display id=4-8-hermiticity
\begin{equation*}
 -\braket{\Phi}{[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]_-\Psi}
 =\braket{[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]_-\Phi}{\Psi},
\end{equation*}

% PCT-SOURCE: pdf=149 print=137 kind=prose id=p137-locality-final
we see that if $\ket{\Phi}\in D$, $(x-y)^2>0$,
$[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]_-\ket{\Phi}$ is orthogonal to all
$\ket{\Psi}\in D_0$ and therefore vanishes.\hfill$\blacksquare$
