It should be pointed out that although the vacuum is cyclic for all algebras
$\PolynomialAlgebra(\mathcal O)$ they are not all irreducible. For example, if
$\mathcal O\ne\R^4$, we cannot prove \eqref{eq:ch4-18} for all $a$ if we are given \eqref{eq:ch4-17}
only for test functions with support in $\mathcal O$. In particular, we
cannot prove the time slice axiom (Section \ref{sec:ch3-2-independence}). For example, in a theory of
nucleons interacting with $\pi$-mesons (we assume the particles are stable) we
may introduce fields $\psi_p,\psi_n$ and $\varphi^+,\varphi^0$ for the $p,n$
and $\pi^+,\pi^0$, respectively. In an asymptotically complete theory,
states of the form $P(\psi_p,\psi_n)\ket{\Omega}$ span the Hilbert space
(including $\pi$-mesons). This follows from the Haag--Ruelle theory if
$P(\psi_p,\psi_n)\ket{\Omega}$ is not orthogonal to the one-meson states.
Theorem \ref{thm:ch4-5-smeared-fields} then asserts that the operators $(\psi_p,\psi_n)$ form an
irreducible set, when smeared with arbitrary test functions. The same remarks
show that $(\psi_p,\varphi^-)$ and $(\psi_n,\varphi^+)$ are always alternative
irreducible sets. However as far as we know it could happen that in one theory
$(\psi_p,\psi_n)$ is irreducible in a time slice, while in another theory it
is reducible, while $(\psi_p,\psi_n,\varphi)$ is an irreducible set. This
subject is closely related to the question of whether the pion is in some
sense a bound state of a nucleon and anti-nucleon, or not.

A natural continuation of the arguments of this section would lead to the
introduction of the notion of the von Neumann algebra $\mathcal R(\mathcal O)$
of an open set $\mathcal O$. This is a $*$-algebra of \emph{bounded operators}.
The most natural way to obtain them would be to use the spectral resolution of
the hermitian elements of $\PolynomialAlgebra(\mathcal O)$, and to take the
algebra generated by their spectral projections. We shall not undertake to
explain these developments here, but only remark that there are good reasons
to believe that the study of the $\mathcal R(\mathcal O)$ is worthwhile. There
are indications that two field theories with the same representation of the
Lorentz group will yield the same $S$ matrix if and only if their
$\mathcal R(\mathcal O)$ are isomorphic.%

\subsection{The PCT Theorem}
\label{sec:ch4-3-pct-theorem}

In Section \ref{sec:ch3-symmetries} we saw that the existence of a $\PCT$ operator, $\Theta$, for
a set of fields was equivalent to the validity of the identities \eqref{eq:ch3-pct-vacuum-identity}:
\begin{equation}
  \bra{\Omega}\varphi_1(x_1)\cdots\varphi_n(x_n)\ket{\Omega}
  =(-1)^J\ii^F
  \bra{\Omega}\varphi_n(-x_n)\cdots\varphi_1(-x_1)\ket{\Omega}.
  \tag{4-19}\label{eq:ch4-19-pct-identity}
\end{equation}

In this section we shall prove that the identities \eqref{eq:ch4-19-pct-identity} hold in every field
theory of local fields. That is the $\PCT$ theorem or Lüders--Pauli theorem.
In fact, a more precise result will be proved which shows that a weaker
condition, so-called \emph{weak local commutativity}, is sufficient
for the validity of the identities \eqref{eq:ch4-19-pct-identity}. This refined form which we also
call the $\PCT$ theorem is due to Jost, as is the method of proof.

For the sake of clarity, we shall first state and prove the $\PCT$ theorem for
the theory of a neutral scalar field and only afterwards extend it to the case
of general fields.

\begin{theorem}[4-6 \emph{(PCT Theorem for a Neutral Scalar Field)}]
\label{thm:ch4-6-neutral-scalar-pct}

Let $\varphi$ be a hermitian scalar field satisfying axioms I and II but not
necessarily III (LC). If the $\PCT$ condition
\begin{equation}
  \bra{\Omega}\varphi(x_1)\cdots\varphi(x_n)\ket{\Omega}
  =\bra{\Omega}\varphi(-x_n)\cdots\varphi(-x_1)\ket{\Omega}
  \tag{4-20}\label{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition}
\end{equation}
holds for all $x_1\ldots x_n$, then for every $x_1\ldots x_n$ such that
$x_1-x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1}-x_n$ is a Jost point, the W(weak) L(local)
C(ommutativity) condition
\begin{equation}
  \bra{\Omega}\varphi(x_1)\cdots\varphi(x_n)\ket{\Omega}
  =\bra{\Omega}\varphi(x_n)\cdots\varphi(x_1)\ket{\Omega}
  \tag{4-21}\label{eq:ch4-21-wlc-condition}
\end{equation}
holds. Conversely, if the WLC condition \eqref{eq:ch4-21-wlc-condition} holds in a (real)
neighborhood of a Jost point, then the $\PCT$ condition \eqref{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition} holds
everywhere. Since LC implies the WLC conditions, every field theory of a local
hermitian scalar field has $\PCT$ symmetry.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Our first step is to translate the $\PCT$ condition \eqref{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition} into an equivalent
relation for a holomorphic function. By Theorem \ref{thm:ch3-5-holomorphic-boundary-values}, we know that there is a
holomorphic function $W$ of the $n-1$ complex vectors
$\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}$, where $\zeta_j=\xi_j-\ii\eta_j$,
$\xi_j=x_j-x_{j+1}$, holomorphic in the extended tube
$\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$ and such that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lim_{\substack{\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_{n-1}\to0\\
                   \eta\in V_+}}
  W(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  &=W(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}) \\
  &=\bra{\Omega}\varphi(x_1)\cdots\varphi(x_n)\ket{\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-22}\label{eq:ch4-22-forward-boundary-value}
\end{equation}

Furthermore, we know by Theorem \ref{thm:ch3-5-holomorphic-boundary-values} that $W$ is invariant under proper complex
Lorentz transformations
\begin{equation}
  W(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  =W(\Lambda\zeta_1,\ldots,\Lambda\zeta_{n-1}),
  \tag{4-23}\label{eq:ch4-23-lorentz-invariance}
\end{equation}
for $\Lambda\in L_+(\mathbb C)$, and all
$\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}\in\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$. This implies, in particular,
\begin{equation}
  W(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  =W(-\zeta_1,\ldots,-\zeta_{n-1})
  \quad\text{for }\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}\in\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}.
  \tag{4-24}\label{eq:ch4-24-sign-reversal}
\end{equation}
a relation that we shall use shortly. The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition} is also a
boundary value of a holomorphic function:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lim_{\substack{\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_{n-1}\to0\\
                   \eta\in V_+}}
  W(\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,\zeta_1)
  &=W(\xi_{n-1},\ldots,\xi_1) \\
  &=\bra{\Omega}\varphi(-x_n)\varphi(-x_{n-1})\cdots
    \varphi(-x_1)\ket{\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-25}\label{eq:ch4-25-reversed-boundary-value}
\end{equation}

Since $W(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})-W(\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,\zeta_1)$ is holomorphic
throughout $\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$ and vanishes for
$\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}$ real by \eqref{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition} we see by Theorem \ref{thm:edge-of-wedge-zero-boundary} that \eqref{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition}
implies
\begin{equation}
  W(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  =W(\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,\zeta_1).
  \tag{4-26}\label{eq:ch4-26-reversed-holomorphic}
\end{equation}

But, conversely, if \eqref{eq:ch4-26-reversed-holomorphic} holds in an arbitrary neighborhood of a point of
$\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$, it holds everywhere in $\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$ and, by
passing to the boundary in the tube $\mathcal{T}_{n-1}$, one recovers \eqref{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition}.
Thus, \eqref{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition} is completely equivalent to the relation \eqref{eq:ch4-26-reversed-holomorphic} for the
holomorphic function $W$.

Now we combine the relations \eqref{eq:ch4-24-sign-reversal} and \eqref{eq:ch4-26-reversed-holomorphic} to get
\begin{equation}
  W(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  =W(-\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,-\zeta_1).
  \tag{4-27}\label{eq:ch4-27-wlc-holomorphic}
\end{equation}
valid throughout $\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$. If we attempted to pass to the boundary
in this relation, we would not get a relation between vacuum expectation
values because if $\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}$ approached real vectors in the
plus tube, $\mathcal{T}_{n-1}$, $-\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,-\zeta_1$ would approach
real vectors in the minus tube. However, at a real point of holomorphy (Jost
point), \eqref{eq:ch4-27-wlc-holomorphic} is a relation between vacuum expectation values, namely,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  W(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  &=\bra{\Omega}\varphi(x_1)\cdots\varphi(x_n)\ket{\Omega} \\
  &=W(-\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,-\zeta_1)
    =\bra{\Omega}\varphi(x_n)\cdots\varphi(x_1)\ket{\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-28}\label{eq:ch4-28-wlc-jost}
\end{equation}
which is just WLC. Thus, we have proved the first half of the theorem.

The converse is easy. If \eqref{eq:ch4-28-wlc-jost} holds in a real neighborhood of a real point
of holomorphy then it holds in a complex neighborhood, and therefore, by
analytic continuation \eqref{eq:ch4-27-wlc-holomorphic} holds throughout $\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$. By the
complex Lorentz invariance \eqref{eq:ch4-24-sign-reversal} this implies the relation \eqref{eq:ch4-26-reversed-holomorphic}, which we
have seen is equivalent to the $\PCT$ condition on the vacuum expectation
value.

The last statement of the theorem is an immediate consequence of the fact that
at a Jost point all differences $x_j-x_k$, $j\ne k$ are space-like (see
Theorem \ref{thm:ch2-12-jost}), so axiom III (LC) clearly implies the WLC condition there.
\end{proof}

The proof of the $\PCT$ theorem for a field theory of fields
$\varphi_\alpha,\varphi_\beta,\ldots$ transforming according to general
irreducible representations under
the Lorentz group follows a similar pattern. The $W$ are not invariant but
instead have a transformation law under $SL(2,\mathbb C)\otimes
SL(2,\mathbb C)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \sum_{\mu'\ldots\nu'}
  S^{(\varphi)}_{\mu\ldots\mu'}(A,B)\cdots
  S^{(\psi)}_{\nu\ldots\nu'}(A,B)
  W_{\mu'\ldots\nu'}(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}) \\
  ={}&W_{\mu\ldots\nu}\bigl(\Lambda(A,B)\zeta_1,\ldots,
  \Lambda(A,B)\zeta_{n-1}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-29}\label{eq:ch4-29-general-lorentz}
\end{equation}

Here $W_{\mu\ldots\nu}$ is holomorphic in $\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$ and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lim_{\substack{\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_{n-1}\to0\\
                   \eta\in V_+}}
  W_{\mu\ldots\nu}(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  &=W_{\mu\ldots\nu}(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}) \\
  &=\bra{\Omega}\varphi_\mu(x_1)\cdots
    \varphi_\nu(x_n)\ket{\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-30}\label{eq:ch4-30-general-boundary}
\end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:ch4-29-general-lorentz} is the analogue of \eqref{eq:ch4-23-lorentz-invariance}. It implies that if the total
number of undotted and dotted indices is odd the vacuum expectation value
vanishes. To see this, compare \eqref{eq:ch4-29-general-lorentz} for $\{A,B\}=\{-1,1\}$ and
$\{1,-1\}$. The right-hand side is the same for these two values, since
$\Lambda(-1,1)=\Lambda(1,-1)=-1$, while the left-hand sides have opposite
signs according to \eqref{eq:ch1-sl2-double-cover-signs}. In any case
\begin{equation}
  S^{(\varphi)}_{\mu\mu'}(-1,1)\cdots
  S^{(\psi)}_{\nu\nu'}(-1,1)
  =\delta_{\mu\mu'}\cdots\delta_{\nu\nu'}(-1)^J,
  \tag{4-31}\label{eq:ch4-31-general-sign}
\end{equation}
where $J$ is the total number of undotted indices, so the analogue of \eqref{eq:ch4-24-sign-reversal}
is
\begin{equation}
  W_{\mu\ldots\nu}(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  =(-1)^J W_{\mu\ldots\nu}(-\zeta_1,\ldots,-\zeta_{n-1})
  \tag{4-32}\label{eq:ch4-32-general-sign-reversal}
\end{equation}

For these general fields, the analogue of the $\PCT$ condition \eqref{eq:ch4-20-pct-condition} is
\eqref{eq:ch4-19-pct-identity}. This gives rise to the relation between holomorphic functions
\begin{equation}
  W_{\mu\ldots\nu}(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  =\ii^F(-1)^J\widehat{W}_{\nu\ldots\mu}
  (\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,\zeta_1),
  \tag{4-33}\label{eq:ch4-33-general-pct-holomorphic}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{W}_{\nu\ldots\mu}$ is holomorphic in $\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$
and has the boundary value
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lim_{\substack{\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_{n-1}\to0\\
                   \eta\in V_+}}
  \widehat{W}_{\nu\ldots\mu}(\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,\zeta_1)
  &=\widehat{W}_{\nu\ldots\mu}(\xi_{n-1},\ldots,\xi_1) \\
  &=\bra{\Omega}\psi_\nu(-x_n)\cdots
    \varphi_\mu(-x_1)\ket{\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4-34}\label{eq:ch4-34-general-pct-boundary}
\end{equation}

The combination of \eqref{eq:ch4-32-general-sign-reversal} and \eqref{eq:ch4-33-general-pct-holomorphic} gives the analogue of \eqref{eq:ch4-27-wlc-holomorphic}:
\begin{equation}
  W_{\mu\ldots\nu}(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})
  =\ii^F\widehat{W}_{\nu\ldots\mu}
  (-\zeta_{n-1},\ldots,-\zeta_1),
  \tag{4-35}\label{eq:ch4-35-general-wlc-holomorphic}
\end{equation}
which, at a real point in $\mathcal{T}'_{n-1}$, is
\begin{equation}
  \bra{\Omega}\varphi_\mu(x_1)\cdots\psi_\nu(x_n)\ket{\Omega}
  =\ii^F\bra{\Omega}\psi_\nu(x_n)\cdots
  \varphi_\mu(x_1)\ket{\Omega}.
  \tag{4-36}\label{eq:ch4-36-general-wlc}
\end{equation}

This is the WLC relation for this case and it only remains to verify that it
is a consequence of the commutation relations of the fields. We are going to
assume ``normal'' commutation relations. That means that all fields commute at
space-like separations except fields whose total number of indices is odd, and
they anti-commute. This
