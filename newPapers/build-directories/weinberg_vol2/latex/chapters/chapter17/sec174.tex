\subsection{Background Field Gauge}

We next turn to a method of calculation that explicitly preserves a sort of
gauge invariance, and that therefore proves extremely convenient, especially
in one-loop calculations. We consider the effective action
$\Gamma[A,\psi,\omega,\omega^*]$ as a functional\footnote{We are now returning to the specific choice of the gauge-fixing functional $B[f]$ as the Gaussian \eqref{eq:15.7.4}, and we are integrating out the auxiliary field $h_a$, so that the gauge-fixing term in the modified Lagrangian is just $-f_a f_a/2\xi$.}
of classical external gauge, matter, ghost and antighost fields:
$A_{a\mu}(x)$, $\psi_\ell(x)$, $\omega_a(x)$,
$\omega_a^*(x)$. Even though ghosts and antighosts never appear in
initial or final states, we are considering background ghost and antighost
fields as well as gauge and matter fields in order to deal with parts of
diagrams that have external ghost or antighost lines.

As described in Section 16.1, $\Gamma[A,\psi,\omega,\omega^*]$ is the sum of
connected one-particle-irreducible graphs for the vacuum--vacuum amplitude,
calculated in a theory in which the quantum fields
$A'$, $\psi'$, $\omega'$, $\omega'{}^*$ over which we integrate are replaced
in the action with shifted fields $A+A'$, $\psi+\psi'$, $\omega+\omega'$,
$\omega^*+\omega'{}^*$, the path integral being taken over primed fields
with the unprimed fields held fixed. We are free to choose the gauge-fixing
function $f_a(x)$ pretty much any way we like; instead of our previous
choice $f_a=\partial_\mu A_a^\mu$ (or
$\partial_\mu[A_a^{\prime\mu}+A_a^\mu]$) we shall now
take~\hyperref[ch17-ref-5]{[5]}
\begin{equation}
f_a=\partial_\mu A_a^{\prime\mu}
+C_{abc}A_b^\mu A'_{c\mu}.
\label{eq:17.4.1}
\end{equation}
The reason for this choice is that it makes the gauge-fixing term
$f_a f_a$ invariant under a formal transformation, in which the
background field $A_a^\mu$ transforms as a gauge field, while the
quantum field $A_a^{\prime\mu}$ transforms homogeneously, like an
ordinary matter field that happens to belong to the adjoint representation
of the gauge group
\begin{equation}
\delta A_a^\mu=\partial^\mu\epsilon_a
-C_{abc}\epsilon_b A_c^\mu,
\label{eq:17.4.2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\delta A_a^{\prime\mu}
=-C_{abc}\epsilon_b A_c^{\prime\mu}.
\label{eq:17.4.3}
\end{equation}
The transformation properties of $f_a$ can be seen most easily by
writing it as a new sort of covariant derivative
\begin{equation}
f_a\equiv\bar D_\mu A_a^{\prime\mu},
\label{eq:17.4.4}
\end{equation}
where for any field $\phi_a$ in the adjoint representation
\begin{equation}
\bar D_\mu\phi_a\equiv
\partial_\mu\phi_a+C_{abc}A_{b\mu}\phi_c.
\label{eq:17.4.5}
\end{equation}
We see that under the transformations \eqref{eq:17.4.2},
\eqref{eq:17.4.3}, the function \eqref{eq:17.4.1} transforms just like
$A'_a$:
\begin{equation}
\delta f_a=-C_{abc}\epsilon_b f_c,
\label{eq:17.4.6}
\end{equation}
so the term $f_a f_a$ in the modified Lagrangian is invariant
\begin{equation}
\delta(f_a f_a)
=-C_{abc}f_a\epsilon_b f_c=0.
\label{eq:17.4.7}
\end{equation}
Also, the original Lagrangian density $\mathcal L$ depends on $A$ and $A'$
only through the sum $A+A'$, which under the combined transformation
\eqref{eq:17.4.2}, \eqref{eq:17.4.3} undergoes an ordinary gauge
transformation
\begin{equation}
\delta(A_a^\mu+A_a^{\prime\mu})
=\partial^\mu\epsilon_a
-C_{abc}\epsilon_b
(A_c^\mu+A_c^{\prime\mu}).
\label{eq:17.4.8}
\end{equation}
If we transform the background and quantum matter fields by
\begin{equation}
\delta\psi=it_a\epsilon_a\psi,
\label{eq:17.4.9}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\delta\psi'=it_a\epsilon_a\psi',
\label{eq:17.4.10}
\end{equation}
then also
\begin{equation}
\delta(\psi+\psi')=it_a\epsilon_a(\psi+\psi').
\label{eq:17.4.11}
\end{equation}

The original Lagrangian $\mathcal L$ is invariant under the original gauge
transformations \eqref{eq:17.4.8}, \eqref{eq:17.4.11}, and only depends on
$A+A'$ and $\psi+\psi'$, so it is also invariant under the new formal
transformations \eqref{eq:17.4.2}, \eqref{eq:17.4.3},
\eqref{eq:17.4.9}, \eqref{eq:17.4.10}.

It will be useful to make this invariance property more explicit, by writing
$\mathcal L$ in terms of the background-covariant derivative $\bar D_\mu$.
In general, we have
\begin{align}
\mathcal L={}&-\frac14\Bigl(
\partial_\mu[A_{a\nu}+A'_{a\nu}]
-\partial_\nu[A_{a\mu}+A'_{a\mu}]
\notag\\
&\hspace{3.5em}
+C_{abc}[A_{b\mu}+A'_{b\mu}]
[A_{c\nu}+A'_{c\nu}]\Bigr)^2
\notag\\
&+\mathcal L_M\Bigl(\psi+\psi',\,
\partial_\mu(\psi+\psi')
-it_a(A_{a\mu}+A'_{a\mu})(\psi+\psi')\Bigr)
\notag\\
={}&-\frac14\Bigl(F_{a\mu\nu}
+\bar D_\mu A'_{a\nu}-\bar D_\nu A'_{a\mu}
+C_{abc}A'_{b\mu}A'_{c\nu}\Bigr)^2
\notag\\
&+\mathcal L_M\Bigl(\psi+\psi',\,
\bar D_\mu(\psi+\psi')-it_a A'_{a\mu}(\psi+\psi')\Bigr),
\label{eq:17.4.12}
\end{align}
where, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:17.4.5},
\begin{equation}
\bar D_\mu A'_{a\nu}\equiv
\partial_\mu A'_{a\nu}
+C_{abc}A_{b\mu}A'_{c\nu},
\label{eq:17.4.13}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\bar D_\mu\psi\equiv\partial_\mu\psi-it_a A_{a\mu}\psi,
\label{eq:17.4.14}
\end{equation}
and $F_{a\mu\nu}$ is the background field strength
\begin{equation}
F_{a\mu\nu}\equiv
\partial_\mu A_{a\nu}-\partial_\nu A_{a\mu}
+C_{abc}A_{b\mu}A_{c\nu}.
\label{eq:17.4.15}
\end{equation}
(The square in the first term of $\mathcal L$ is intended to imply obvious
index contractions.) Clearly $\mathcal L$ is invariant under the new
transformations \eqref{eq:17.4.2}, \eqref{eq:17.4.3},
\eqref{eq:17.4.9}, \eqref{eq:17.4.10}, because it involves
$A_{a\mu}$ only in the field strength $F_{a\mu\nu}$ and in
background covariant derivatives $\bar D_\mu$ of `matter' fields
$A'_{a\mu}$, $\psi'$ and $\psi$.

This new transformation should be carefully distinguished from a true gauge
transformation. Such a transformation can have no effect on $A$ or $\psi$,
which are just prescribed classical background fields, and induces an
ordinary gauge transformation on $A+A'$ and $\psi+\psi'$, so
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\mathrm{TRUE}}A_a^\mu=0,
\label{eq:17.4.16}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
\delta_{\mathrm{TRUE}}A_a^{\prime\mu}
&=\partial^\mu\epsilon_a
-C_{abc}\epsilon_b
(A_c^\mu+A_c^{\prime\mu}) \notag\\
&=\bar D^\mu\epsilon_a
-C_{abc}\epsilon_b A_c^{\prime\mu},
\label{eq:17.4.17}
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\mathrm{TRUE}}\psi=0,
\label{eq:17.4.18}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\mathrm{TRUE}}\psi'
=it_a\epsilon_a(\psi+\psi').
\label{eq:17.4.19}
\end{equation}
Of course, this is the same as the formal transformations
\eqref{eq:17.4.2}, \eqref{eq:17.4.3}, \eqref{eq:17.4.9},
\eqref{eq:17.4.10} in its effect on $A+A'$ and $\psi+\psi'$, and therefore
also leaves the original Lagrangian $\mathcal L$ invariant. However, for our
new choice \eqref{eq:17.4.1} of $f_a$, the term $f_a f_a$ does
not depend only on $A+A'$, and is not invariant under \eqref{eq:17.4.16}
and \eqref{eq:17.4.17}. Instead,
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\mathrm{TRUE}}f_a
=\bar D_\mu\bigl(\bar D^\mu\epsilon_a
-C_{abc}\epsilon_b A_c^{\prime\mu}\bigr)
\label{eq:17.4.20}
\end{equation}
with $\bar D_\mu$ given by \eqref{eq:17.4.5}.

Finally, let us consider the ghost Lagrangian in this new gauge. The quantity
\eqref{eq:15.7.3} in the ghost action is given in general by just replacing
$\epsilon_a$ with the ghost field $\omega_a+\omega'_a$ in
$\delta_{\mathrm{TRUE}}f_a$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_a=\bar D_\mu\left[
\bar D^\mu(\omega_a+\omega'_a)
-C_{abc}(\omega_b+\omega'_b)
A_c^{\prime\mu}\right].
\label{eq:17.4.21}
\end{equation}
The ghost Lagrangian in Eq.~\eqref{eq:15.6.2} is therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{GH}}
=(\omega_a^*+\omega'_a{}^*)\bar D_\mu\left[
\bar D^\mu(\omega_a+\omega'_a)
-C_{abc}(\omega_b+\omega'_b)
A_c^{\prime\mu}\right]
\label{eq:17.4.22}
\end{equation}
or, integrating by parts,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{GH}}
=-\Bigl(\bar D_\mu(\omega_a^*+\omega'_a{}^*)\Bigr)
\Bigl(\bar D^\mu(\omega_a+\omega'_a)
-C_{abc}(\omega_b+\omega'_b)
A_c^{\prime\mu}\Bigr).
\label{eq:17.4.23}
\end{equation}
This is manifestly invariant under the joint transformations
\eqref{eq:17.4.2}, \eqref{eq:17.4.3}, supplemented now with
transformations on $\omega$ and $\omega'$:
\begin{equation}
\delta\omega_a
=-C_{abc}\epsilon_b\omega_c,
\label{eq:17.4.24}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\delta\omega'_a
=-C_{abc}\epsilon_b\omega'_c,
\label{eq:17.4.25}
\end{equation}
and likewise
\begin{equation}
\delta\omega_a^*
=-C_{abc}\epsilon_b\omega_c^*,
\label{eq:17.4.26}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\delta\omega'_a{}^*
=-C_{abc}\epsilon_b\omega'_c{}^*.
\label{eq:17.4.27}
\end{equation}
We see that the formal combined transformations \eqref{eq:17.4.2},
\eqref{eq:17.4.3}, \eqref{eq:17.4.9}, \eqref{eq:17.4.10}, and
\eqref{eq:17.4.24}--\eqref{eq:17.4.27} leave invariant the complete
Lagrangian in the modified action \eqref{eq:15.6.4}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{MOD}}
=\mathcal L-\frac1{2\xi}f_a f_a+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{GH}}.
\label{eq:17.4.28}
\end{equation}

We are integrating over $A'$, $\psi'$, $\omega'$, and $\omega'{}^*$ with a
measure that is presumed to be invariant under the simple matrix
transformations \eqref{eq:17.4.3}, \eqref{eq:17.4.10},
\eqref{eq:17.4.25}, and \eqref{eq:17.4.27}, so the effective action
$\Gamma[A,\psi,\omega,\omega^*]$ is invariant under the remaining
transformations \eqref{eq:17.4.2}, \eqref{eq:17.4.9},
\eqref{eq:17.4.24}, and \eqref{eq:17.4.26}. In other words, it is
gauge-invariant in the same sense as the original action
$I[A,\psi,\omega,\omega^*]$.

This formal gauge invariance sets powerful constraints on the infinities
that can occur in the effective action. The ultraviolet divergences in
$\Gamma$ appear in the coefficients of terms whose dimensionalities are
$[\mathrm{mass}]^d$ with $d\leq4$, but here these terms are invariant under
the background gauge transformations \eqref{eq:17.4.2},
\eqref{eq:17.4.9}, \eqref{eq:17.4.24}, and \eqref{eq:17.4.26}. For
instance, in a gauge theory based on a simple gauge group, with spin $1/2$
fermions belonging to an irreducible representation of this group, the only
such terms are of the form
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_\infty=\int d^4x\,\mathcal L_\infty,
\label{eq:17.4.29}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_\infty={}&-\frac14L_A F_{a\mu\nu}F_a^{\mu\nu}
-L_\psi\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\bar D_\mu\psi \notag\\
&-mL_m\bar\psi\psi
-L_\omega(\bar D_\mu\omega_a^*)(\bar D^\mu\omega_a),
\label{eq:17.4.30}
\end{align}
where here $F_{a\mu\nu}$, $\bar D_\mu\psi$,
$\bar D_\mu\omega_a$, and $\bar D_\mu\omega_a^*$ are constructed
entirely from background fields:\footnote{The condition of a simple gauge group insures that there is just a single kinetic term for $A$ and for $\omega$, proportional to $F_{a\mu\nu}F_a^{\mu\nu}$ and $(\bar D_\mu\omega_a^*)(\bar D^\mu\omega_a)$ respectively, while the condition that $\psi$ transforms irreducibly insures that there is just a single kinetic term and a single mass term for $\psi$. It would be easy to treat more general possibilities, at the cost of a slight complication in notation. We are also implicitly using the conservation of ghost number.}
\begin{equation}
F_{a\mu\nu}\equiv
\partial_\mu A_{a\nu}-\partial_\nu A_{a\mu}
+C_{abc}A_{b\mu}A_{c\nu},
\label{eq:17.4.31}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\bar D_\mu\psi\equiv\partial_\mu\psi-it_a A_{a\mu}\psi,
\label{eq:17.4.32}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\bar D_\mu\omega_a\equiv
\partial_\mu\omega_a
+C_{abc}A_{b\mu}\omega_c,
\label{eq:17.4.33}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\bar D_\mu\omega_a^*\equiv
\partial_\mu\omega_a^*
+C_{abc}A_{b\mu}\omega_c^*.
\label{eq:17.4.34}
\end{equation}
Dimensional analysis leads us to expect that the constants $L_A$, $L_\psi$,
$L_m$, and $L_\omega$ are logarithmically divergent.

To deal with these infinities, we note that the Lagrangian \eqref{eq:17.4.12}
contains a purely classical piece
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CLASS}}
=-\frac14F_{a\mu\nu}F_a^{\mu\nu}
-\bar\psi(\gamma^\mu\bar D_\mu+m)\psi
-(\bar D_\mu\omega_a^*)(\bar D^\mu\omega_a)
\label{eq:17.4.35}
\end{equation}
obtained by dropping all terms in $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{MOD}}$ that involve
the quantum fields $A'$, $\psi'$, $\omega'$, $\omega'{}^*$. We define
renormalized fields
\begin{equation}
A_{a\mu}^R\equiv\sqrt{1+L_A}\,A_{a\mu},
\label{eq:17.4.36}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\psi_\ell^R\equiv\sqrt{1+L_\psi}\,\psi_\ell,
\label{eq:17.4.37}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\omega_a^R\equiv\sqrt{1+L_\omega}\,\omega_a,
\label{eq:17.4.38}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\omega_a^{R*}\equiv\sqrt{1+L_\omega}\,\omega_a^*,
\label{eq:17.4.39}
\end{equation}
so that the sum of the terms \eqref{eq:17.4.30} and \eqref{eq:17.4.35}
takes the form
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CLASS}}+\mathcal L_\infty
={}&-\frac14F_{a\mu\nu}^R F_a^{R\mu\nu}
-\bar\psi^R\gamma^\mu D_\mu^R\psi^R \notag\\
&-m^R\bar\psi^R\psi^R
-(D_\mu^R\omega_a^{*R})(D^{\mu R}\omega_a^R),
\label{eq:17.4.40}
\end{align}
where $m^R$ is the renormalized mass
\begin{equation}
m^R\equiv m(1+L_m)/(1+L_\psi),
\label{eq:17.4.41}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
F_{a\mu\nu}^R\equiv
\partial_\mu A_{a\nu}^R-\partial_\nu A_{a\mu}^R
+C_{abc}^R A_{b\mu}^R A_{c\nu}^R,
\label{eq:17.4.42}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
D_\mu^R\psi^R\equiv
\partial_\mu\psi^R-it_a^R A_{a\mu}^R\psi^R,
\label{eq:17.4.43}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
D_\mu^R\omega_a^R\equiv
\partial_\mu\omega_a^R
+C_{abc}^R A_{b\mu}^R\omega_c^R,
\label{eq:17.4.44}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
D_\mu^R\omega_a^{*R}\equiv
\partial_\mu\omega_a^{*R}
+C_{abc}^R A_{b\mu}^R\omega_c^{*R}.
\label{eq:17.4.45}
\end{equation}
The renormalized structure constants and group generators here are just
\begin{equation}
C_{abc}^R\equiv
(1+L_A)^{-1/2}C_{abc},
\label{eq:17.4.46}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
t_a^R\equiv(1+L_A)^{-1/2}t_a.
\label{eq:17.4.47}
\end{equation}
Because we assumed that the Lie algebra here is simple, the structure
constant $C_{abc}$ and group generator $t_a$ are fixed by
the group structure except for a single common factor, the unrenormalized
gauge coupling constant $g$. Eqs.~\eqref{eq:17.4.46} and
\eqref{eq:17.4.47} thus simply tell us that the gauge coupling constant
$g^R$ factor in $C^R$ and $t^R$ is renormalized by
\begin{equation}
g^R=g(1+L_A)^{-1/2}.
\label{eq:17.4.48}
\end{equation}

This result exhibits the particular virtue of the background field gauge.
In a general gauge we would encounter independent renormalization factors
for the gauge field and the gauge coupling constant, and we would have to
calculate two separate amplitudes (say, the vacuum polarization and
three-gauge-field vertex function) in order to be able to sort these out.
In background field gauge, background field gauge invariance ties these two
renormalizations together by requiring that the infinite terms in the
effective Lagrangian involve the field strength in its original form
\eqref{eq:17.4.31}, and so we can calculate the charge renormalization factor
by studying just one gauge field amplitude.
