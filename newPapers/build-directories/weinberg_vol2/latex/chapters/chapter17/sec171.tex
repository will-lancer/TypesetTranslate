\subsection{The Zinn--Justin Equation}
\label{sec:17.1}

In this section the BRST symmetry described in Section 15.7 will be used to demonstrate a fundamental property of the quantum effective action, first derived by Zinn--Justin~\hyperref[ch17-ref-1]{[1]}. According to the general rules outlined in Section 16.4, the BRST invariance of the action $I[\chi]$ imposes on the effective action $\Gamma[\chi]$ the condition
\begin{equation}
\int d^4x\,\langle\Delta^n(x)\rangle_{J_\chi}
\frac{\delta_L\Gamma[\chi]}{\delta\chi^n(x)}=0,
\tag{17.1.1}\label{eq:17.1.1}
\end{equation}
where the change in $\chi^n(x)$ under a BRST transformation with infinitesimal fermionic parameter $\theta$ is
\begin{equation}
\delta_\theta\chi^n(x)=\theta\Delta^n(x)
\tag{17.1.2}\label{eq:17.1.2}
\end{equation}
and $\langle\cdots\rangle$ here denotes a vacuum expectation value taken in the presence of a current $J_\chi$ that makes the vacuum expectation value of the operator fields $X^n(x)$ equal to the c-number functions $\chi^n(x)$. The implied sum over $n$ runs over all of the fields in the BRST formalism; that is, over $\omega_\alpha$, $\omega_\alpha^*$, and $h_\alpha$ as well as the gauge and matter fields that in Section 15.8 we have collectively called $\phi^r$. Because $\Delta^n(x)$ is quadratic in the fields when $\chi^n$ is a gauge or matter field or $\omega_\alpha$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:17.1.1} does not in general tell us that the effective action is invariant under the same BRST transformations as the action itself.

To handle this complication, we employ a trick that proves useful in dealing with any sort of nilpotent symmetry transformation. First, we introduce a set of c-number external fields $K_n(x)$, and define a new effective action by
\begin{equation}
\Gamma[\chi,K]\equiv W[J_{\chi,K},K]
-\int d^4x\,\chi^n(x)J_{\chi,K\,n}(x),
\tag{17.1.3}\label{eq:17.1.3}
\end{equation}
where the connected vacuum persistence amplitude $W$ is here calculated with the gauge-fixed action\footnote{Here $I$ is the action $I_{\mathrm{NEW}}$ modified as described in Section 15.7 to depend on the ghost and antighost fields $\omega_\alpha$ and $\omega_\alpha^*$ and on the auxiliary field $h_\alpha$, with the subscript `NEW' dropped from now on.} $I+\int d^4x\,\Delta^nK_n$:
\begin{equation}
e^{iW[J,K]}\equiv
\int\left[\prod_{n,x}d\chi^n(x)\right]
\exp\left(iI+i\int d^4x\,\Delta^nK_n
+i\int d^4x\,\chi^nJ_n\right),
\tag{17.1.4}\label{eq:17.1.4}
\end{equation}
and $J_{\chi,K}$ is the current required to give the fields the expectation values $\chi$ in the presence of the external fields $K$:
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{\delta_R W[J,K]}{\delta J_n(x)}\right|_{J=J_{\chi,K}}
=\chi^n(x).
\tag{17.1.5}\label{eq:17.1.5}
\end{equation}
(The $K_n$ must have the same fermionic or bosonic statistics as $\Delta^n$, which is opposite to that of $\chi^n$.) Since the BRST transformation is nilpotent, the quantities $\Delta^n(x)$ are BRST-invariant, so in the same way as in Section 16.4 we can show that the new effective action $\Gamma[\chi,K]$ satisfies a BRST-invariance condition:
\begin{equation}
\int d^4x\,\langle\Delta^n(x)\rangle_{J_{\chi,K}}
\frac{\delta_L\Gamma[\chi,K]}{\delta\chi^n(x)}=0,
\tag{17.1.6}\label{eq:17.1.6}
\end{equation}
where $\langle\cdots\rangle_{J,K}$ denotes a vacuum expectation value calculated in the presence of the current $J$ and the external fields $K$:
\begin{equation}
\langle\mathcal O[\chi]\rangle_{J,K}\equiv
\frac{
\int\left[\prod_{n,x}d\chi^n(x)\right]\mathcal O[\chi]
\exp\left(iI+i\int d^4x\,\Delta^nK_n+i\int d^4x\,\chi^nJ_n\right)
}{
\int\left[\prod_{n,x}d\chi^n(x)\right]
\exp\left(iI+i\int d^4x\,\Delta^nK_n+i\int d^4x\,\chi^nJ_n\right)
}.
\tag{17.1.7}\label{eq:17.1.7}
\end{equation}
It is convenient to express the expectation value of $\Delta^n$ as a variational derivative of the effective action. Taking the right variational derivative of Eq.~\eqref{eq:17.1.3} with respect to $K$ gives
\begin{align*}
\frac{\delta_R\Gamma[\chi,K]}{\delta K_n(x)}
={}&\left.\frac{\delta_R W[J,K]}{\delta K_n(x)}\right|_{J=J_{\chi,K}}
+\int d^4y\,
\left.\frac{\delta_R W[J,K]}{\delta J_m(y)}\right|_{J=J_{\chi,K}}
\frac{\delta_R J_{\chi,K\,m}(y)}{\delta K_n(x)}
\\
&-\int d^4y\,\chi^m(y)
\frac{\delta_R J_{\chi,K\,m}(y)}{\delta K_n(x)}.
\end{align*}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:17.1.5} we see that the last two terms cancel, and using the definitions \eqref{eq:17.1.4} and \eqref{eq:17.1.7} gives us then our desired relation
\begin{equation}
\frac{\delta_R\Gamma[\chi,K]}{\delta K_n(x)}
=\left.\frac{\delta_R W[J,K]}{\delta K_n(x)}\right|_{J=J_{\chi,K}}
=\langle\Delta^n(x)\rangle_{J_{\chi,K},K}.
\tag{17.1.8}\label{eq:17.1.8}
\end{equation}
The BRST symmetry condition \eqref{eq:17.1.6} may now be written as a simple condition, the Zinn--Justin equation, involving the effective action alone:
\begin{equation}
\int d^4x\,
\frac{\delta_R\Gamma[\chi,K]}{\delta K_n(x)}
\frac{\delta_L\Gamma[\chi,K]}{\delta\chi^n(x)}=0.
\tag{17.1.9}\label{eq:17.1.9}
\end{equation}
As remarked after Eq.~\eqref{eq:15.9.3}, the interchange of fields and antifields (or in this case $\chi^n$ and $K_n$) results simply in a change of sign of the left-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:17.1.9}, so this can be written as
\begin{equation}
(\Gamma,\Gamma)=0,
\tag{17.1.10}\label{eq:17.1.10}
\end{equation}
where the antibracket is calculated here with $K_n$ in place of the antifield of $\chi^n$:
\begin{align}
(F,G)\equiv{}&\int d^4x\,
\frac{\delta_R F[\chi,K]}{\delta\chi^n(x)}
\frac{\delta_L G[\chi,K]}{\delta K_n(x)}
\notag\\
&-\int d^4x\,
\frac{\delta_R F[\chi,K]}{\delta K_n(x)}
\frac{\delta_L G[\chi,K]}{\delta\chi^n(x)}.
\tag{17.1.11}\label{eq:17.1.11}
\end{align}
This is formally the same as the Batalin--Vilkovisky `master equation' discussed in Section 15.9, but appears here as a constraint on the quantum effective action $\Gamma[\chi,K]$ rather than on the fundamental action $S[\chi,\chi^{\ddagger}]$. The Zinn--Justin equation \eqref{eq:17.1.10} will be used in the next two sections to show how to renormalize gauge theories, and in Section 22.6 to study anomalies in these theories.
