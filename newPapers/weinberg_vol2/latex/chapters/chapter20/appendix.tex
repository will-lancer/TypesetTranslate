\chapterappendix{}{Momentum Flow: The General Case}
\label{app:20.A}

In this appendix we shall consider the asymptotic behavior of an amplitude in a general renormalizable quantum field theory when the momenta of any set of two or more external lines become large, taking into account operators containing arbitrary numbers of field factors and derivatives, with dimensionality up to some limit $N$. To deal with this problem we shall have to introduce a more compact notation than that of Sections 20.1 or 20.2. A letter $\ell$, $\ell'$, etc. will denote a set of external lines $i$ of specified types entering or leaving a Feynman diagram or part of a Feynman diagram. A letter $k$, $k'$, etc. will denote the set of four-momenta $k_i$ of such lines, subject to the condition that their sum has some fixed value $p$. The amplitude $\Gamma_{\ell\ell'}(k,k',p)$ is the sum of all graphs with a set $\ell$ of incoming lines carrying momenta $k$ and a set $\ell'$ of outgoing lines carrying momenta $k'$, including the final bare propagators for the set $\ell$ but not $\ell'$.

As shown by the power-counting theorem~\hyperref[ch20-ref-3]{[3]} quoted in Section 12.1, the part of the region of integration where the momenta of order $k$ flow only through a subgraph with external lines $\ell$ and $\ell''$ makes a contribution to $\Gamma_{\ell\ell'}(k,k',p)$ of order $k^{d(\ell,\ell'')}$, where $d(\ell,\ell'')$ is the dimensionality (in powers of mass or momentum) of this subgraph:
\begin{equation}
d(\ell,\ell'')
=4-\sum_{i\in\ell,\ell''}(1+s_i)
-\sum_{i\in\ell}(2-2s_i).
\tag{20.A.1}\label{eq:20.A.1}
\end{equation}
(The last term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.1} arises from the propagators for the lines in the set $\ell$.) It will be convenient to rewrite Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.1} as
\begin{equation}
d(\ell,\ell')
=4-4n(\ell)-N(\ell')+N(\ell),
\tag{20.A.2}\label{eq:20.A.2}
\end{equation}
where $n(\ell)$ is the number of lines in the set $\ell$, and $N(\ell)$ is the total dimensionality of the fields for these lines:
\begin{equation}
n(\ell)\equiv\sum_{i\in\ell}1,
\qquad
N(\ell)\equiv\sum_{i\in\ell}(s_i+1).
\tag{20.A.3}\label{eq:20.A.3}
\end{equation}
Here $s_i$ is the `spin' of a line of type $i$, in the sense used in Section 12.3: the dimensionality of the field of type $i$ is $1+s_i$, and the bare propagator of such a field goes as $k^{-2+2s_i}$. (For scalars and gauge bosons, $s_i=0$; for spin $1/2$, $s_i=1/2$.)

By taking account of the asymptotic behavior associated with these subgraphs, we wish to show that as $k\to\infty$ (all components going to infinity together, in generic directions), with $k'$ and $p$ fixed, the asymptotic behavior of $\Gamma_{\ell\ell'}(k,k',p)$ in each order of perturbation theory is of the form
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\ell\ell'}(k,k',p)
\longrightarrow
\sum_{\mathcal O}^{(N)}
U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell}(k)
F_{\mathcal O,\ell'}(k',p)
+o\left(k^{4-4n(\ell)+N(\ell)-N}\right),
\tag{20.A.4}\label{eq:20.A.4}
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over operators $\mathcal O$ with dimensionality $N(\mathcal O)\leq N$; the function $U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell}(k)$ is of order $k^{4+N(\ell)-4n(\ell)-N(\mathcal O)}$; and $o(k^A)$ denotes terms that vanish faster (by at least one factor $1/k$) than $k^A$.

To isolate the contribution of operators of dimensionality $\leq N$, we shall define an `$N$-irreducible' amplitude $I_{\ell\ell'}^N(k,k',p)$ as the sum of all graphs for $\Gamma_{\ell\ell'}(k,k',p)$ in which the lines in the set $\ell$ cannot be disconnected from the lines in the set $\ell'$ by cutting through any set $\ell''$ of internal lines with $N(\ell'')\leq N$. Since the difference $\Gamma-I^N$ consists of graphs that can be disconnected in this way, it may be written as
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\ell\ell'}(k,k',p)-I_{\ell\ell'}^N(k,k',p)
=\sum_{\ell''}^{(N)}
\int dk''\,
I_{\ell\ell''}^N(k,k'',p)
\Gamma_{\ell''\ell'}(k'',k',p),
\tag{20.A.5}\label{eq:20.A.5}
\end{equation}
where $\sum_{\ell''}^{(N)}$ denotes the sum over sets $\ell''$ of particle lines with $N(\ell'')\leq N$, and $\int dk''$ is the integral over the components of the four-momenta in the set $\ell''$, subject to the constraint that the sum of these momenta is $p$.

The asymptotic behavior of the kernel $I_{\ell\ell''}^N(k,k'',p)$ is much simpler than that of $\Gamma_{\ell\ell''}(k,k'',p)$. For $k\to\infty$ with $k''$ and $p$ fixed it is dominated by the part of the region of integration where every internal line carries a momentum of order $k$, which gives an asymptotic behavior $k^{d(\ell,\ell'')}$, because the terms where only some subregion carries such large momenta would have to be connected to the rest of the graph by a bridge $\ell'''$ of lines with $N(\ell''')>N\geq N(\ell'')$, and this would be smaller as $k\to\infty$ by at least a factor $k^{N(\ell'')-N-1}$. Thus differentiating $I_{\ell\ell''}^N(k,k'',p)$ with respect to any component of $k''$ or $p$ reduces its asymptotic behavior to order $k^{d(\ell,\ell'')-1}$. But differentiating $d$ times lowers the asymptotic behavior to $k^{d(\ell,\ell'')-d}$ only if $d\leq N-N(\ell'')+1$, because for higher derivatives we get a larger contribution from the region of integration where the subgraph carrying momenta of order $k$ is connected to the rest of the graph by a bridge of lines $\ell'''$ with total dimensionality greater than $N$, with the derivatives all acting on lines carrying momenta of order $k''$ or $p$. Therefore in order to take into account the contribution of operators containing derivatives of fields, we may write the asymptotic behavior of $I^N$ as
\begin{equation}
I_{\ell\ell''}^N(k,k'',p)
=
\sum_{\nu:d_\nu+N(\ell'')\leq N}
I_{\ell\ell''\nu}^N(k)
P_{\ell''\nu}(k'',p)
+o\left(k^{d(\ell,\ell'')-N+N(\ell'')}\right),
\tag{20.A.6}\label{eq:20.A.6}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\ell''\nu}(k'',p)$ are a complete set of homogeneous polynomials of order $d_\nu$ in the $n(\ell'')$ momenta $k''$ and $p$, and $I_{\ell\ell''\nu}^N(k)$ are functions only of $k$, of order $k^{d(\ell,\ell'')-d_\nu}$ as $k\to\infty$.

We cannot immediately use Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.6} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.5}, because the integral over $k''$ receives important contributions from the region where some of the $k''$ are of order $k$. To deal with this, we use mathematical induction: we assume that Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.4} holds up to some given order in perturbation theory, and use it on the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.5} to calculate the asymptotic behavior of $\Gamma$ to the next order of perturbation theory. We rewrite Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.5} in the form
\begin{align}
\Gamma_{\ell\ell'}(k,k',p)
={}&I_{\ell\ell'}^N(k,k',p)
\notag\\
&+\sum_{\ell''}^{(N)}
\int dk''\,I_{\ell\ell''}^N(k,k'',p)
\left[
\Gamma_{\ell''\ell'}(k'',k',p)
-\sum_{\mathcal O}^{(N)}
U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell''}(k'')
F_{\mathcal O,\ell'}(k',p)
\right]
\notag\\
&+\sum_{\mathcal O}^{(N)}
F_{\mathcal O,\ell'}(k',p)
\sum_{\ell'':N(\ell'')\leq N}
\int dk''\,I_{\ell\ell''}^N(k,k'',p)
U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell''}(k'').
\tag{20.A.7}\label{eq:20.A.7}
\end{align}
According to Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.4}, the quantity in square brackets in the second term on the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.7} vanishes for $k''\to\infty$ faster than $(k'')^{4-4n(\ell'')+N(\ell'')-N}$, so the product of this factor with a polynomial $P_{\ell''\nu}(k'')$ of order $\nu\leq N-N(\ell'')$ vanishes faster than $(k'')^{4-4n(\ell'')}$, and therefore has a finite integral over the $4(n(\ell'')-1)$ independent components of $k''$.\footnote{This overlooks the possibility that even though power-counting indicates the convergence of the integral in the second term on the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.7} over the region where all $k''$ go to infinity together, for $n(\ell'')\geq3$ subintegrations may diverge. For this reason the arguments given in this appendix do not constitute a proof of the operator product expansion except for the simple case treated in Section 20.2, where we consider the momenta of just two external lines to go to infinity, and we look for the terms in the power series expansion associated with operators that are quadratic in the fields.} Hence we may use Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.6} in this term, and find
\begingroup
\allowdisplaybreaks[0]
\begin{align}
\Gamma_{\ell\ell'}(k,k',p)
\longrightarrow{}&
\sum_{\nu:d_\nu+N(\ell')\leq N}
I_{\ell\ell'\nu}^N(k)
P_{\ell'\nu}(k',p)
\notag\\
&+\sum_{\ell''}^{(N)}
\sum_{\nu:d_\nu+N(\ell'')\leq N}
I_{\ell\ell''\nu}^N(k)
\int dk''\,P_{\ell''\nu}(k'',p)
\notag\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{18mm}\cdot
\left[
\Gamma_{\ell''\ell'}(k'',k',p)
-\sum_{\mathcal O}^{(N)}
U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell''}(k'')
F_{\mathcal O,\ell'}(k',p)
\right]
\notag\\
&+\sum_{\mathcal O}^{(N)}
F_{\mathcal O,\ell'}(k',p)
\sum_{\ell''}^{(N)}
\int dk''\,I_{\ell\ell''}^N(k,k'',p)
U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell''}(k''),
\tag{20.A.8}\label{eq:20.A.8}
\end{align}
\endgroup
the correction being smaller than the terms shown by a factor $1/k$. (Of course, the first term on the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.8} is present only if $N(\ell')\leq N$.)

Now, for each value of $\ell$ and $\nu$ with $d_\nu+N(\ell)\leq N$, there is an operator $\mathcal O$ with field factors corresponding to the lines in $\ell$ and with $d_\nu$ derivatives, such that in zeroth-order perturbation theory the vertex function, with incoming momentum $p$ carried by the operator $\mathcal O$ and outgoing momenta $k$ carried by external lines $\ell$, is the polynomial $P_{\ell\nu}(k,p)$. Then the corresponding complete vertex function for a renormalized operator $\mathcal O_R=\sum_{\mathcal O'}Z_{\mathcal O,\mathcal O'}\mathcal O'$ is
\begin{align}
F_{\mathcal O,\ell}(k,p)
=\sum_{\mathcal O'}Z_{\mathcal O,\mathcal O'}
\Bigg\{&
\delta_{\ell_{\mathcal O'},\ell}
P_{\ell\nu_{\mathcal O'}}(k,p)
\notag\\
&+\int dk'\,
P_{\ell_{\mathcal O'}\nu_{\mathcal O'}}(k',p)
\Gamma_{\ell_{\mathcal O'}\ell}(k',k,p)
\Bigg\},
\tag{20.A.9}\label{eq:20.A.9}
\end{align}
where $\ell_{\mathcal O}$ and $\nu_{\mathcal O}$ label the types of fields and spacetime derivatives in the operator $\mathcal O$. We see then that Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.4} is satisfied, with
\begin{align}
U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell}(k)
={}&
\sum_{\mathcal O'}^{(N)}
I_{\ell\mathcal O'}^N(k)
\left[
Z_{\mathcal O',\mathcal O}^{-1}
-\int dk''\,
U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell_{\mathcal O'}}(k'')
P_{\mathcal O'}(k'')
\right]
\notag\\
&+\sum_{\ell''}^{(N)}
\int dk''\,
I_{\ell\ell''}^N(k,k'')
U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell''}(k''),
\tag{20.A.10}\label{eq:20.A.10}
\end{align}
in which we now use the abbreviations
\begin{equation}
I_{\ell\mathcal O}(k)
\equiv I_{\ell\ell_{\mathcal O}\nu_{\mathcal O}}(k),
\qquad
P_{\mathcal O}(k)
\equiv P_{\ell_{\mathcal O}\nu_{\mathcal O}}(k).
\tag{20.A.11}\label{eq:20.A.11}
\end{equation}
(The $p$ dependence of $\Gamma_{\ell_{\mathcal O''}\ell'}$ is dropped for the same reason as in Section 20.2: whether or not $k''$ is comparable with $k$, $p$ is negligible here compared with $k$.) We will define the normalization constant $Z_{\mathcal O',\mathcal O}$ so that at a renormalization point $k(\mu),p(\mu)$ the function $F_{\mathcal O,\ell}(k(\mu),p(\mu))$ has the same value $\delta_{\ell\ell_{\mathcal O}}P_{\mathcal O}(k(\mu),p(\mu))$ that it would have in the absence of interactions:
\begin{align}
\delta_{\ell\ell_{\mathcal O}}
P_{\mathcal O}\bigl(k(\mu),p(\mu)\bigr)
&=F_{\mathcal O,\ell}\bigl(k(\mu),p(\mu)\bigr)
\notag\\
&=\sum_{\mathcal O'}Z_{\mathcal O,\mathcal O'}
\Bigg\{
\delta_{\ell_{\mathcal O'},\ell}
P_{\ell\nu_{\mathcal O'}}\bigl(k(\mu),p(\mu)\bigr)
\notag\\
&\hspace{24mm}
+\int dk'\,
P_{\ell_{\mathcal O'}\nu_{\mathcal O'}}\bigl(k',p(\mu)\bigr)
\Gamma_{\ell_{\mathcal O'}\ell}
\bigl(k',k(\mu),p(\mu)\bigr)
\Bigg\}.
\tag{20.A.12}\label{eq:20.A.12}
\end{align}
For $\Gamma=0$ this has a solution $Z=\mathbf{1}$ which is unique (because the polynomials for a given set of lines are supposed to be linearly independent), so by continuity Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.12} will have a unique solution for coupling constants in some finite range. Eq.~\eqref{eq:20.A.10} therefore provides a recursive definition of the coefficient functions $U_{\mathcal O}^{\ell}(k)$ appearing in the general operator product expansion \eqref{eq:20.A.4}.
